I set out on the road again with Zhu Qizhen, but the journey was not as peaceful as I'd expected.

"What a pathetic fool. Why did you just send the horse away? Were We of no concern to you at all? Tsk."

"Shh! Walk quietly."

"This wouldn't have happened if you hadn't sent it away! You showed consideration to a lowly beast even while forcing Us to walk like this, and now you dare speak so presumptuously?! If you insist, then you may carry Us on your back!"

Not even a moment after regaining his senses, Zhu Qizhen began to press me about why I had sent the horse away.

An emperor, a Son of Heaven... my wonderment was shorter than a fleeting dream. If I could turn back time, I would stop myself from briefly thinking of him as a former emperor, even if it meant shattering the Qianding Acupoint of my Governing Vessel.

"Didn't mud just splash on me again? Must We tread on such a filthy road?"

He was like that the entire time!

Eventually, I stopped walking again and shouted, as if to lecture him.

"If it weren't for that _lowly beast_, your precious life would be over!"

"Hmph! It was but a barbarian's horse!"

Was he saying that it was fine to drive a horse into death by exhaustion so long as it belonged to a barbarian? A feeling of injustice welled up in my chest.

"Does a horse choose its master? Even a mere piece of steel, depending on its wielder, may become a sword that kills or a sword that saves..."

That. That was true, but...

"...Why did you stop talking mid-sentence?" Zhu Qizhen asked.

I couldn't continue because a question had suddenly occurred to me.

_Have I, until now, thought of the Demonic Cult as something worse than barbarians?_

As the son of a Murim Alliance lord and the Commander of the Wind Dragon Brigade, the Demonic Cult was a target that had to be annihilated.

So I was taught, and so I was raised.

However—

_Is being a follower of the Demonic Cult a sin punishable by death?_

The Demonic Cult I had met firsthand was remarkably different from the one I had imagined.

The mere practice of demonic arts did not automatically make one evil, and merely opposing the laws of the Ming did not mean they didn't uphold their own morality.

The Seven Hallowed Palaces were like martial sects, and the lay followers were no different from the common folk of the Central Plains.

As the saying went, humans are close by nature, and separated by habits. Weren't we all similar in nature, only to be driven apart by our customs?

"Hmph, fine, if you don't want to talk. We shall rest here for a while, so be aware of that."

_Shwaaaaa—_

Snapping out of my thoughts at Zhu Qizhen's words, I saw a clear river flowing through the gorge just below us. It was so transparent that if not for the ripples, one might have thought it was empty.

"We are thirsty!" he shouted, plopping down near the water's edge.

"What are you talking about? If you're thirsty, there's water right there."

Truly, an incomprehensible man. I shook my head and walked over to stand nearby.

Then.

"What are you doing? Didn't I say I was thirsty? Hurry up and drink some first."

"Why should I?"

"What if there's poison in it?"

What do you mean 'what if'? It would just become your death anniversary.

I ignored him, cupped my hands, and took a gulp.

"It's cool and refreshing."

Only then did Zhu Qizhen approach with a sullen face and drink the water.

Fed up, I asked him.

"Have you always lived like that?"

"What do you mean?"

"I'm asking if you've lived so strictly that you couldn't even ride a horse or get your own drink of water."

"Is that... strange?"

There was no other intention behind Zhu Qizhen's question. It was just a pure, simple question.

So I couldn't just say yes.

Then, this time, Zhu Qizhen asked, "Then what about you? What life did you lead to become so crude and reckless?"

Well.

Isn't it kind of if you lived as an emperor and then were reborn as the Khan of the Northern Plains?

"...The nature of the place called Murim is different."

"Murim? Then are you a martial artist?"

When I gave a slight nod, Zhu Qizhen's eyes widened and he ran over like a child to ask.

"Really? I have heard of the Murim. What kind of place is it? Tell me already!"

"Why are you so excited? Are you a child?"

"I'm just curious. I heard there are many strange people there. Monks and Taoists who train in martial arts. And... yes! I heard even the beggars have formed a faction. Is that right?"

"You must mean the Beggars' Gang. Yes, that's right. We call all of them the Murim Alliance..."

As distant memories surfaced, a serene smile spread across my face on its own.

At this, Zhu Qizhen also smiled along, asking excitedly.

"I heard that they are truly free. They say that even before coming of age, they wander all over the Central Plains, making friends and memories, right?" Zhu Qizhen asked, his face alight with excitement.

But we were not as free as he described.

One must abide by the Murim's own unwritten laws, and follow the rules of one's clan or sect. If I were to go into details, there would be no end.

Still.

"Yes."

I replied with a smile.

Because the nostalgia of those days was clearly telling me so.

"I see. So that's how it is. The Murim..."

"As long as you're born a member of one faction, there is no such thing as unrestrained freedom. It's just that... a martial artist has a wider range of options. Everyone has limitations. That's why there are bureaucrats and an emperor, to allow for diversity and the choice to pursue what one wants."

I said it casually, but Zhu Qizhen seemed to be lost in deep thought. After a while of silence, he opened his mouth to ask again.

"Why did you come here?"

"I told you. I came to find those trapped in the prison."

"So you are a righteous person."

"Maybe..."

_Am_ I righteous? Or is this just a learned habit?

I suddenly recalled the slaughter I had committed by the yurt not too long ago.

How was I any different from a bloodthirsty demon?

"In that case, are you in the Murim Alliance?"

Uh, um. I'm in the Demonic Cult.

"Why are you looking at me like that?"

Like a fading scent, I was no longer part of the Murim Alliance.

I can no longer go back.

I won't be able to stay in Shanxi, and I might have to cross swords with the Wind Dragon Brigade.

Because Yang Jagak is dead.

I am...

"...a cultist."

"What...? So are you a Hindu?"

"No."

"Then?"

"...The Demonic Cult. The Shining Light Cult in the Heavenly Mountains is where I live."

"The... the Demonic Cult!"

He stumbled back three or four steps, utterly horrified.

"A... a traitor! H-how could you..."

I wanted to ask him the same thing— him, and Yang Jagak.

"How... do I look to you? Like a demonic beast that craves blood and devours flesh?"

Zhu Qizhen said nothing.

However, his violently trembling eyes gave me the answer.

So that's how it is.

Am I a traitor now, a demon? Just because I belong to the Demonic Cult?

A bitter feeling swept through my chest.

However...

"It doesn't matter if I'm a traitor. You can call me a demon all you want. But I'm going to save you, and I'm going to save the prisoners. That's what brought me here..."

For without that reason, I would have no place to stand.

No matter who I am, or where I am. I will do what I must.

Even if that means being a traitor and the Sect Leader of the Demonic Cult.

"To be a burden to a traitor..."

Even if others couldn't accept it.

The sun set, and a crescent moon, like a fingernail clipping, rose in the sky; we built a fire in a secluded spot and tried to sleep, and then the sky brightened once more.

Zhu Qizhen didn't say a single word the entire time, just stared into space or lay lost in thought. That was all he did.

I, too, kept my mouth shut.

I didn't want to challenge the conclusion he had reached.

I would have been the same.

It wasn't a matter of right or wrong, just different.

We walked in silence, rested our bodies, and then walked again. And after a day and a half, we crossed a mountain ridge, and the prison we had been searching for finally came into view.

The sight was truly horrific.

On a field surrounded by mountains on three sides, hundreds of square cages made of yellow-brown birchwood were packed with countless people, leaving no room to even sit.

A whole spectrum of humanity was laid out before me. Hundreds? No, thousands. Even more than that.

After staring for a long time from a distance, Zhu Qizhen muttered as if he had spotted someone he knew.

"Commandant...?"

His voice trembled. Following his gaze, I saw warriors in yellowish robes with dragon patterns locked away.

One of them stood with a straight posture, his gaze fixed forward, unwavering. It was as if he were waiting for someone.

I knew of them as well. I had never met them, but I knew what they did.

No, how many people wouldn't know? They were emblazoned with the flying fish insignia of the Embroidered Uniform Guard.

"They were all alive? Truly... Mmph!"

Just as Zhu Qizhen cried out and tried to rush forward, I grabbed the hem of his clothes. I also clamped a hand over his mouth.

"Stop."

When I removed my hand, he glared at me with displeasure and demanded, "What are you doing?"

"Something's not right."

"What are you talking about? We have to save them..."

Zhu Qizhen must have sensed the strangeness in the unnatural scene, because he clamped his mouth shut.

There were thousands of people imprisoned here. Not only lay members of the Demonic Cult but also imperial soldiers were bound.

And yet... there was no one. Not a single person was guarding this place.

This couldn't possibly be a coincidence. So...

_A trap._

Closing my eyes, I opened my senses to my surroundings. I felt the deep sighs and shallow breaths of those trapped in the prison, then the rustling of the bushes and the currents of the wind.

And—

_Is that him?_

Amidst the hundreds of refined energies, a presence as immense as a great mountain suddenly washed over me.

I snapped my eyes open and scanned the area, feeling as if sharp thorns were hidden in the bushes ahead.

_I was short-sighted. From the direction of our flight, it would be easy for him to guess we were heading for the prison. And Zhu Qizhen is..._

The emperor.

They'd naturally predict that he would head to his imprisoned soldiers.

"Why... are you acting like this?"

"...It's a trap. They're in the forest over there."

"I see... is that all?"

I thought he might suggest we flee immediately or make a fuss, but he remained calm without any change in expression.

Instead, he suddenly straightened his clothes and asked me, "How do I look?"

"What?"

"Do I look like an emperor?"

Not at all. He looked like a country bumpkin.

His clothes were loose, frayed, and tattered, stained and dirty in several places.

"I may not be wearing the dragon robe, but I am still the emperor of this nation. Just as you may be a traitor, but you are the righteous man who saved me."

What was his real intention in bringing this up so suddenly?

I looked at him, filled with questions. But he had turned his body and was muttering while looking only at the prisoners.

"When I turned nine, I ascended the dragon throne knowing nothing. Deceived by the eunuch Wang Jin, I sentenced people I didn't even know to death. I led a half-million-strong army against the invasion of the Oirats, only to suffer a crushing defeat."

Wang Jin... was he the eunuch who had abused his power, the one I found dead with his legs cut off?

But why is he talking about this now?

"Several months have already passed since my capture in Hebei. What do you think they've had me do all this time?"

Has it been that long? I'd been trapped in the Heavenly Demon Cave, so I had no way of knowing.

"Well...?"

"I fed and bathed their horses in the stables."

...How miserable.

"And?"

"Was I the Emperor, or the Minister of Horse Affairs?"

"What are you trying to say?"

His sudden words left a strange feeling in me. Zhu Qizhen gave a hollow laugh and asked again, "Do you know what I thought about while I was imprisoned?"

A single-minded desire to return and get revenge? Concern for the safety of the imperial family? Various thoughts flashed through my mind, but no clear answer emerged.

Just then, Zhu Qizhen smiled brightly, turned to me, and said, "It was just one thought. I wanted to live. I wanted to live, even if it meant giving up the Dragon Throne in the Hall of Supreme Harmony. What do you think? Am I not a truly pathetic emperor?"

I couldn't give him any answer.

I wanted to ask how he could think that way as an emperor, to tell him that he shouldn't. But his eyes were so sorrowful that I couldn't bring myself to say it.

He smiled gently and took my hand; I lowered my head and gazed at his small, grimy one.

"I never once wished to be emperor. I just became one someday. I never had the freedom or the opportunity to choose... so I always wanted to step down from this position. But now that it's happened as I wished, I feel this greed growing inside me. Isn't it funny?"

What on earth is he talking about? I can't understand a single word. Why is he acting so unsettling? I urgently grabbed his hand and asked, "What are you doing right now?"

"Even if I go back, there will be no place for me. I'll just be the former emperor. My younger brother, the Prince of Cheng Zhu Qiyu[^1], will already be sitting on the dragon throne he coveted so much."

"What...?"
