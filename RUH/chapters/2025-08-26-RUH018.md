_—Valley of Sorrow, guest pavilion_

Yayul Tak paced around, unable to sit still. He couldn't stop biting his thumb, his discomfort on full display for a single reason.

"Seriously, where in the world did he go? This is driving me crazy!"

It was all because the Young Cult Leader had vanished without a word.

Yayul Tak could tolerate being drugged yesterday. But whatever the Young Cult Leader had done during the day, the current situation was beyond words.

"How could the Palace of Minor Light abandon us like this? Is this the doctrine we are supposed to follow?"

"That's right!"

Peeking his head out, he could see hundreds of lay followers gathered in front of the building. Their once dazed eyes were gone, now filled with a fighting spirit. It was as if they had awakened overnight, like warriors rising up against oppression.

"What in the world happened? I'm losing my mind. At this rate, they're going to break down the door. Hey. You know anything?"

Yayul Tak turned his head and asked irritably. In front of him, Eul Haryeong sat primly, her face was stiff as she replied, clearly just as confused.

"I don't know either."

How could she know? Eul Haryeong was just as frustrated. He had left as if he was going to save her father, but where had he disappeared to?

"Dammit! Not a single day has been peaceful since he returned. Maybe I should just..."

"Why? Are you trying to leave the Palace of Minor Light? If you wish, you may remain in the Valley of Sorrow. I'll allow it."

"Y-Your Reverence!"

A captivating, deep voice echoed through the building. When Yayul Tak turned his head, there he was, with his long, fine hair and enchanting eyes.

"Where on earth have you been? How did you get in? Did you see outside? No. This isn't the time for that. We need to hide..."

"Why should I?"

"No, I'm telling you to look outside! They'll tear you apart if you go out there now."

"There's no need to make a fuss."

Despite Yayul Tak's words, Ma Cheonso confidently strode toward the window and calmly surveyed the sight, as if nothing were amiss.

At that moment, the crowd erupted.

"I-It's the Young Cult Leader!"

"The Young Cult Leader has appeared!"

"Young Cult Leader, you who abandoned the lay followers, come out at once!"

Swept up in a thick mob mentality, the usual honorifics had vanished, and a deafening roar of shouts poured in.

In response, Ma Cheonso sat down at a side table with a placid expression.

He seemed to know something. Eul Haryeong and Yayul Tak looked at him expectantly, but there were no answers. He simply kept his silence, his fingers interlaced, as if waiting for someone.

Eventually, the commotion outside began to subside. When Yayul Tak peeked out, it looked as though the gathered crowd was making way.

_CRASH!_

Before he could say anything, a noise like a door being smashed echoed from the bottom of the stairs, and a tremor began to rise.

_Thud, shhhk. Thud. Shhhh._

Someone was definitely coming up. A strange, scraping sound accompanied the steps, as if someone was dragging a sack of barley.

_Gulp._ Eul Haryeong swallowed a dry lump in her throat.

The moment the sound reached their vicinity, a middle-aged man finally revealed himself. His sharp eyes radiated a rugged spirit. His hair was tied back neatly with a headband, and his gray robes fluttered.

He was like a tiger, and exuded the aura of a great master.

"Ah...?"

Eul Haryeong's eyes trembled uncontrollably. She staggered with shallow, weak breaths.

Her head was telling her no, but her heart was pounding. _Thump. Thump._ Was this what her father, whom she had longed for somewhere deep inside, would have looked like when healthy?

Strangely, tears welled up in her eyes.

"Ah... I'm sorry."

Eul Haryeong forced back her tears and bowed her head deeply, squeezing her eyes shut.

That was definitely not her father. He didn't have her father's deep wrinkles, stooped frame, or childlike demeanor. This was surely a great lord she had no right to look upon... she must have committed a great discourtesy.

So she thought.

_Thud, thud._

She felt a shadow fall over her, and a pair of large feet came into her view.

Was he going to punish her for her offense?

"...raise your head. I will not let you live in fear anymore."

A deep, heavy voice.

Haryeong cautiously raised her head.

It was clearly not him, so what was this feeling?

"I'm sorry. Haryeong. This worthless father of yours... I'm sorry."

Her sight blurred, and the tears she had been holding back burst forth.

"_Sob... sniff..._ Is it really... you, Father? Is it... really you?"

Her childlike father, whose back was so bent they could see eye to eye— now, he held her against a chest as sturdy as a great mountain.

"Haryeong... I'm sorry. From now on, this father will protect you. I promise I will."

She felt his hands petting her gently.

Only then did Haryeong believe... this man was her father, Eul Taesik.

"Father... _sob... sob..._"

She cried, and he cried, and their tears flowed unceasingly. Not tears of sorrow, but tears of joy.

"Young Cult Leader, what is this? Why are they doing this right here? Is this a family reunion?"

"In such moments, you should just let them be."

There was also Yayul Tak, watching in bewilderment, and Ma Cheonso, smiling inwardly with satisfaction.

_—Shining Light Cult, Water Peak Market_

_SHWAAAAA_. The rain poured down so fiercely, even the leaves of the old trees couldn't keep up, fumbling droplets of water left and right.

The ever-bustling market at the Water Peak felt relatively deserted. The rainy season had begun. A significant number of shops had bolted their doors, the wooden planks fitted into their windows an indication that they would not be soon reopened.

Old man Choi, who managed the Shining Light Archives, also had few visitors for a change. He sat in a spacious chair, leaned his head back, and gently closed his eyes.

Hoo. A shallow, weary breath escaped him. Since the Palace of Minor Light's visit, and the subsequent orders of Jaegal Tong, the master of the Rogues' Guild, he had become much busier.

It was because they had doubled the amount of information being collected.

Normally, the Rogues' Guild started by having storytellers listen to the daily happenings from servants, musicians, courtesans, carpenters, boatmen, blacksmiths, paper-makers, and tanners scattered in various places, and giving them a small wage. The stories they heard were delivered as short, clipped documents to specific locations according to the place and time of the event. The Rogues' Guild would then collect and organize them into valuable intelligence.

Doubling the amount of information meant increasing the number of storytellers, which in turn meant the number of documents old man Choi had to collate had increased by more than five times. So, old man Choi was very pleased with this rare moment of rest.

Until they arrived.

_BANG, BANG!_

His heavy eyelids fluttered open at the sound of someone knocking on the door. He tried to close them again, assuming it was the sound of the rain, but whoever it was seemed to be in a great hurry.

_BANG, BANG!_

There was another loud knock on the door. Finally, he slapped the armrest, pushed himself up with effort, and opened the door. _CREEEAAAK._

And then.

"Wow, damn. Took you long enough to open up. Young Cult Leader, hurry and come in."

"What's the rush? You should learn to wait."

"What are you talking about? We're completely soaked from the rain."

"Haryeong, you go in too."

"You first, Father."

"Little tyke..."

Uninvited guests had stormed into the once peaceful Shining Light Archives.

{sep}

After lingering in the Valley of Sorrow for a few days, we stopped by the Shining Light Archives. It was partly because it was located in the same Water Peak, and also to take shelter from the rain, since Eul Haryeong had not learned martial arts.

But why are you looking at me like that, Senior? Are you that happy to see me?

He greeted us with a sarcastic smirk, one corner of his mouth drawing an arc as he tapped his fingers on the desk. It was clear he was not pleased.

"Young Cult Leader. I can't even begin to guess what this all means. Would you care to explain it to this foolish subordinate?"

Hearing Senior Jaegal Tong's words, I looked around. Eul Taesik and Eul Haryeong were exclaiming as they examined the portraits, while Yayul Tak had stripped down to his undergarments and was spreading his wet clothes on the floor.

It really was a total mess.

"We're on our way home from the Valley of Sorrow. We decided to make a quick stop on our way to the Palace because of the heavy rain."

I replied lightly and found a spot near Yayul Tak, plopping down.

He then rolled his wheelchair right up to me and answered in a loud voice for everyone to hear.

"Fufufu. Did my words sound difficult to you? Only just today I've learned that anyone can enter this place as they please."

At his words, Eul Haryeong's face fell in guilt, and Eul Taesik approached with a grave expression.

Yayul Tak... was busy drying his clothes.

Senior Jaegal was entirely correct. However...

I replied bluntly without even looking at him.

"You're the one who opened the door."

If it was truly not allowed, then Senior would not have opened the door in the first place. He wasn't the type to welcome me with open arms just because I had arrived.

After staring at each other for a long while, Jaegal Tong covered his mouth with his hand and let out a series of chuckles.

"Kuhu... kuhuhuhu..."

He didn't even look like a real person at first glance.

The Senior soon shook his head, approached Eul Taesik, and spoke with a clasped hands greeting.

"Now that you mention it, I did. I am Jaegal Tong. As you can see, I am in a state where I cannot even support my own body, so please forgive my discourtesy."

"I am Eul Taesik. I didn't expect the master of the Shining Light Library to live in such a gloomy place. Then again, it seems to suit you."

Eul Taesik replied while pacing around, not even making eye contact. A complete dismissal. Judging by his temperament, he was clearly displeased that Jaegal Tong had been sarcastic toward me.

"Could any place compare to the Valley of Sorrow? Kuhuhu. I had a feeling, but... as expected, you hadn't lost your martial arts."

"I heard you were the one who advised him to find me. How did you know to do so?"

"Well. Would it be a sufficient answer to say that I have been watching you with interest for a long time?"

"That's an answer I don't like. Spying from the shadows like a crow may be your job, but if you do it again, I will not stand for it."

"You make a difficult request as soon as we meet. You must be unaware of many things, having been in the Valley of Sorrow for ten years. I will watch over you carefully and catch you up."

"I'll accept, if you're still alive by then."

"It seems I have one more reason to live a long life."

"The reasons for you to die also continue to grow."

The martial and the scholarly, huh? They don't mix, like water and oil. I found myself needlessly worried about a future I couldn't even see.

The tense war of nerves intensified the humid air. Eul Haryeong's complexion had turned pale.

Hah. I swallowed a sigh and spoke as if to mediate.

"Enough. I will not permit disputes among the members of the Palace of Minor Light."

"As you command."

"Kuhuhu. As you wish."

They were good on their words, even while glaring at each other as if they wanted to kill one another.

I shook my head slowly and looked around at everyone with a serious tone.

"It is said that all under heaven are brothers. Though our beginnings may have been different, we have ultimately gathered in one place. Therefore, I hope for this: that we can combine our strength to restore the true doctrine as loyal members of the Palace of Minor Light. I, too, solemnly swear not to let you down."

This was my promise, as Yang Jagak. To embrace and carry those who have become family to the Palace of Minor Light, until the very end.

After my vow, which was more like a monologue, I looked around again. But...

"Ah... sure..."

"...As you command."

"Young Cult Leader... isn't it about time you got obsessed with something other than being a scholar? That gave me the creeps."

"Kukuku... Kuhahahaha!"

...vows were made to be broken, right?

{sep}

Having settled in, we exchanged brief greetings and spoke of the events in the Valley of Sorrow.

"So, has public sentiment in the Valley of Sorrow truly calmed down?"

No one could answer Jaegal Tong's question.

To state the conclusion, the third party known as the Star of Black Flames had gained a small measure of fame, but the name Ma Cheonso had only gained more _in_famy. It was the intended result, but it wasn't a pleasant feeling.

"Very well. Let us move on from that matter. What we must focus on from now on is undoubtedly the Seven Hallowed Palaces."

Jaegal Tong began to explain our future direction.

"The Young Cult Leader wanted those without affiliation, but we don't have time to be picky. The Great Heavenly Festival will be held in just four months, and we must achieve one of two things by then: either we increase our power, or we diminish theirs."

He pointed to the portraits of the Palace Lords right below the missing Cult Leader as he spoke.

The Lord of Martial Blood, Yayul Cheon, who held the greatest influence in the Demonic Cult.

The Lord of Murder, Guyang Ryung, who managed the assassins.

The Lady of Bliss, Han Soso, who was in charge of brothels and information.

The Lord of Merchants, Cheon Semin, who ran an escort agency and was in charge of commerce.

The Sacred Flame Saintess, Yoo Soha, who presided over the annual Shining Light Cult's Great Heavenly Festival and propagated its doctrines.

The Lord of Blades, Dan Jeonghak, who led a small group of warriors comprised solely of members of the Dan clan.

The Lord of Poison, Dongbang Eul, who researched medicine and poisons.

All of them were individuals who occupied the highest ranks in the Shining Light Cult's hierarchy; even though I already knew as much, a sense of awe washed over me again.

Each of the Seven Hallowed Palaces had its own independent armed force and possessed power comparable to the Nine Sects One Gang. Adding to that the Council of Ten Elders, whose members were all masters of the transcendent level or higher, even if their faction wasn't large, the Demonic Cult was like a sea that never ran dry.
