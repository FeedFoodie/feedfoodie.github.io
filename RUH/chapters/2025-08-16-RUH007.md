We finally reached our destination sometime after the sun had set behind the mountain pass. Despite the late hour, the place was teeming with a massive crowd; shops were lined up in a grid pattern across the wide plaza, making it feel completely packed.

"Is this a night market?"

"A night market? Nah. It's just that we're way to deep in the Shiwan[^1] Mountain Range. Ah, basically, the Great Heavenly Mountain where the Cult is based on has several peaks, and the followers are scattered across seven of them..."

Yayul Tak wiped away his sweat, looking exhausted from the journey, and continued his explanation.

"The tallest is the Sun Peak, where you get the Palace of Minor Light and the Palace of Shining Light. Then there are the Moon Peak, the Fire Peak, the Water Peak, the Wood Peak, the Metal Peak, and the Earth Peak. The Sun Peak is the Cult Leader's residence and main seat of power, so it has everything you could ever need, but the others aren't so lucky, you know. That's why people in other peaks have to come all the way to this market here on Water Peak."

"Hmm..."

"So, you know, some people arrive at night, some arrive during the day. That means it has to stay open at all times. It's more like a day-_and_-night market."

I had never considered the hardships of the followers living so high in the mountains. Merchants had to physically carry these goods here, and customers had to cross literal mountains to carry them back. It might be easier for those trained in martial arts, but for the average cultist, it would be a grueling life.

Yayul Tak resumed walking, and I followed him while studying our surroundings. There was plenty of ordinary folk, from children to the elderly. In fact, it was harder to find a martial artist than not.

A question suddenly popped into my head. "Does no one ever go down to find a better place to live?"

"Sir? Go down what? The Great Heavenly Mountain?"

"Yes."

"Ha, if we do, we'll get torn apart by all the orthodox and unorthodox sects, and the Imperial Army to boot. How could we possibly go down?" Yayul Tak chided me as if I'd asked something obvious.

"But that's because the Cult inflicted indiscriminate harm upon the Central Plains. It can simply stop doing so."

"Hey, it's not like we wanted to fight. The Emperor was the one who branded us a Demonic Cult to be executed on sight. We fought for our lives."

That might be true, but there was a contradiction in Yayul Tak's words. Countless lives had been lost to the Demonic Cult's invasions. Moreover, not even a single sack of rice was left in the places they passed through. They were as indiscriminate in their passage as a swarm of locusts.

"That's an excuse. The Cult's atrocities were far too excessive to have been for self-defense."

At my rebuke, Yayul Tak stopped in his tracks. Then he leaned down to whisper in my ear in a completely disbelieving tone. "Young Cult Leader. Don't say things like that. Your infamy as the Little Lecher has already reached the Great Lake Gate, so can you even criticize others? Come _on_."

I shut my mouth tight at his words. As he said, I was no longer a member of the orthodox sects. But for that very reason, the Demonic Cult had to change.

"We're here. This is it."

We had arrived at a small, humble building at the entrance of an alley. The shabby sign read 'Shining Light Archives'.

"The Shining Light Archives?"

"Yes. It's the place to go if you want information. They say as a source of news, it's second to none, not even the Palace of Bliss."

Yayul Tak finished speaking and glanced around. Then, he boldly opened the door, entered, and called out. "Is there anything worth reading today?"

He sauntered in with his hands clasped behind his back like a high-ranking official.

An elderly man suddenly popped up and replied indifferently. "Depends. How's your place?"

"It's got some light... but way too little. Bad for the eyes."

"...I don't think we have a book for that kind of ambience."

"I'm sure you do. By the way, how's the market these days?"

"There's more than one market out there. Which one are you talking about?"

"Which one? _This_ one, of course."

"...Wait a moment."

Their conversation was a little disjointed, as if they were using code words. Sure enough, a moment later, the old man pushed us outside and bolted the library door.

_BANG, BANG!_ Yayul Tak pounded angrily on the door, yelling after being thrown out. "What the!! Why did you suddenly lock the door? Didn't you hear _Little Light_? Where we're from?"

But the old man paid him no heed and even latched the windows shut.

"Let's just... wait for now."

"Ah, seriously! This has never happened before... I'm sorry."

"It's fine."

A strange premonition had washed over me— a certainty that there was a great significance to this place.

{sep}

The old librarian pulled down the curtains, then removed some floorboards from under a table. _THUNK_. A hidden staircase revealed itself.

Endless darkness stretched downwards, like a road into an abyss, but the old man descended without a shred of unease; and after a long while he reached an obscured landing, where a hallway stretched into the gloom. It was incredibly long, longer than a shabby library in a cramped marketplace should logically be able to conceal.

_Tap, tap._

The old man's footsteps reverberated down the long corridor. _Tap._ When he finally came to a halt, a thick iron door, apparently forged from ten-thousand-year-old cold steel, blocked his path.

"It seems they come from the Palace of Minor Light. What are your instructions?"

One would assume the old man was the library's owner, but even while blocked by the iron door and out of sight from its occupant, he still clasped his hands respectfully as he asked his question.

"...Did _he_ come in person?"

A surprisingly calm voice flowed out through the door, from what appeared to be the true owner of the Shining Light Archives.

"He wears a black bamboo hat, but his beauty is unmistakable. It is the Young Cult Leader."

"Hmm, whatever did happen at the Heavenly Demon Cave? To cause such a scene at the Parlor upon returning, and then seek me out next...?"

He was seemingly aware of the previous day's incident, down to details.

"He certainly... carries himself differently... but he has always been so fickle, a humble man like me could hardly..."

"He sought us out before the Palace of Merchants or the Palace of Shining Light. What is his request?"

"A registry of names."

"A registry of names? Do you mean the ranks within the Murim Alliance? Or the Evil Alliance?"

"It was neither."

"Neither?"

"...It seems he is looking for the Cult of Shining Light's Order of Succession."

"Hah... Then he is even more opaque. The Heir to the Demonic Cult, looking for the Order of Succession to his own Cult… does that even make sense?" The more he heard, the less he understood.

"What should be done?"

"Interesting. I will see him for myself."

"Personally, Master?"

The Shining Light Archives. It may present itself as a second-hand bookstore, but its inner workings were not to be underestimated. Only a select few within the Cult of Shining Light were aware of the Archives' true owner; for such a person to express this much interest in the Young Cult Leader, and even a wish to meet in person, was an exceptional event.

"But... is it safe? He might note—"

"Is he not known to be a foolish cur? He may have eyes, but is unlikely to use them."

"But what if by some chance he does notice..."

"Then isn't that even better? If he turns out to be more than the dog-son of a tiger-father, he may prove to be an asset to us."

"...understood."

_TAP, TAP_. As the old man climbed back up the stairs, a voice laden with misgivings seeped through the door.

"The Young Cult Leader... Is he a fraying rope on a cliff's edge, or...?"

{sep}

_CREAK._

The library door finally reopened. "Follow me."

The old man reappeared, uttered that one single phrase, and led us straight down a trap door. He didn't even react to Yayul Tak' whining.

"This is..."

"Wow, this is a first for me too. You were hiding something like this?"

The spiral staircase went deeper than I expected. It was unbelievable that such a place could be hidden beneath such a shabby building. After an interminable descent and a long corridor, we ended up at a thick iron door with only a tiny window for airflow.

"The guest has arrived."

"...you may come in."

_CLANG_. The door swung open, casting the light of a luminous pearl into the dark corridor. The old man simply gestured for us to enter, then returned to the stairs.

Once inside, a wide space unfolded, unlike the narrow corridor outside.

"Wh-what is all this?" Yayul Tak asked, his mouth agape. But I was just as awed.

The walls were densely packed with portraits— maybe hundreds of them.

"It is merely an old man's pastime. Greetings, Young Cult Leader."

When I turned around, I was greeted by an old man, his long white hair tied up and a blackened brush in hand. He was shabbily dressed, but his gaze was as sharp as a long-honed sword.

"I haven't said a word... yet you recognize me immediately."

"As one lives through the years, one learns to perceive even that which one is not explicitly told."

I couldn't feel any inner qi from him, yet he exuded an indescribable aura. There was too much to this man to dismiss him as a mere commoner.

"Is that so? But your body..." What caught my eye was the fact that he sat in a wooden chair with two large wheels. Judging by the blanket covering his legs, he must have been disabled.

"As Your Grace can see, this is a wretched body that cannot even stand on its own. Pray grant me your magnanimous pardon for greeting you from my seat."

"It is quite alright. That device... is it something you made yourself?"

A rolling chair. How cunning.

"I call it a wheelchair. It is but a humble trinket I cobbled together, so it is hardly worthy of note."

"I disagree. I came expecting a simple library, yet found this secret vault coiled in its innards and a remarkable man greeting us as its owner. How could I not take note?"

His eyebrow twitched. I nodded as I walked along the wall, examining the portraits. If my first feeling was surprise, it soon turned to admiration; he must truly be a master painter, as each figure felt so alive I might as well be meeting them in person.

And then, my admiration turned to horror. A chilling realization ran through my mind.

_This is... a registry! It's undoubtedly the registry of all notable martial artists within the Central Plains. The Evil Alliance, the Murim Alliance, even the Imperial Service. It's a record of all the masters who have surpassed the Transcendent State! How on earth? Who is this old man?!_

I couldn't believe my own eyes, but there was no mistake. The portraits before me showed masters from the Murim Alliance, all personally known to me, looking down in groups of three and five. The Divine Monk of Shaolin, the Seven Heroes of Wudang, the Nine Cloud Dragons of Kongtong... and at the very end, even my father, the Sword Venerable.

How on earth could a man who wasn't even able to stand on his own two legs acquire this much information...

"The Young Cult Leader seems to recognize someone. Although that side should contain only righteous members of the Murim Alliance..."

Oh crap. I'd accidentally let on too much.

I hastily cleared my throat a couple of times and turned my gaze back to the old man, asking, "This information is too sensitive for a mere archive to handle... Who are you?"

The man in the wheelchair quietly studied me for a long time. Eventually, with a quirk of his lips, he finally replied. "Well now. I am but a country bumpkin who gathers and sells street gossip."

"This is hardly street gossip. If this information were leaked, there would be bloodshed."

These were detailed portraits of the most renowned masters in the realm. To those not in the know, they might be but scraps of paper, but their hidden value was unfathomable.

"Thus they lie buried here, with me."

"The sun does not cease burning once it passes behind a roof. You surely did not gather these as a hobby. What if evildoers come to demand this information? Will you still spout these coy little words?"

"For the Young Cult Leader of the Cult of Shining Light to show such concern for the Central Plains... you might just make a fine ruler."

It was sarcasm; the owner's lips were curling up in a smirk. At this, Yayul Tak stepped forward with a scowl. "Hey, _geezer_. You wanna die here? Who the hell do you think you're spouting that crap to? Huh?"

"Heh, with such valuable information in my grasp, there is little I want. However, if this old man dies here, he will do so in rarefied company."

_CLANG!_

As soon as he spoke, the iron door slammed shut.
