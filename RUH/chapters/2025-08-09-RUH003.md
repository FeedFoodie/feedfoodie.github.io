Despite his youth, this man had wickedness in his eyes and a smirk to his lips; in but one glance anyone could tell he was the quintessential demonic practitioner.

Yayul Tak.

He alone visited me, repeatedly, over the past three days. He was a bodyguard and warrior for the Palace of Minor Light, and a close friend of Ma Cheonso.

In short, the only members of this large household, the Palace of Minor Light, were myself and Yayul Tak.

"Oh, you even called me by name. Is your memory finally coming back? I've been bored with you coming back all listless like that. It's totally because you're low on yin energy, right? Haha. Shall I book a brothel for you?"

Birds of a feather flock together. How could he be so vulgar? I clicked my tongue and shook my head.

"Stop with the foolish talk. My memory isn't fully intact yet, so I've got a few things to ask."

Yayul Tak didn't answer. Instead, he padded over in his socks and placed a hand on my forehead.

"What do you think you're doing?"

"This is strange. Your way of speaking is just like one of those bastards from the orthodox sects."

"Nonsense!"

Could it be that he noticed? A bead of sweat trickled down my spine. Should I kill him and escape right now? Or...

Just as my worries deepened, he spoke again.

"Hahaha! It's a joke, a joke. Don't be so surprised. Scholars are your new thing, right? This is like back when you were really into monks. Well, I'm busy, but if the Young Leader demands, I have to obey. Please, come in!"

I could say with certainty that the original Ma Cheonso must have been beyond anything one could imagine.

"A busy man sleeps in broad daylight? At least wipe the drool from your face before you talk."

"Haha, is it that obvious? So, what are you so curious about today? To think you'd grace me with your presence all the way here."

He was a door away from me. What did he mean by _gracing_ him with my presence? I tutted and entered his annex. Unlike his appearance, Yayul Tak's room was quite neat. I was especially surprised to see so many orchids, which are notoriously difficult to grow.

"Do you raise orchids?"

As I gently stroked the fully bloomed petals, old memories settled in comfortably.

"Why? Are you trying to tease me again? No, no, no! Not my orchids..."

"These leaves are very thin and soft, and they curve so gently. This must be a Sancheonbose. What a wonderful fragrance."

"Huh...?"

Whenever the fiery energy of anger seeped into my heart, I used to tend to and prune orchids.

Smelling the deep fragrance after so long, a smile bloomed on my face on its own.

"Are you really... our Young Cult Leader? Did you get a qi deviation to your upper dantian or something?"

He asked, his eyes full of disbelief.

I definitely needed to change my speech patterns.

"Back then... how would I speak in such occasions?"

"Usually..."

Yayul Tak seemed to ponder for a moment. Then he furrowed his brow, rolled his eyes back to show the whites, and shouted.

"Hey, you stinky tofu bastard. Say that to my face. I'll chop that long tongue to pieces."

"......"

So, Ma Cheonso shouldn't talk ever.

I decided it would be best to postpone the matter of my speech patterns.

"Why are you looking at me like that? I mean, well. It's true that the Young Cult Leader was pretty rude... but isn't it natural for a demonic practitioner to curse?"

"Sigh..."

To straight up describe himself as a demonic practitioner... I was speechless with embarrassment. But I would have to deal with such things, over and over, so long as I remained here.

It was an unbearable thought.

"More importantly..."

A thought occurred to me as I looked around.

Had this man been guarding the Palace of Minor Light all on his own during the Young Cult Leader's seclusion?

"Why are you still here?"

"Well, that's because..."

Yayul Tak mumbled like a shy, newlywed bride.

Could it be... that even he held a sense of righteousness and loyalty?

"Hehe. Where else is there a place as comfortable as this to just loaf around? If I joined one of the Seven Hallowed Palaces, I'd have to rise at the crack of dawn for martial arts training. That's a grueling path! And what's more! They're always on about conquering the Central Plains or what have you... I'd rather stick by the Young Cult Leader where the pay is good, and even when we go out, it's just to a brothel or a gambling den. Much more relaxing! I'll get to live it up and die at ease."

Of course. What an immature man.

I sat down on a high-backed chair with a huff, and got to the point.

"Cut the nonsense. Just tell me how the Cult of Shining Light is structured."

"Ha, you really don't remember that either? This is serious."

The way he shook his head and frowned at me was truly unbearable.

"Just answer me."

"The Two Palaces of Light and Seven Hallowed Palaces! Those are the Nine Palaces that comprise our cult. The Two Palaces of Light at the very top refer to the Palace of Shining Light, where the Lord Cult Leader resides, and our Palace of Minor Light, where the Young Cult Leader stays."

I gave a slight nod.

"Then the Seven Hallowed Palaces must be the powers that support them from below."

"Yes, that's right. But now... they are also the ones who pose the greatest threat to the Palace of Shining Light."

Yayul Tak put on a rather serious expression. He sat on the edge of his bed and even clasped his hands to set the mood.

It was a little cringe.

"Why is that?"

"Because your Lord Father is absent, duh. No one knows where he's gone. That's why there's so much talk these days about choosing the next Cult Leader. Those arrogant vultures..."

Ma Taepyeong, the Cult Leader of the Demonic Cult, had apparently disappeared about two years ago, leaving only a note telling people not to look for him.

Once the tiger is gone, the wolves are free to come out and fight over the mountain.

"Even so, there's still the Young Cult Leader, isn't there?"

Yet this question remained: no matter how incompetent he was, Ma Cheonso was still the Heir in name. It made no sense to discuss the position of Cult Leader while excluding him.

"Are you talking in third person now?"

Oh, I'd momentarily forgotten.

"Just keep talking."

"Well, who would even see the Young Lord as a potential Cult Leader? Your martial arts are abysmal, you're not very bright, and you act like a thug. You've got nothing to reccomend you but your looks. Hah! Even I wouldn't pick you. If any dog or cow could become Cult Leader, would we even be the Shining Light Cult? Or the Dull Cult?"

He certainly wasn't wrong. And yet... why did my gut roil? Why couldn't I stop thinking about grinding stinky tofu in a mortar?

"You have quite the nerve, talking like that right in front of me."

When I glared at him, Yayul Tak jumped in surprise and waved his hands frantically.

"Ha, haha... I mean, that's what other people say! Not me, I'm forever the Young Cult Leader's loyal servant! Totally!"

I would sooner believe him were he to say he would repent and join the orthodox sects.

"But... didn't you lose your martial arts, Young Cult Leader?" He tilted his head and changed the subject. "I heard that if you fail while learning the Heavenly Demon Divine Art, you can lose your qi or even your life... your complexion looks great, though."

"You were not hoping for that, were you?"

Of course, I wouldn't bother with a straight answer.

"No way, of course not! It's just... Never mind."

Yayul Tak scratched his head with a suspicious look, then laughed like an idiot again.

A man's word is worth a thousand pieces of gold. Although I'd solemnly sworn to revere the Heavenly Demon as my Master, his was not a style I could recklessly reveal.

A heavy resolve seemed to press down on my shoulders.

Perhaps noticing the stuffy atmosphere, Yayul Tak immediately changed his tune and asked.

"Do you know how many members are in the Shining Light Cult?"

"Let's see... Wouldn't it be in the thousands?"

Considering the number of warriors in the Murim Alliance and the Unorthodox Faction, along with their families, I figured it would be around that number.

However.

"Pfft. Thousands? Listen here. Including the lay followers spread across the Central Plains, the Shining Light Cult numbers in the hundreds of thousands, if not millions."

"But that's outrageous!"

"It sure is! That's why the empire makes such a fuss about us. You'll never see them talk about the Murim Alliance or the Unorthodox Faction, but when it comes to us, they'll single us out and openly brand us as some evil Demonic Cult!"

It was just as he said; no emperor had ever branded a martial sect, orthodox or not, an enemy of the state to be suppressed on a national level. But even so, millions? At this scale, did it not rival small kingdoms like the Western Xia or the Dali Kingdom?

If so, then the Cult was more than a simple martial arts organization...

"Don't tell me the Seven Hallowed Palaces also handle administrative roles like the Three Departments and Six Ministries?!"

"Whoa..."

Yayul Tak stared at me, his mouth agape.

It seemed my guess was correct; the Seven Hallowed Palaces held far more influence than mere sects.

Still a question remained.

"But why have they been so quiet until now? You said it's been over two years since your Cult Leader, I mean, His Lordship, disappeared. That should have been more than enough time for a rebellion to break out, shouldn't it?"

Yayul Tak's expression twisted strangely.

He then let out a sigh and answered.

"Of course, they haven't been sitting still. But each Palace has its own agenda, and... there are far more lay followers who don't belong to any specific palace. And there's the Council of Elders above them."

"So the Two Palaces of Light, the Seven Hallowed Palaces, and the Council of Elders are all keeping each other in check. And they share the authority to decide the next Cult Leader?"

"Exactly! So, just as they were waiting for the Grand Festival of Heavenly Shining Light, which is held every two years, _you_, the official heir, the Young Cult Leader, suddenly reappeared, making the situation even more complicated."

"I see..."

Now I understood.

There were many different wolves in the Demonic Cult aiming to become king of the mountain. That was why it was peaceful now-- because they were all checking and restraining one another.

But this was a precarious balancing act. Should the balance shift even a little, a bloodbath would unfold.

And eventually...

That bloodbath might overflow into the Central Plains.

"But... why is it just the two of us in the Palace of Minor Light? Are there no other other members in the household?"

"There were more originally. It's just that the Young Cult Leader chased them all out."

"I did?"

"Yes. You chased some out for being ugly, some for being too skinny. Well, what else is there to say? Now, they wouldn't come back even if you begged them to."

Ugh.

Ma Cheonso was truly a hopeless case.

But at least I finally had a rough understanding of the Demonic Cult. All that was left was the Murim Alliance...

**_Grooooowl._**

A deep hunger churned shamelessly in my stomach.

At that, Yayul Tak grinned slyly and chattered on.

"Hehehe. I told you, you ought to eat something! Let's go out. Remember the Bright Spring Parlor? Oh, their pork belly with garlic sauce-- they cook the pig fresh from the butchering, slice the meat, and yum! It really just melts in your mouth. Like, _melts._"

_SLURP._ Just hearing about it made my mouth water.

Out of respect for my father, who'd raised me to be frugal, the number of times I'd indulged a decadent meal could be counted on one hand.

And now... _garlic pork belly_.

My resolve began to crumble.

"And what's more! There's their hearty corn soup, where they take top-shelf corn from Henan and crack an egg fresh into it. One spoonful will soothe you from the inside out!"

"Fine! Since you want to go so badly, I'll keep you company. But just this once."

"Hehehe. Sure thing, Young Lord. I understand. Let's go!"

Yayul Tak stood up to lead the way, and my own feet felt light in his wake. But then he suddenly stopped and looked back with a troubled expression.

"Ah..." he started. "Now that I think about it, the Bright Spring Parlor won't do. Let's just eat something simple somewhere else."

"_Why?!_" I wailed, feeling weirdly choked up.

But Yayul Tak answered with a furrowed brow, as if it were a truly difficult matter.

"The regular meeting of the Scions of the Shining Light Cult is being held there today. It's better if you don't go."

His expression was filled with sincerity.

In short, it was a gathering of the scions of all the prominent families...

Was he afraid I might be shunned by them? But that was fine. I was not taught to back down from injustice and prejudice; I am a scion of the righteous path, the son of the leader of the Murim Alliance, and...

**_GROOOWL._**

"Then that's even better."

"Huh? Better?"

"Now that I've exited my seclusion, it's only proper to greet them. And as the scions of prestigious families, our ages should be similar, which will make things easier."

"Whaaaat?"

"What are you so surprised about? Lead the way."

"Ugh...!"

Pfft. Seeing him so startled, he clearly had a simple side to him.

I rose from my seat and threw open the door with a bang, letting in the bright sunlight that streamed down the hallway.

Think fast. Act faster.

If I could not escape this fate, then there was but one choice to be made: either change it, or break out of it-- for I was Yang Jagak, follower of the righteous path, and _not_ a member of the demonic cult!

A gentle breeze blew through my long hair, and I turned to Yayul Tak with a bright smile.

"Are there more dishes besides the garlic pork belly?"

"Th-there are! Of course, there are! Ah, screw it. Let's go! Come on!"

Yayul Tak stared at me with a dazed expression before awkwardly exiting the annex.
