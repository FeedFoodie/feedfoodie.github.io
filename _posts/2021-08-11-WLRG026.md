The "incident" unfolded just as Lee Sungmin remembered it would.

Half a year after his arrival in Zenavis, the old doctor at the Recovery Bone Setting Clinic died. He was beaten to death by a Third-Rate martial artist who had flown into a rage after being denied the Martial Skeleton procedure.

While disputes between otherworlders rarely led to punishment, harming a resident was another matter entirely. The lord sentenced the martial artist to death for his crime.

Lee Sungmin knew it would happen, for the doctor had died the same way in his past life. He knew, yet he did nothing to stop it. He saw no reason to interfere. His sense of justice was a malleable thing, always shifting with his circumstances. Had he shared a deep friendship with the old man, he might have prevented his death, but no such bond existed. Their relationship had been one of mutual interest. The old man needed a living "success case" for his procedure, and Lee Sungmin had simply needed the Martial Skeleton.

To be honest, he just had not wanted to get involved.

Time passed after the old man's death. Lee Sungmin paid his inn fees with his savings, and whenever his funds ran low, he ventured into the forest to hunt. He did not discriminate between goblins and orcs, returning with his Subspace Pocket filled to the brim with loot. He sold his spoils through Jack or Hans, using the money for his lodging and adding the rest to his savings.

Wiji Hoyeon was gone.

He could no longer receive her teachings, and ten long years would have to pass before he could meet her again, assuming he survived that long. It went without saying that Lee Sungmin had every intention of surviving.

Zenavis held tournaments periodically. They served as entertainment for the residents who had to endure the constant influx of otherworlders and as a place for the newly summoned to test their skills. That was not all, of course. The prizes, offered by the lord, varied each time but were always fundamentally helpful for an otherworlder's survival, a reasonable payment for the entertainment provided.

The tournaments were divided into three types. One allowed mages to participate, another prohibited them, and the last was the Classless Tournament, open only to the Classless.

Lee Sungmin, naturally, aimed for that one.

Winning was easy.

In the time between Wiji Hoyeon's departure and the tournament that offered the Holy Spirit Pill as its prize, Lee Sungmin endured long, arduous days. He swung his spear relentlessly, cutting down on sleep to devote himself to the Violet Dawn Divine Art. Unlike his past life, where he lacked a clear purpose, this life was different. With Wiji Hoyeon as his goal, he knew he had to put in a commensurate amount of effort.

*It's not just Wiji Hoyeon.*

Lee Sungmin looked down at the wooden box resting in his hands.

The Classless tournament had been so easy it almost made his year of effort feel futile, though it was a natural outcome. The other Classless participants were simply not on his level. They were the very people hunted by Third-Rate martial artists, and even the talented ones who remained in Zenavis were nothing special.

And what of Lee Sungmin? He had learned two divine arts, the Violet Dawn Divine Art and the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, and had received personal instruction from the great calamity, Wiji Hoyeon herself. He had not only received her instruction but her inner qi as well. Supplemented by his past life's experience, which filled the gaps in his fundamentals, it was fair to say the current Lee Sungmin had reached the level of a First-Rate martial artist, even if his own inner qi were still lacking.

*Taking the Holy Spirit Pill will solve that to some extent.*

He entered the tournament, fought five times, and won every match without revealing his full strength. He felt nothing good would come from showing it.

"You should eat it here before you go."

A man was waiting for him in the participant's waiting room. He was one of the knights in service to Zenavis's lord.

"If you carry it out, you might attract some rats. The lord's being considerate, so you should be grateful to him."

"...I understand."

"If you'd rather sell it than eat it, just say the word. We can convert it to cash for you. Don't worry about the small details, you'll still get a great effect even if you haven't learned an inner arts technique."

He had no intention of selling. A miracle herb of this caliber would fetch a considerable sum, but what Lee Sungmin needed was the herb itself, not money. Miracle herbs were not something one could easily buy, even with wealth.

Lee Sungmin immediately sat in a cross-legged position. The question of whether he could trust the knight was not worth considering. In his past life, a Classless had obtained this very pill and received the knight's help.

*Come to think of it, what happened to that bastard?*

The man who had gotten the Holy Spirit Pill in his past life. By obtaining the pill now, Lee Sungmin had prevented its original recipient from getting it.

*Not my problem.*

He opened the wooden box. A pure white pill lay inside. As Lee Sungmin lifted the Holy Spirit Pill, the knight moved to guard the door, clearly intending to protect him.

Lee Sungmin placed the pill in his mouth. The moment it touched his tongue, it melted away without flavor or taste. He slowly circulated the Violet Dawn Divine Art. His Danfighting spirit stirred, welcoming the new energy from the Holy Spirit Pill. The pill's inner qi followed the mnemonic of the Violet Dawn Divine Art, circulating through his blood vessels alongside his own. Not all of it could be stored in his qi center. Though the inner qi of a refined miracle herb had undergone a basic purification, it could not be stored directly.

Usually, one could only absorb half of it. The rest would either scatter into the blood vessels or be expelled from the body.

The Violet Dawn Divine Art, however, was an outstanding technique. When he finished the Great Heavenly Cycle, Lee Sungmin had managed to store two-thirds of the Holy Spirit Pill's inner qi in his qi center. The remaining third was not expelled but instead dissolved into his blood vessels, just like the energy Wiji Hoyeon had infused into him. As he continued to circulate the Violet Dawn Divine Art, that scattered energy would eventually be guided to his qi center.

"Are you done?" the knight asked when Lee Sungmin opened his eyes.

Lee Sungmin steadied his breathing and rose to his feet. "Yes. Thank you."

His qi center felt full, a Danfighting spirit weightiness he had never known in his past life.

"For a Classless, your martial arts are... quite good. What do you say? If you want, I can speak to the lord and recommend you as a knight apprentice." The knight's eyes gleamed as he spoke. To him, who knew nothing of the circumstances, a Classless with such skill must have seemed like a prodigy with outstanding talent.

"Ah... thank you for the offer... but I'm fine. There's something I have to do."

"If that's the case, it can't be helped."

Though he looked disappointed, the knight did not press the matter. It was a precious opportunity for Lee Sungmin to pass up. Anyone could become a mercenary, but becoming a knight was not so easy.

Lee Sungmin, however, had no lingering regrets. Having finished absorbing the Holy Spirit Pill, he left the waiting room. He was leaving Zenavis, and he had no intention of changing his mind.

"I came to say my goodbyes."

He did not plan to leave without a word. He had lived in the city for a year, after all, though he had not formed many connections in that time.

"I knew it."

First was Hans, the street vendor who had helped Lee Sungmin since his second day in the city. He had sold him goods at reasonable prices and had even given him a used Subspace Pocket out of a "misunderstanding" of his talent.

"I heard you won the Classless Tournament," Hans said with a chuckle. "To be honest, I thought you would've left sooner. You were hunting the monsters in this forest with ease after just half a year."

He grinned. "Probably. A guy like you will do just fine even after leaving this city. It's the same everywhere. The quick-witted ones survive for a long time." Just like me, he meant. Hans reached into his coat and tossed a small pouch to Lee Sungmin.

"Take it."

"...This is a Subspace Pocket, isn't it?" Lee Sungmin asked with surprise as he caught it.

"It's used. Still, its performance is a bit better than the one you've been using. The storage space is about one and a half times bigger than your pocket. It'll be useful for your life ahead."

"...Why are you giving this to me?"

"It's a farewell gift. Just take it. I'll probably be in Zenavis for good, so if you really want to repay the favor, send something my way later," Hans said with a grin.

Lee Sungmin stared blankly at him for a moment before bowing his head deeply. "Thank you."

"What's to thank? I gave it to you because I wanted to. Since you're leaving anyway, go get famous. Famous enough that rumors about you reach all the way back here. After that, you can brag all you want. Tell everyone how a street vendor named Hans helped you a lot back in Zenavis."

"......"

After bidding farewell to Hans, Lee Sungmin headed for Jack's Inn.

"You're leaving, aren't you?" Lula spoke up as he entered.

Lee Sungmin froze for a second before tilting his head. "How'd you know?"

"Your room was all cleaned up. The blanket was folded and everything. Why'd you do that? That's my job." Sitting on a table, Lula swung her legs and pouted. "It's not that surprising. You stayed for a year, that's a long time. I know, too. The otherworlders who arrive in this city all leave someday."

"...That's right. I have to leave."

"Don't you want to get a job at the inn?" Lula asked, turning to look at him. "My dad. He really likes you. Says you're friendly and diligent. If you take the job, yeah, maybe Dad will even let you inherit the inn later."

"Why would he give the inn to me? He has you."

"You idiot." Lula's shoulders trembled at his feignedly innocent reply. He knew what she meant but deliberately said nothing about it. That was the right thing to do.

"Don't die," Lula said, her pouting lips parting. "If you're going to leave and live out there, live a long, long time. Don't die. Don't get too caught up in martial arts theory. If you think you can't make it, just come back to this city."

"...Okay. Thanks."

"You say that, but you won't come back in the end, will you?" Lula hopped down from the table and stared intently at him. The eyes of the girl, who had turned from fifteen to sixteen, were unusually calm for her age.

"Probably."

"I knew it. Don't ever come back, you stupid idiot," Lula snapped, then spun around. Her steps toward the kitchen halted. She spun back around again. "But don't die. I mean it."

"I won't."

"Hmph!"

Lula snorted loudly and, with that final word, disappeared into the kitchen.

"She's still young. Though you're even younger." Jack walked out after Lula had gone, holding a large basket. "Although sometimes, you look much older than your age."

"...I can't help it. Life just made me that way."

"Anyone would think you've lived for decades," Jack laughed as if he found it absurd. He handed the basket to Lee Sungmin. "Traveling on an empty stomach is hard. Bread spoils easily, but with a Subspace Pocket, it won't rot. Eat it sparingly on your journey, and I put some milk in, too. You seem to like milk."

"...Thank you." In truth, he did not particularly like milk. He just drank it because it was good for his body.

"And take this, too. It's a cloak I used when I was young... It might look a bit worn, but it's enchanted with magic. Blades don't pierce it easily, and it has temperature-regulating magic, but don't be overconfident in it. The magic isn't that great. If it gets too cold or too hot, the magic won't be able to keep up."

Even for a second-hand item, a cloak with magic would be quite expensive. Lee Sungmin put the basket into his subspace and draped the cloak over his body.

"...It's too big..."

"It'll fit when your body gets bigger," Jack said with an indifferent expression. He patted the cloaked Lee Sungmin on the shoulder and continued. "Still, you were the best of all the Classless I've met while working at the inn. You'll be able to live well in other cities, too."

"Thank you."

"What's to thank? I'm just telling the truth. If you have the chance later, come visit and show your face. After my daughter gets married, that is." Jack grinned, revealing his yellowed teeth.

"......"

Lula shrieked from the kitchen. It seemed she had been secretly eavesdropping. Jack flinched in surprise, hunched his shoulders, and whispered.

"Or, you know. Ahem! You could come back soon and with my daughter..."

"Dad, shut up!" Lula's shout turned into a scream.

Lee Sungmin gave a bitter smile and nodded. "I'll think about it."

Though he had no intention of doing so.

He left Jack's Inn. It was July. In the Eria Year, it was 1104, and a little over a year had passed since Lee Sungmin came to Eria.

The cloak he wore was a little big, but Lee Sungmin actually liked its size. He rolled up the top of the cloak so the hem would not drag on the ground and walked toward the North Gate.

*Three years in my past life. This time, one year.*

He had graduated from Zenavis two years faster.
