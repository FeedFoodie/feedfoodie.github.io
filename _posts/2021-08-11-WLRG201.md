A week passed in smooth sailing after they left the port town. The coastal waters remained placid, presenting no major problems. Even the local monsters, more interested in hunting fish than ships, seemed to possess a primal instinct to leave the large vessel alone.

This peaceful passage left little for a mercenary to do. The pirates who prowled these waters were small-fry, preying on fishing boats, not merchant ships of this size. Lee Sungmin took advantage of the quiet, spending most of the week in his room, deep in meditation.

He had entered the Transcendent Realm. Though still a novice who had only just crossed the threshold, he had managed to overcome the Sword Saint's spatial sword and kill him. This was only possible because his body, upon fully entering that realm, had finally reached the state he had previously attained in the Mental World.

*The world beyond this… I don't know it either.*

He had reached the peak of his mental training, but now he stood at a new precipice, with no guide to show him what came next. From this point on, he had to advance solely through his own efforts. In truth, he still had plenty of room to grow stronger. He possessed the Black Heart, and the Dragon Heart had not yet fully assimilated into his being. Furthermore, his Yokai-transformed body could regenerate from most injuries in an instant.

However, with Heaven Beyond Heaven now considering him a primary enemy, he could not afford to relax. He had defeated the Sword Saint, an undeniable fact that placed his martial prowess above that of the Blade Supreme or the Dark Supreme. However, the Black Dragon Knight, whose strength was a terrifying unknown, still remained. Then there were the Spear King and the Moon Queen, both said to be far stronger than the Sword Saint, and above them all loomed the Martial God of the Outer Heavens. If he were to truly fight Heaven Beyond Heaven, he would eventually have to face that absolute master, a being whose level was unfathomable even to the current Lee Sungmin.

*The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman.*

Lee Sungmin's thoughts turned to the man who had helped him simply because he had said, 'I like you'. If the Chairman had not intervened back then, the Dark Supreme would have killed him and taken Wiji Hoyeon. He did not understand the reason for the man's favor, but he knew that to become a nemesis to the Martial God, he would need the Chairman's help. Lee Sungmin could not gauge the man's martial prowess either, but he was certain he was a martial artist standing in a distant, high place that he had yet to reach.

KNOCK, KNOCK.

The sound at his door pulled him from his thoughts. Lee Sungmin unfolded his legs from their meditative posture and rose. The presence he felt was Yangsu's. He had told the man to knock only if it was urgent, and for the entire week, Yangsu had not disturbed him.

"What is it?" Lee Sungmin asked as he opened the door.

Yangsu immediately bowed his head the moment their eyes met. The swelling on his face from Lee Sungmin's beating had completely subsided over the past week.

"Uh… the shipowner's calling for you."

"The shipowner? For me?"

"Yeah. Well… not just you, Hyunsoo. He's called for all the mercenaries," Yangsu said, sweating profusely.

At his words, Lee Sungmin slowly nodded. The name Jung Hyunsoo, the owner of the C-Rank Mercenary Badge, still felt awkward on his tongue. He followed Yangsu out onto the deck.

The deck was scorching under an ocean sun hot enough to cook skin. A shimmering white haze rose from the planks, a testament to the heat of the South. Atop the sun-drenched forecastle, the ship's owner, Zod, lounged in what looked like a plush chair. He was flanked by prostitutes he had brought aboard, their faces plain but their bodies voluptuous, holding large parasols to shield him from the light.

"My dear mercenaries."

The mercenaries gathered on the deck looked up at Zod with sour expressions. Lee Sungmin glanced at the shipowner before taking his place among the others.

"This ship has just passed the coastal waters and has now begun its main journey to Bellador," Zod began to ramble in a high-pitched voice. "For the past week, I haven't needed to borrow your hands, but… from now on, I'll be needing your help. Monsters! Those damn monsters, I mean. Ah, of course. I'm not doubting your skills, gentlemen. I'm just saying this, just in case."

Zod went on like that for a while, droning on about sea monsters.

Yangsu, standing beside Lee Sungmin, grumbled under his breath. "Damn it. How the hell are we supposed to catch a Sea Serpent?"

Lee Sungmin silently agreed. The Sea Serpent was one of the most threatening monsters in these waters, and it was impossible for ten C-Rank mercenaries to subjugate one. He glanced at the mediator standing beside Zod. In the worst-case scenario, the man would likely have to step in.

"And this is a really important matter," Zod continued. "If we're attacked by pirates, don't do anything foolish."

"……"

One of the mercenaries let out a dumbfounded sound.

"I'm telling you to wait for my orders first. Understand? Just do what you're told, only what you're told."

Lee Sungmin understood the reason for Zod's strange command. It was because of the mediator. If the mercenaries needlessly provoked the pirates, the amount of money the mediator would have to hand over would increase. Of course, they wouldn't pay off every pirate crew. If they were weak enough to handle, they could just subjugate them without wasting money, but if they were outmatched, the mediator's presence would decide the ship's fate.

When Zod's long-winded speech finally sputtered to a close, it was clear all he had wanted was to gather everyone and ramble. The man was full of bluff and vanity. True to form, he never summoned the mercenaries again. According to the schedule, they would be on this ship for two months.

Fortunately, the sea remained calm and the weather fair. Zod was not trustworthy, but the ship's captain and crew were skilled, so the voyage continued without major incident. In truth, it was not entirely without problems. Just as Zod had warned, monster attacks on the ship became more frequent. No Sea Serpents appeared, but hideous merfolk and monstrous birds assaulted the vessel.

They were called merfolk, but they were a grotesque combination of fish and human, nothing like the beautiful creatures from stories. They were classified as mutants, but their intelligence was so low they were often called the Orcs of the sea. Despite the repeated attacks, the number of mercenaries did not decrease. There were minor injuries, but these were men who had gained experience living as C-Rank mercenaries. At the very least, they were veterans well-versed in fighting merfolk.

"This is killing me," Yangsu grumbled, wrapping a bandage tightly around his arm where a merfolk's spear had grazed him. He couldn't afford a potion, so the rough cloth would have to do. Sucking on his lower lip, he looked down at the corpse of a merfolk with its head split open. "Riding a ship pays well, but living like a monk on board is too damn hard. Don't you think so?"

He shot Lee Sungmin a grin, looking for agreement. Lee Sungmin might have been swayed by lust in his past life, but not in this one. He ignored Yangsu's words, though he did sympathize to some extent. He still remembered his days as a mercenary. C-Rank Mercenaries lived hand-to-mouth, their lives filled with cheap liquor and cheaper prostitutes. They gave up on ambition in the face of reality, forgetting the pressure of not knowing when they would die with alcohol and women.

"Hey, Hyunsoo, you ever been to Bellador?" Yangsu persisted, undeterred by the silence. "The brothel district there is amazing… I know a really good place. How about we go together when we get there?"

More than a fortnight had passed since they set sail. In the monster attacks that began after they left the coastal waters, Yangsu and the other mercenaries had all sustained minor injuries, but Lee Sungmin alone remained unscathed. Yangsu had noticed that Lee Sungmin was different from the rest of them, which was why he was trying so hard to act friendly and form a connection.

Of course, Lee Sungmin had no intention of doing so, but then something caught his attention, and he paused to look at the man.

"What… are you doing?"

"……"

Yangsu, who was carving up the merfolk's body with a large knife, looked up at him.

*Is he planning to eat it?* The thought was a natural one, though their food supplies were hardly scarce. Thanks to a Subspace Pocket, the ship was stocked with ample food in addition to the goods being transported.

"Ah… well," Yangsu said with an awkward smile. "This one's a female, see."

"......"

"Aww, don't play dumb…" Yangsu's suggestive smile made his eyes crinkle.

The Void Monarch spat a curse inside his head. Lee Sungmin said nothing more. It was not just Yangsu. Several other mercenaries were on deck, collecting the lower halves of the dead female merfolk.

The voyage was like that. Dirty, disgusting. Still, the journey was smooth. Lee Sungmin continued his work, hoping the rest of the voyage would pass just as uneventfully.

"A ship!"

The smooth journey finally hit a snag three weeks after they set sail. The shout came from the sailor in the crow's nest, and it sent a jolt through the vessel. All the mercenaries spilled onto the deck, their minds naturally leaping to pirates. The shipowner, Zod, and the mediator from the Merchants' Guild also emerged.

"That's not a pirate ship…?"

The vessel on the horizon couldn't possibly be a pirate ship. The small boat did not even have a sail. It was barely moving, propelled by rowing, looking less like it was advancing and more like it was drifting aimlessly with the current. Lee Sungmin narrowed his eyes. Though it was far, his vision had no trouble making out who was on board.

"They look like castaways," the captain said to Zod. "What are your orders?"

Zod glanced at the boat with an indifferent expression and shook his head without a hint of hesitation. "Ignore them. We have enough people as it is."

The words had barely left his mouth when a plume of bright blue smoke erupted from the small boat. The smoke soared high into the air, burst with a POP, and then split into five different colors that scattered in all directions.

Seeing it, the mediator's jaw dropped.

"You can't!" he suddenly barked, grabbing Zod's shoulder with a bone-crushing grip that made the shipowner gasp. "That smoke. It's the symbol of the Delard Family."

"The… the Delard Family?" Zod's face went pale. Even he knew of the Delard Family, one of the three great families that led the Merchants' Guild. It was no exaggeration to say that most commerce conducted at sea fell under their influence. Zod swallowed hard.

"Head the ship over there immediately," the mediator commanded. Though his words were polite, his eyes blazed with a fierceness that suggested he would tear Zod to shreds. He may have been on this ship to ensure its safety, but he ultimately belonged to the Merchants' Guild.

Following Zod's eventual order, the ship changed course toward the small boat. There were three people on board. Two women who appeared to be handmaidens and a boy in luxurious clothes who still had a youthful air about him. The mercenaries swallowed hard as they watched the handmaidens climb the ladder, earning them a murderous glare from the mediator.

"Oh, God." The handmaidens collapsed weakly upon reaching the deck, calling out to God, certain they had been about to die adrift on the vast ocean.

The boy who followed them looked around, his face a mixture of exhaustion and irritation. "A merchant ship?"

"……"

The mediator was the one who answered, bowing his head deeply to the boy. The boy's eyes lit up when he saw the Merchants' Guild mark engraved on the mediator's left breast.

"A mediator. Which branch are you from?"

"I'm from the Palais Merchants' Guild."

"Is that so? Good." The boy sighed and ran a hand through his sweat-drenched hair. "I'm Engrai Delard. My ship was destroyed in an attack by some crazy pirates, and I barely managed to survive."

At the name Engrai Delard, the mediator's eyebrows twitched. To bear the surname Delard meant he carried the blood of the family head. However, the mediator did not recognize the name Engrai. That was understandable, as the head of the Delard Family had dozens of children. If his name was not recognizable, it meant that despite his surname, he was not a prominent figure.

"What is this ship's destination?"

"We are heading to Bellador," the mediator replied.

Zod was frantically trying to wrap his head around the situation. He was well aware of the Delard Family's power. This was an opportunity. A chance to get closer to his ambitions had fallen right into his lap. He had saved a boy with the Delard surname who would have otherwise died at sea. With this, he could form a connection with the great family, and after that? He could ride their coattails all the way to success.

"The ship I was on sank," Engrai said. "I need to report this to the Patriarch as soon as possible."

"Where are you headed?" Zod asked quickly.

Engrai looked at him. "Are you the shipowner?"

"Yes, that's right."

"We're going to Rovcos, not Bellador. There's no reason to go to Bellador. My ship departed from there."

Rovcos was a large port city a little south of Palais, the town this ship had departed from. At Engrai's words, Zod nodded without a second thought. He figured that listening to the boy and going to Rovcos was a far better opportunity to connect with the Delard Family than any trade in Bellador.

"We're going to Rovcos!" Zod shouted for all the crew to hear.

The mercenaries grumbled but did not object. As long as they were paid properly, it made no difference to them whether they went to Bellador or Rovcos.

However, a quiet murmur cut through Zod's excited shout, a voice that sliced through the celebratory noise.

"That's a problem."

Everyone's gaze snapped toward Lee Sungmin.

"I need to go to Bellador."
