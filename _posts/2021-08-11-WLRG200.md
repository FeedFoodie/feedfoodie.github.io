The tavern was a bustling place, and as Lee Sungmin sat nursing a beer, a figure approached his table.

"You planning to take a ship too?"

Lee Sungmin lifted his head. A man with a sturdy build stood before him, his skin tanned a deep bronze over a muscular physique. A tattoo snaked around one forearm. At Lee Sungmin's silent, blank stare, the man let out a hearty laugh.

"Whoa, don't be so tense. I'm just asking, is all."

As he spoke, the man extended a large, calloused hand. Lee Sungmin slowly reached out and shook it.

"Are you a sailor?"

"Huh? Well, yeah," the man nodded. He had a good build, but Lee Sungmin could feel almost no trace of martial arts training from him.

He supposed the man could be a master of Origin Reversal, but that suspicion was faint. Martial arts theory held that even if someone reached Origin Reversal and concealed their aura, a martial artist of a sufficiently higher level could see through the deception. Lee Sungmin concluded the man was no martial artist.

"So, where are you headed?"

"To Bellador," Lee Sungmin replied. Bellador was a port city on the western side of the continent, relatively close to his destination, the Legro Forest. He had chosen to travel by sea instead of running because he knew the Murim Alliance would surely pursue him once he left the South.

*The sea won't be completely safe either, though.*

It could not be helped. The pursuit was not the only reason he had chosen a ship. Considering the time it would take, a ship was simply faster.

"Bellador?"

Lee Sungmin stared intently at the man's face. *Why did he start a conversation?* It did not seem like he had struck up a conversation just out of boredom. The man was sizing him up, clearly thinking about something. He doubted the man was a lackey from an information guild. Lee Sungmin was currently wearing a human skin mask he had purchased through Erebrisa, and his Magic Armor was hidden away.

"...By any chance, are you interested in making a lump sum of money?" the man asked slyly.

"A lump sum?"

"Yeah. It's not a very difficult job. You just have to deliver a certain item to Bellador..."

"What is it?"

"It's not a very difficult job," the man repeated.

"No, what exactly is it?"

"Are you going to do it?" the man asked persistently.

Lee Sungmin had a rough idea of what this was about. Smuggling. It was not an uncommon occurrence. Most commercial transactions in Eria went through the Merchants' Guild, which took a commission from every deal. For certain trade items, they controlled the market completely, monopolizing it for their own profit.

"No, I'm fine."

"You're worried, aren't you? There's no need. You just have to run an errand. I'll pay you well, too."

"I have plenty of money."

"Plenty of money, huh? And you're booking passage on a merchant ship?" the man sneered, as if he knew everything.

Lee Sungmin stopped listening. It was best not to create any troublesome situations. If he got involved in smuggling and was caught, he would become entangled with the Merchants' Guild, and by extension, the Murim Alliance. The two organizations had a close relationship.

"Hey, if you're not going to do it, then fine. It's your loss. Don't come crying to me later."

The man shot a final glare at Lee Sungmin's blatant dismissal, then got up from the table. He scanned the tavern before heading toward another man who was drinking alone. It seemed he was targeting outsiders who had come to take a ship, not the locals.

After finishing his beer, Lee Sungmin stood. His fingers fiddled with the mercenary badge tucked inside his clothes. It was a forgery purchased through Erebrisa, but there was no fear of being caught. If it were a forgery crude enough to be discovered, Erebrisa would never have dealt in it.

C-Rank Mercenary, Jung Hyunsoo. He did not know who the owner of this name was. He was probably dead, or perhaps he had suffered a fate worse than death.

Leaving the tavern, Lee Sungmin checked the time. It was almost time to board. Among the many merchant ships heading to Bellador, he had chosen a small, insignificant vessel. He would have preferred a larger ship, but a C-Rank Mercenary with no connections could not board such a vessel even if he wanted to. It was not impossible if he bribed someone, of course.

*Doing something like that would only make me stand out.*

And that would naturally be processed into information. He had changed his face and his attire. Having purchased a C-Rank Mercenary Badge, Lee Sungmin intended to act faithfully as one. As long as he was careful, no information about him would circulate. Even Erebrisa could not purchase information that did not exist.

[So you're just going to stuff this Elder into this shabby armor?] Void Monarch grumbled.

It could not be helped. The Magic Armor inhabited by Void Monarch was far too extravagant for a C-Rank Mercenary. Because of that, Void Monarch had to transfer his consciousness to a set of old, worn armor.

[Such a troublesome method. Why not just take over the ship? A simple 'Shut up and sail to Bellador' would be far easier, wouldn't it?]

*That would only increase my notoriety. Besides, weren't you the one who advised me to be careful?*

[Of course I was joking. This method is better, even if it's a hassle,] Void Monarch grumbled. The Void Monarch of the past would not have actively tried to restrain Lee Sungmin's actions, but that was no longer the case. He knew Lee Sungmin did not want to become a Yokai, and Void Monarch himself wanted Lee Sungmin to maintain his humanity. That was why he had stopped him in the Elf Forest.

*Well. In the worst-case scenario, that method might be fine.*

With that thought, Lee Sungmin headed for the ship.

Zod had ambition.

He was just a mediocre merchant now, but he dreamed of one day becoming an executive of the Merchants' Guild, no, of standing at its very top. His retired father always told him to stop his bullshit and live within his means, but Zod remained faithful to his ambition. His ship was an old caravel inherited from his father, and his budget was so tight he could only hire about ten third-rate mercenaries as escorts for his first long-distance voyage to Bellador.

Still, it was fine. Zod opened a bottle of cheap wine in his room. To him, wine was purely for show, and he did not know the first thing about its profound flavors.

*When I get to Bellador, I should buy a bottle of high-quality wine.*

He swirled the wine in his glass, putting on airs he felt befitted his future station. They would be departing soon. Zod liked the moment just before departure, the fleeting hope that a large profit might be waiting at the end of the voyage.

BANG!

Before he could finish his glass, the door burst open. Zod, who had been leaning back in his chair savoring the awful wine, did not even flinch. This sort of thing happened all the time.

*The mercenaries must be bickering amongst themselves again.*

He could pretend not to notice something of this level. The mercenaries Zod had hired were free agents, unaffiliated with any corps. They were of lower quality in many ways, but men like that were cheap. Besides, even if their quality was low, as long as they were registered with the Mercenary Guild, they would not betray you if you paid them.

It was just as Zod had thought.

Lee Sungmin slowly turned his head. A fist had suddenly struck him, but the blow carried so little force it barely registered as pain. It lacked the power to even snap his head back, but he turned it anyway. He figured he had to do at least that much to seem like a C-Rank.

"Does it hurt?"

His attacker was a huge man with bulging muscles. What was his name again? He had heard the introduction earlier. Right, his name was Yangsu, a C-Rank Mercenary. He had mentioned a nickname, but Lee Sungmin could not remember that part.

Lee Sungmin was not the only one Yangsu had hit. Eight other mercenaries were rubbing their struck cheeks, suppressing their anger. None of them challenged Yangsu, however. As C-Rank Mercenaries, they were only around the level of second-rate murim warriors. It was not just masters who could gauge each other's levels. The second-rate had their own ways of distinguishing strength.

"If you just listen to me, I won't have to hit you again," Yangsu threatened.

Lee Sungmin rubbed his cheek, just as the other mercenaries were doing. It was a simple move to establish dominance, and Yangsu's intention seemed to have succeeded. Among the mercenaries on this ship, none resisted him.

Yangsu made eye contact with each of the men he had hit. Everyone lowered their gaze. When his eyes met Lee Sungmin's, however, Lee Sungmin did not look down but stared back intently.

[A master of the Transcendent Realm getting punched in the face by a pipsqueak like that!] Void Monarch burst out laughing.

When Lee Sungmin glared back, Yangsu widened his own eyes and glared fiercely. The man's large fist suddenly rose.

"......"

However, Yangsu could not swing. Lee Sungmin's foot shot out and connected squarely with the man's shin. He held back nearly all of his strength. A proper kick would have shattered the bone completely.

Yangsu screamed, grabbing his injured leg. Lee Sungmin followed with a punch of his own.

"......"

Yangsu's head snapped violently to the side. Lee Sungmin had considered just taking the hit and letting the man have his way, but he decided it would be more convenient in the long run to establish his own dominance.

"Does it hurt?" Lee Sungmin asked.

His kicked leg hurt, and his cheek hurt. Yangsu was sprawled on the floor, looking up at him.

"Don't bother me from now on," Lee Sungmin said, tapping the spear on his back with his fingertips. It was a cheap spear bought from a weapon shop, but the blade was real. Yangsu watched the spearhead glint in the sunlight and swallowed hard.

"Ye-yes."

By taking down Yangsu, he had established himself above the other mercenaries. Satisfied, Lee Sungmin turned away. Sailing the ship was the crew's job, not theirs. A mercenary's job was to catch monsters that appeared from the sea or to fight pirates. Or, to suppress a mutiny among the crew. In other words, as long as no trouble broke out, there was nothing for a mercenary to do.

"I'll be in my room. If a problem comes up..." Lee Sungmin pointed a finger at Yangsu. "You come and get me yourself."

"Yes..." Yangsu answered in an intimidated voice.

Lee Sungmin headed for the room assigned to him. As he crossed the deck, he made eye contact with some of the crew. They only glanced at him and said nothing. They knew well that mercenaries were different from them.

[Well, at least the trip will be comfortable.]

*I don't think so.*

He had never been on a ship while working as a mercenary. In his past life, he had never left Behenger. He had heard stories many times, however. On a long-distance voyage, incidents were bound to happen. The sea was a cage for giant monsters, and the pirates who made it their stage were more ferocious than mountain bandits. Some of the more audacious ones would even attack large sailing ships or vessels of the Murim Alliance.

This ship was small. For monsters or pirates, it was perfect prey. Honestly, he did not quite understand. Weren't ten C-Rank Mercenaries far too few troops?

"So you're the mercenary captain for this voyage."

A voice cut in from the side. It was a middle-aged man who had been watching the commotion. Lee Sungmin glanced at him. The man wore a decent-looking sword at his waist, and Lee Sungmin discerned that he was a martial artist who had reached the peak realm.

"And you are?"

"The ship owner's bodyguard. Don't cause any unnecessary trouble. We're on the same ship, after all, and I'd rather not kill a comrade," the man said.

Surely, the confidence to set sail did not stem from having a single peak realm martial artist as a bodyguard, right? Lee Sungmin looked at the man and asked, "Are you a mercenary too?"

"Technically, yes. Though I'm in a different league from you lot. I'm affiliated with the Merchants' Guild. I boarded this ship to prevent any unnecessary mishaps during this voyage."

Lee Sungmin understood. He had heard about this in his past life. If you paid the Merchants' Guild, you could have a 'mediator' from the Guild board your ship. They acted as the owner's bodyguards, and if they ran into pirates, they would negotiate on the owner's behalf in the name of the Merchants' Guild. Negotiation really just meant paying them off, and the money paid by the mediator would become the ship owner's debt. It was the price for their lives. Most pirates did not want to cause friction with the Merchants' Guild, so if a mediator was confirmed to be on board, they would choose to take the money and let the ship go.

"If a mediator like you is on board, then this ship should be safe."

"That's right. There's an agreement between the pirates in the waters leading to Bellador and the Merchants' Guild not to attack ships with a mediator," the man said, looking Lee Sungmin up and down. "Do you understand? My presence here is what's protecting your life."

"I appreciate your hard work."

Lee Sungmin gave a slight bow and walked past him.

[There are so many bastards who don't know their place.]

*Tell me about it,* Lee Sungmin agreed with Void Monarch as he entered his room.

*Still, it's a relief. It looks like we'll reach Bellador without getting involved with pirates.*

[I wonder,] Void Monarch chuckled. [If it'll be that easy.]
