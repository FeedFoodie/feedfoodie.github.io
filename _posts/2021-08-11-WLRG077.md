"You dueled with Jihak?"

The question came from Namgung Heewon, who stood beside Scarlet. At Lee Sungmin's confirmation, his jaw went slack. He stared for a long moment, his shoulders beginning to tremble.

"Grandmaster Bulyeong," he seethed. "That son of a bitch. I begged him for a duel, but he just kept bullshitting me, saying I couldn't!"

"Did you know who he was?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"Of course I did, brother. Jihak is the future of Shaolin, Grandmaster Bulyeong's direct disciple. No, that's a little off…" Namgung Heewon corrected himself. "He's learned martial arts from all of Shaolin's monks since he was a child. An incredible feat." He let out a deep sigh.

"The heirs of the Four Great Clans, including my own, are outstanding, but we're a step below the true prodigies of the Nine Sects One Gang. Jihak of Shaolin, Cheongmyeong of Wudang, and Chwi Geol of the Beggars' Gang… those three in particular have mastered all their sects' famous ultimate techniques. You could call them the future of their sects."

The names meant nothing to him. In his past life as a mercenary, Lee Sungmin had picked up countless rumors, yet not once had he heard of Jihak of Shaolin, Cheongmyeong of Wudang, or Chwi Geol of the Beggars' Gang.

"I've never heard any rumors about them," he admitted.

"That's to be expected," Namgung Heewon muttered, licking his lips. "It's the same for Jihak. The three of them never leave their sects. They're like secret weapons, holed up like reclusive masters doing nothing but training, and most of them aren't even thirty yet. Every year I come to Shaolin, I hear stories about him. I really wanted to have a duel with him just once…"

*If they fought, who would win?* Lee Sungmin wondered. He had never seen Namgung Heewon's full strength, nor had he seen Jihak's. Still, based on his limited experience, Jihak seemed the stronger of the two.

"So, brother," Namgung Heewon's voice cut through his thoughts. "Where do you plan on going now?"

"...For the time being, I plan to stay in Shaolin."

At his answer, Scarlet's eyes widened, and she glared at him. "What? You're staying here?"

"Yes," Lee Sungmin confirmed. "I have a lot to think about and a lot I want to refine. Fortunately, fate has allowed me to train here at Shaolin."

"It seems Grandmaster Bulyeong has taken quite a liking to you, brother," Namgung Heewon mused. "I was thinking of taking you to the Namgung Clan if you were free."

"...I'll visit later if I get the chance. What happened with your business, Hyung-nim?"

"Me? Oh, it was the same as last year. I showed a bit of my martial arts in front of Grandmaster Bulyeong, and that was it. We're all too lacking to become the Chairman of the Heavenly Dragon Association. I can't even beat my own father, so how could I become the Chairman?"

A sense of emptiness washed over Lee Sungmin as he listened to Namgung Heewon's muttering. The world was too vast. Namgung Heewon, who had toyed with him, was weaker than Jihak. That same Jihak had been made a fool of by Wiji Hoyeon, and Namgung Heewon couldn't even defeat his own father, the Heavenly Venerable Sword King.

*I'm... so small.*

Was it because his standing was different now? Or his perspective? The world had seemed vast in his past life too, but the scale he felt then was nothing compared to the scale he felt now. It was only natural. As a C-Rank Mercenary, he had never experienced encounters like these. What one sees and feels changes with experience. This connected to the enlightenment he had felt on Mount Mushii. When 'I' change, the world changes. Because 'I' had changed, what he could see had changed.

"...If you're going to stay here, I think I'll wander around a bit more," Scarlet muttered, drawing his attention.

"......"

"I haven't learned the Void Path, so there's nothing for me to gain by staying. I only came this far because I was worried about you… but well, you won't be in any danger here."

"...Where are you thinking of going?"

"Anywhere and everywhere. I have more than enough skill to take care of myself."

Lee Sungmin considered trying to stop her but decided against it. The existence known as Lee Sungmin had not been involved in the incident Scarlet experienced in his past life. If he became deeply involved with her for no reason, her fate could change drastically.

"Will we be able to meet again?"

"Of course we will, you idiot," Scarlet grumbled. "Did you forget what I said? You promised to help me if I was in danger." She reached inside her robe and pulled out a bracelet woven from red thread. "Take it."

"What is this?"

"It's a bracelet connected to my spirit. I gave one to Baek Sogo, too. I never thought I'd end up giving one to you."

The bracelet looked familiar. He remembered seeing Baek Sogo wear a similar one on her wrist before he lost his sight.

"It's not some convenient tool that allows for telepathy," she explained. "However, no matter where you are in the world, if I wish it, a signal will be sent through that bracelet. A signal of where I am."

Lee Sungmin nodded and put the bracelet on his wrist.

The next day, Scarlet left Shaolin. She claimed that staying any longer would only create lingering attachments, though her departure still felt abrupt. Namgung Heewon left as well.

"Come visit the Namgung Clan when you're free," he said, patting Lee Sungmin's shoulder a few times. "If you come, brother, I'll welcome you anytime."

Despite their sudden meeting, their bond was not a shallow one. Lee Sungmin watched him descend the mountain gate, learning that the other heirs had already left the day before. The monks of Shaolin offered him a room, but he refused their consideration. Gripping his spear, he left through the rear gate, heading for Grandmaster Bulyeong's residence from memory.

He decided to postpone his trip to Dreamoor to meet Denir, the God of Time. It wasn't far, and the chance to receive guidance from Great Monk Bulyeong and Jihak felt like a miraculous encounter in its own right.

*Five years from now... when winter freezes over.*

The words were ambiguous, but he didn't need to think too hard about them. The Spirit in Bulyeong's body had told him to go to a place where perpetual snow never melts, which could only mean the northernmost region of Eria. 'When winter freezes over' likely meant the dead of winter.

If it was five years from now, he still had time. His mind raced, mapping out the future. He was supposed to meet Wiji Hoyeon when he turned twenty-four, six years from now, but five years from now, the Beggars' Gang would discover the Dungeon that entangled both Baek Sogo and Wiji Hoyeon. He had to prevent Baek Sogo's death before his reunion. In other words, regardless of the order of events, he had at most five years.

*How far can I go?*

*How much can I achieve?* The distance between him and Wiji Hoyeon was a vast, impassable chasm. Lee Sungmin looked down at his own hands. His mind, ki, and body were distorted. If he couldn't overcome this, he would lose everything within ten years. He decided to hold off on taking the Great Cycle Pill for now, fearing that with his current imbalance, it might only hasten the qi deviation.

*What... should I do?*

His gaze fell blankly on the spear in his hand, a weapon commissioned out of self-satisfaction, a desire for a reward. He then looked down at the Magic Armor he wore, something he had desperately wanted as a shortcut to overcome the wall he faced.

With a metallic sound, he infused his inner arts and took off the armor, placing it in a visible spot. He did the same with his spear, plunging it into a large rock near the cave. The enhanced qi on its tip allowed it to pierce the stone as if it were tofu.

From his Subspace Pocket, he withdrew an ordinary spear, one he hadn't held in a long time. It was a spare he had bought, its shaft stiff and unfamiliar from disuse. He ran a bare hand over the wood. Could the calluses on his palm truly be called proof of his effort? A low, bitter laugh escaped him.

He didn't think that a calamity was someone who put in less effort than an ordinary person. Even if they started from a point ahead of ordinary people and could run faster, the effort they put into running was surely real. An ordinary person starting behind a calamity had to put in more, more, and even more effort just to keep up. The tortoise and the hare. He remembered the fable from his childhood. The hare ran fast and the tortoise crawled slowly. The hare grew complacent and fell asleep, while the tortoise continued to crawl, reaching the finish line first.

Lee Sungmin was not the hare. He might even be worse than the tortoise, and yet, he had been complacent. He hadn't even crawled diligently, letting his eyes wander.

"Fucking idiot."

Lee Sungmin muttered, tightening his grip on the spear. The calluses on his palm. The Spear Technique in his head. The training he did on Mount Mushii. It wasn't enough. Everything was lacking. He slowly began to perform the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear.

"That's an excellent Spear Technique."

A voice spoke just as he finished the entire form. Lee Sungmin steadied his unwavering breath and looked toward the sound. Jihak, dressed in a monk's robe, was standing there.

"That's why it's a pity."

"Is it because my skills are lacking?" Lee Sungmin asked, meeting his gaze.

Jihak slowly shook his head. "Didn't I tell you? What you lack most is experience." His gaze drifted to the cave entrance, where the discarded armor and spear rested. "Why did you take off your armor and put down your good spear?"

"They don't suit me right now."

"Is that how you're steeling your mind and resolve?"

"Do you think it's hypocrisy?"

"Not at all," Jihak replied. "Martial arts is the study of using the body, and the body ultimately follows the mind's commands. An inner arts technique is a way of using the mind. Mind, ki, and body. People often say those three are the factors that complete one's martial arts." He muttered this as he took the spear from his back, its red tassel fluttering. "But those three are what complete a person, even before martial arts. The mind is enveloped by the spirit and tempered by ki."

"...Is that what martial arts is?"

"Ah, this is merely Shaolin's martial theory. There are many types of martial arts, and each has a different goal. The martial theory of Buddhists, like Shaolin, and the martial theory of Taoists, like Wudang or Mount Hua, aim to achieve great enlightenment by completing self-cultivation through martial arts."

"Great enlightenment?"

"The expressions and words are different, but to put it simply, it's about becoming something other than human. Taoist martial arts aim to become a deity through Celestial Ascension, and Buddhist martial arts aim to become a Buddha through Nirvana. Neither a deity nor a Buddha is human. They were just originally human."

It was the first time Lee Sungmin had heard this kind of martial theory, and he listened intently. Seeing his focus, Jihak smiled and added, "As I said, there are many types of martial arts. It's not just Taoist and Buddhist. The martial arts commonly called unorthodox are more intuitive, techniques for killing people. Besides those, many are refined from the characteristics of animals or insects. Even Shaolin has such techniques. However, even if the beginning is the same, the ultimate goal is different."

"Do you want to become a Buddha?"

"Haha! I am far too immature to become a Buddha. The reason I'm telling you this is because I want you to understand. All I know is Shaolin's martial arts, and the only martial theory I know is that of becoming a Buddha. I'm not pressuring you to become a Buddhist, but if I am to look over your martial arts, you must first understand the martial arts of Shaolin."

Jihak approached, shaking his spear tassel.

"In Shaolin, there are seventy-two ultimate techniques known as the Seventy-two Peerless Arts. People often think of unarmed martial arts like the Hundred Steps Divine Fist and the Great Strength Vajra Palm when they think of Shaolin, but I can say with confidence that this is certain. Among the current Nine Sects One Gang, there is no place that understands and has established spear and staff arts as deeply as Shaolin."

Jihak lowered his stance, holding the spear with both hands. In response, Lee Sungmin assumed the starting stance of the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear.

"Look at Shaolin," Jihak said, a warm smile on his face.

"And feel it."

A powerful fighting spirit flowed from him, filled with an unshakeable pride for his sect.
