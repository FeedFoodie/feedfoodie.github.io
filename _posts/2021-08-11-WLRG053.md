Lee Sungmin began to organize his thoughts, laying out the path ahead. His most immediate priority was the Dark Mage Dungeon subjugation, set to take place in half a year. The Magic Armor was his primary goal, though he would seize any other artifacts if the opportunity arose. Once that was done, nothing else required his immediate attention. With no reason to linger in the city of Behenger, he planned to leave.

However, looming over everything was his most important task, the one that truly mattered. He had to prevent Baek Sogo's death, an event still six years away.

An obvious solution presented itself. *Couldn't I just clear the Dungeon in advance?* If its value disappeared, neither Baek Sogo nor Wiji Hoyeon would have a reason to go there.

The thought was fleeting, however, because he knew it was impossible. A true Dungeon, not some artificial creation, was a force of nature, akin to a sudden disaster. They appeared without warning. While some Dungeons might lie dormant and undiscovered for a time, the one fated to appear in six years was not one of them. It would be created then, and only then, discovered by nearby adventurers who would choose to sell the information rather than conquer it themselves. That was how the rumors would begin.

In other words, even if he went to the location now, there would be nothing to clear. Besides, he didn't even know the Dungeon's exact location, only a rough idea of the area. Searching such a vast region would be a fool's errand.

His plan to save Baek Sogo carried an unavoidable consequence. *I might have a reunion with Wiji Hoyeon.* He had promised to meet her in ten years, but this path might force their reunion much sooner. Should he be happy about that? The question, one he had pondered several times, left only a bitter taste in his mouth.

Why did Wiji Hoyeon kill Baek Sogo? It wasn't just her. Wiji Hoyeon had killed everyone who entered that Dungeon, but why? She must have had a reason. The Wiji Hoyeon he knew…

*The Wiji Hoyeon I know.*

The image began to distort. CRACKLE. Static seemed to fill his head, and the unpleasant drone of cicadas from Mount Mushii echoed in his ears. Lee Sungmin clamped his hands over his ears as the words of Nebul and Kim Jonghyun threw his mind into chaos.

The Wiji Hoyeon he knew. The Baek Sogo he knew. Everyone he knew, everyone he had met, all the people who had shown him kindness… He had doubted their kindness before but had eventually come to accept it, believing it had its own reasons. *…Was that really true? Was their kindness truly born from reason?* There are no coincidences. Mushii's words haunted him. A life. His life. Whose was it, really?

"Ah."

A wave of dizziness washed over him, and his mind grew cloudy. Lee Sungmin collapsed, his legs giving out beneath him. Why had he returned to the past? It must have been a simple coincidence. Entering that Dungeon was a coincidence, and grabbing the Stone of Reincarnation was also a coincidence. Yes. That was all it was. Mushii had said there were no coincidences… but Lee Sungmin just couldn't accept those words.

If it wasn't a coincidence, then what in the world was it?

His thoughts latched onto a name. *God of Time.*

Denir. The reclusive god living in Dreamoor. Mushii had told him to seek Denir out, saying that what he gained from the meeting was entirely up to him.

Could Denir answer his questions? Kim Jonghyun couldn't, or wouldn't, only hinting that something forceful and aggressive was entangled with the truth. Even Mushii had offered no answers, seemingly unable to. A human couldn't answer, and a god wouldn't. Could another god, Denir, truly provide what he sought?

His heart was already set on the journey to Dreamoor, but he couldn't leave for the city just yet. It was simply too far, a journey of over a month from Behenger even on horseback. He couldn't depart so recklessly, not knowing what might happen when he met Denir or how long the round trip would take.

For now, he decided, he would focus on the more immediate goal. He would obtain the Magic Armor.

"It's been a while."

Nebul appeared at his call, bowing his head deeply. Lee Sungmin hadn't summoned him for a week, feeling it was more urgent to shake the bewitchment from his heart than to check Erebrisa's available requests.

"I'd like to check the requests," Lee Sungmin said.

"I've already organized them," Nebul replied, raising a hand. He tapped the empty air a few times, and a translucent window materialized before him. *Is that magic, too?* Lee Sungmin wondered, peering at the window Nebul was viewing.

"There are various S-Rank requests. Any particular preferences?"

"Something I can do alone," Lee Sungmin specified. "Preferably something that won't take long and isn't too far from here."

"If you're looking for something you can do alone that doesn't take long, most of them are subjugation requests."

"That's fine."

"Hmm…" Nebul scratched his head. "There aren't many subjugation requests near Behenger. As you know, the Corona Mercenary Group operating here takes most of the local jobs."

He continued, "The requests we connect you with are generally shared with the Mercenary Guild, though not all of them, of course. Top-secret requests from our members aren't disclosed to the Guild, but those are impossible to undertake at just S-Rank."

"Is that so?"

"Yes. I could theoretically set you up with one… but with your level of skill, you'd most likely die."

Nebul's assessment was blunt, and Lee Sungmin didn't argue. He had proven his SS-Rank skill against the Ogre Chimera, but he had no intention of choosing a request that dangerous.

"Let's see… Ah, here's one that fits your conditions." Nebul scrolled through the list before picking out a request and showing it to Lee Sungmin. It was a bit far from Behenger, but a village he could reach in two days on horseback.

"This one wasn't chosen by the Corona Mercenary Group. A Twin-Head Troll. Ever heard of it?"

"I haven't," Lee Sungmin answered.

He knew trolls were similar to Ogres but were generally considered a lesser version, with lower intelligence and less physical strength. Their one overwhelming superiority, however, was their powerful regeneration and immunity to magic, traits that earned them an A-Rank classification. A twin-headed variant was certainly a monster worthy of an S-Rank.

"Will you accept it?" Nebul asked. "The reward is thirty million Eru."

"I'll do it," Lee Sungmin nodded.

*

"Heard you caught a Twin-Head Troll?"

Rudd's voice called out as Lee Sungmin entered the Behenger Mercenary Guild. It was the first time they had seen each other since the Ogre Chimera subjugation. Lee Sungmin met Rudd's steady gaze and nodded.

"......"

"You didn't get that request through the Guild, did you?" Rudd pressed. "What, you got another source for jobs?"

"...It's a bit difficult for me to answer that," Lee Sungmin said with a bitter smile.

Rudd just shrugged. "Well, it's not like there's a rule saying you have to get requests from the Guild. If you're skilled enough, you can find work on your own. More importantly… I heard you turned down Zeon's scouting offer?"

Lee Sungmin hadn't spread that rumor, and he doubted Bern would have either. *Then was it Zeon?* He sat down next to Rudd and nodded.

"Where'd you hear that?"

"Zeon said it himself. Told everyone he tried to scout you and you refused. Thanks to that, the other mercenaries are keeping an eye on you." After saying that, Rudd chuckled. "No, maybe that's not it. Most of the guys here have been watching you ever since you first came in and unleashed your sword ki. Especially since you're Classless."

Lee Sungmin didn't react. He already knew he was drawing attention, particularly from the other Classless mercenaries. Most of them were low-ranked, and they looked at him with a mixture of admiration and envy, a Classless who had surpassed the wall of the peak realm. He was all too familiar with such gazes, having felt the same way in his past life when he joined Zeon's Corona Guild.

"Keep your distance from Zeon," Rudd said, his voice lowering. Bern, who was usually behind the bar, was nowhere to be seen. "Zeon is… what's the word… he's a black-hearted bastard. Very suspicious. Doesn't even trust his own people. All the A-Rank and higher mercenaries in his Guild are completely under his thumb."

"...I know," Lee Sungmin replied. "I don't trust him. That's why I refused his offer."

"Thanks to that, he's probably in a pretty bad mood," Rudd muttered, downing the alcohol in his cup. "It's best not to fight with him. Just… it's easier to bow your head a little."

"Why are you telling me this?" Lee Sungmin asked, glancing at Rudd after listening quietly.

Rudd blinked at the question. "You think I'm meddling for no reason?"

"No, that's not it. I appreciate the concern, but… does this mean I can trust you?"

"That's for you to decide," Rudd said with an indifferent expression. "But I can swear this. I don't particularly dislike you."

"Then?"

"Then what? You want me to say I love you or something? Sorry, but I'm not into that," Rudd grumbled, swirling the cup in his hand. "I hate Zeon. A few years ago, back when I was still A-Rank, I got a scouting offer from him. I turned it down because he asked me to reveal my livelihood. I had a pretty hard time after that."

"...Your livelihood?"

"I'm a Spirit Magic user," Rudd answered. Lee Sungmin already knew that. Rudd used a sword but no martial arts, relying instead on Spirit Magic, an extremely rare profession even in Eria. "Zeon told me to reveal the promise I made in my contract with my Elemental. Do you know what a restriction is?"

"...No. I don't."

"Spirit Magic is… well, it's about making a contract with an Elemental from another world. In that contract, the user has to make a promise to the Elemental. It's nothing major, but if the user breaks it, the contract is terminated." Rudd's brow furrowed as he spoke. "The son of a bitch. Asking a Spirit Magic user something like that. So I told him absolutely not. After that… I had a really hard time. As you know, the Corona Mercenary Group has a tight grip on most of the requests that come into this Guild. The jobs just stopped coming my way. There are plenty of A-Rank Mercenaries, after all."

"...And so?"

"I left Behenger, earned my S-Rank in another city, and came back. I was going to get my revenge on that bastard, but… when I returned, that son of a bitch had become an SS-Rank. Damn it." Rudd chuckled humorlessly. "Well, you're S-Rank, so it'll be hard for him to mess with you carelessly. Still, be careful. That bastard Zeon is a real piece of work. He doesn't trust his own people, and he treats outsiders like absolute shit."

He had never heard that Zeon was that kind of person. In his past life, Lee Sungmin had been too insignificant to be caught up in such stories.

"I understand."

This life, however, was different. A slight bitterness rose within him nonetheless. It was like discovering the ugliness of a figure he had once admired.
