When he truly thought about it, the whole affair had been utterly futile. The Hunter who had massacred the Classless of Zenavis for an entire week in his past life was, in the end, nothing more than a third-rate martial artist. Lee Sungmin looked down at the man's corpse, which lay foaming at the mouth with blood. He did not know the Hunter's name, and now he never would. His thoughts on the fight were simple.

*One wrong move and I would have died.*

That was his reality. He had returned to the past and, through his meeting with Wiji Hoyeon, had even learned the divine Violet Dawn Divine Art. Nevertheless, Lee Sungmin still had to risk his life just to kill a single third-rate martial artist.

It was an unavoidable truth. Learning a divine art did not magically grant him talent. For Lee Sungmin, simply accepting and understanding the technique was a task that bordered on the impossible.

"Damn it."

Lee Sungmin cursed, vigorously wiping the blood from his hands on the dead Hunter's clothes. The corpse's fingers were still locked in a death grip around the Subspace Pocket he had thrown.

He retrieved the pocket before prying the sword from the Hunter's grasp. It was too large to fit inside, so Lee Sungmin took the weapon and began searching the man's body. There was nothing of value besides 210,000 Eru. He had hoped the man might be carrying a martial arts manual, but found nothing of the sort.

*Then again, what good would a martial arts manual be? I have my own desperate problems to deal with...*

Just clinging to the Violet Dawn Divine Art was overwhelming enough. He could not even properly use the Soul Chasing Spear Art, a technique that pursued form without insight. If he tried to learn another martial art on top of that, he would be completely swamped.

Lee Sungmin left the corpse behind and departed from the forest. He felt little revulsion at having killed someone, for he had grown accustomed to such things in his past life. It was not that he would unconditionally try to kill his opponents. If, however, an opponent was trying to kill him, and killing the man was the only way to resolve the situation, then he had no choice.

He did not feel good about it. A bitter feeling lingered. The fight had made him truly realize just how weak the Classless of Zenavis were. How many dozens of them had been killed by a mere third-rate martial artist of that level?

*The Hunter was active for a week. After that, he never appeared again.*

He had probably died. Either he had ignorantly picked a fight with a proper martial artist or a mage, like this time, or he had messed with a Classless who was not like the others. In truth, whether the Hunter from his past life had lived or died was none of Lee Sungmin's business.

In this life, the Hunter was dead. The Classless hunt was over.

"You've brought a peculiar sword," Jack, the street vendor, muttered as he looked the weapon up and down. The sword Lee Sungmin handed him had a design that blatantly proclaimed it was from the murim.

"It's rare for a martial artist to die in the forest."

"Not everyone from the murim is strong, you know."

"That's true," Jack conceded. "The third-rate and second-rate ones come here too."

He did not ask about the sword's origin, yet he still gave Lee Sungmin a good price for it.

"That kid who's been hanging around you lately," Jack said after a moment.

"...Huh? Ah, you mean Hoyeon?"

"Yeah. That kid... what's his deal?" Jack's eyes narrowed.

Lee Sungmin tilted his head, his own eyes widening. Why was he suddenly asking about Wiji Hoyeon? "Why do you ask about Hoyeon?"

"Well, I see him in the square sometimes, and I just can't figure out what he's doing. He'll just be sitting by the fountain doing nothing, then suddenly he'll start wandering around." Jack frowned as he muttered. "Strangely, that kid makes me really uncomfortable. That... what should I call it? An instinctual... yeah. That's it. An instinctual feeling of not wanting to get close. What is it... a gut feeling? Yeah, is it a gut feeling?"

Jack let out a dry laugh, finding his own words ridiculous. To feel instinctively wary of a green thirteen-year-old kid was absurd. He was, however, someone who trusted his gut feelings a great deal. It was thanks to those feelings that he had saved his own life several times when he was on the verge of death, wandering the monster-infested forest.

"I don't really know what he's doing either," Lee Sungmin answered honestly. He and Wiji Hoyeon had been moving separately for the past week, and he was even giving him an allowance, but he had no idea what he was actually doing in Zenavis.

In his past life, Wiji Hoyeon had stayed in Zenavis for a month before leaving for parts unknown. At the time, Lee Sungmin had been too busy just trying to take care of himself to pay him any mind. He only heard rumors about him after becoming a mercenary. He was eighteen then, based in Bronne, the city closest to Zenavis. By that point, Wiji Hoyeon had already been making a name for herself as the Little Heavenly Demon for a year.

Thinking back on it now, it felt a little strange. What had Wiji Hoyeon been doing for those three years? In his past life, Lee Sungmin had lived in Zenavis for three years, and after leaving, he arrived in Bronne and became a mercenary a year later. Wiji Hoyeon, who had left Zenavis just a month after being summoned, had stayed out of sight for three years before suddenly becoming famous. A three-year gap. What had the Wiji Hoyeon of his past life done during that time?

It had been a little over a month since Wiji Hoyeon was summoned to Zenavis. If things proceeded as they had before, he would leave the city soon.

*But the Wiji Hoyeon of the past and the Wiji Hoyeon of now are different.*

The person herself had not changed, but the events he experienced had. The Wiji Hoyeon of his past life had never met him. He remembered what he had done during his one-month stay in Zenavis back then. In that single month, he had stirred up a storm. Four goblin settlements were annihilated, and three orc settlements were wiped out. Even for creatures with high reproductive rates, the annihilation of seven settlements was an event sufficient to distort the balance of the forest.

The Wiji Hoyeon of this life, however, was living differently. It was not that he had not gone to the forest. He had not gone for the past week, but for the three weeks before that, he had accompanied Lee Sungmin. He only went, he did not do anything. While he fought monsters, Wiji Hoyeon would observe the mushrooms growing at the base of trees, look at flowers, or watch the insects crawling around. Occasionally, he would point out flaws in his movements.

"You're back?"

When he returned to the inn, Wiji Hoyeon was already there. He gave him a wide smile as he entered the room.

"You're back early today."

"You too," he replied.

Lee Sungmin woke at the crack of dawn. He would train in the Violet Dawn Divine Art as soon as he was up, and when he felt he had done enough, he would go to the inn's backyard to swing his spear. After that, he would head to the forest to hunt monsters and return a little after noon. Then, it was back to training again.

Today, however, he had returned before noon. He had earned quite a bit of money by killing the Hunter, so there was no need to hunt more monsters.

"You usually come back around evening," he noted.

"As of today, I've finished all the books in the library," Wiji Hoyeon said with a bashful smile.

When he had first heard the rumors about the Little Heavenly Demon, the image Lee Sungmin had of Wiji Hoyeon was that of a cold-blooded demonic practitioner, but after actually spending time with him, the title did not seem to fit him very well. He was expressive and laughed a lot. Seeing how he beat people down with facts, he did feel he was quite rude, but he did have basic decency. At least, he was very kind when dealing with people like Lula or Hans.

"By the way, you," he began.

Today's lunch was a sandwich with thick cuts of meat. Lula brought them over on a plate. Wiji Hoyeon was about to say something more but closed his mouth when he saw Lula approaching. After a brief exchange, Lula returned to the kitchen. Once he was gone, Wiji Hoyeon spoke again, his expression nonchalant.

"You killed someone."

Lee Sungmin, who had been raising a knife to cut his sandwich, froze.

"...Huh?"

"You smell of blood. Human blood." Wiji Hoyeon raised a finger and tapped the tip of his nose, then smiled, showing his teeth. "I'm used to the smell of human blood, you see. I killed so many in my childhood. I couldn't help but get used to it."

"The smell of blood is just the smell of blood."

"No, it's different. Why did you kill?"

"......"

Lee Sungmin kept his mouth shut. With a deep sigh, he finished cutting his sandwich. "He was a third-rate martial artist. He tried to kill me, so..."

"So you're saying you won against a third-rate martial artist. If you've learned the Violet Dawn Divine Art, you should at least be able to do that much."

"I couldn't face him head-on."

"Because your inner qi is lacking," Wiji Hoyeon replied as if he already knew. He picked up a napkin and wiped the sauce from the corner of his mouth. His eyes gleamed. "You're strange. While you were out hunting, I wandered around this city and tried to understand it, and this world, in my own way. I also learned about the plight of the Classless. Most of them are miserable."

"That's right."

"I can understand. It's one thing for an insect, but a person has to steel themselves to kill even a small animal. If someone can kill without that resolve, they must be broken somewhere." 

Wiji Hoyeon knew that fact well. He too, since childhood, had constantly practiced killing other things to get used to murder. It was not about becoming accustomed to it, but about being worn down by it.

"But you're strange. It's been two months, you said. I heard that you went into the forest the very next day after being summoned to this world and hunted monsters there."

"...What's so strange about that? If I didn't, I wouldn't be able to survive."

"Haha! Do you think other Classless fail to act because they don't understand that? They know, but they can't do it. Risking your own life to kill something else, that's what it is. Something you know you have to do, but can't."

That was also the biggest difference between a Classless and a martial artist or a mage. Most martial artists were used to killing. No matter how you dressed it up, the martial arts they learned were, in the end, techniques for efficiently killing people. 

It was the same for a mage. It was easy to think of mages as people who just locked themselves in workshops to research magic, but the reality was completely different. The magic they researched and learned made killing a person as easy as swatting a mosquito.

Compared to them, what about the Classless? They had no skills and no resolve. That was why it was so hard for them to survive in this world.

"You're fourteen years old," Wiji Hoyeon continued. "That's an age where it's difficult to get used to things like murder. It's an age where it's hard to kill even a small animal, let alone a person, but you do it without a second thought."

"Because I have to live."

"Fine. Then let me bring up another question." Wiji Hoyeon shrugged.

*This brat. What the hell is he trying to say?* Lee Sungmin glared at him and shoved the sandwich into his mouth.

"You have no talent."

"Keuk!"

The sudden, blunt fact struck him squarely in the chest. The sandwich he was swallowing got stuck in his throat. Lee Sungmin coughed and sputtered, hastily gulping down his milk.

"But it's really strange," he went on. "You have no talent, yet the way you wield a spear looks quite plausible. It has a lot of unnecessary movements, but you can kill things without a problem."

"Wh-what are you trying to say?"

"Two months. The time that has passed since you were summoned to Eria. Your skill with the spear technique is quite remarkable to have been achieved in just two months, and it's not just the spear technique. You're quite used to handling inner qi. Your footwork when you use movement arts is also quite clean. Did you know? When a mortal learns and uses movement arts, the first thing that happens is their feet get tangled. It takes a very long time to get used to it."

Lee Sungmin knew that for a fact. He too had struggled immensely to fix his tangled feet when he first learned the One Thunder Movement Arts.

"Your movement arts are smooth and familiar. The spear technique you use is a second-rate martial art, but you've refined it well for practical combat. In just two months? And on top of that, you have no talent. What is all this?" Wiji Hoyeon smiled.

*Movement arts? Of course I'm used to it. I've been using it for ten years. Spear technique? Same thing. I swung it for ten years and refined its weak points while working as a mercenary.*

"It's strange. Really... strange. You. What exactly are you hiding?" His gaze bored into him.

Lee Sungmin calmed his startled heart and roughly wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. "I don't know what you're talking about."

"I've pointed it all out one by one, and you're still lying. I don't like liars."

"...Everyone's got a secret or two. Isn't the one trying to force it out the bad guy?" Lee Sungmin grumbled.

Hans had said it. That Wiji Hoyeon was unsettling. Because of an instinctual, gut feeling. Lee Sungmin agreed. Dealing with him right now was extremely awkward. How could a thirteen-year-old kid's head work so well? He could have just let it go.

*I guess he's not the type to think so loosely.*

He swallowed a sigh and gulped down his milk, falling into thought. The way to dispel his suspicion was simple. He could reveal that he was a regressor, but was that okay? The issue was not whether Wiji Hoyeon would believe him, but whether he himself could accept doing it.

"A secret, huh. Right. Everyone has a secret or two. Me too," Wiji Hoyeon chuckled. "Then how about this? I'll tell you one of my secrets."

"I don't think the secret you tell me will be more valuable than the one I have."

"How calculative. Then let's do this. I'll add something else along with my secret. A spear technique? Movement arts? Either is fine."

"...Even if I had one, I couldn't use it. Just like you beat me down with, I have no talent."

"When did I ever beat you down?"

"Words can be violent too," Lee Sungmin said with a gloomy expression.

Wiji Hoyeon looked like he did not quite understand what he meant. "You say the strangest things. Well... you're not wrong. Even if you master that second-rate martial art, the Soul Chasing Spear Art, it can't compare to a peak realm spear technique. In the long run, wouldn't it be better to learn a good one?"

"I probably couldn't even use it properly because I lack inner qi."

"You're playing hard to get."

"That's how much I don't want to talk about it."

"Fine," he declared. "Then I'll give you two-tenths of my inner qi."

At his words, Lee Sungmin's jaw dropped. It was not that inner qi was something that could be given and taken so easily, but he could not understand why he wanted to hear his secret so badly that he would go this far.

"Why on earth do you want to hear it that badly?"

"Because I'm curious. You're my one and only friend. Is it strange to want to know a friend's secret?"

"I'm not particularly curious about yours..."

"That's because your idea of friendship and mine are different. I don't intend to blame you for that, but I'm curious. So. What will you do?" Wiji Hoyeon pressed him.

Lee Sungmin hesitated for a moment. A spear technique, inner qi, and Wiji Hoyeon's secret. This was a miraculous encounter that would never happen again. He let out a deep sigh and nodded his head.

"...Fine. In exchange, promise me. That after you hear my secret... you won't kill me."

"Why would I kill you?"

"Just promise."

"Fine. I promise." He nodded his head. "I will never kill you. Absolutely, no matter what you say from now on, no matter what happens. I will not kill you. Because you're my friend."

Hearing his words, Lee Sungmin felt a slight sense of guilt. "Alright."

"I'll go first."

Just as Lee Sungmin was about to open his mouth, Wiji Hoyeon raised his hand.

"Actually, I'm a girl."
