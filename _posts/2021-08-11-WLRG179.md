A tremor shook the barrier, and Arbes, who was in the middle of infusing the magic circle with power, trembled with it. He looked around in disbelief as a surge of magic power became visible even to his eyes. He narrowed them.

"So it wasn't a rat," Arbes muttered, raising both hands. As his lips moved, he gently caressed the empty air, and a pitch-black sphere materialized where his hand had passed. The sphere would automatically channel magic power into the circle without his direct control, and once it had absorbed enough, it would activate and draw up all the Demonic Energy in the land.

"Stand in the center," Arbes commanded Aine.

Engraved with the Mark of Obedience, Aine nodded without refusal and moved to do as she was told.

Once Aine was positioned in the heart of the magic circle, Arbes formed another hand seal.

CRACKLE!

Black currents sparked through the air as a powerful defensive barrier completely enclosed both Aine and the circle. It was a precaution, meant to protect them in case of an emergency.

With that done, Arbes skillfully continued his work. Although it was his first time in this place, an archmage of his caliber was not greatly restricted by location. Besides, the most important preliminary preparations had already been made while he was drawing the circle. He had chosen Urmuri's Central Plaza precisely because it was situated over the largest vein of Mana in the city, and the black sphere was now connected directly to it.

Arbes reached a hand toward the sphere.

POP!

A small black droplet shot out from its surface and flew toward him, meeting his palm and absorbing into his skin.

"Connected," Arbes muttered with a sinister smile. He had now linked himself to the sphere, and through it, to the vein of Mana. With this connection, Arbes could draw upon the very magic power of the land itself.

He did wonder if it was necessary to go this far. The magic power he had accumulated over hundreds of years was so immense that he had no real need to tap into a Mana vein. Still, he had plenty of time, and there was no harm in making the most thorough, perfect preparations possible.

The preparations continued.

Arbes's lips moved ceaselessly as he formed one hand seal after another. The magic he prepared did not manifest immediately but instead concealed itself within the gaps of space, waiting.

There were two taboos when fighting a mage. Do not enter their domain. Do not give them time.

Arbes knew this well, and he also knew that he was in an absolutely advantageous position. This Central Plaza was his domain, and he had all the time in the world.

{sep}

Envirus knew that as well.

He stopped flying. Not far away, a colossal magic power was surging. Waiting in the Central Plaza was perhaps the Black Mage who had reached the greatest heights in this world. No, it was almost certainly him. Envirus was inwardly certain of it. As far as he knew, only one person had ever reached that realm. The Sage of Two Faces.

"Arbes," Envirus muttered, the name feeling foreign on his tongue after so long.

Arbes wouldn't know his true identity, of course. The Envirus that Arbes knew and the Envirus of today were completely different people. He had gone to great lengths to escape the inescapable Destiny Force, but all of that was useless now. He had met Lee Sungmin, the owner of a Destiny Force so strong it was not the fate of an individual, but the fate of the whole. The destiny of this entire world was intertwined with that young man.

He had hesitated to meet him, but he did not regret the encounter. In the end, one never knew how fate would turn out. All that remained was worry. What kind of being would Lee Sungmin become? In which direction would the fate tied to him proceed?

For a moment, Envirus hesitated, wondering if he should really be doing this. The hesitation was brief. He couldn't pretend not to know what was happening.

*Is this also fate?* he thought, letting out a hollow laugh.

"Laplace," Envirus said aloud.

The shadow at his feet stirred, and a man rose from it. The man who appeared was a Broker of Erebrisa, but he exuded a heterogeneous feeling, different from the others like Nebul. Laplace stood blankly, looking at Envirus without bowing his head.

"The fact that you've called me," Laplace began, "it must be to take back what you entrusted to us, right?"

"That's right."

"And you know what that means?" Laplace let out a chuckle.

Envirus slowly nodded. A long time ago, he had met the mysterious beings known as Erebrisa. Not the Brokers, but the ones who had created them. He had met them and succeeded in making a deal.

"One hundred years," Laplace said, his white-gloved hand opening wide.

CRACKLE!

A flash of pure white electricity sparked, and a sphere glowing with a faint light floated above his palm. It was what Envirus had entrusted to Erebrisa a century ago. He gazed at it with a complicated expression.

"Do you know what it means to take this back?" Laplace pressed.

"I know."

"I can't understand it. You still want it back? The only reason you were able to barely escape your fate was because you entrusted this to us."

"That's right."

"It was a ridiculous deal. We only accepted that absurd transaction because the Spirit Queen herself asked us directly at the time, but we won't listen to such unreasonable demands anymore. We already got what we needed from her through that incident. No matter what you offer, we will never accept such a deal again."

"I know that, too," Envirus replied with a sigh.

Laplace said no more. He slowly extended the sphere forward, and Envirus spread his hands to receive it.

"Looks like you've given up struggling, huh?"

"I had no choice," Envirus muttered, then opened his mouth wide and shoved the sphere inside.

Laplace clicked his tongue and shook his head. "I don't know if we'll see each other again. The Spirit Queen will be sad."

Leaving those words behind, Laplace vanished. After Envirus swallowed the sphere, the light in his eyes disappeared.

What he had entrusted to Erebrisa one hundred years ago was none other than himself. For the century that followed, Envirus had lived a life where he was himself, yet not himself. Through that act, he had managed to escape his own fate and the great Destiny Force flowing through the world.

However, not anymore.

By meeting Lee Sungmin, Envirus, who had escaped the Destiny Force, was once again swept up in it. By taking back the 'self' he had entrusted to Erebrisa, he had also embraced his individual fate. That was why his causality had been twisted.

*There's not much time left.*

The light returned to Envirus's eyes. He lifted his bowed head, the traces of emotion on his face now faint. He looked down at the wand in his hand. He no longer needed such a thing. Gripping it with both hands, he snapped it in two.

An enormous amount of Mana erupted from the broken ends and poured back into his body. It was a feeling he hadn't experienced in a hundred years. Envirus looked up at the sky with complicated eyes.

The fate he had sought to escape was one of a catastrophic death. Someday, without fail, he was fated to meet it. It is natural for any living being to die, and most people, upon learning of their appointed death, would struggle to avoid it. Envirus had been the same. Entrusting his 'self' to Erebrisa had meant entrusting the fate that belonged to it as well, which meant losing all the magic power and abilities he had accumulated over a long time.

For the next hundred years, Envirus had relearned magic from the very beginning. He could not, however, reach the level he had once known as his 'former self'.

*Not yet, I suppose,* Envirus thought with a bitter smile. At least for now, it didn't seem like he would face that catastrophic death determined by fate.

{sep}

"...This can't be!"

The blood drained from the face of Arbes, who had been calmly preparing for battle. He whipped his head around. He couldn't see anything yet, but he felt it clearly. The being that had been approaching until just a moment ago hadn't been that remarkable. Sufficient to be called an archmage, perhaps, but not to the extent that Arbes needed to be wary.

That was no longer the case. He didn't know what had happened, but the approaching mage was now on a higher level than he was.

It was unbelievable. Among all living mages, there was none more skilled than Arbes. Even those who boasted of being a Magic Tower Master of the Mages Guild were nothing more than children in his eyes.

*No way.*

Recalling a certain name, Arbes's face contorted. It could only be him, but a hundred years had already passed since that bastard had last shown himself.

*And now he shows up? Why?*

Arbes couldn't understand it, but he hurriedly completed the magic he had been preparing. If his opponent was really him, then Arbes had to prepare for the worst.

His prediction was correct.

Arbes's expression hardened as he saw Envirus approaching the plaza. His lips twitched upward into a grin, baring his teeth. The face was different from the one he remembered, but after a hundred years, that much was understandable, yet even with the changed face, Arbes knew who Envirus was. He recognized the unique tattoos engraved on the man's arms.

"Cain...!"

"It's been a while since I've been called that," Envirus muttered. Cain was the name he had used a hundred years ago. He gave a bitter smile as he looked at Arbes. Just as he had thought, the one here was Arbes, one of the Black Star of the Predators.

The tattoos on Envirus's arms began to squirm.

"What in the world are you trying to do here?" Envirus asked.

"What in the world have you been doing for a hundred years?" Arbes shot back.

They asked each other different questions, and just as Envirus didn't answer, Arbes didn't answer either. They were meaningless questions in the first place.

Arbes immediately formed a hand seal, and the magic he had prepared manifested instantly. Normally, an incantation was required, but thanks to his preparations, it was omitted.

KWAAAAA!

Pitch-black flames roared to life, engulfing Envirus. They were the flames of hell, incinerating everything they touched.

Just as Arbes hadn't chanted, Envirus didn't chant either. Now that he had reclaimed his time as Cain, he was the great mage who had been hailed as the strongest a century ago.

Envirus simply thrust his open left hand forward. The black, geometric tattoos on his forearm squirmed. They weren't simple tattoos, but countless, tiny engraved letters arranged so closely together that they resembled a pattern. A white curtain materialized from his outstretched palm, blunting the rush of flames. As it held, Envirus rotated the index finger of his right hand, which was extended downward.

A chilling cold, enough to give one goosebumps, coalesced into a mist that drifted around him.

PABABAK!

From within the swirling mist, hundreds of ice arrows shot toward Arbes.

As if waiting, Arbes formed another hand seal. The flames, which had been blocked by the curtain, spread sideways and incinerated every last ice arrow. Without stopping there, he changed the form of his seal.

GRRRRUMBLE!

The earth trembled in a violent earthquake. The ground around Envirus split open, and pitch-black vines covered in venomous thorns shot up, trying to wrap around his body.

Silent Casting. This was a special skill that only Cain, among countless mages, could perform. It couldn't compare to that of a Dragon, who could manifest magic with words of power alone, but among all mages who had ever existed, Cain was the only one who could cast magic silently. This was possible because he calculated magic as letters and had all the letters he used tattooed on both his arms. When he had entrusted Cain to Erebrisa, he had lost this ability. Instead, he had used a wand containing the magic power from his time as Cain and lived as Envirus, relearning magic through his former memories.

The abilities of Cain and Envirus, however, were worlds apart.

Cain moved his hands without a hint of panic.

SHSHSHSHK!

The vines that tried to bind his body disintegrated into dust and scattered on the wind.
