"I apologize for the delay."

The words were hardly necessary. Nebul had reappeared in less than five minutes, bowing his head deeply to Lee Sungmin as he always did.

"The blacksmith and the folding fan are ready," he announced. "She wishes to speak with you. Is now a good time?"

"I don't mind."

"Understood."

At Lee Sungmin's reply, Nebul raised a hand. He drew a long finger down through the empty air, and with a loud CRACK, the scenery split open.

"I will take my leave."

With that, Nebul vanished into Lee Sungmin's shadow, leaving him to stare blankly at the wide-open rift, completely baffled by what had just happened.

"So young."

A voice drifted from the tear in space. A Dark Elf with dull blonde hair emerged from beyond the spatial rift, and at first, Lee Sungmin did not even realize what she was. It was his first time seeing one in person.

"No need to be so surprised."

The Dark Elf chuckled at his expression. She ran a hand through her messy hair.

"The name's Celgerus. I don't know how many more times we'll be seeing each other, but we should at least know each other's names. Right?"

"...Uh... I'm Lee Sungmin," he stammered.

Elves and Dark Elves were known as mutants, a term humans had created for any intelligent non-human. Broadly speaking, even Orcs could be considered mutants since they were capable of communication, but most people simply treated them as monsters.

"What weapon do you use?" Celgerus asked. She wore baggy work clothes with a large apron tied over them, yet the attire could not completely hide the well-developed curves of her body.

"I use... a spear."

He had been told he would meet a blacksmith, but he was quite flustered. He had never imagined the smith would be a Dark Elf, let alone a woman. In his mind, a blacksmith was a burly man with bulging muscles or, if a mutant, a short Dwarf. Of course, he had never actually seen a Dwarf before. Mutants were a rare sight, their numbers few.

"A spear. A spear, you say... Any particular shape you prefer?"

"...A shape like this would be good."

Lee Sungmin retrieved a spear from his Subspace Pocket. Celgerus nodded as she examined it.

"It's the Central Plains style. You must be a martial artist."

"I'm not from murim, but..."

"Can you show me what kind of Spear Technique you use?" she asked.

Lee Sungmin hesitated. For a martial artist, demonstrating one's techniques was tantamount to a taboo.

"Why? You don't want to?"

"...Is it necessary?"

"It is," Celgerus answered without a moment's pause. "How am I supposed to make a spear tailored to your martial arts if I don't know what they are?"

"You're saying you can match it just by watching?"

"You're misunderstanding something," she said with a low laugh that seemed to mock his ignorance. "I'm not a martial artist. I'm a blacksmith. My role isn't to understand your martial arts, but to understand *you* as you perform them. How you swing the spear, how you grip it, how you use it. I watch that and adjust accordingly. If you really don't want to show me, you don't have to. I'll just make a spear worth the money I received."

Only then did Lee Sungmin recall what Nebul had said about lowering his standards and the stubbornness of artisans. He swallowed a sigh.

"I understand."

"You're surprisingly quick to agree. I thought you'd be more stubborn. Don't you suspect me?"

"I don't know you," Lee Sungmin admitted. "However, I have some faith in Erebrisa for connecting me to you."

He had not used Erebrisa properly yet, so his trust was not absolute. Still, even Mushii, the God of Hardship and Ordeals, used the service. He did not know the specifics, but that alone meant Erebrisa held significant value.

"I'm four hundred years old," Celgerus said suddenly.

The unrealistic age made Lee Sungmin's eyes twitch, though he was not greatly surprised. He already knew Elves and Dark Elves lived for a long time.

"I started hammering because I liked it. That was about three hundred and fifty years ago. Of course, my starting line is different from the naturally gifted blacksmiths, the Dwarves... but my life, lived as a blacksmith for three hundred and fifty years, is real. Do you know why I'm telling you this?"

"I don't."

"The one you should trust isn't Erebrisa. It's me." Celgerus smiled bitterly. "Well, I know it's ridiculous to ask someone I've just met to trust me, but."

Lee Sungmin pressed his lips together. It was not her plea that moved his heart, but her words about having a different starting line.

The Dwarf race were natural-born blacksmiths. Everything they touched became a masterpiece, for by their very existence, they received the favor of Ader, the God of Flame and Passion, and Gaen, the God of Earth and Creation. An item crafted by a young Dwarf was superior to one made by a human artisan with decades of experience.

Compared to them, the three hundred and fifty years the Dark Elf Celgerus had spent hammering might not seem to hold much value, but those years she had dedicated herself to were real. A different starting line. Different talents. These were the very things that had made Lee Sungmin feel self-loathing time and time again.

"I'll trust you."

Having made his decision, Lee Sungmin raised his spear. Celgerus looked a little surprised as he began to perform his Spear Technique. She had met various clients through Erebrisa, but not many martial artists had ever agreed to her request. To them, their martial arts were techniques for killing enemies and protecting themselves, not something to be demonstrated to a complete stranger. Though she had asked, she had not truly expected him to comply.

He started with Rannachal. From that carefully executed form, the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear began in earnest. Lee Sungmin did not just perform the spear art alone. He mixed in the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, because in a real battle, one had to incorporate movement. An opponent would never just stand still.

Celgerus watched silently as he moved, her eyes never leaving him. After a long while, his Spear Technique came to an end. She had not blinked once.

"You trust me?" she finally asked.

"......"

"Why? Because I asked you to?"

"Because you said the three hundred and fifty years you've worked hard are real."

At his answer, Celgerus let out a soft laugh. "Aren't you a bit too gullible?"

"This is the first time I've heard that. I have been told that I'm needlessly suspicious, though."

"I can't betray someone who trusts me," she chuckled, pulling her hands from her apron pockets. "Twenty-seven million Eru. I'm usually strict with my calculations, but since I'm in a good mood, I'll make you something worth more than that. It'll take quite a while, though."

"I don't mind."

"You seem like you'll be a good regular," Celgerus shrugged. "As long as you don't die, that is."

The spatial rift closed. Not long after, Nebul emerged from Lee Sungmin's shadow and bowed deeply.

"Were you satisfied with the blacksmith I introduced you to?"

"She said I seem like I'll be a good regular."

Nebul's expression turned peculiar. He watched Lee Sungmin for a moment before speaking. "There's such a thing as a first impression. An image. To be honest, Celgerus is not a particularly preferred style of blacksmith."

"Because she's a Dark Elf?"

"That's right. As I mentioned, our Erebrisa provides only the best of what our members desire. When it comes to the best blacksmith... almost all our members think of a Dwarf. They don't even consider a Dark Elf, let alone a female one." Nebul smiled bitterly as he explained. "That is, in fact, why I connected you with her. Because she isn't preferred, Celgerus is undervalued for her skill. Thanks to that, her commission fee is also a bit cheaper compared to other blacksmiths of similar ability."

"As long as her skill is real, I don't care," Lee Sungmin replied indifferently.

Nebul chuckled. "Did I not tell you? We always provide only the best. Celgerus's skill is real. She is the only Dark Elf blacksmith to have earned the title of Meister. Originally, she doesn't particularly like the clients we connect her with... but if she said you seem like you'll be a regular, she will undoubtedly craft the best weapon for you. Ah, of course," he added, "only within the limits of twenty-seven million Eru."

"Are you saying she won't do anything more than that?"

"That depends on her mood. We are only in charge of the brokerage role, after all."

Lee Sungmin nodded at Nebul's answer. After a brief silence, he asked about something else. "I have a question about mercenary requests. You said Erebrisa takes a five percent brokerage fee, but what happens to the Mercenary Guild's fee if I accept a request through you?"

"It's waived," Nebul answered immediately. "The fee Erebrisa takes includes the Mercenary Guild's fee. The payment for the request is also received through us, not the Guild."

"What about the performance record?"

"That is automatically updated with the Mercenary Guild. Would you like to check the requests?"

"No. It's fine for now. My rank plate hasn't been issued yet."

"It will probably be issued soon. I will organize some good S-rank requests for you, Lee Sungmin."

"Is that a service?"

"Since you are my dedicated client, this much is a matter of course, and..." Nebul gazed at Lee Sungmin's face for a moment. "Strangely, the more I interact with you, the more I feel... a desire to help you. It's just a simple feeling, but... it's quite peculiar."

"This is the first time I've heard something like that."

"It's just a feeling. Isn't that what favorability is like? It's sudden and without reason. You don't necessarily need a reason to feel favorable toward someone," Nebul said with a chuckle. "Well then, I'll see you next time."

Nebul disappeared. Lee Sungmin felt strange about the words he had left behind. *Favorable?* He did not think Nebul had a preference for men, but hearing such a thing for the first time was undeniably odd.

*Come to think of it.*

He felt he had been strangely lucky so far. It started with Jack and Hans, then continued with Wiji Hoyeon, Baek Sogo, and others. All those who had become deeply involved with him had shown him goodwill.

*There are no coincidences.*

The words Mushii had told him not to forget echoed in his head.
