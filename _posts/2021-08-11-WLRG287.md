"Why should we be the ones to step aside?"

Ju Won lifted his head at the grumbling voice. The Lycanthrope who had dared to voice his dissatisfaction met Ju Won's gaze and swallowed hard, the sound loud in the tense silence. He was a trusted subordinate, one who had been by Ju Won's side since the days of the Mad Wolf, and he knew precisely what that look meant.

Though centuries of weariness now clouded his gaze, the subordinate recognized the same light that had burned there when Ju Won was known as the Mad Wolf. Back when Howon, the previous leader, was still alive, Ju Won had been a monster of belligerent ferocity, picking fights with anyone and everyone, a force that only Howon could ever hope to control.

The subordinate watched him with a tense expression. Despite that familiar, dangerous glint in his eyes, Ju Won was not acting like his old self. He had even abandoned his own territory, the Snowfield, stepping aside at Jeniela's request. He couldn't understand why Ju Won would go to such lengths for her, but he dared not voice his doubts, afraid the question might wound the old wolf's pride.

"There must be a reason," Ju Won said, slowly rising to his feet. Though the Snowfield was a great distance away, his gaze seemed to cross the expanse, reflecting the home he had known for over a hundred years.

He saw a carriage crossing the snow.

A carriage.

From within it, he felt a powerful presence that made his heart race. If not for Jeniela's words, he would have remained there to greet the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. His competitive spirit had waned since Howon's death and the long passage of time, but the Mad Wolf had not vanished entirely, merely slumbered within the man now called Ju Won. He suppressed the boiling fighting spirit that surged within him.

It had been sudden. Just this morning, Jeniela had come to him in person. She had told him to leave the Snowfield for the time being, and to not interfere, no matter what happened there.

"You're holding back well, aren't you?"

The chuckling laugh startled the subordinate and the other Lycanthropes, but Ju Won was not surprised. He slowly turned his head. Jeniela stood not far away, lifting the hem of her skirt slightly as she met his gaze with a gentle smile.

"Isn't it hard?" she asked.

"A little."

"But you have to endure it. You jumping in wasn't a scene in the future I saw."

"Are you saying my participation could act as a variable?"

"A being with your power could naturally become a variable," she said. "I don't want that. You know that, don't you?"

Ju Won didn't answer. Her words were a final confirmation, and he forced down the impulse and desire churning in his heart. Jeniela's eyes sparkled as she approached and stood beside him.

"Sometimes, watching is more fun than fighting yourself, you know."

"I don't think that's the case for me."

"Still, you can't. You have to endure it. It won't be long now, anyway. Are you going to make over a hundred years of patience meaningless?"

"The future you want isn't necessarily the future I want."

"Let's not say things that'll make us sad," Jeniela said with a laugh. She looked toward the Snowfield, in the same direction Ju Won was facing. She saw the carriage crossing it, and she saw a single human running from the opposite direction.

"Still," she said, her voice filled with excitement, "it's a clash between those who have reached the pinnacle as humans, isn't it? It'll be fun to watch."

This was one of the scenes from the future she had seen before meeting the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. She had not told him about this scene, not because she thought he would avoid it, but because she wanted to stage it as a chance encounter.

"This is the first time I'll be seeing the power of the Martial God, too," Jeniela said.

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman took off his mask inside the swaying carriage, startling Lee Sungmin from his thoughts.

"You're taking that off a lot today. Is something wrong?"

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman didn't answer. He simply looked down at the mask before chuckling and tucking it into his robes.

"You haven't forgotten what I asked of you, have you?"

"I haven't forgotten," Scarlet replied. She was no fool and had an inkling of what he meant by suddenly removing his mask and asking such a thing. Her face froze as she looked at him.

"...Don't tell me."

"No. Not today," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman said, his voice full of conviction. He slowly rose to his feet, and Lee Sungmin shot up to follow his lead.

"What's going on?"

"Not today," the Chairman muttered again, an absolute certainty in his tone. Yes, not today. "Still, it's only polite to show a minimum level of vigilance."

"What in the world...?"

"You don't know?" the Chairman chuckled at Lee Sungmin's uncomprehending expression.

Lee Sungmin recognized that look. He had seen it before in Kron, at the Murim Alliance headquarters, right before the Chairman had faced the Beggars' Gang's Dog Beating Stick Formation. It was a look of pure joy and excitement. Though he had been greatly disappointed then, finding the formation far easier than expected, it was too early now to feel such a thing.

"You'll find out," the Chairman said with a smile.

He was right.

RUUUUMBLE!

A Great Being's mental pressure washed over the carriage, and Lee Sungmin gasped. It was a tyrannical force that made it impossible even to lift one's head, a stark contrast to Jeniela's overwhelming terror that stopped the breath and drove one mad.

"Th-this is..."

Amidst the intense pressure, Lee Sungmin quickly grabbed Scarlet's wrist to steady her. This time, however, she had been prepared and didn't react as violently as she had with Jeniela.

The carriage ground to a halt.

"Yehwa is having a hard time," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman muttered, opening the door. The Black Dragon Knight spat out in a horrified voice.

"This is... the Martial God... the presence of the Martial God. Don't tell me...?!"

"That's right," the Chairman nodded truthfully. "I didn't expect to meet an old friend in a place like this."

Yehwa, sitting in the driver's seat, was deathly pale. Lee Sungmin quickly followed the Chairman out of the carriage. The Chairman patted the trembling Yehwa's shoulder.

"This one brought you here for nothing."

"N-no, sir..."

"Go inside and wait."

"Master." Lee Sungmin grabbed the Chairman's shoulder, an act of disrespect he knew he was committing. He had often talked back, but never before had he dared to physically restrain the man, yet the Chairman showed no displeasure, his eyes brimming with the pure anticipation of a child.

"I said, not today."

"But...!"

"I believe I told you. Not today."

"...Yes...?"

"The one who chooses where this one will lie is not the heavens, but this one himself. If this one is to die, it will not be the heavens, fate, or the future that kills this one. It will be this one himself who desires and chooses his own death, and, I'll say it again. Not today."

With that, the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman pushed Lee Sungmin's hand from his shoulder.

"The Martial God is not enough."

There was no need to ask what he meant.

{sep}

The Martial God, who had been charging forward, came to a halt. He was surprised, but not enough to hesitate. He strode toward the carriage, thinking he never expected to run into the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman here in the North. Then again, perhaps this was for the best. He had planned to seek the man out after meeting the Vampire Queen anyway. This simply saved him the trouble.

"The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman."

"Don't be so cold, calling me by my title."

"Was there ever any warmth between us?"

"Isn't a hateful bond a bond all the same?"

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman left Lee Sungmin behind him and slowly approached the Martial God, whose gaze shifted to the Chairman's smiling face before he ground his teeth. At the same time, the Martial God glared past the Chairman's shoulder at Lee Sungmin.

"...The Phantom Spear."

"Is this the first time you've seen him in person? I suppose it would be. If this one's incompetent disciple had met you, he would have died long ago."

"...It was an ill-fated connection in many ways."

The Martial God had let the Phantom Spear live until now only because the Spirit Medium had told him not to. He did not doubt her and believed there must have been a good reason for it.

"I should have killed him long ago."

However, now, he sincerely regretted it. He had felt sorrow at the deaths of the Fist Honored One and the Sword Saint, but he hadn't been angry, believing it was also the Spirit Medium's will. The death of the Dark Supreme, however, filled him with fury. The Martial God was not a cold-blooded man. He knew the Dark Supreme had followed him with true sincerity, and he deeply understood the man's grudge after losing his daughter to a monster. The Dark Supreme's martial arts were inferior to the Sword Saint's, but the Martial God had cherished him more, even teaching him the essence of movement arts himself.

"...Do you have the White Fang?" the Martial God demanded, glaring at Lee Sungmin. When Lee Sungmin didn't understand, the Martial God roared.

"I'm talking about the White Fang that the Dark Supreme had! Do I have to call it the mistletoe for you to understand?!"

Only then did Lee Sungmin recall the pure white awl the Dark Supreme had tried to stab him with, a long, handleless spike that felt less like metal and more like a sharply carved tree branch.

*The mistletoe?*

Even he knew of such a famous weapon. From Lee Sungmin's expression, the Martial God realized he now possessed the White Fang that had been entrusted to the Dark Supreme.

GRRRRUMBLE!

A colossal power began to surge around the Martial God as he began to stride forward.

"The reason I entrusted the White Fang to the Dark Supreme, who was weaker than the Sword Saint, the Spear King, and the Moon Queen, was as proof of my affection, showing how much I cherished him. He followed me to avenge his daughter, and he was more sincere and devoted than anyone in Heaven Beyond Heaven, and yet... he's dead. He died without ever seeing the world he'd longed for his entire life."

"Enough." The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman blocked his path and shook his head. "Let's not discuss something as trivial as revenge. You know as well as I do that neither of us has the right to talk about such things."

"I had no intention of fighting you," the Martial God spat, glaring at the Chairman. "Even while you were in seclusion, I didn't put much pressure on the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance, but you have trampled on my respect. Why did you attack the Murim Alliance? Why did you kidnap the Black Dragon Knight?"

"I didn't kill him. The Black Dragon Knight is perfectly fine inside the carriage, but it seems he's too afraid of you to come out."

"Did he betray me?"

"He must have, to still be alive."

At the Chairman's reply, the Martial God's shoulders trembled. "You... you're trying to ruin my entire life's work."

"It doesn't seem like you put much effort into your life's work."

"Do you know... what Heaven Beyond Heaven desires?"

"To stop the end."

"And you're trying to ruin it, knowing that?!"

"Even if we both want the same thing, our methods can be different, can't they?" the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman replied to the shout with a smile. "That absurd story about exterminating the inhuman. Yes, I've heard you have your own way... but this one doesn't actually believe it. That's why, even though this one wishes to stop the end, I do not agree with your methods."

"Shut up."

"This works out perfectly. If you consider this one an obstacle, then why not just kill this one right here? I don't know what it is, but it seems my disciple has something you cherish. If you want it back, all you have to do is kill this one, kill my disciple, and take it."

"Yes, that would be the easiest way."

CRACKLE.

Black electricity sparked around the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's shoulders. The Martial God glared at it and spat out in a powerful voice.

"So that's what I intend to do."

"That's all well and good," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman grinned, "but you've got one thing wrong."

The Martial God's figure vanished. Unfazed, the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman raised both his hands.

"It won't be the easiest way. It will be the hardest."
