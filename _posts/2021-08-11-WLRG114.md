The door burst open, unleashing a fierce, raging wind. Outside, the snowy gales of the North howled, sharp and harsh enough to slice through unprotected skin. A man wrapped in a thick robe, a necessary precaution against the biting cold, stepped inside.

"Welcome," a voice rasped.

The inn had no other guests. In a chair before the fireplace, the innkeeper, who had been dozing, jerked his head up. He shivered, hugging his shoulders with one arm while rubbing the sleep from his eyes with the other.

"First, how about closing that door?" he asked, his voice strained over the rattling of the open door as it fought the wind.

Lee Sungmin raised a hand and pushed the door shut against the gale. The innkeeper's eyebrows shot up at the casual display of strength.

"You don't look like a Hunter," he muttered.

Lee Sungmin swept back the hood of his robe, revealing a pale face. The innkeeper studied him for a moment.

"Would you like some coffee?" he asked. "Or maybe hot water? I've got milk and some soup, too."

"I'll have the soup," Lee Sungmin said.

"And I'd like milk," a second voice added.

Rubia peeked her head out from behind Lee Sungmin to place her order. The innkeeper's eyes widened at the sight of the cat ears perched atop her head.

"A beastfolk," he said, his tone shifting. "They're rare around these parts. Are you a slave trader?"

"I'm not," Lee Sungmin replied, his expression blank.

The innkeeper's words seemed to sting Rubia, but she held her tongue. It was the unfortunate reality for her kind. Elves were a different matter entirely. Exceptionally rare and blessed by Elementals and Mana, they lived for ages, each one a skilled warrior, mage, or archer. The wealthy dreamed of owning an Elf slave, but enslaving one was a monumental task. Beastfolk, however, were weaker and more numerous. They were relatively easy to capture, and obedience could be beaten into them with sadistic violence.

"First, have a seat here," the innkeeper offered, bringing two chairs and placing them before the fireplace. "Hot soup and milk. I'll bring them right out, so just wait a moment."

He turned and disappeared into the kitchen. Rubia scurried over to the fire and sat down, taking off the robe she wore and letting out a relieved breath.

"Technically, I'm not a beastfolk, you know," she chattered, tossing the removed robe carelessly behind her.

Lee Sungmin came to her side and sat in the other chair. Rubia was a familiar created by Envirus. Her beastfolk appearance was merely a reflection of her creator's preference, not her actual race.

"I didn't think we needed to come to an inn," she said.

"The wind suddenly got stronger."

"Your body doesn't feel the cold, and neither does mine."

"Still, I don't want to sleep outdoors while the wind is howling," Lee Sungmin countered. He had achieved Extreme Temperature Immunity and felt no cold, which was why he had seen no need to stop. They were only here because Rubia had insisted so strongly.

"We'll arrive in Trabia soon," she grumbled. "Even before the Blood Heavenly Demon reigned, Trabia was a land of the unorthodox, Black Mages, and the inhuman. Honestly, it's a place I really don't want to go."

"It can't be helped," Lee Sungmin said, taking off his own robe. "I know the situation in the North isn't good. The cornerstone that was the Blood Heavenly Demon has lost its weight, and the powerful demonic practitioners are choosing to enjoy themselves rather than rule. Thanks to that, the public order in Trabia has become a mess."

He had been continuously updated on the situation through Nebul on his way here. Things had been better when the Blood Heavenly Demon ruled Trabia. At least he was a being who knew his limits, but that was before Wiji Hoyeon defeated him. Losing an arm was fatal for a martial artist. The Blood Heavenly Demon had been a giant capable of ruling Trabia, but he lost much when his arm was severed and the Blood Heavenly Alliance collapsed. Now, one thing was certain. There was no law in Trabia. The city's lord had reportedly given up on governing, left the city, and holed himself up in his villa. With no public order, Trabia was teeming with all sorts of crime.

"Since the situation is what it is, the Murim Alliance is also keeping an eye on that area."

"...It was a Spirit, right?" Rubia pouted. "The North, a place where the snow never melts. If fate allows, you will meet a nobleman. When winter is at its coldest." She grumbled, "That's so incredibly vague. To come all this way to this distant, dangerous place, following words whose authenticity isn't even certain."

[It's not vague,] the Magic Armor hummed as the Void Monarch snapped at her. [It's true we don't know what kind of beings Spirits are or what their intentions are. However, one thing is certain. If they say something will happen, it usually does.]

"Even that sounds vague."

[That's just how those bastards are. They glimpse into fate. If you hadn't come to the North at all, it might be different, but now that you're here, the 'meeting' the Spirit spoke of will definitely happen.]

To be honest, Lee Sungmin had also been skeptical on the way here. There was more than one place in the North where the snow never melted, and he had no idea when 'winter is at its coldest' would be. Long before arriving, Rubia had pestered him about why they were going to Trabia. After hearing his answer, the Void Monarch had forcefully insisted, 'Just go,' so he had come all this way without a solid plan.

"Where are you headed?" the innkeeper asked, returning with two large mugs.

Rubia took her mug with both hands, blowing on it gently before sipping the milk.

"We're going to Trabia."

"Oh, dear," the innkeeper burst out laughing at Lee Sungmin's answer. "You're heading to a dangerous place. Why? You want to make a name for yourselves there too?"

"That's not it."

"Well, it's not my place to meddle. Be careful. Especially you, the beastfolk young lady," the innkeeper said, gesturing toward Rubia with his chin. "Trabia is a place where the law no longer exists. I hear there are many lechers, as well as sorcerers and Black Mages who perform human sacrifices. If you enter the city, you might get tangled up in danger."

"Thank you for your concern," Rubia said with a bright smile.

Lee Sungmin swished the soup around in his mouth for a moment before speaking.

"You're being uncharacteristically kind," he said, his voice low.

The innkeeper froze.

"For someone who served poisoned soup, I mean. What is this, pathetic hypocrisy? Or just deceit?" Lee Sungmin grumbled, tossing the mug he held into the fireplace. Flames licked at the ceramic as the innkeeper's expression soured.

"...Well, I'll be," the innkeeper muttered.

He had not achieved Myriad Poison Immunity, but a poison this crude could not harm Lee Sungmin's body. Still, the foul taste lingered in his mouth, and he pursed his lips and spat.

"P-Poison?" Rubia cried, jumping in surprise. She tried to stand, but her legs gave out from under her, and she collapsed to the floor.

"It's probably not a fatal poison," Lee Sungmin noted. "At least, not the one meant for you."

A beastfolk was a valuable commodity. The innkeeper glared at Lee Sungmin with a sour expression before letting out a deep sigh. He pulled over a chair and sat down.

"I have my reasons."

"Reasons? What reasons...!" Rubia spat, her face contorting in pain.

As Lee Sungmin had said, the poison she had ingested was not fatal, merely a paralytic. That did not mean it was not painful. Lee Sungmin glanced at the gasping Rubia and extended a hand toward the innkeeper.

"Don't you have the antidote?"

"If I give it to you, will you let me live?"

"Don't try to bargain. It wouldn't be difficult to kill you and search your body for it, and I have other ways to get one right now." He could simply summon Erebrisa's Nebul and purchase an antidote. Since the poison was not fatal, there was no need to hurry.

"...I see." The innkeeper gave up quickly. His plan had failed, and now they held all the power. "I thought I'd gotten pretty good at sizing people up doing this job. Guess that's not always the case."

He grumbled as he reached into his clothes. After rummaging around, he pulled out a small glass vial and held it out.

"Don't worry. I'm not giving you more poison just to screw you over now that things have gone south."

Lee Sungmin did not take the medicine. Instead, he sat back down in his chair. Rubia, still collapsed on the floor, stared at him.

"Wh-what are you doing?" she stammered. "Why the medicine..."

"I can't just give you some unknown substance," Lee Sungmin stated calmly.

"But it's the real antidote..."

"I can't just blindly trust him. Besides," he added, glancing down at her, "you're the one who dragged me to this inn. It's not a fatal poison, so just sit tight for a bit."

"You son of a bitch...!" Rubia cursed.

Lee Sungmin ignored her and looked up at the innkeeper. "Why did you do this?"

"...Because beastfolk are worth money."

"Is that all?"

"...This area is the territory of the Ghost Wolf Sect. I have to pay them a monthly fee, but as you know, with Trabia in the state it's in, the customers have disappeared."

"So you planned to sell a beastfolk to get the money?"

"Please understand. My circumstances being what they are..."

"You tried to harm an innocent person to save your own skin, and you want me to understand that? You're quite the selfish one," Lee Sungmin said with a small, cold laugh.

The innkeeper's face stiffened as a creeping killing intent rose from Lee Sungmin.

"How many have you handed over like this?"

"This is the first time..."

"That's a lie."

The innkeeper fell silent. Just as the man opened his mouth to make an excuse, death arrived in an instant.

THWACK!

With a wet, sickening sound, his head exploded. Lee Sungmin did not spare the fallen corpse a glance as he helped Rubia to her feet.

"Acting like you care now...!"

"Didn't I say it might have been poison?"

"Forget it! I don't need your help," Rubia spat, pushing him away.

Seeing her stumble, unable to support her own weight, Lee Sungmin let out a dry laugh. He pulled over a chair and sat her down in it.

[There's a presence upstairs,] the Void Monarch hummed. [Doesn't seem like a guest. Probably the family of the man you just killed.]

"A man his age would have been married and had children."

[You don't seem to feel any guilt.]

"It wasn't something to feel guilty about," Lee Sungmin replied flatly. "If I'd been affected by that poison, I would have died. Rubia would have been sold off as a sex slave or sacrificed in some magic experiment."

"Don't say such disgusting things," Rubia snapped.

Lee Sungmin shrugged and looked up at the ceiling. He could feel the presence too, but he did not go to check.

[He was a man with circumstances he couldn't help. He probably had no choice if he wanted to protect his family.]

"And I should have shown him mercy for that? Do you think he would have had a change of heart just because I forgave him? He might have just kept poisoning others to save himself."

[Maybe. Or maybe he would have changed.]

"Just because it's a fifty-fifty chance doesn't give me a reason to be merciful."

[So killing him here to prevent future incidents is your idea of chivalry?]

"Chivalry? I wasn't thinking anything so grand. I just didn't feel any reason to show mercy. You... what are you trying to say? Has the great yokai suddenly developed a conscience?"

[Hahaha! Of course not. I was just curious. Curious about what kind of human you are. What about the ones upstairs? Aren't they technically his accomplices?]

"They didn't harm me. I have no reason to go up there and kill them," Lee Sungmin grumbled, looking at Rubia. She was still pouting but no longer complained.

A short while later, the paralysis wore off, and Rubia got to her feet.

"I can't even hide these stupid cat ears."

"Just hide your body for now. It looks like more trouble is brewing."

"...This isn't easy, you know? The magic power consumption is huge."

"It's not like you're out of magic power right now, is it?"

At his words, Rubia let out a deep sigh and changed her form into a sphere of light. Lee Sungmin put the robe she had taken off into his Subspace Pocket and wrapped his own robe tightly around himself.

Footsteps sounded from the floor above. Not wanting an awkward confrontation, Lee Sungmin left the inn.

"We shouldn't have come to this village."

[Oh, I'm so sorry. I guess none of this would have happened if I hadn't insisted we go inside!]

"If you know that, then be more careful from now on," Lee Sungmin replied.

Rubia trembled with indignation. The wind had grown even harsher. He pulled up his hood and walked forward, but screams cut through the gale.

Aaaargh! Kyaaaak!

"Father!"

The cry was followed by the sight of a boy, still with a youthful look about him, running out of the inn's open door. The boy's wide eyes locked onto Lee Sungmin. He clutched a kitchen knife, likely grabbed on his way out, and screamed as he tried to charge. The attempt was futile. The strong wind caught him, making him lose his balance and tumble helplessly to the ground.

[Oh, dear,] the Void Monarch clicked his tongue.

The boy screamed in fury and tried to get up again, but a middle-aged woman who had followed him out wrapped her arms around his waist. It seemed to be the wife of the man Lee Sungmin had killed, and the boy's mother. She desperately shook her head, unable to look directly at him.

"I'll kill you!"

Ignoring the boy's venomous shout, Lee Sungmin turned away. As he crossed the village, he felt eyes on him, wary gazes watching from between closed shutters. Over the sound of the wind, he heard the long howl of a wolf. Lee Sungmin sighed and shook his head.

He had taken only a few more steps when the wolf's howl sounded again, much closer this time.

"Good grief," Lee Sungmin muttered.

The snowy wind made it impossible to see far ahead, but a foul, bestial stench now mixed with the storm's howl. In a flash, Lee Sungmin thrust his hand into his Subspace Pocket. The spear he drew shot forward instantly.

"......"

A loud noise rang out as steel met steel, but Lee Sungmin was not pushed back in the slightest. Instead, his attacker, who had hidden in the snow to launch the assault, was sent flying.

"You're an expert!" the man shouted as he was knocked back, doing a somersault to land on his hands. Covered in snow, his eyes were not human.

Lee Sungmin sniffed the air. "What's with this dog smell..."

[A Lycanthrope,] the Void Monarch muttered.

At the words 'dog smell', the man's eyes flipped with rage.

"It's wolf!"

The man's body began to swell with his shout. Muscles bulged as fur sprouted all over his body. Lee Sungmin watched this transformation begin, his expression blank, before moving the hand that held his spear.

THWACK!

An instantaneous thrust pierced the man's chest.

"......"

"If you were going to attack," Lee Sungmin said coolly, "you should have transformed first."

He infused the embedded spear with enhanced qi. The Orichalcum weapon reacted to the surging inner arts, amplifying the energy. Before the man could say another word, his body exploded with a wet POP.

"What an idiot."

Lee Sungmin slung the spear over his back and started walking forward again.
