"......"

SHHHHHHH.

The sand pouring from the hourglass on the floor was the only way to perceive the flow of time. In truth, it was not a very convenient clock, as it was impossible to know exactly how many days had passed.

He tried not to care. It was a world of nothingness, of quiet silence, something he had already grown accustomed to on Mount Mushii. The same was true for abstinence. This training, in fact, felt better than his time on Mount Mushii or at Shaolin. At least here, there was no pain from prohibitions, nor was there a Jihak to make him keenly aware of his own inferiority.

Training by himself in such a world was, at first, rather peaceful.

However, that peace did not last. Before long, the world devoid of any sound twisted into a hell more terrible than Mount Mushii. No matter how much he moved, his body felt no fatigue. He never felt hungry. He began to miss the approaching Sleep Demon, the gnawing ache of starvation, the simple burn of thirst.

Though he had no need to, he imagined food and ate it. He drank water and even alcohol. He could feel the taste, but there was never any sense of fullness. No matter how much he drank, he could not get drunk.

There was no one to point out his mistakes. His memory of martial arts was clear, but performing them without the aid of skills felt awkward. *Something feels wrong.* He thought this constantly, trying to find what was wrong, but it was impossible to confirm.

That was why he hit a wall. It was a world where only his consciousness existed, yet he could still use his inner qi. The only difference was that as his inner qi were depleted, they would replenish instantly. Any mistake in his form immediately tangled the flow of his inner qi, unleashing a pain more vivid than anything in reality.

How much time had passed?

*Hasn't it been 100 years yet?*

At some point, he began to wish for it.

10.

A great deal of sand still remained in the hourglass. He abandoned the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, escaping into a flurry of sweeps, parries, and thrusts.

*I am trash.*

13.

He belatedly realized this couldn't go on. Humans do not change. It is hard to change. *So, I'll act.* He tried to be a different person, someone who was not him, but the role did not fit. *That's why my sword is hideous.* He recalled what Namgung Heewon had said, then attempted to perform the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear again.

It did not go well.

15.

Remember the duel with Jihak. Remember what you learned from him. In the world of consciousness, memories surfaced with perfect clarity. *I haven't forgotten the training I did at Shaolin.* He considered that a relief.

27.

Thrust, thrust, thrust, thrust, thrust, yet no blisters formed on his hands. He did not get tired. Just how many times had he thrust this spear?

30.

A spear is for thrusting and swinging. The shear shaft, the spear tassel, the spearhead, and Changjun, the Harmonizing Spear, are ultimate techniques. The Shaolin spear is exceptional among them, but I am not a monk.

41.

Why is a person like me so...

50.

The hourglass was half empty.

53.

However, what have I gained?

60.

Just a little more.

74.

Even if I leave like this, nothing about me will have changed. I have to kill myself. I have to kill the weak, trash-like me, but what will I fill the void created by that death with? Is the me that fills it still me?

81.

Elder. I'm sorry for killing you. Someone like me should never have killed you.

90.

I realized how little talent I have. With only a little sand left, I've barely progressed. Was I not diligent enough? I have been swinging this spear for nearly one hundred years without sleeping.

Even without a calamity beside me to instill a sense of inferiority, I feel it. The ten people who took this trial from Denir before me. He said they all passed the first hundred years easily, but what about me?

Did I work hard? Really? I did so without sleeping, eating, drinking, or even relieving myself.

No, I didn't work hard. At some point, I stopped holding the spear and just blankly killed time.

Changing is hard. I came here because I wanted to kill my weak self, because I wanted to become stronger, yet a person like me is still crawling, unable to walk. I never learned to take my first steps. I only learned to step backward. Ah, Flam. Flam. I didn't overcome you back then. Mushii. You were wrong.

95.

Let's stop.

97.

I can't stop.

99.

The sand is almost gone.

100.

"Again," Lee Sungmin muttered, his voice hoarse.

Denir had said the others who challenged this trial passed the first hundred years with ease and gained something from it. Lee Sungmin had not.

For a century, he had prided himself on swinging his spear more times than he ever had before, yet his martial arts had barely progressed. Here, there was no bonus from being Classless, no forced growth from skills. He had to discover a new realm simply by swinging, swinging, and swinging again, advancing through that repetition all on his own.

It was a difficult task for him. Over those one hundred years, how many times had he fallen into despair? How many times had he given up? For how many years had he not even swung his spear at all? What did I do during that time? What did I discard, and what did I gain?

Nothing had changed.

Lee Sungmin dragged his feet toward the hourglass. The sand was no longer falling. He reached for it and swallowed hard. *Can't I just... stop here?*

No. I can't. If I stop now, nothing will be different. I'll just turn and run again, engaging in some disgusting self-consolation deep down, telling myself, *I've changed. I did it. I overcame it.* Then, the moment I hit another wall, I'll just feel that same inferiority and self-loathing all over again.

I hate that.

He did not want to be like that anymore. Lee Sungmin gritted his teeth and grabbed the hourglass.

[Idiot.]

Denir's mocking laughter seemed to echo in his head as the first day of the two-hundredth year began.

Know your powerlessness. Know your weakness. Know your worthlessness. Shake it off. Discard it. Don't just think about wanting to change, change. Don't be satisfied with a small change. Crave it, crave it, and keep going.

When he lost consciousness and opened his eyes again, a significant amount of sand had already fallen. What had happened? It was not hard to understand. *I must have gone mad for a moment. Or maybe for a long time. What did I do during that insane moment I can't even remember?*

No. I'm not curious about that. He just felt it was a waste of time and despised his own weak mind.

There was more than enough time. Even if the training here did not reflect on his physical body in reality, he had clearly felt after his regression that the 'memory' was what was important. The very thing that had allowed him to survive that period, the thing that gave him the confidence to live differently from his past life, was the memory of his days as a Second-Rate mercenary.

Training his Spear Technique was excruciatingly boring. To refresh himself, he began training the Dancing Shadow Soul Break. There was plenty of time, and no need to use it efficiently.

He just had to endure.

As long as he did not go mad, one way or another.

The hourglass was about half empty, meaning another hundred years had passed. This century was far more rewarding than the first. He could confidently say he had taken a few steps forward, but it was not enough. His fundamentals, the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, and the Dancing Shadow Soul Break were all lacking.

Let's try learning the Violet Dawn Divine Art.

To balance the distorted Mind, Qi, and Body, training in an inner arts technique was necessary. When an inner art reached a certain realm, one naturally faced a wall, and breaking through that wall depended on one's own capabilities. Was it possible to face such a wall in this world?

Let's try. There's plenty of time, after all. If it's not enough, I'll just flip the hourglass. Feeling as though he had finally found a proper purpose, Lee Sungmin felt a sense of joy.

It was a joy he had not felt in hundreds of years.

The sand no longer fell. Two hundred years had ended. Lee Sungmin, who had been meditating, opened his eyes.

"Well, this is pretty amazing at this point," he muttered with an indifferent face.

He had clung to the Violet Dawn Divine Art for nearly a hundred years, yet he had not even felt like he was moving forward, let alone seen a wall. Lee Sungmin once again realized just how pathetic and trash-like his talent was. In the real world, he had at least made some small achievements as he devoted himself to martial arts. Thinking back, it seemed the only reason he could achieve even that much was because of the growth bonuses from his skill-learned martial arts, being Classless, and his Low-Grade Martial Skeleton.

In the world of consciousness, however, such bonuses did not apply. The Lee Sungmin here was not receiving any growth bonuses. He was not performing martial arts with skills, but directly. That was why his growth was so slow. He was newly aware of the prodigious talents and efforts that beings like Jihak or Namgung Heewon possessed, martial artists born in this world who had reached such a high realm without using anything like skills.

*It makes sense that Wiji Hoyeon is monstrously strong. She has overwhelming talent on top of the bonuses from her skills.*

Alright. That's enough of that. They are they, and I am me. Lee Sungmin slowly rose to his feet. He had felt lethargic until a few decades ago, but recently, even that had mostly disappeared. Practicing the Violet Dawn Divine Art yielded almost no growth, but it still cleared his head.

"Has it been 300 years so far?" he muttered as he reached for the hourglass.

If he flipped this clock, another three hundred years would follow. Once he finished all that time, he would have completed six hundred years of training. The thought was daunting, but Lee Sungmin flipped the hourglass without hesitation.

It was only after six hundred years had passed that he began to feel that learning martial arts was enjoyable.

He realized he could not just focus on the Violet Dawn Divine Art. Mind, Qi, Body. Training only that one art would not balance them. Although qi deviation probably did not exist in this world of consciousness, he needed to train in various martial arts evenly for the future.

Three hundred years was a convenient amount of time to divide. He drew marks on the hourglass, dividing it into three sections that would each represent roughly one hundred years. One hundred years to learn the Violet Dawn Divine Art, one hundred for the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, and one hundred for the Dancing Shadow Soul Break.

That was his plan.

The three hundred years of training were tedious yet enjoyable. Surprisingly, his mind was clear. The first century of this trial had been so terrible he thought he would go mad, but the training that followed was better than the beginning. Had he gotten used to it? Lee Sungmin tilted his head.

From a certain point on, he no longer engaged in self-torment or felt a sense of inferiority. Perhaps he had just buried it deep in his heart, and it might rear its head again later.

He would make sure it did not.

He flipped the hourglass again.

He set a higher goal. Lee Sungmin tried to reproduce the spear path from his fight with the Sword Phantom, the one that had dealt the fatal wound. He failed. Even after six hundred years of training, he could not reach the spear path that had killed the Sword Phantom back then.

"Good. If I succeeded after just a few tries, it wouldn't be me," Lee Sungmin said with a laugh, nodding his head.

The expected failure was enjoyable. There was no need to be frustrated. There was all the time in the world. So, let's move on. Further, to a more distant place.

When one thousand years had passed, he saw the wall. Lee Sungmin clutched his stomach and laughed.

"To see the wall of the Supreme Peak after training for 1000 years!"

Denir had lied to him. This trial was not simply about training one's consciousness. Achievement here was slow. No matter how much one trained, one could not reap rewards proportional to the effort. That was why even the most renowned calamities went mad and died. They were not used to frustration or failure. Because they knew they were calamities, they naturally assumed they could succeed, but they failed, and they went mad because they continued to fail even after dedicating hundreds of years.

Lee Sungmin, however, was different. He was used to it all. To being foolish. To being an idiot. To having no talent. He was used to inferiority, self-deprecation, and despair.

"I saw the wall after 1000 years. How much longer will it take to overcome it?"

Lee Sungmin challenged it with a feeling of joy.

The next five hundred years began.

"I've reached it."

He succeeded in reproducing the spear path that killed the Sword Phantom. Yes. He had merely reproduced it. He could do nothing more than that. He could draw the same spear path, but it was impossible to perform other Spear Techniques with that same power.

"Yep, that's me. A pitiful fucking idiot," Lee Sungmin hummed as he gripped his spear.

Six hundred years.

"When will I become a Supreme Peak master?"

A dazed thought.

"Maybe I'm already crazy. Or maybe I died."

"......"

"Should I go a little longer?" Lee Sungmin asked, cackling.
