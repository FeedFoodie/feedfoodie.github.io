Lee Sungmin found himself standing before the branch manager of the Zenavis Mercenary Guild.

In his past life, he had never once set foot in this place. He hadn't been at that level, nor had the thought ever crossed his mind. It was only after leaving Zenavis that he had finally gone to a Mercenary Guild and become an official mercenary.

*As I thought.*

He wasn't flustered, though. A simple quest report from Do Sangryang would have been one thing, but this particular quest had stirred up some noise. The subjugation of an orc village was a significant undertaking for the Zenavis Mercenary Guild, and the trouble that had followed meant it wouldn't be dismissed with a simple report. The news must have climbed all the way to the branch manager, who had clearly taken an interest.

And the source of that noise was him.

A fourteen-year-old martial artist. While martial artists themselves weren't uncommon in Eria—Do Sangryang was one, after all—a martial artist with the potential to be a mercenary certainly was.

"You're young."

That was the first thing Lee Sungmin heard upon entering the branch manager's office. The stale smell of cigarettes hit him the moment he opened the door. Beyond the desk sat a middle-aged man with a large, muscular frame, wearing a pair of thick glasses that seemed entirely out of place.

"I'm Dox," the man said. "The branch manager of the Zenavis Mercenary Guild."

"...I'm Lee Sungmin," he replied, cupping his fist in his hand.

Dox stared intently at his face for a moment before speaking again. "I have a lot of questions for you, but let's start with one. Why did you lie?"

"...Pardon?"

"You're not a martial artist, are you?" Dox tapped the glasses perched on his nose with a fingertip. "Do you know what an artifact is?"

"...Roughly."

An artifact was a term for any item imbued with magic. The Subspace Pocket he possessed was a prime example.

"These glasses are one of them," Dox explained. "While wearing them, I can see a person's profession. Yours is... Classless. What an unbelievable story."

Dox muttered to himself. Lee Sungmin felt a flicker of surprise but kept his expression neutral. If he was caught, he was caught. He couldn't afford to lose his composure and panic over every little thing.

"A Classless who learned martial arts... Well, it's not entirely unbelievable," Dox mused. "Isn't that the new trend among the Classless? That skeletal modification surgery at the Recovery Bone Setting Clinic. I suppose it's possible to get a Martial Skeleton there and learn a few things."

"...You know a lot," Lee Sungmin let out a sigh. He had no choice but to go along with it.

"Why did you lie?"

"I didn't think you'd believe me," Lee Sungmin answered.

Dox nodded his head. "That's understandable. To be honest, I'm the same. You. How many months has it been since you came to Zenavis?"

"...Are you interrogating me right now? I don't think I've done anything wrong enough to be interrogated."

"Ah, you're right. I apologize for that," Dox admitted with an indifferent face. "I'm not in a position to interrogate you, and you're not in a position to be interrogated by me."

Although his large frame might suggest he was an outsider, Lee Sungmin didn't think so. Not all mercenaries were assholes. Of course, there was a high probability they were, but being the branch manager of a city's Mercenary Guild meant he was a man of considerable common sense.

*Though there are plenty of bastards among the leaders of mercenary groups belonging to the Guild.*

That much he knew from past experience. He had been a mercenary, after all.

"I just found it strange that you lied. Well... it's not an unbelievable story, I suppose," Dox muttered, reaching a hand under his desk. "Now, let's disregard that. It's not that important." He pulled out a white paper envelope.

"The reward for subjugating the orc village is two million Eru. However, there was some trouble involved. Two survivors. After giving the apprentice mercenaries one hundred thousand Eru each, you and Do Sangryang decided to split the remaining one point eight million. Do Sangryang said so himself. He said that thanks to you, they not only succeeded but also managed to kill those two idiots."

The words were a little unexpected. He hadn't thought Do Sangryang would say something like that. Honestly, Lee Sungmin had expected him to emphasize his own contributions.

"Do Sangryang agreed to the split as well. He decided you would get one million Eru, and he would receive eight hundred thousand. Also, regarding the spoils... after deducting the sales commission, it comes out to six hundred thousand Eru. You'll receive four hundred thousand of that."

"...Is it really okay for you to give me that much?"

"It doesn't matter to me," Dox said with a shrug. "That's what Do Sangryang said. Enough of this talk, I'd like to get to the main point."

"Pardon?" Lee Sungmin replied, feigning ignorance, though he knew very well why Dox had called him here.

"You. Have you ever thought of becoming a mercenary?"

This was it.

"Do Sangryang told me you have skills somewhere between Second-Rate and First-Rate, leaning closer to First-Rate. That's quite a feat, and even more surprising for a Classless." Dox fiddled with the glasses he had set down on the desk. "Especially since you're so young. If you continue to grow like this, you'll likely enter the realm of the First-Rate without any trouble. Depending on your effort and talent, you might even become a master of the peak realm."

"...Is... that so?" Lee Sungmin answered evasively. It probably wouldn't be that easy, but in the eyes of Dox, who didn't know he was a regressor, he must seem like a prodigy. Even if that wasn't actually the case.

"If you become a mercenary, I'll assign you D-Rank to start," Dox offered. "Complete a few quests and you'll naturally rise to C-Rank. If you reach the level of a First-Rate, you'll even make it to B-Rank. What do you think?"

It wasn't a bad offer. As a D-Rank mercenary, he would receive a salary sufficient to live on even without completing any quests.

"...I appreciate the offer, but..."

However, Lee Sungmin refused. He couldn't become a mercenary, not yet. His sights were set on the grand prize of the Classless Tournament in half a year, the Holy Spirit Pill. He needed to consume it and enhance his inner qi to guarantee his advancement. If he became a mercenary, he couldn't participate in the tournament.

"That's a shame." When Lee Sungmin refused, Dox simply nodded. He didn't press the matter further and handed over the envelope filled with money. "If you change your mind, come find me anytime."

"...Thank you."

Lee Sungmin tucked the money inside his clothes. Dox watched him intently, then muttered as if it were a passing thought.

"You don't act your age."

"Pardon?"

"You don't act like a fourteen-year-old. Then again, incidents have a way of changing people. If I were summoned to Eria at a young age, I would've gone crazy too."

It seemed Dox had convinced himself with that reasoning. Lee Sungmin gave a bitter smile and left the Mercenary Guild.

*I need to get older, fast.*

Putting everything else aside, acting like a child was the hardest part.

*"What took you so long?"*

A complaint greeted him the moment he stepped back into Jack's Inn. It was Wiji Hoyeon, sitting at a table with a sullen look on her face. He noticed a plate of cold food placed before her.

"Why didn't you eat?"

"Why would I eat alone when my friend is here?"

"You could have..."

He wanted to refute her strange logic at length—did having a friend mean you couldn't even eat alone?—but he suppressed the urge.

"You smell of blood," Wiji Hoyeon muttered, wrinkling her nose as he sat down opposite her. "You killed someone. What happened?"

"It was nothing. Someone was trying to kill me."

"Oh?" Wiji Hoyeon seemed entirely uninterested. She picked up a piece of cold bread, tore it, and dipped it into the equally cold soup before putting it in her mouth.

"I've been thinking," she began, just as Lula brought Lee Sungmin his dinner. He picked up his fork, and her mouth opened again. "It'll take about a month, give or take, to modify the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear for you. After I pass it on and you've got the basics down... I'm leaving."

The fork in Lee Sungmin's hand stopped moving.

"...Is that so?"

"Why? Are you disappointed?"

"...I don't want to stop you," he muttered with a sigh. In his past life, Wiji Hoyeon had only stayed in Zenavis for a month. The only reason she was staying longer now was purely because of him.

"I've been thinking," Wiji Hoyeon said again. "You have a purpose. Your immediate goal is to get that miracle herb, the Holy Spirit Pill. After that, you'll leave Zenavis. I was thinking, what if I waited for you until then, and we left together."

Lee Sungmin wasn't shaken. He had a good idea of what she would say next.

"But I can't do that. Why do you think that is?"

"Because I won't be of any help," Lee Sungmin answered. To him, it was the only correct answer. He was weak. He had no talent. Wiji Hoyeon, on the other hand, was a calamity learning the divine arts needed to perfect that calamity.

"Wrong," Wiji Hoyeon replied, her eyes growing cold. "I really don't like that side of you."

"...What do you mean?"

"Your inferiority complex," she spat, her voice as cold as ice, her words like blades. Her words were the correct answer for him, tearing through his chest and making his emotions sink heavily.

"Because you're no help? Is that why you think I'm leaving you? Why do you think that?"

"Because it's true that I'm no help."

"Right. You are weak," she conceded. "If you traveled with me, I would have to protect you, but that's not the reason I'm leaving you behind." Her eyes narrowed, a sharp glint flashing in them as if they could pierce right through him. "If I'm by your side, you won't be able to grow."

If he were to travel with her, any trouble he encountered would be solved by her instead.

"The memories of your past life are acting as an inferiority complex and holding you back. That's not good. That disgusting inferiority complex creates self-deprecation and wears you down."

"...What on earth are you trying to say?"

"Understand this clearly. The one you've befriended is the Little Heavenly Demon of the Demonic Cult, Wiji Hoyeon." Power entered her voice. It was low, but it dug deep into his emotions. "The Violet Dawn Divine Art you've learned is one of the best divine arts. The Supreme Nine Heavens Spear you're about to learn was known as the greatest Spear Technique in the Central Plains Murim where I lived, and I am modifying that very technique for you."

"...You were always the one who said my talent was Ilcheon. Just because the martial arts you're teaching are high-level doesn't guarantee I'll be able to properly digest them."

"Have you even tried?" Wiji Hoyeon asked. "Do the memories of your past life tell you that? Do they give you a definite answer, that it'll be useless?"

"...It's better to know my place than to have hope and be betrayed."

"That's the inferiority complex."

"You wouldn't know. You're a calamity," Lee Sungmin said sarcastically. "How can a calamity understand someone who isn't one?"

"That's right. Because I'm a calamity, I can't completely understand you," she acknowledged. "But I just don't like the sight of you whining, steeped in your inferiority complex, without even trying. Do you see me as irresponsible?"

"......"

"You see correctly. That's the kind of person I am."

*This fucking bitch. What the hell is she trying to say?* Lee Sungmin ground his teeth, biting his lower lip as he glared at her.

"Throw away the thought that things will be the same as your past life," she continued. "You've started from the beginning, and aren't you already living in a different direction from the past you lived?"

"The essence of who I am as a person doesn't change."

"No. People change," Wiji Hoyeon immediately denied. "I've changed too. It's not even about a past life. I've changed from who I was as the Demonic Cult's Young Cult Leader. These few months I've experienced here have changed me."

He didn't know the Wiji Hoyeon from her time as the Young Cult Leader, but he knew the girl who was first summoned to Eria.

"Even though you lived for thirteen years and died in your past life, this life might be different. The you from your past and the you now are different. What you experienced then and what you're experiencing now are also different. When the experiences are different, how can the result be the same?"

Lee Sungmin's mouth closed. He couldn't argue. He was left speechless by the words of the thirteen-year-old girl before him.

"Talent," Wiji Hoyeon said, shaking her head. "You can't receive something you don't have. Still, you can't do anything while steeped in an inferiority complex. First, you..." She raised her hand and pointed at him. "You need a purpose. I told you last time, didn't I? I asked what you wanted to do, now that you've started over."

"You did."

"Back then, you said you wanted to live a better life than your past one. Yes, that's quite easy. No matter what you do right now, as long as you're alive, it'll be better than your past life. However, you won't be satisfied with that, will you?"

It was the correct answer. He couldn't be satisfied. That's how people are, even without a concrete purpose. Because he knew what would happen in the future, what opportunities would arise, he was even more dissatisfied.

"You gain power by seizing opportunities, but in reality, you're not thinking about what you'll do with that power. Isn't it actually like this? In a corner of your mind, you're thinking that your life won't turn out that well."

Lee Sungmin's mouth opened to argue, then closed again. He couldn't deny it.

"Of course, you'll try to make things go as you think they will, all while having a vague sense of purpose. That's why you need a proper, definite purpose."

"...What on earth should I make my purpose?" Lee Sungmin asked with a sigh.

In response, Wiji Hoyeon placed a hand on her chest without a moment's hesitation.

"Me," she answered, her voice ringing with power.

"Make me your purpose."

Her words were ripe with the potential for misunderstanding.
