She could not see. Feeling had long since fled her arms. An unpleasant ringing echoed constantly in her ears, and her body felt as heavy as a thousand-pound iron block. She could not remember the last time she had washed or changed her clothes, nor how long it had been.

Conversation and Thought Transmission were distant memories. She was afraid to sleep, for nightmares assailed her daily, each taking a new and terrifying form. Everything that passed her lips had a creatively horrible taste, a torment mirrored by every scent she inhaled.

And on top of it all, Baek Sogo had added several more restrictions.

The first was pain. It was a constant companion, an agony she felt even when standing still. With every step, it felt as if her legs were shattering and tearing apart. With every breath, her lungs threatened to burst.

Then came the restriction on her breathing. She could not draw a breath unless she consciously willed it. If her focus wavered, her respiration would cease, making the prospect of sleep even more terrifying. Other, more minor restrictions piled on top of these.

Life itself had become Buddhist능하게, yet Baek Sogo kept moving.

She felt herself being worn down, breaking, or perhaps going mad. Maybe it was both.

However, she was still alive. Of that, she was certain. The constant pain was agonizing enough to make her wish for death, but it also served as undeniable proof that Baek Sogo was still living.

[You are mad.]

A voice cut through the ringing in her head. It was Mushii.

Her two eyes were blind, but Baek Sogo was seeing something else entirely. A restriction on hallucinations had been added to the one on her sight, and what she saw was a terrible illusion. In truth, she preferred it this way. If she had to endure pitch-black darkness in her current state, she might have truly lost her mind.

[No, you are mad,] Mushii repeated.

Since re-entering the mountain, Mushii had been her only companion. Baek Sogo never asked if it was out of boredom or simple curiosity. She simply moved her body, consciously forcing herself to breathe as her heavy limbs performed the steps of the Dancing Shadow Soul Break.

[I don't understand why you're going this far. That martial artist kid... Lee Sungmin, was it? He was crazy too, but you're something else entirely. Since this mountain has existed, you're the first human to endure so many terrible restrictions.]

*Should I take that as a compliment?* She did not know. Lately, her thoughts had been cutting out intermittently. *Is it because my fatigue has reached its limit?*

[It would be much better to just die. What are you pursuing by doing all this?]

There was a time when Baek Sogo had pursued righteousness and chivalry. She never doubted that she was a good person. That belief had been the most fundamental part of her identity, the very foundation of her being.

Now, however, she was not so sure.

Righteousness and chivalry. She had grown disillusioned with the Orthodox Murim Alliance, which should have been its greatest champion, and she felt the same disillusionment with the Beggars' Gang, who made those ideals their identity. That was why she had left the Murim Alliance.

"......"

Baek Sogo did not answer with righteousness and chivalry. What she desperately wanted was power. She knew well now that to speak of such ideals, one had to be strong enough to silence any who opposed them.

It was not just that. She hated her weak self. She hated the self that had been pushed away by her Junior Disciple, forced to flee.

An old memory surfaced. The first time she met him on this very mountain. She had felt sorry for the young boy, pushed into a Buddhist능하게 situation just to survive, and so she had helped him. She did it because she was a good person. That was how their connection began, how they formed the bond of a Junior Disciple.

They had lived like that for several years. On this terrible mountain, such a bond was precious, and it had been good to see her Junior Disciple working so hard. After they left the mountain... when she met him again, she did not show it, but she had been so happy, so glad.

And when they met again in that Dungeon. How her heart had ached to see how much he had grown. When she received his help, when she had to flee and leave him behind, she had felt so ashamed, so sorry, so guilty.

That was why she wanted to become stronger. So that the next time she met her Junior Disciple, if he were ever in a dangerous situation, she could throw her body on the line to help him, just as he had done for her.

Baek Sogo could not think of any other way. She did not even try to think of one. She was a martial artist, one who ignored any method that was not martial arts.

[Desperation, I see,] Mushii muttered. [And determination.]

Those were things most ascetics who entered this mountain possessed. However, even Mushii had never witnessed such desperate, determined malice and obsession as Baek Sogo's. Lee Sungmin had been like that too, but the woman Mushii was watching now was even more so. Their pursuit of strength was the same, but the fundamental reason was different. In Baek Sogo's case, it was not for herself, but for another.

[I like you,] Mushii muttered.

Something had changed. He had been watching her continuously since she entered the mountain. When she first declared she would re-enter to train, he had not thought much of it. Ascetics like that had come before, and they would quickly give up and leave again, but Baek Sogo had already been training on this mountain for several years.

[Whenever you decide to leave this mountain, I, a god, will personally bestow my blessing upon you,] Mushii said.

Baek Sogo paid his words no mind. She just wanted to see her Junior Disciple.

Aladur walked in the lead. He had confidently stated he could track the tribe's location through the Crazy Heavenly Demon's bracelet, and just as he had said, it was not very far. After three days of travel, Aladur raised his hand.

"It seems to be in that forest," he began. They had passed many forests on their way, and the one before them looked no different from the others. "I've tracked the lingering thoughts in the bracelet, but there's no guarantee that what you're looking for is in there."

They had already heard this from him. The owner of the bracelet might be there, but the entire tribe was not a certainty. Even so, it was the only lead they had.

"There's no guarantee they're alive, either," Aladur grumbled. He had only followed the lingering thoughts and could not tell if the bracelet's owner was living or dead. He meant that the person might already be a corpse buried in the ground.

Hearing this, the Crazy Heavenly Demon's expression turned grim. Even after ten years, he had not forgotten what happened back then.

"Well, even if it's a corpse... it's not like there's no way," Aladur continued. "Unless they became a Bound Spirit, their soul would have already entered the cycle of reincarnation전생, but the corpse would still remain, even if it's rotten. If we pick up even a single bone fragment and use a spell, we could find out more useful things than that damn bracelet can tell us."

"'That damn bracelet?'" the Crazy Heavenly Demon spat, unable to hold back his fury. "Do you want to die? And stop spouting such unlucky nonsense about corpses, or I might just turn you into one!" His eyes blazed as he shot a glare at Aladur.

"Jeez... you're not some petty brat, so why're you getting so worked up?" Aladur puffed out his chest and retorted boldly. "If you want to kill me so badly, then go ahead and try! Huh? If I die, who's gonna show you the way? Huh?!"

The Crazy Heavenly Demon suppressed his anger, his tightly clenched fists trembling. He knew he could not kill Aladur, despite what he said. Rubia clicked her tongue at the two of them arguing, while Lee Sungmin, who had been staring at the forest, finally spoke.

"That's enough. Let's go in."

"See? The Elder had to yell because of you!" Aladur sneered.

"You son of a bitch...!" The Crazy Heavenly Demon's body trembled with rage.

Lee Sungmin ignored their squabble and stepped forward first, with Aladur quickly moving to his side. They entered the forest. Having passed through many such woods on their journey, they were now used to them. His sharp senses picked up other presences, monsters or perhaps Yokai. It was questionable whether they possessed reason, but they did not approach the group easily. Thanks to this, their speed crossing the forest increased.

"We're almost there," Aladur announced.

He was right. A small village came into view not far away. Seeing it, the Crazy Heavenly Demon's expression grew excited. Lee Sungmin felt a surge of anticipation as well. The whole reason he had come this far south was to meet the tribe that knew how to handle Demonic Energy.

"Uh..."

When they arrived, however, the village showed almost no signs of life. The Crazy Heavenly Demon looked around with a dazed expression, a bad feeling creeping over him. The atmosphere of the deserted village was desolate.

"Who are you?" a voice called from nearby.

Startled, Aladur looked toward the sound. A haggard old man was standing there, staring at them with wary eyes. Before Aladur could say anything, Lee Sungmin stepped forward.

"Who are you?" he asked.

"I'm a resident of this village," the old man replied. His face, covered in faded tattoos, wrinkled into a frown.

Looking at the old man's face, the Crazy Heavenly Demon let out a small gasp and walked forward. "Pervas? Aren't you Pervas?"

"...Huh?" At the shout, the old man named Pervas widened his eyes. He stared at the Crazy Heavenly Demon for a moment before a look of recognition dawned. "You're... yes, I remember now. You're Wonpae. Has it been ten years...?"

"So you remember me. Pervas, you've gotten so old..." The Crazy Heavenly Demon nodded, newly aware of the decade that had passed.

"I never thought I'd see you again. What brings you here?"

"I came looking for you all. There's something I want to ask," the Crazy Heavenly Demon said. Lee Sungmin listened quietly to their conversation.

Pervas gave a low, hollow laugh. "I don't know what you want to ask, but I'm not sure if I'll be able to answer."

"The breathing technique you taught me," the Crazy Heavenly Demon began. The moment he heard those words, Pervas's face stiffened. The Crazy Heavenly Demon watched his expression and continued, "...and the miracle herbs you gave me, and... well..." He hesitated slightly before asking his next question. "...Where is Dersia?"

Pervas answered, his face etched with pain. "He's dead."

The words struck the Crazy Heavenly Demon like a physical blow. His face went blank, his legs gave out, and he nearly collapsed before forcing strength back into them to remain standing. Even Aladur, who had spoken so callously but had not thought the person would actually be dead, flinched and shut his mouth.

"...But why...?"

"Most of the tribesmen are dead," Pervas muttered in a small voice, looking around the shabby village. "I'm the only one left here now."

"Just who... no, more importantly, why?"

"It's because of that breathing technique you mentioned." Pervas's face twisted in anguish. "This tribe was wrong from its very existence. To think of a Yokai... a Yokai that died long ago, as a god and make it our faith. I've hated that for a long time. Yokai are nothing but monsters that devour humans."

[Ahem.] Not expecting such a situation, the Void Monarch cleared his throat in embarrassment. Until arriving, he had been feeling rather expectant, curious about the people who worshipped him as a god four hundred years after his death.

"And we paid the price for it," Pervas continued. "It's only natural, for worshipping the wrong monster..."

"What do you mean?" Lee Sungmin asked.

Pervas turned his gaze to him. "Three years ago, this village was attacked by a Yokai. It was a terrible... monster." His shoulders trembled slightly, as if the mere memory was terrifying.

"What kind of monster was it?"

"That... I don't know for sure, but it was the first time I'd seen a Yokai like that. It had a huge body and breathed fire from its mouth... The tribe's warriors tried to resist, but it was useless. The ones who got close were burned to death." Pervas replied, his head hanging low. "I don't even know that Yokai's name. Demonic Energy... most of the warriors and villagers who used that damned power were killed."

"...Why didn't you die?" Lee Sungmin asked.

The question could have sounded rude, but Pervas seemed to lack even the energy to be angry. He sighed as he answered, "Just as that monster was about to finish us off, another Yokai appeared."

"Another Yokai?"

"It was a Nine-Tailed Fox." As he said it, Pervas himself did not seem to know what kind of expression to make. He hated Yokai, yet he was alive now thanks to one. "...She was a beautiful Nine-Tailed Fox. When she appeared, the Yokai that was slaughtering us immediately fled. The Nine-Tailed Fox told us survivors that the other one would never come to this forest again, and... she took those among us who wanted revenge and left."

A Nine-Tailed Fox. Lee Sungmin naturally thought of Yana, the ruler of Urmuri. He had never met her, but she was also said to be a Nine-Tailed Fox.

"In the end, I was the only one who remained," Pervas said. "All the other survivors followed that Nine-Tailed Fox and left."

"...Why did you... stay here?"

"Because I was scared." Pervas trembled slightly. "Since that day, not a single Yokai has remained in this forest. Maybe it was her doing. I can hunt for myself, so I have no problem surviving... If I needlessly leave the forest, that terrifying Yokai might come find me and kill me...!"

The words tumbled out, stammered from a terrified face. Lee Sungmin noticed that Pervas's mind was not sound. Perhaps lost in the fearful memory, Pervas gripped his head with both hands and muttered a string of disconnected words.

"To think that Dersia is dead..." the Crazy Heavenly Demon mumbled in a dazed voice, watching Pervas collapse to the ground, lost in his own torment.
