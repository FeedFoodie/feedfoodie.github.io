Once Baek Sogo descended the mountain, Lee Sungmin was left in complete solitude. His days became a monotonous existence filled with static and foul odors, his body perpetually heavy. Nightmares plagued him, each one a different torment, and as they repeated, their dreadful details began to sear themselves into his memory.

The forms of his nightmares were varied and cruel. Sometimes he fought countless monsters until his strength gave out, his body torn apart in a death trap. Other times he wandered a giant maze, a horrifying creature hunting him through its endless corridors. He dreamed of running without destination, of falling into bottomless pits, and of enduring unspeakable torture.

If anyone ever dared to say that you don't feel pain in dreams, Lee Sungmin decided he would punch them square in the face without a second thought. He felt every ounce of agony in his vivid nightmares. While the pain vanished the moment he woke, the memory of it lingered, a phantom ache that clung to him. A dream was just a dream, and reality was reality, but knowing this offered no comfort. Even when he became lucid, aware that he was trapped in a nightmare, its terrifying form never wavered.

However, the most difficult nightmare to endure...

"You haven't changed at all."

His reunion with Wiji Hoyeon haunted him as a nightmare. In this dream, ten years had passed in an instant, and they met again in Ruvus. The Wiji Hoyeon who stood before him was exactly as he had imagined she would be, a vision of power and grace.

He, on the other hand, had not changed at all. While the Wiji Hoyeon in his dream was the version he had imagined ten years in the future, Lee Sungmin looked as if not a single day had passed.

"What have you been doing for ten years?"

He tried to answer her, to tell her he had done everything he could, that he had given his best, but his desperate cries could not persuade the Wiji Hoyeon of his dream. She would just give him a single, chilling look. "Was that your limit?"

That was all she would mutter.

It was a nightmare free of physical pain, yet the mental anguish it caused lingered long after he woke. The future he truly feared had manifested in his sleep, and the first time he experienced it, he was gripped by a profound terror of his own powerlessness and the uncertainty that lay ahead.

*What if... what if it really turns out like that when I have my reunion with Wiji Hoyeon?*

The Fatigue Recovery and Brainwashing spells were only temporary solutions. They could not stop the nightmares, which continued their relentless assault on his mind.

Then came the dream where he failed to prevent Baek Sogo's death.

It was a particularly painful one.

He wanted to save her, but he didn't know exactly what had happened in that Dungeon. He hadn't been there, after all. All he knew for certain was that in seven years, Baek Sogo would enter the same Dungeon as Wiji Hoyeon and die.

Lee Sungmin couldn't bring himself to think of Wiji Hoyeon as a villain, but he knew she wasn't a good samaritan either. If he had to classify her, she was gray. Wiji Hoyeon would not hesitate to kill if the situation demanded it. He didn't know the circumstances, but it was clear that something in that Dungeon had forced her hand.

*I.*

Lee Sungmin suppressed a throbbing headache. The Brainwashing magic must have been losing its effect. Had his mind adapted, or had the nightmares simply grown stronger? The throbbing wouldn't go away.

*I have to become strong enough to stop Wiji Hoyeon.*

He knew it wasn't the right answer. The truly right answer was to stop Baek Sogo from ever entering that Dungeon, but Lee Sungmin knew all too well that things involving people rarely went as planned. He had to prepare for the worst. He didn't hope to defeat Wiji Hoyeon, just to become strong enough to prevent Baek Sogo's death. He had just over seven years left.

It was a short time.

Summer arrived, and with it came the cry of the cicadas. Lee Sungmin couldn't hear them, of course, but he could see them clinging to the trees, their bodies trembling with effort. The constant static in his ears could, at best, be compared to their sound.

A cicada larva spends more than ten years underground just to emerge. Right. Even a mere insect spent that long maturing.

*Seven years. Isn't that too short a time for even a larva to become a cicada? How far could I, who am not a larva, get in seven years?* No, it was not a question of 'can I'. He had to. He simply had to.

The static-filled mountain was anything but quiet. His heavy body, the terrible food, and the foul smell offered no comfort, and the daily nightmares stretched each day into an eternity. He was utterly alone. Other ascetics might have come and gone, but Lee Sungmin paid them no mind.

As Lee Sungmin changed, so did his world. Circulating his energy became easier than before, the inner qi flowing through his meridians without obstruction. The fundamentals of his spear technique, practiced daily, became second nature. His mastery of the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear deepened, and he felt a small sense of freedom whenever he performed the Dancing Shadow Soul Break.

It wasn't enough.

A desire for more took root in him, and the density of his days increased accordingly. He cut back on sleep, which was no great loss since the nightmares prevented him from sleeping properly anyway. Every waking moment was spent training. The weight on his body grew heavier each day, yet his Dancing Shadow Soul Break became faster, his spear technique sharper.

Then came the next restriction.

Lee Sungmin had prepared for it, memorizing the surrounding geography until he could walk it with his eyes closed. He had filled his Subspace Pocket with food and smoked enough meat to last, ensuring it would not spoil. He was ready when he received the restriction of his sight.

His world plunged into blackness. With his hearing and smell already useless, touch was the only sense he could depend on. He tried to feel the wind on his skin, a task far harder than it sounded. His senses needed to be sharper, so he devoted himself to the Violet Dawn Divine Art, enduring on preserved food that tasted disgusting.

A week passed before he could move with any confidence. The wooden stick he used to write on the ground became his new eyes, tapping the earth before him as he walked. He waved his hand through the air, trying to feel the currents, the subtle touch of the wind on his skin. A little over a month later, just as his prepared food was about to run out, he finally succeeded in a hunt. A wave of happiness washed over him. Though no one was there to praise him, he felt a deep satisfaction in the accomplishment. It was a worthy hunt, but the food still tasted disgusting.

MEM MEM.

MEM MEM MEM.

The static in his ears became the endless cry of cicadas. Sometimes it twisted into a scream that sounded like Baek Sogo, and other times it was a shriek that was his own. The pitch-black darkness behind his unseeing eyes bred new nightmares, and somewhere in that void, Lee Sungmin lost all track of time. He began to yearn to become a machine, a simple moving machine, a machine that did nothing but perform martial arts.

In his black world, he could not see the form of his own spear technique. *Where is the spear I'm wielding headed? What is its trajectory? When I perform the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, where does my body go? Where do I fly?*

*Do I just want to fly? Do I want to become a cicada?* Did he wish to be like the cicada that crouched in the earth for long years, only to emerge, cry loudly, and die? No. The thought filled him with rage. He hated the cicada cries that echoed in his ears. He did not want to become something like a cicada. What Lee Sungmin wanted to become, what he had always wanted to become, was something more, something much, much bigger.

*What is it?*

*What did I want to become? Why am I doing this? Why am I here? Why did I come back?*

*I am.*

The wind on his skin grew colder, and winter came. His daily routine did not change. He was always moving in his pitch-black world. He skillfully prepared for the cold, and hunting was no longer a difficult task. He was solitary and lonely, but he never gave up on living, never gave up on training.

The wind changed again. Winter turned to spring, and spring turned to summer. Two years had passed. When Mushii told him another restriction had to be added, Lee Sungmin accepted the restriction of not washing. He figured it hardly mattered, since a foul stench already lingered in his nose. Another half a year passed, and he received the restriction of not changing his clothes. The changing temperature of the wind and Mushii's visits were the only ways he could mark the passage of time.

When three years had passed, Lee Sungmin knew it was time. *Mushii.* He mouthed her name.

*What is it?* her voice echoed in his mind.

He slowly evened his breathing before forming his next words.

*I want to end my training.*

Mushii did not answer immediately. The God of Trials and Asceticism, draped in rags, simply looked at the ascetic standing before her.

Three years.

Enduring three years on this mountain was a harsh trial, for restrictions were continuously added. The ordeal known as Flam constantly visited, applying mental pressure while the ascetic struggled within those restrictions.

Not every ascetic who entered this mountain obtained what they desired. Most gave up long before they could, unable to endure.

However, this ascetic, this human with the twisted causality, he was tenacious. The last year he had spent on this mountain had been so harsh that survival itself had seemed impossible.

Nevertheless, he had endured.

"Have you obtained what you desired?" Mushii asked.

Lee Sungmin did not answer. After a short yet long silence, he mouthed his reply.

*If I were a calamity, I might have.*

Did that mean he, who was not a calamity, had failed to obtain what he wanted? What a bitter reality. Was it a sign that overcoming one's inherent limits was ultimately impossible?

*Still, I saw a little,* Lee Sungmin muttered.

He was not satisfied with that. He wanted more, so much more. He was grateful that he had returned to the past, grateful to have overcome death. That was why he was able to feel this way, to feel this burning dissatisfaction.

"Do you think the training on this mountain was valuable to you?"

*If I hadn't come to this mountain, I wouldn't have become like this.*

Mushii seemed quite pleased with his answer. She waved her hand lightly, and the restrictions that had bound Lee Sungmin's body were released.

When all the restrictions vanished, Lee Sungmin staggered. For three years, he had carried that weight. Now that it was gone, the natural lightness of his own body felt awkward. The foul odor that had plagued his senses disappeared. The static in his ears faded to silence.

He opened the eyes he had kept closed for so long.

"......"

Lee Sungmin used his own voice for the first time in what felt like an eternity. He looked at Mushii and smiled bitterly.

"It's a welcome feeling."
