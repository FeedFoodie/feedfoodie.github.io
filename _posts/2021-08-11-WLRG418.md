Despair was a luxury he could no longer afford. He knew it would change nothing. Wiji Hoyeon had shouldered so many burdens, and now, so did he.

He remembered the long, twisted path that had led him here. His past life had been nothing more than a contingency plan for her, his entire growth a carefully constructed deception to fool the Spirit's watchful eyes.

*I know.*

If everything had gone as planned, he was fated to die at Wiji Hoyeon's hands. The Demonic Spirit had never said it directly, but Lee Sungmin had pieced it together and understood. The Spirit remained ignorant of the key's existence, just as it was blind to the Slaughter Predator slumbering within his mind. The Stone of Reincarnation was that key, bound to his very soul. Someday, he was meant to awaken as the Slaughter Predator, and Wiji Hoyeon... she was the nemesis of The End. Had he fully awakened, she would have had no choice but to kill the monster he had become.

In that scenario, the key would have passed to her. The risk was immense, but the Demonic Spirit, desperate to conceal Wiji Hoyeon, had been left with no other choice but to bind the key to him. Once fused with a soul, it could not be taken, not even if the reincarnator awakened as the Slaughter Predator. That they had resorted to such a cumbersome method was proof of their limitations, showing they were mere administrators with no true authority beyond their station.

*I was fated to be killed by you.*

However, fate had changed, and it had changed because of her. Wiji Hoyeon could not accept a future where he became the Slaughter Predator. Through the Demonic Spirit, she had arranged for him to grasp the mistletoe, and because of that, he had extinguished the beast within and escaped his grim destiny.

*Thanks to you.*

That was why he could not Despair. The woman who saved him, the woman who brought him here, the woman who cared for him. She was right there, broken before him, and that was why he had to stand firm.

His conversation with Abel surfaced in his mind.

*Is it your true desire to save this world?* Abel had asked. *Not because the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman died for it, and you feel responsible? Or is it the responsibility you feel for bringing The End to this world by becoming an Observer?*

To Abel's probing question, Lee Sungmin had retorted, *Is it wrong for that to be the reason?*

Abel had only answered with a dry laugh. *If that reason makes you desperate, it doesn't really matter. As long as it's a reason desperate enough to make you give up your own life, it's fine.*

*I am desperate.*

Abel had lost his life overusing Gries, a contingency of the Spirit. In the end, it was absolutely impossible for a man using the Spirit's magic to stop The End. What Abel had attempted, connecting this world to another to alter fate, would have failed even without Envirus's interference, yet Lee Sungmin did not think his death was meaningless. He had, after all, succeeded in banishing the Demon Lord Kim Jonghyun, one of the calamities of The End, to another dimension.

*Are you desperate too?* Abel's final question had never reached him, but Lee Sungmin could answer the absent man now.

*I am desperate.*

He did not deny that he was here to take responsibility. So many duties rested on his shoulders, so many deaths left in his wake. The Crazy Heavenly Demon, the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, Abel, the Sword Immortal, the Spear King, and countless more. He carried the desperation of everyone who sought to stop The End, living and dead, upon his shoulders. It was a weight that pushed him from behind, a force he chose to move ahead of rather than be driven by.

*You must have been desperate too,* he thought, his gaze fixed on Wiji Hoyeon. *Just as I was, you were more desperate than anyone. I know you. I know you so well.*

Because he was desperate, he could not Despair. He had experienced it so many times and had seen it in the eyes of others. When Baek Sogo learned of The End and fell into its clutches, he had held her and promised.

*The me that you saved will stop The End. I won't let your life become meaningless. I will create the good and peaceful world you spoke of. Don't Despair. Just as I haven't Despaired.*

He recalled Jeniela laughing under the full moon, asking if he was in Despair, a question he never answered. He remembered Osla's words, *If you've returned, you must prepare. To become a being worthy of the reason for your return.* And Mushii's, *Do not Despair. Do not break. Do not die.* The Void Monarch's words supported his heart, while the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's pushed him from behind. *Do as you wish. As long as you do what you want and don't regret it, that's all that matters.*

Yes. All of those moments, all of those people, had created the man he was today. They were the reason he now moved forward.

He had started with jack shit. No talent, no background, nothing. The original him was just a pathetic, unmotivated C-Rank Mercenary, the kind of guy who hated the academy and would rather play games at a PC bang than study. That person was now here, facing the world-destroying End. Everything it took to get here had forged him. The boy who had nothing had regressed and become the man of today.

"And there's you."

*I know you. I know the you that others don't. I know the you who would blush slightly while held in my arms. I know the you who made mischievous jokes but was actually shy and would look away. I know the you who loved flowers and butterflies, who acted confident but was lonely, who wanted to return to an ordinary life instead of living as a martial artist.*

*I always chased after your back. I wanted it to be that way, for you to always be stronger than me, for me to always stand behind and follow you. I thought it was excessive greed for someone like me to stand on the same level as you, or to go further. Yes. Ever since we parted in Zenavis when I was fourteen, I've harbored those feelings. The truth is, I was just scared. When I became stronger than you, I worried if you would treat me the same as before. I know it was a ridiculous thought. You weren't that kind of person. I was the narrow-minded one.*

*Still, I hated it.*

*Even knowing that, I simply hated the idea. I liked the you who was stronger than me. I just... liked chasing after you.*

However, it could not be that way now. Lee Sungmin raised his spear, his heart calm, his mind a tranquil sea. The boy who had nothing had met many people and received many things to become the man he was today. That man did not Despair. He must not.

Wiji Hoyeon raised both her arms. Blossoming flowers filled the space around her as butterflies took flight between them. A strange emotion stirred within her as she glared at Lee Sungmin, fragments of distant memories drifting through the ruins of her mind. They were too far away, and to piece them together, she would have to trace back through the eons of slaughter she had committed here. The thought made her feel disgusted.

*I don't want to remember.* The smell of blood was thick on her, and she hated the person she had become. So she thought of nothing and killed every emotion that arose. The man before her was the cause of this turmoil. He was saying things she could not understand, and she did not want to look at him.

*I want to kill him...*

*Do I really want to?* Her mind was in chaos. She ignored the thought. It was better to have the mind of a machine. A machine would not have regrets.

Flowers and butterflies. A scene she had once loved. Lee Sungmin approached it as the blossoming flowers concealed her body. He raised a single spear toward it. *Can I do it?* The thought always came at crucial moments. He usually lacked confidence because...

"I had jack shit, you see."

And he always answered himself. *I have to.* It was the same now. He let out a short laugh.

Time seemed to stop. Everything stood still, but in reality, he was moving so fast that time itself could not keep up.

He leaped into the storm of flowers and butterflies. Lan's Spearmanship became a whirlwind that tore the insects from the air while his own technique shredded the petals into confetti. His spearpoint thrust forward, carving a path through her beautiful, deadly world.

"...Don't come."

Wiji Hoyeon muttered the words without realizing it, waving her hands continuously to block his path.

Lee Sungmin did not stop. His long strides devoured the distance between them, his spear containing every martial theory he had ever learned. They were not his own, but gifts from others. In the end, that was who he was.

"Don't come..."

Her eyes trembled violently as her unstable mind and emotions churned. The Spirit's curse, laid upon that fragile mind, crackled and weakened.

AAAAAAH!

Lee Sungmin roared as he shattered her martial arts. The Dragonic power and mysterious phenomena shook her to her core. The smell of blood. Wiji Hoyeon swallowed hard and dragged her feet back, but he moved that much closer.

*I was always afraid of chasing your back, of standing with you.*

*The me of today... I am no longer your goal. I don't want to remain just a goal. Friend? Goal? Ideal? Aspiration? Is that... all?*

No, it was not. He roared the words he had realized back then.

*The me of today is stronger than you.*

The obstructing force vanished. With another stride, he closed the final distance and was suddenly right before her.

Wiji Hoyeon chewed on her lip and brought her hands together before her chest, but his spear thrust was faster. A tremendous wave of power sent her body flying backward. She gasped and raised her Protective Qi Aura, knowing it was too late to dodge. She had to endure. As she unleashed her own attacks, a brilliant light swelled from the tip of his spear. She gritted her teeth.

"No..." she muttered in a small voice. "I, I can't fail. If I fail... I..."

"It's okay," he said, cutting her off. "Even if you fail... it's okay."

The woman before him did not yet realize she had already failed. Her face went blank.

"......"

Wiji Hoyeon's eyes trembled. The world she had created was disappearing. The flowers, the butterflies, all of her power scattered before the light of creation that held the pinnacle of martial arts. In that moment, a figure surfaced in her hazy mind. The one who always chased her back, the one she had secretly wished would continue to do so forever.

"...I see..." she muttered and closed her eyes. The landscape of corpses she had built was already gone. Her mind drifted past the terrible hell engraved upon her heart, settling instead on the scene she truly loved, the one she had always wanted to return to. The sunset sky, the flowers, and the butterflies. The time she spent sitting there, waiting for 'him' to return.

"Before I knew it... you were so far ahead."

A faint smile touched Wiji Hoyeon's lips. Her hands fell limply to her sides.

Lee Sungmin's spear stopped an inch from her chest. He gasped for breath and lowered the weapon, making it vanish from his hand. Reaching out, he gently caressed her cheeks, wiping away the bloodstains.

"Thank you," Lee Sungmin said. "For doing this... for me."

Her eyes trembled.

"For caring for me so much that you broke yourself."

"...I... failed."

"It's okay to fail. I promised you, didn't I? That even if you fail, I'll be here. Nothing has gone wrong."

At his words, Wiji Hoyeon's shoulders flinched. She finally let her eyes close completely as a single tear trickled down her cheek.

"...Yeah."

She accepted her failure. She accepted the fate she had been given and the person it had turned her into. She accepted that she had been crushed by a mission she never wanted, shattered by her own actions, and had failed.

"...Thank you..."

Hearing her small whisper, Lee Sungmin pulled her into an embrace.

The woman in his arms was not an iron man.

She was just an ordinary human being.
