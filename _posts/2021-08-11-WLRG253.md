Baek Sogo gazed into the mirror at hair that had turned completely white. She had endured so much, beginning with her time on Mount Mushii. The years she had spent after returning to the mountain, the thousand years she had endured in Denil's Mental World, and the several more it took to fully internalize the training she gained there had all left their mark.

And now, her time was done. Baek Sogo was finally leaving Dreamoor, the place where Denir had been. She put the mirror away, unsettled by how unfamiliar her own reflection had become. Her whitened hair and a gaze that had grown far too deep were stark reminders of her ordeal. She pressed her temples firmly, feeling the lingering aftereffects of her training in the Mental World.

It had been a thousand years, not mere decades or centuries. She had lived utterly alone in a white world of nothingness, where she did nothing but train, and train, and train. Though she had already mastered the Dancing Shadow Soul Break to its peak, the enlightenment she found there compelled her to deconstruct and relearn it from scratch dozens of times over.

The result was undeniable. She had grown stronger. Mushii's blessing had even worked within the Mental World, allowing her to completely transcend the wall of the Supreme Peak.

Ever since re-entering Mount Mushii, Baek Sogo had blindly pursued power. She felt that to achieve her ideals, she needed a strength that would bow to no one. Her ideals were supported by her convictions, a simple desire to be righteous and proper. She wanted to be a nemesis to evil and establish justice.

She had once believed the Murim Alliance was the most suitable place for that purpose, but she had only found great disappointment there. How foolish she had been, and how many truths she had ignored, even when she knew them. During her countless days in the Mental World, Baek Sogo had thought about it endlessly.

The orthodox sects and the unorthodox sects were meaningless in this world. The murim that existed here was not worthy of the name. Within the orthodox sects, not only were righteousness and propriety gone, but even cooperation no longer existed. They refused to let go of their vested interests, filling their bellies with greed as the haves did not hesitate to take from the have-nots. The conscience of a martial artist, which had initially resisted, had been worn down by time and repeated malice until the very spirit of the orthodox sects had been corrupted.

Even the Nine Sects One Gang, with its long and storied tradition, had become no different from a band of degenerates. They sought profit by gaining power over others rather than striving for enlightenment through martial arts. Among the powerful, there was no one who could be called a true martial artist. What was chivalry in the first place? What were righteousness and propriety? Justice? In this world, who on earth could judge such things with a clear standard?

Everyone had their own criteria, and Baek Sogo felt that she herself was no different. Humans, all humans, are different. Not only could they never fully understand one another, but a truly righteous person did not exist.

It was a contradiction, and Baek Sogo knew it. Her ideals, the convictions that established them, and the standards that formed their very foundation might not seem right to others. She knew how contradictory and selfish it was to consider her own standards an absolute, to speak of righteousness, and to judge others while asserting her own chivalry.

Because she knew this, Baek Sogo did not know where she should go.

*I want to see Junior Disciple.*

It was one of her deepest wishes. She wanted to meet the junior disciple who had thrown his life away for her. His action... that was chivalry. He had not cared for his own safety, abandoning his life for another in a moment of mortal danger. By Baek Sogo's standards, the actions of her Junior Disciple, Lee Sungmin, were more than enough to be called righteous chivalry.

That same junior disciple, the one she so desperately wanted to see again, had apparently become the disciple of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. The man known as the Demon Emperor, Yang Ilcheon, and the unorthodox organization he led were considered evil by most people.

And wasn't that actually the case? It would be arrogant to say that all members of the unorthodox sects were evil, but most of those who were called demonic leaders and committed evil deeds were indeed from their ranks.

*And Junior Disciple. You too.*

By killing the Sword Phantom who had murdered the Sect Leader of the Mount Hua Sect, her junior disciple had earned the title of the Phantom Spear. Until then, Lee Sungmin had been one of the rising new masters of the orthodox sects, but at some point, he had become a demonic practitioner.

When Baek Sogo, who had been ignorant of the world, finally descended from the mountain, she investigated the rumors about Lee Sungmin and was utterly shocked. He had murdered the Murim Alliance's Iron Armored Divine Spear. He had murdered Jaegal Taeryeong and Moyong Seojin in the Bewitching Forest, and now, as the disciple of the Demon Emperor, he had apparently become the successor to the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance.

There had to be a reason. She didn't want to doubt her junior disciple recklessly. Perhaps there was some misunderstanding. The Murim Alliance, the very organization she had ignored, seemed like the type of group that could easily create misunderstandings and frame people intentionally.

However, what kind of misunderstanding or false accusation could possibly explain becoming the disciple of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman and the successor of the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance?

*I can go see him now.*

There was no point in just thinking about it. Baek Sogo set her course for Haras, where the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance was located. It would be a long journey, for Haras was very far from here, but she had no intention of rushing. Before going to Haras, before meeting her junior disciple, she planned to properly face this world, the one she had never truly seen before, or had ignored even when she had.

Denir's words came to mind.

-You must become a dagger.

Those were the words he had spoken before she left. Baek Sogo did not understand what he meant. Just as Mushii had done, Denir had also made her his incarnation. Why he had done so was another thing she could not comprehend.

One of the prestigious clans of the orthodox sects, the Tang Clan, was located in Nerod, a city near the Wudang Sect.

*Wudang is close.*

The name of the Wudang Sect did not frighten him. The current Lee Sungmin could annihilate one of the Nine Sects One Gang with ease. The name of the Sword Immortal, however, was a different story. The Sword Immortal was in Wudang.

He was one of the masters acknowledged by the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, one of the few in the world who could be his nemesis.

*The Martial God and the Sword Immortal.*

And the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman as well. Lee Sungmin couldn't tell which of the three was the strongest, but if a master possessed power equal to the Chairman, it was impossible for the current Lee Sungmin to oppose them. He couldn't even begin to imagine the monstrously strong Chairman being defeated or struggling. As his disciple, Lee Sungmin had experienced beatings disguised as duels several times and knew his master's strength all too well. If the Sword Immortal was on par with him, Lee Sungmin could never win.

His only comfort was that the Sword Immortal lived in seclusion deep within Mount Wudang and never came down. Still, before setting out to kill the Dark Supreme, the Chairman had warned him. He had told him never to attack if he ended up fighting the Sword Immortal.

"You might run into the Sword Immortal. That fellow is a strange one whose intentions even I can't fathom. Even if he sees through your identity and attacks you, you must never fight back."

"So you're telling me to just let him cut me down and die?"

"In the first place, if the Sword Immortal swings his sword with the intent to kill you, you couldn't possibly survive with your skills. Don't confront him. Run away. Though it would be impossible for you to dodge his sword and escape."

He recalled his master's warning. He didn't think the Chairman had said that because he was looking down on him. The Sword Immortal's blade was simply so fearsome that he had no choice but to say it.

Lee Sungmin became conscious of the Magic Armor he wore under his clothes. It was a new set, crafted by the Dwarf chieftain Mackendor and the Meister Celgerus together, using the whole scales and hide of a Dragon. He had made time in his tight schedule to visit the Dwarf Village to have the armor and a new spear crafted, preparing for a potential encounter with the Sword Immortal or any other crisis he might face.

He was extremely satisfied with the new armor. It wouldn't get a single scratch even if it took a direct hit of enhanced qi. The armor was so sturdy that he wouldn't be too afraid even if attacked by someone in the Transcendent Realm. It was also imbued with various magical enchantments, feeling weightless and comfortable while maintaining its top-tier condition without needing repairs thanks to a high-level self-repair spell. If he were to meet the Sword Immortal, he might be able to block one of his strikes without even needing martial arts theory.

The spear was the same, though he wasn't currently carrying it. A sharp, hard Dragon's tooth was used as the spearhead, reinforced with its scales. It was so sharp even without channeling enhanced qi that it felt like there was nothing in this world it couldn't pierce. Perhaps with this spear, he could even penetrate the scales of the Black Dragon Knight, which his previous attacks had failed to do.

*Before that, the Dark Supreme will die by this spear first.*

"Here's the dish you ordered."

A bowl of noodles in a clear broth was placed before him. Lee Sungmin had a headband tied tightly around his forehead and wore a magic-infused human skin mask, imitating a common vagrant who pretended to be a young martial artist despite having learned only crude techniques. Since he was avoiding involvement with anyone and acting as cautiously as possible, there would be no cause for trouble like there was during the subjugation. An incident like that could not be allowed to happen again.

Tang Ahee's birthday was tomorrow. Would the Dark Supreme come this time as well? He couldn't be sure. Perhaps he wouldn't come this year. However, there was a chance he might, and Lee Sungmin didn't want to ignore the opportunity.

The Dark Supreme's movements were stealthy. He still remembered those ghost-like movement arts and various hidden weapons techniques. It was true that he had almost died back then.

*I know the place.*

He had heard it from Tang Ahee in the forest. Midnight on her birthday, every year, under the large tree in the backyard of the annex she used. Tang Mugi would supposedly appear there to give her a birthday present. There was no guarantee it was true. It was possible Tang Ahee had lied just to escape the situation, but he would be able to confirm it tomorrow.

From a conventional standpoint, the task was not easy. First, he had to infiltrate the Tang Clan without anyone knowing. Specifically, he had to break into the annex of Tang Ahee, the Patriarch's own daughter, which would be under especially tight security, and make his way to the backyard.

*I'm not that confident in my stealth.*

He wasn't completely incompetent at it. A master of his level could use stealth to some degree even without formal training, as all rivers flow into the sea. However, the Dark Supreme was not an opponent one could challenge in stealth while citing such a proverb. If he relied on his clumsy skills, the Dark Supreme would notice and escape, which would make catching him even more difficult.

However, he hadn't come here recklessly. He wasn't entertaining the naive thought that things would somehow work out without a plan.

Lee Sungmin finished the bowl of noodles and decided he would stay at this inn for the day. He had no intention of leaving the room he rented until tomorrow night.

*The Black Dragon Knight won't show up again, will he?*

He had considered the worst-case scenario, but even so, he didn't think the Black Dragon Knight would appear this time. It would have been better if he had died at Ju Won's hands in that forest. Lee Sungmin felt a pang of regret as he set down his chopsticks.

*Still, it's certain he was injured to some extent.*

Upon returning to the Murim Alliance, the Black Dragon Knight had passed his duties as Chairman to the Vice-Chairman, citing personal reasons, and then vanished. As the one who had sent Ju Won after him, Lee Sungmin could only assume that the Black Dragon Knight had suffered a grave injury in their fight and had gone into hiding to heal.

*A wound that can't be healed even with an Elixir.*

He had no way of knowing what it was. Lee Sungmin rose from his seat, a lingering sense of disappointment hanging over him. In truth, the most ideal outcome he had hoped for was for both Ju Won and the Black Dragon Knight to die. It would have been even better if the Spear King had died fighting alongside them as well.
