"You're an otherworlder, I see."

A passerby's voice cut through the boy's daze. He was standing blankly in the middle of a street, a place that felt both nostalgic and familiar. The boy blinked, slowly coming to his senses.

"Yes, yes?" he stammered.

"Tsk, tsk! Hey, I get that you're confused, but you need to pull yourself together," the man said, clicking his tongue. "This place is full of otherworlders like you, but that doesn't mean it's a kind city to them."

"No way..."

The boy's mind was a chaotic storm. He tried to grasp his memories, but a throbbing headache sent a pained groan tearing from his lips. He collapsed to the ground, and the man who had spoken to him reached out in alarm.

"What? What's wrong all of a sudden? Are you hurt?"

"Ah, no. It's just a slight headache..." the boy mumbled, clutching his head with both hands.

A great wave of compressed memories crashed into his mind.

"Gasp!" He took a sharp breath, his body trembling violently. "...Zenavis?"

"Huh? What? Weren't you an otherworlder?" 

The man looked even more bewildered by the boy's mention of Zenavis. After all, this was the name of the City of Beginnings, the first city where summoned otherworlders arrived, and this newly summoned child should not have heard of it yet.

*...What in the world happened?*

The boy stared blankly down at his hands. They were small. The calluses that should have covered his palms were gone. 

It was not just the calluses. His line of sight was lower. His height... he had gotten shorter. 

The boy quickly raised his hands to his face, rubbing his skin. The scars that should have covered his face had vanished, leaving only smooth, unblemished skin.

After confirming this, he immediately lifted the t-shirt he was wearing.

Gone. The defined abs that should have been there were nowhere to be seen, and neither were the scars.

"Are you really okay?" the man asked, his expression worried. From his perspective, the dazed kid looked like he was about to strip off his clothes right there in the street.

"...What year is it?"

"What?"

"What year is it in the Eria Calendar?"

"...It's the year 1103," the man replied, his face reluctant.

Eria Year 1103. There was no mistake. He had returned to the past.

He did not know what this damn world of Eria was, but the greedy world had been summoning various otherworlders from all dimensions for ages. 

Thirteen years ago, he, Lee Sungmin, had also been summoned here, to the Eria Continent, for no reason at all.

Classless.

That was the class he had been given thirteen years ago, when he was first summoned. 

To put it simply, Classless was a blank slate. They could learn anything and received a special growth bonus for learning, which in a way was a fair 'rule' as some people had already learned martial arts before coming to Eria, and some had learned magic. 

The Classless, however, had nothing. They had not learned martial arts, nor had they learned magic. If they had not been summoned to Eria, they would have lived their entire lives as ordinary people, never knowing such things even existed.

In other words, Classless was a term for people who were completely ordinary before their arrival.

Before returning, Lee Sungmin had survived for thirteen years on the Eria Continent as one of them. He had never shown outstanding talent, but he had acquired enough strength not to be ignored.

However, in the end, he died. It had been an insignificant death, too.

Leaning against an alley wall, Lee Sungmin recalled his pathetic past life. Starting as a Classless, his limits were clear. 

A Classless was a blank slate that could learn anything, but you had to have something to learn in the first place. Some were summoned having already mastered peerless divine arts, while many others started with outstanding magic skills. 

The Classless, who had learned nothing, had a completely different starting line. Even if they all began in Zenavis, a Classless like Lee Sungmin would struggle just to avoid being killed by low-level monsters, while the others would laughably slaughter those same creatures and move on.

*...The Stone of Reincarnation.*

He searched his memory. He was sure he had heard those words just before he regained consciousness.

*No way.*

Something clicked. The dungeon he had entered, shouting that he was lucky. Lucky his ass. It was the dungeon where he had met his end. 

The memory was vivid, his disappointment upon opening the first treasure chest he found, only to see a single small stone lying there. He had considered throwing it away since an appraisal skill yielded nothing, but he had kept it, wondering if a professional appraiser in the city might get a different result.

*The Stone of Reincarnation... haha! I thought I was unlucky, but I was actually really lucky after all, huh.*

The memory grew clearer, and a voice echoed in his mind. 

[You cannot use the Stone of Reincarnation again.]

The voice was distinct. His return to this life was a stroke of luck, a coincidence he would never be able to benefit from again.

> **Status Window**
>
> Name: Lee Sungmin
>
> Occupation: Classless
>
> Skills: None.

The faint hope he had harbored was shattered. He had returned to thirteen years in the past, but that was all. His class was the same, and he did not even have any skills.

*No. The important thing is that I survived death and returned to the past.*

He decided to think positively. To have returned without dying was enough. 

Lee Sungmin quickly got to his feet. It was not like he had any amazing skills in his past life anyway. The masters of peerless divine arts rarely passed on their techniques, and the occasional secret martial arts manuals that were discovered were so expensive he could not even dream of affording them. It was the same for magic. 

As a result, the only skills Lee Sunggmin could learn were those he could afford, which at best were second-rate. He had struggled for thirteen years to learn them, but he felt no great attachment to them.

In fact, his current state of having learned nothing was better.

The memories of the thirteen years he had lived. Even if they were not perfectly clear, he remembered the major events.

"Hey. Are you okay?"

It was the man who had helped him up. Lee Sungmin stood and gave him a slight bow. 

Zenavis. Just as the man had said, this city was where all otherworlders first arrived, but it was not a very kind place for them. That was why Lee Sungmin felt a flicker of gratitude. The man had shown him a minimum of kindness, even knowing he was an otherworlder.

"I'm fine."

"...That's a relief, then. Here. I brought you some water." The man handed him a water bottle with a smile. 

Lee Sungmin accepted it with both hands. "Thank you."

"Don't mention it. People should help each other out. You're a Classless, right? I can tell just by looking. It's tough for a Classless... to adapt to this city. You'll probably have a hard time ahead of you."

The man wore a pitying expression, reflecting the common perception of the Classless. Because they were completely unprepared, it was difficult for them to survive on this damn Eria Continent.

"You're right," Lee Sungmin muttered, bringing the water bottle to his lips.

He was grateful for the memories of his past life. Especially in situations like this, his experience was an excellent weapon. 

A faint, fishy scent wafted from the water, something he would not have noticed if he had not been wary. For now, it was just a suspicion. He opened his lips and took a tiny bit of water into his mouth.

A slight tingling sensation touched the tip of his tongue. It was fine. As long as the poison did not go down his throat, it would not take effect. 

Zenavis. This damn city. He had been burned so many times here thirteen years ago.

PFFT!

Lee Sungmin spat the mouthful of water directly into the man's face. The man, who had been waiting for him to finish drinking, let out a startled cry.

"......"

otherworlders who suddenly arrived in this city were good prey for its residents, and a Classless with no abilities was the easiest prey of all. 

What would have happened if he had drunk the water? His whole body would have been paralyzed, and then what? He might have been sold to a black mage, or maybe to a slave trader. One thing was certain, it would not have been anything good.

"W-What are you doing?!"

"That's my line."

Living in Eria for thirteen years had taught him one thing for certain, and that was not to trust others easily. Not all otherworlders were villains, but that did not mean they were all good samaritans either. This was a place teeming with trash who would stab others in the back for their own gain.

The paralyzing poison in the water got into the man's eyes, and he let out a pained groan. It was probably not a fatal poison, but it was impossible for him to open his eyes.

Lee Sungmin clenched his fists and rushed him. He had no skills, but his past experience made him bold. This thirteen-year-old body, which had never known a real fight, was pathetically weak, but he threw a clenched fist toward the man's jaw.

"......"

With a dull thud, the man's mouth flew open. Lee Sungmin brought his knee up, smashing it into the man's groin.

"GAAH!"

His physical strength was pathetic, but a man was an animal that would be immobilized by a kid's kick to the crotch. He wondered if his balls had burst, but that was not his problem. Lee Sungmin kicked the head of the fallen man with all his might.

Blood spurted from the man's mouth. Lee Sungmin kicked him a few more times before stopping, already out of breath just from those few forceful blows.

"Fucking city," he spat, and searched the unconscious man's body. He took the dagger hidden in his clothes and snatched his wallet, securing immediate funds and a weapon.

He paused, hesitating. Should he let this guy live or not? The answer was obvious. If he let him live and he came back for revenge later, things would get annoying.

The thirteen-year-old Lee Sungmin had no experience with murder. Back then, he was just an ordinary middle school student whose only experience with killing was squashing ants, cockroaches, or flies. That was the mental weakness most Classless possessed.

However, the current Lee Sungmin was different. Without hesitation, he plunged the dagger into the man's chest, pushing it between the ribs and piercing the heart.

With that, he had eliminated any future trouble.
