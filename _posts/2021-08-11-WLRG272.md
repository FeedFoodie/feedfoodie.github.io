Deep within the mountain forest stood a small cabin, a place shrouded in the whispers of the nearest village. Many stories were spun about the old man and his granddaughter who lived in that secluded home. One such rumor suggested the old man had caught a plague, leaving his poor young granddaughter to care for her only living relative.

The tales varied, of course. Some villagers claimed it was the granddaughter, not the old man, who suffered from the plague. Others insisted her affliction was not a disease but a form of lunacy. These were not baseless speculations. The old man would occasionally appear on the outskirts of the village to purchase supplies, and each time he came, he was wrapped in a long, thick robe that concealed everything from his face to the very tips of his fingers. Naturally, people concluded that the plague had twisted his face into something hideous and caused the flesh of his fingertips to rot away.

The rumors of the granddaughter's madness had their own source. Hunters and woodcutters who wandered the forest had seen her on several occasions, running wildly through the trees while letting out strange, incomprehensible screams.

"How is it? The mountains are quite enjoyable, aren't they?" Preskan asked, pushing back the hood of his robe. The gesture revealed nothing, for as a Lich, Preskan had no face.

From her seat before the crackling fireplace, Aine muttered her reply. "It's not enjoyable."

"Why is that? There's plenty to eat in the mountains. Aren't the wild berries quite delicious?"

Though he said it, Preskan had no idea what wild berries tasted like. He seemed to recall eating them before he became a Lich, but the memory of their flavor was lost to the vast expanse of time. A Lich had no need for food or drink. Eating was an impossibility, and so was tasting.

"Meat isn't the only thing to eat, you know," Preskan said, his voice benevolent. It was a tone hard to reconcile with the infamous Black Mage and Lich who had once conducted countless human experiments.

*What does it matter?* he thought. *That was then, and this is now.* Preskan genuinely believed it. This was not hypocrisy. He had come to understand the most beautiful and brilliant emotion in the world. It was love, and among all its forms, the noblest, truest, and purest was a parent's love for their child.

*That's right. Fatherly love.* At that very moment, Preskan was filled with a warm, paternal affection. He felt a pang of regret that he had no face and could not form an expression. If he could, he would have given his daughter a smile more full of love than any other father in the world.

"When are we going to the sea?" Aine asked.

"We have all the time in the world. After we've enjoyed our leisure here, let's go see the sea together."

"I want to eat meat," she grumbled. Ever since the incident in Urmuri, she had been intermittently swept up by an overwhelming urge to binge eat. It was not enough to make her lose her reason, but when the intense craving wrapped around her body, Aine would try to devour everything in sight without a second thought.

He wanted to let his daughter do as she pleased, but Preskan knew it was sometimes a father's role to be stern and stop her. He had never been married, nor had he ever fathered a child, yet because he genuinely considered Aine his daughter, he understood what he had to do. Besides, letting her rampage was not good in the long run. If she devoured every person she saw, she would naturally make more enemies. Most would be unable to threaten them, but Preskan knew the world was wide and full of beings with power more monstrous than monsters themselves.

That was why he had brought them deep into the mountains. It was partly to try living a leisurely life as a father and daughter, and partly to suppress Aine's urges as much as possible, but that was not the only reason. Preskan hoped, even if it was just a little, that Aine would find joy in being human. He knew it was a contradiction, but this too was a father's heart.

Preskan had never greatly regretted becoming a Lich, but on very rare occasions, he missed the times when he was human. Eating delicious food, drinking enough to get pleasantly drunk, and laying a weary body and mind down to sleep in a soft, wide bed. He wanted Aine to feel and experience many things, even beyond that kind of life.

Their existence in the mountains was boring and dull, but it was leisurely. While living here, he had barely conducted any magic research at all. He traveled back and forth to the village to buy daily necessities just for Aine. Having never lived like that before, Preskan was feeling a small, simple joy.

"Haven't my cooking skills improved quite a bit?"

"Not at all."

He would sometimes cook for Aine himself. Though she ate everything he made, she always voiced her dissatisfaction. It could not be helped. The seasoning in dishes made by someone incapable of eating was always a mess.

Preskan chuckled at Aine's grumbling, so lost in the moment that he did not even notice the cabin door opening.

Aine was the first to react. Her senses had become incomparably sharp after the incident in Urmuri. While her understanding of martial arts was lacking, the toughness of her body and her instincts were on par with a master of the Transcendent Realm.

PWAK!

Her body shot backward as if a coiled spring had been released. She landed before the open door, her right arm twitching. Various magic spells had been prepared around the cabin to deal with intruders, yet until the door swung open, neither Preskan nor Aine had sensed a presence.

"Behave."

"......"

A whispering voice drifted from beyond the slowly opening door. At the sound of it, Aine's face turned pale. As if to deny the emotion that seized her, she swung her right arm, which had already transformed into a blade. The blade bisected the space at a speed too fast to see, and in that same instant, Preskan heard the clear sound of a sneer.

CRUNCH!

The blade never completed its swing. Aine's right arm, connected to the weapon, twisted like a pretzel. Bones shattered while muscles, blood vessels, and skin tore apart. It was not just her arm.

CRACK... RIIIIP!

Her entire body below her head contorted, and blood gushed incessantly from her torn skin.

"Why are you acting like this? It's not like you don't know me."

"Kh... rrrrgh...!"

Even in that state, Aine did not die. Instead, her twisted body rapidly healed, returning to its original form.

Jeniela stepped through the doorway, looking down at Aine with a smile that bared her fangs. "You've gotten tougher than before."

"Kgh...!" Aine staggered as she pushed herself up.

CRACK, CRACKLE!

Her entire body transformed. Countless blades and tentacles erupted from her flesh. Even faced with such a hideous form, Jeniela did not flinch. Preskan, who had shot up from his seat, trembled in horror. This place was a long way from North Trabia, where Jeniela resided. Why on earth was the Vampire Queen, who had not left her domain for hundreds of years, standing here?

"Don't be so wary. I didn't come here with malicious intent."

"W-Why are you here...?" Preskan stammered.

"Because it seemed like the time had come to act like this." Jeniela's actions were aimed at getting as close as possible to the future she had seen. She had no intention of explaining any of that to them, of course. Among the fragmented visions she had witnessed with her All-Seeing Eye, there was one where she was with Aine and Preskan.

*Could that Chimera be the Slaughter Predator?*

She thought it was not entirely impossible. The heart Preskan had created possessed an abundance of potential to become one. However, if this Chimera truly was the Slaughter Predator, Jeniela inwardly clicked her tongue.

*As of now, isn't its level far too low?* Its regeneration was outstanding, and its physical toughness was quite excellent, but its method of controlling it all was far too immature.

"I have no intention of killing you," Jeniela said calmly. "In the first place, you haven't done anything to deserve death from me." She had lent Preskan and Aine to Arbes in order to connect with the future she had seen. In fact, until recently, she had thought Arbes would achieve his goal, take over Aine's body, and become the Slaughter Predator himself.

*I never thought Arbes would die like that, though.* Jeniela did not know that Lee Sungmin had also been in Urmuri. That was not all. In the futures she had seen, Lee Sungmin had never been captured.

"Was playing house fun?" Jeniela asked, looking around the charmingly decorated cabin. It was a style that did not suit them at all. She reached a hand toward Aine, who glared back without lessening her hostility.

Jeniela warned her with a smile. "You artificial thing that doesn't know its place."

"S-Stop!" Preskan cried, hurriedly stepping forward to block her path just as she was about to crush Aine's body.

Jeniela sneered at him. "Are you standing before me because you trust in your Life Force Vessel?"

"I-I just don't want to see that child in pain..."

"You're saying things that don't suit you."

"What do you want from us? Why have you come all this way?"

"Why do you think I came?" Jeniela said, lowering her outstretched hand. "I came to take you with me."

"...What?"

"To my mansion," she clarified, her red eyes curving into crescents. "To Trabia, that is."

"Are you really going to be okay?" Lee Sungmin asked Scarlet, his expression worried.

Scarlet stood before a mirror, turning this way and that in a light brown robe. She pressed down her messy hair with both hands as she answered. "What's there to not be okay about?"

"It might be dangerous."

"In my opinion, it's more dangerous for me to stay here alone," Scarlet replied with a snort. "If you and the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman leave, the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance will be empty, won't it? Thanks to you turning the Tang Clan upside down, the orthodox sects' dissatisfaction with us has grown. What am I supposed to do if you two go out and cause more trouble, and those orthodox bastards decide to attack an empty house and come charging in?"

"...I don't think they'd do something that foolish."

"Did they know the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's successor would suddenly turn the Tang Clan upside down? You never know with people. The world is wide, and it's full of psychos."

"...Ahem." Lee Sungmin let out an embarrassed cough when she did not back down. He had already spoken with the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. The two of them planned to leave the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance for a while and head to the Orthodox Murim Alliance. Judging it was dangerous to remain behind, Scarlet had decided to accompany them.

"Why? Is it uncomfortable for you to travel with me?"

"That's not it."

"Remember? A long time ago, when you left Behenger for Shaolin, we traveled together. It was for less than a year, but it was pretty fun," Scarlet muttered with a chuckle. "Back then, only your expression was old, but you still had a youthful look to you. You were pretty cute."

"I wasn't cute."

"That's what you think. Still, you grew up well, so it's fine. You know, during the Kim Jonghyun subjugation, when you showed up wearing that human skin mask? I felt so bad for you because your face was so ruined compared to when you were younger."

"What was there to feel bad about?"

"Your face, which was at least decent to look at, was ruined. I felt bad for your life," Scarlet giggled, teasing him. She spun around to face him, grabbing the robe she was wearing with both hands and making it flutter. "How do I look?"

"You look pretty."

"You're so soulless," Scarlet grumbled, her expression hardening at his blunt response.
