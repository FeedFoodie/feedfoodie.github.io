"Where are you headed?"

The gate guard of Bredon bowed his head to Lee Sungmin, his voice polite. Perhaps it was the monk's robes. Bredon was a city heavily influenced by Shaolin, and while Lee Sungmin's head was not shaved, the robes he wore afforded him a measure of respect.

"I'm going to Dreamoor," Lee Sungmin answered.

The guard nodded in understanding. Once through the gate, Lee Sungmin chose not to ride a horse. Though Bredon and Dreamoor were relatively close cities, the journey on foot would still take half a month.

An errand for Grandmaster Bulyeong was what had brought him down from Shaolin so suddenly. He was to deliver a letter to the Mount Hua Sect in Dreamoor. The task had originally been assigned to Jihak, but when Lee Sungmin heard the destination, he had volunteered to go instead. He explained it as a desire to repay the kindness he had received, but there was more to it than that.

Mount Hua was one of the Nine Sects One Gang, a Taoist sect renowned for its swordsmanship. This focus often led to comparisons with the Wudang Sect, though most considered Mount Hua's swordplay to be slightly inferior. The common saying about the Nine Sects One Gang's Mount Tai and Big Dipper made this clear. Shaolin was Mount Tai, and Wudang was the Big Dipper. Mount Hua was not mentioned.

Though it was judged as slightly lesser, the swordsmanship of Mount Hua pursued a fundamentally different path from Wudang's. The Mount Hua style was flashy, full of transformations and illusions. Wudang's sword, on the other hand, favored weightiness over flashiness and stillness over change.

*It would be good to see Mount Hua, too.*

The sect had originally been named for its location on Mount Hua in the Central Plains, but this was Eria, not the Central Plains, so there was no Mount Hua. Just as Shaolin had done, the sect had simply chosen a different mountain, named it Mount Hua, and established their home there.

The timing of the errand had been perfect, a mere catalyst for a journey he needed to take. His training at Shaolin had been valuable and fulfilling, but Lee Sungmin had another, more pressing reason to go to Dreamoor. He had to meet the God of Time, Denir, just as Mushii had told him.

"Nebul."

After leaving the city, Lee Sungmin walked until sunset before preparing to camp for the night. He had not called for Nebul once during his three years at Shaolin, but now he did.

"It's been a while."

Nebul rose from the shadows, looking completely unchanged despite the considerable time that had passed. He bowed his head. Lee Sungmin stared blankly at the man's uncanny stillness for a moment before speaking.

"Please connect me with Celgerus."

"Understood."

Nebul asked no questions about the past three years. He simply melted back into Lee Sungmin's shadow and vanished. A moment later, the space before Lee Sungmin tore open with a crack.

"I thought you were dead," a voice said.

Just like Nebul, the Dark Elf Celgerus had not changed much either. Her time flowed far more slowly than a human's. Lee Sungmin offered a bitter smile at her words.

"Were you hoping I would die?"

"Of course not," she replied. "You've changed a lot. Then again, three years is enough time for a human to age." As she spoke, Celgerus pulled off the thick gloves she was wearing. She wiped her sweaty hands on her apron and looked at him expectantly. "Did you call just to report that you're alive?"

"Ah… that's not it."

Lee Sungmin reached into his Subspace Pocket. The spear and Magic Armor he withdrew were worn and weathered from three years of neglect. Celgerus's eyebrows furrowed sharply at the sight of them.

"How on earth did you use them for them to end up like this? Did you go rolling around on some battlefield? I didn't hear any rumors about a war…"

"There were… some circumstances," Lee Sungmin explained. "I couldn't maintain them at all, so they've gotten very worn. Is it possible to repair them?"

For three years, he had not touched the spear or the armor. He had left them at the entrance of his training cave, a visible symbol of his arrogant and lazy self. He had wanted to sever that weakness within him, training every day while looking upon the items he had once clung to as compensation for running away from reality.

"It's possible," Celgerus said, her attention shifting. "This is the first time I'm seeing the armor, though. Can I take a look?"

"Ah, yes. Of course."

Nebul passed the Magic Armor and spear to her. She showed far more interest in the armor than the spear.

"This is… an artifact," she murmured, examining it closely. "The central magic seems to have disappeared, but the auxiliary magics are still functioning properly."

The armor was designed to equip itself automatically when infused with magic power, and most damage would repair itself over time.

"Since you didn't maintain it properly, it'll need some work, but there shouldn't be any major problems," she concluded. "Same goes for the spear."

Lee Sungmin let out a sigh of relief. He had worried they might be damaged beyond use from being neglected for so long, but it seemed that was not the case.

"Any other requests to add?" Celgerus asked.

"Not yet. For now, just the repairs, please."

"Got it. It should take about three days."

"Ah… if it's alright, could you hold onto them for a while?"

"......"

"I don't think they suit me yet," he admitted.

Celgerus's eyes widened. She studied him for a moment, then smacked her lips and nodded.

"Well, holding onto them isn't a difficult task, but just in case, let me ask. It's okay if I tinker with them a bit when I'm free, right?"

"Pardon?"

"Just to keep my hands busy," she clarified. "…You said they don't suit you, right? Surely you didn't mean that in a bad way?"

"Of course not. It's just… it's too good of a spear. It's too precious for the current me to handle. That's why I don't want to use it right now."

"That's the highest praise for a blacksmith," Celgerus said with a smirk. "So, whenever I have time and I'm bored, I'll tinker with it to keep my hands busy."

"If it becomes even better, it'll be harder for me to use…"

"Then you just have to try to become better too, don't you?"

With that, her image wavered and the connection closed. Lee Sungmin turned his attention back to Nebul.

"I'd like to purchase some information."

"What information do you desire?"

"Information on the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun, and Silent Flash Baek Sogo."

"That is not a difficult request," Nebul replied. "Those two are martial artists who have been gaining considerable fame recently. What aspect should I focus on for you?"

"Their current locations, please."

"Understood. I will bring you the information you want by tomorrow. I will also tell you the price tomorrow."

"Ah, and…" Lee Sungmin hesitated for a second. "Information on the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon, please."

"Understood," Nebul said with a bow. With their business concluded, he disappeared.

{sep}

The next day, a little past noon, a voice echoed in Lee Sungmin's mind as he walked the road to Dreamoor.

[May I deliver the information now?]

Lee Sungmin stopped and nodded. Nebul materialized before him, holding a small crystal ball.

"This is information compiled and selected from the information guilds connected to Erebrisa," Nebul explained with a slight smirk. "I can vouch for its accuracy. The information is inside this."

The fee was a bit expensive, but he had not spent a single penny while living at Shaolin, so he had no trouble paying it. Once the payment was settled, Nebul handed him the crystal.

"How do I use it?"

"Try infusing it with your inner arts."

Lee Sungmin did as instructed, channeling his energy into the crystal ball. It immediately turned a dull gray as the information it contained flooded directly into his mind.

The first report was on Baek Sogo. She was making a name for herself as a vigilante, performing selfless good deeds for the powerless. She had been staying in the giant dao city of Ruvus for a week, apparently just traveling without a specific purpose.

While Baek Sogo was gaining fame as a vigilante, the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun was earning his as a duelist. He was traveling around, challenging famous martial artists and sects. Lee Sungmin's expression flickered with surprise as he absorbed the details.

*He's heading to Mount Hua?*

It was not a confirmed fact, merely a speculation by the information guilds based on Dok Bijun's movements. He was currently heading towards Dreamoor, and among the city's martial sects, only Mount Hua would likely interest him. Lee Sungmin thought he might meet Dok Bijun there. He had not been deeply involved with him on Mount Mushii, but he felt it would be nice to see the man with whom he had once trained.

*No. On second thought, Dok Bijun wouldn't know my face.*

When they had met on the mountain, Dok Bijun's eyes had been under a restriction.

After Dok Bijun, the information about Wiji Hoyeon was engraved in his memory. Lee Sungmin swallowed hard as he processed it.

*It's different from my past life.*

The Little Heavenly Demon had not been unknown in his previous life, but she had truly made her name after the incident in the Dungeon. Back then, the Dungeon had held not only Baek Sogo and Dok Bijun but also other renowned masters and highly skilled mages. Wiji Hoyeon had slaughtered them all, emerging alone after conquering it to reign as a monster revered as the Little Heavenly Demon.

However, that was not the 'present'. Unlike the past he remembered, Wiji Hoyeon was making a name for herself much earlier. He had heard as much from Namgung Heewon a year ago, but in the time since, she had become an even greater giant.

The crystal ball had also provided an image of her recent appearance, as vivid as if he were seeing her with his own eyes. Lee Sungmin trembled as he recalled the image now burned into his mind.

She had grown her hair long and was no longer dressed as a man. She wore a lavish fur coat over clothes that did not hide the curves of her body. A long, silk-like shawl draped over her shoulders, making her seem much larger than her actual frame.

The amount of information on Wiji Hoyeon was far greater than that on Baek Sogo and Dok Bijun. It was a testament to the huge storm her name was creating across Eria. The followers who had joined her path called themselves the Heavenly Demon Army and reportedly never left her side. Their number was small, only about twenty people, but it seemed they were all masters who had surpassed the peak realm. Because of this, despite their small numbers, many factions were keeping her in check.

The prestigious clans, centered around the Nine Sects One Gang and the Four Great Clans, were particularly wary. As orthodox sects, they did not look favorably upon the woman called the Little Heavenly Demon or the power gathering around her. Wiji Hoyeon had recently been spotted near the northern city of Gerard, and the information guilds had predicted her next move.

A clash with the Blood Heavenly Demon, Baek Museon.

The Blood Heavenly Demon was a monster stirring in the northernmost part of Eria, a land of perpetual snow where he held more authority than the nobility. The Little Heavenly Demon's path was now heading towards Triggia, the northern city that served as the headquarters for Baek Museon and his Blood Heavenly Alliance. The information guilds predicted that the two would clash, basing their forecast on the dispositions of two figures both called 'Heavenly Demon'.

*This is also different from my past life. Wiji Hoyeon and Baek Museon never clashed. Wiji Hoyeon was in the central region, and Baek Museon only came south after completely conquering the north…*

Why was everything flowing so differently? It was not just her movements. Her attire had changed. The fact that she had completely given up dressing as a man… did that mean her values had changed?

It bothered him, but he could not head north right now. He had an errand to run for Grandmaster Bulyeong, and he had to meet the God of Time. Besides, what could he even do if he went north now?

He had to do what he could. That was one thing he had learned for certain at Shaolin, and what he had to do now was head to Dreamoor. Lee Sungmin glanced down at the gray crystal ball, then looked up at Nebul.

"Are you satisfied with the information provided?"

"......"

"That crystal ball is single-use," Nebul said, bowing his head deeply. "It doesn't have much value, so it would be better to throw it away. I look forward to our next meeting."

Nebul vanished. Lee Sungmin tightened his grip on the crystal ball.

"......"

The cracked crystal shattered into pieces, falling to the ground. He took a deep breath, the image of Wiji Hoyeon still vivid in his mind.

He was fourteen when they parted. Now he was twenty-two. Had it really been eight years?

Even though he had not seen her in person, the Wiji Hoyeon he saw now had changed so much it was hard to imagine her younger self.

She was beautiful.

She had become beautiful.

*Though her chest didn't seem that big.*

Recalling the last thing she had ever said to him, Lee Sungmin let out a small, quiet laugh.
