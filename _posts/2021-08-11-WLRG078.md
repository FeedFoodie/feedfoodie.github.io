He looked at Shaolin, and he felt it.

Setting down his spear and shedding his armor, Lee Sungmin swung his worn-out weapon relentlessly. The martial arts of Jihak, who was reputed to be the future of Shaolin, seemed truly endless. Having learned from every monk in the temple and lived amidst miracle herbs, martial manuals, and profound enlightenment since childhood, Jihak was not just a monk. He was the living embodiment of Shaolin itself.

Lee Sungmin could easily understand why Grandmaster Bulyeong had entrusted his guidance to such a man. As the future of Shaolin, Jihak was capable of skillfully executing all Seventy-two Peerless Arts of the temple. This meant that against Sungmin, Jihak became a legion, playing the role of seventy-two different masters wielding seventy-two different styles.

Moreover, Jihak was a skilled teacher, a stark contrast to the two masters who had guided him before. Wiji Hoyeon and Baek Sogo, to be frank, had not been excellent instructors. Wiji Hoyeon, a calamity of immense talent, could instinctively modify techniques to fit Sungmin's body, yet she could not properly teach him how. All she could truly offer was a patch for the gaps in his foundational knowledge and a guide to the basics of the spear.

It had been the same with Baek Sogo. He had learned the Dancing Shadow Soul Break from her, but the technique felt like an ill-fitting suit on him, whereas on her it was perfectly tailored. The restriction of 'heaviness' from Mount Mushii was partly to blame, making it naturally difficult to master movement arts when his body felt so leaden. Adding to that, Baek Sogo's own restrictions on her voice and Thought Transmission made the process of teaching incredibly inconvenient.

Jihak, however, was different. Perhaps because he had been educated by every monk in Shaolin since childhood, Jihak knew how to impart knowledge as well as he wielded it. The only problem was the vast chasm between Jihak's instructed insight and Sungmin's own, but even that was a gap Jihak eventually learned to bridge.

"The only way to make up for a lack of experience," Jihak had said, "is to gain a lot of it."

It was said that seeing something once was better than hearing of it a hundred times, and Jihak took that principle to heart. He did not just show Sungmin. He dueled him, day after day, relentlessly, until Sungmin collapsed from sheer exhaustion. With every bout, Jihak changed his martial art.

How weak, insignificant, and lacking he was. Lee Sungmin felt it keenly as he sank to the ground each day. Jihak was a true calamity. When Sungmin lay gasping for breath, Jihak's own breathing remained perfectly steady. The techniques Sungmin executed with all his might posed no threat, while the attacks Jihak swung with casual ease pierced every one of his blind spots.

"Broaden your vision. See more," Jihak instructed. "Before you try to understand the big picture of martial arts, you must first understand your opponent. Martial arts are ultimately performed with the body. No matter the feints or illusions, as long as they are executed with a body and a weapon, they cannot escape that fact. A master of handling ki might launch attacks invisible to the naked eye, but even so, as long as the body and a weapon are used, there are limits to movement."

Swords, sabers, and spears were straight-line weapons. The way one used their joints and muscles, the way they swung, created openings and realities. By mixing ki into it, one created speed and weight, drew afterimages, and produced feints and illusions.

"Before trying to see the unseen, you must see what is visible more clearly. Your senses are strangely tangled. Your non-existent sixth sense is sharp, but your five senses are dull. The penance you endured led you to a high state, but it also caused you to stagnate. You learned to ride a horse and gallop before you even learned to walk."

Jihak's critique was scathing. The penance on Mount Mushii had made Lee Sungmin a peak master, but that same training, which had blocked his senses, had also ruined them. With his senses blocked, his sixth sense had sharpened, and he had come to rely on it. In exchange, his sight had narrowed, his hearing had dimmed, and his sense of smell had dulled.

"Lee Sungmin. You are not using everything you have."

He needed to sharpen his dulled senses. Lee Sungmin was aware of this need, but he had not been able to do it properly until now. No, he corrected himself. He *hadn't done it*.

"The Spear Technique you've learned is flawless enough to be called a peerless martial art," Jihak continued. "Sweep, parry, and thrust are ultimately just the basics. If you could defeat advanced martial arts with basics alone, why would martial arts even exist? The basics are nothing more than an aid to help you learn martial arts better."

Sweep, parry, and thrust were the basics Lee Sungmin practiced every day. From a certain point on, he had focused more on practicing them than on the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear. That, too, had been an act of escapism. Lee Sungmin felt anxious that his proficiency with the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear was no longer improving. He felt that his own aptitude was far too lacking to master such an incredible martial art.

So he ran away, retreating to the familiar and easy Rannachal rather than confronting the difficult-to-learn Supreme Nine Heavens Spear.

Namgung Heewon had told him that his basics were at the level of a master, but his other Spear Technique was lacking. He had attributed it to a lack of experience. Jihak had made the exact same assessment, but by continuously dueling with him, he had identified the true shortcomings.

"Why did you run away?" Jihak asked. "While learning such an outstanding Spear Technique, why did you insist on the basics?"

"...Because I was lacking."

"So you gave up on learning it?" Jihak's face was cold as he said this, his usual kindness nowhere to be seen. "The idea of becoming a peerless swordsman by mastering only the Three Extremes Sword Technique is something people who have only learned the Three Extremes Sword Technique say in their dreams."

He continued, "I'm not criticizing those who have only learned the Three Extremes Sword Technique as foolish. Do you think they learned only that because they wanted to? They probably wanted to learn other advanced martial arts but couldn't. Martial arts, especially the more outstanding ones, are difficult to learn without a miraculous encounter. Yes, I'm saying you can't learn them without luck. Wouldn't those who couldn't learn advanced martial arts... feel a thirst to learn them?"

He knew. He knew how terrible that thirst was. In his past life, Lee Sungmin had learned a second-rate martial art and had always wanted a more superior one, but he couldn't get one. He had no luck.

"Lee Sungmin. You have learned outstanding martial arts through repeated miraculous encounters. You've been learning martial arts that most people spend their entire lives searching for since you were young, and yet, you gave up on learning them and ran away, claiming you were lacking. Why... didn't you strive to improve?"

Lee Sungmin could not answer. Seeing his face drenched in gloom, Jihak let out a sigh.

"...I'll be blunt. Lee Sungmin. You are not a calamity."

He knew.

"Your insight isn't exceptional either. You're not a fast learner. However, that doesn't mean you should give up easily."

He knew.

"I don't know what your goal is. However... I, Lee Sungmin, believe that if you've been given opportunities others would die for, you should put in as much effort as they would in their place."

"...I know." Lee Sungmin sighed and nodded his head. "I know that I've been running away until now."

He ran away despite getting the chance he so desperately wanted, time and again, believing he was lacking.

"I don't want to run away anymore," Lee Sungmin said.

At his words, Jihak's expression softened. He brought his palms together and bowed his head.

"The path of martial arts is long and endless. If you wish to become a martial artist by mastering martial arts, and if you wish to see the end of that path, you must constantly strive and move forward. You must not stop at knowing your shortcomings, but acknowledge and overcome them. You may Despair, but you must not be swallowed by Despair. If you cannot fly, you must run, and if you cannot run, you must walk. If you cannot walk, you must crawl, and if you cannot even crawl, you must desire to crawl. It is important to have that desire and act on it."

"Are you flying?"

"The world is vast. I thought I was flying, but after meeting the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon, I came to understand what a person who truly has wings and flies is like. I am not flying. However, I desire to fly, and so I try to."

*What about me?*

*What does the larva in the ground desire? Does it desire to crawl out of the cold earth? I want to walk rather than squirm and crawl. I want to run rather than walk. I want to have wings and fly rather than run.*

The training at Shaolin was less arduous than the training at Mushii. There were no restrictions here and no rewards for restrictions and trials. However, Jihak was here. Jihak, a man with calamitous talent incomparable to Lee Sungmin's, was here to guide him in martial arts.

For most of his life until now, Lee Sungmin had practiced martial arts alone. It was narrow. The world that the solitary Lee Sungmin had seen was far too narrow. On Mount Mushii, he felt the world change, but the size of the world had not. After coming down from Mount Mushii, Lee Sungmin felt repeated defeat and Despair. He came to realize how vast the world was.

He looked at Shaolin. He felt Shaolin. He was lacking. Lee Sungmin stood blankly, watching the breaking dawn. The brightness of the morning sun rising from the edge of the mountain wet Lee Sungmin's eyes, and the cold dawn air froze his chest. What he needed to see and feel was not Shaolin. What he truly needed to see and feel was the world, not Shaolin.

The world is large, and I am small. Just as a larva in the ground knows only the pitch-black, cold darkness as its entire world, so too had Lee Sungmin. *I don't want to be a larva. I don't want to be a cicada either. I don't want to know this narrow view as the whole world.*

*I want to fly. I dream of flying, even without wings. I dream of flying, even though I've never flown before.* Lee Sungmin took a deep breath as he watched the dawn. *I want to try flying... through that vast world. I, small and insignificant, want to see the world.*

The seasons change.

His daily routine did not change. Lee Sungmin dueled, dueled, and dueled with Jihak. At some point, Jihak's teachings became actions rather than words. The martial arts they exchanged instead of conversation spoke to each other. *Are you changing? I am changing. Do you want to walk? I want to fly. What do you want to become?*

*Anything.*

When he turned nineteen from eighteen, Grandmaster Bulyeong emerged from the cave. It had been a little over half a year since Lee Sungmin had started living at the cave's entrance. In all that time, he had never seen Grandmaster Bulyeong come out.

"My boy. You've changed."

The Grandmaster Bulyeong who came out of the cave looked no different than he had half a year ago. He still looked more like an ancient Yokai than a devout high monk. Seeing the smile that crinkled his face like a mischievous child, Lee Sungmin bowed his head deeply.

"Is the frog in the well trying to leave it? With your martial arts, you could live happily inside that well. What is it you desire?"

"I want to see the world."

"Kekeke! Anyone can see the world. Yes. Anyone can see." Grandmaster Bulyeong said, clasping his hands behind his back. "If you are not satisfied with just seeing, and wish to discuss the world, you will have to become a giant. Do you want to become a giant?"

"I want to fly."

"Kekeke! Your ambition is high. You don't want to become a giant and look down, but to have wings and place the world beneath you? You are far too small to speak of such things."

"I know." Lee Sungmin smiled bitterly and nodded his head. "However, I want to dream."

"Will you leave your dream as just a dream?"

"I don't want to."

"For the past half-year, what have you done?" Grandmaster Bulyeong asked.

Lee Sungmin closed his eyes for a moment. Everything he had done for the past half-year flashed through his mind.

"I learned to crawl."

"Good." Grandmaster Bulyeong nodded and laughed. "Now, learn to walk."

With those words, Grandmaster Bulyeong went back inside the cave. After watching his back, Lee Sungmin turned around.

The dawn was growing brighter.
