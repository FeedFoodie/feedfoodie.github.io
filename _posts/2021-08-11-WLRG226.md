A woman stood beside a pond where koi of various colors swam about. She watched them gather as she sprinkled feed from her palm onto the water's surface, humming a wordless tune. Her eyes were a murky, clouded white. She was blind, unable to see a thing, but feeding the fish was a task that hardly required sight.

"You look well."

The woman's shoulders flinched at the words muttered from behind her, though she did not bother to turn around. After all, what was the point when her eyes could see nothing? Instead, she poured the last of the fish feed from her palm into the pond.

"You've come out?" she asked.

"Didn't I tell you it was time?" the Martial God replied to the Spirit Medium's question.

He wore loose, comfortable-looking gray martial arts robes, his white hair unkempt and wild. For a man who had lived hundreds of years, the only remaining signs of his age were that white hair and his deep-set eyes. He looked at the Spirit Medium's back with an indifferent expression. After nearly a hundred years of seclusion, the scent of the outside air for the first time in ages stirred no great emotion within him. He felt no satisfaction either, despite having completed his training.

"I heard two of them died," the Spirit Medium said.

"Fist Honored One and the Sword Saint are dead."

"So it came to that in the end. Was it unavoidable?"

"I foresaw the death of Fist Honored One," she admitted, "but I did not see the death of the Sword Saint."

The Martial God was not interrogating her but asking out of genuine curiosity. "That... is something I do not know either."

The Spirit Medium sighed and turned around completely, her murky, lightless eyes facing him. "My foresight is performed through a Spirit. If the Spirit doesn't perform the possession or doesn't show me... I can't foresee it."

"I see." The Martial God nodded. The power of foresight was not perfect, and an absolute nemesis to it did not exist in this world. Many shamans, mages, and astrologers caught fragmented glimpses of the future. In all of Eria, however, no one other than the Spirit Medium could continuously foresee a definite future as she could.

"Are you certain?" he asked.

"I don't know how to tell a lie."

"Perhaps that very statement is a lie."

The Martial God chuckled at her reply. He was not doubting her, just being mischievous. The Spirit Medium cleared her throat and shook her head.

"The fates have begun to move," she whispered, her voice low. "The energies of countless fates and Constellations are gathering around the Little Heavenly Demon."

"It would've been better if we had secured her beforehand."

"If we had, there would have been no meaning in securing her. It's because we left the Little Heavenly Demon alone until now that the energies of fate and the Constellations have gathered around her."

"The Spirit's will, what the Spirit saw, and what it knew," the Martial God muttered. "This one, who is still human, cannot know such things. What about the Phantom Spear?"

"His whereabouts haven't been confirmed, but I surmise he's made contact with the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman and is with him."

"That Ilcheon is truly an unpredictable fellow." The Martial God frowned at the thought of his old rival, Demon Emperor Yang Ilcheon. The level he had reached before his seclusion was so high that even after nearly a hundred years of training, he had not advanced much. Thanks to that, he still wasn't confident he could win a life-or-death battle against the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman.

"Did the Spirit say nothing about the Phantom Spear?"

"No."

Heaven Beyond Heaven had several opportunities to kill Lee Sungmin. Even when he had gone to the South, beyond the Murim Alliance's reach, their power was not limited to that single faction. Had they sent just two of the Six Honored Ones, they could have killed the Lee Sungmin of that time, but they did not kill him. The Martial God had ordered it so, and the Spirit Medium had agreed.

It had all started in Ruvus. Originally, the Spirit had told them to secure Wiji Hoyeon in advance, and Heaven Beyond Heaven acted according to its will. Thinking it was better for her to come somewhat voluntarily rather than by force, they sent Fist Honored One to place a curse on her, making her weak. The goal was for the weakened Wiji Hoyeon to despair over her situation, crave greater strength, and throw herself into their arms.

However, the Spirit changed its will in Ruvus. To be precise, it changed when the Dark Supreme tried to kill the Phantom Spear. At first, they thought the Spirit had changed its mind because of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's presence, but the more they considered it, the more suspicious the Phantom Spear seemed. What had blocked the Dark Supreme at the time was a clone of the Chairman. If the Dark Supreme had risked his life, he could have defeated the clone and secured the Little Heavenly Demon. In that process, the Phantom Spear, who was struggling desperately to stop them, would have died.

*But the Spirit said nothing about the Phantom Spear.*

Although the Spirit remained silent, the Martial God ordered them not to kill the Phantom Spear for the time being. Still, just in case, they had the Black Dragon Knight, who had become the head of the Murim Alliance, frame the Phantom Spear as a demonic practitioner to restrict his actions.

"Where is the Little Heavenly Demon?" the Martial God asked.

"Her location has not been confirmed either," the Spirit Medium replied, bowing her head.

At her words, the Martial God let out a hearty laugh and nodded. "The Black Dragon Knight is serving as the Murim Alliance Chairman, so it'll be difficult for him to move, and the Moon Queen doesn't listen to this one very well. Right. How about the Spear King?"

"The Spear King is more interested in the Phantom Spear than the Little Heavenly Demon."

"Is that so? Then tell him to do as he pleases. This one will look for the Little Heavenly Demon myself."

The Spirit Medium's sightless eyes widened, revealing her emotion at his words, but she voiced no opposition. It was impossible to predict how much stronger the Little Heavenly Demon had become. If they carelessly sent Blade Supreme or the Dark Supreme, they might be killed instead. Sending the Spear King or the Moon Queen would surely allow them to subdue her, but they were special even among the Six Honored Ones. The Martial God knew this and was making some concessions for them.

"In that case, I will tell the Spear King to do as he pleases," she said. "But do you intend to kill the Phantom Spear?"

"That, too, I will leave to the Spear King's will," the Martial God said, turning his body. "That fellow, the Phantom Spear. I've never met him, but he's a very strange one. Didn't the Spirit change its will because of his life? And this is just my thought, but it seems the Spirit wanted the Sword Saint to die by his hand. Or perhaps, Spirit Medium, was it you who wished for it?"

The Spirit Medium shook her head. "How could I possibly do such a thing?"

"We sent the Sword Saint to prevent Fist Honored One's death, yet the Sword Saint also died at the hands of the Phantom Spear. It was a death that could have been stopped, no, that could have been avoided. I'm not doubting you, but the situation is unfortunate."

The Spirit Medium bowed her head deeply at his muttering. "I, too, feel regret over their deaths."

The Martial God did not reply, instead laughing heartily. His body vanished before her, becoming like the wind. Though she could not see him disappear, she felt his presence vanish and let out a sigh. She, too, did not fully understand the Spirit's will.

The world was not an easy place. Namgung Heewon knew this well, even before leaving his Noble Clan for the Murim Alliance. He understood the great gap between what was seen and what was hidden, that the truth most people believed might be a lie, buried and incited by the words of those at the top.

He was neither stubborn nor foolish. He knew that if he went to the Murim Alliance empty-handed and questioned the Chairman about the Jaegal couple's death, or rather, Moyong Seojin's death, there was no way the Chairman would answer honestly. It would be impossible to even meet him in the first place.

Namgung Heewon was also aware of what had happened at his own clan. As a member of Erebrisa, he could not have failed to hear about the incident, which had happened so openly that anyone with ears knew the story. There was no need to seek information through the organization. He could walk into any tavern and hear people chattering about it. Over a year had passed since the Namgung Clan suffered at the hands of an unidentified master, a C-Rank Mercenary who had appeared out of nowhere.

At his travel speed, he should have arrived at the Murim Alliance long ago, but he had not yet gone there. He understood reality and could not let himself be swept away by hot-blooded passion. The suspicion that the Murim Alliance was involved in Moyong Seojin's death was a certainty in his mind. If that were true, the Alliance could not be called the sympathy group the public perceived it to be. If he went to such a place and rebuked them for their hypocrisy and lies, he would not meet a good end.

He knew all this. So, what should he do?

After leaving the Namgung Clan, Namgung Heewon headed not for the Murim Alliance, but for Bredon, where Shaolin was located. Shaolin's Grandmaster Bulyeong was one of the few people he could trust. The Grandmaster did not turn away Namgung Heewon when he arrived late at night, avoiding the eyes of Shaolin, and took him in. Since then, Namgung Heewon had been living at the Grandmaster's residence, meditating more than he swung his sword. It was there he met Jihak, known as the future of Shaolin, for the first time.

When he spoke of Moyong Seojin's death, Grandmaster Bulyeong was not greatly surprised.

"I thought as much," the Grandmaster had said. He would often chuckle when the Phantom Spear was mentioned. Knowing that Lee Sungmin had once trained at Shaolin, both he and Jihak were certain that Lee Sungmin had not committed such an act.

"The death of the Iron Armored Divine Spear is also quite suspicious," Grandmaster Bulyeong would sometimes say while rolling his Flame Curse. "The same goes for the deaths of Seojin and Taeryeong. The Murim Alliance of today is strange. Very strange, but we can't openly express our dissatisfaction. If you rebuke a hypocrite for their hypocrisy, do you think they'll say, 'I'm sorry, I won't do it again'? Heh heh! It's not just the Murim Alliance or the Black Dragon Knight. How many among the current Noble Clans and sects do you think are not hypocrites?"

Namgung Heewon knew. He clearly remembered how his own father, the once spirited Namgung Patriarch, had changed as he aged, trading his youth for the shrewdness of calculating gains and losses.

A year after entering the Grandmaster's residence, Namgung Heewon and Jihak left to carry out an errand. 'There's nothing left to teach you, so why are you here wasting food?' Grandmaster Bulyeong had said as he kicked them out. He had not just kicked them out, though. Jihak fumbled for the letter from the Grandmaster tucked inside his robes and rolled his Flame Curse.

"Have you ever been to Wudang?" Jihak asked.

"No," Namgung Heewon replied, shaking his head. "But I've heard many rumors about Wudang, and even more about the Sword Immortal."

Jihak gave a bitter smile at his mutter. The two of them were on their way to meet the Sword Immortal, carrying Grandmaster Bulyeong's letter. They did not know what was written inside, but since the Grandmaster had sent both of them just to deliver one letter, it had to be important.

"And about Cheongmyeong, too," Namgung Heewon added.

At his words, Jihak stopped rolling his Flame Curse. Chwi Geol of the Beggars' Gang, Jihak of Shaolin, and Cheongmyeong of Wudang were the three most outstanding prodigies of the Nine Sects One Gang. Among them, Chwi Geol's fame was not as great as it once was, as he had lost his left arm in a Dungeon, but the reputations of Jihak and Cheongmyeong had not diminished. While Chwi Geol, the Young Sect Leader of the Beggars' Gang, often appeared in public, Cheongmyeong and Jihak had never shown themselves in such settings. Cheongmyeong, in particular, was a figure shrouded in mystery, known only as the disciple of Wudang's greatest master, the beastfolk Sword Immortal.

Namgung Heewon glanced at Jihak, who had started fiddling with his Flame Curse again. "Have you ever met Cheongmyeong?"

"I haven't. So, to be honest, I'm looking forward to it. I wonder just how outstanding the person called the future of Wudang is."

"Aren't you also called the future of Shaolin?"

"It's a heavy title," Jihak said with a bitter smile. "Indeed. I have learned all of Shaolin's martial arts, but I'm not sure if that's enough to be called its future."

Namgung Heewon nodded as he listened. Jihak was strong, a fact he readily acknowledged. While living at the Grandmaster's residence, he had dueled with Jihak several times but had never once managed to win.

"When Lee Sungmin was at Shaolin, he never once gained the upper hand against me," Jihak continued. "But, Namgung Heewon, didn't you say it yourself? That if I were to fight him now, I would surely lose."

"That's right."

"It's not that I'm offended. It's just, how can I be satisfied with being called the future of Shaolin when that's all I amount to? If I am truly the future of Shaolin, then I must not lose to anyone." Jihak's words were filled with pride for Shaolin's martial arts. That was why he called the title heavy. As long as he accepted it, he could never lose to anyone else.

"Cheongmyeong... Cheongmyeong," Namgung Heewon muttered, his thoughts turning to Lee Sungmin. He remembered the man he had seen in the Bewitching Forest and the one in the square. Honestly, no matter how outstanding Cheongmyeong's martial arts theory was, Namgung Heewon could not imagine him being superior to Lee Sungmin.

At that moment, Lee Sungmin was passing through the gates of Odun.
