He felt no pain as the White Fang plunged into his chest, piercing through his body without any resistance, yet the strength still drained from his legs.

"No," Slaughter Predator cried out from a distance.

CRACK, CRACKLE.

White cracks spiderwebbed across the pitch-black space. Like a shattered window, the world broke into pieces and crumbled around him.

"You… foolish boy…"

Pinned beneath the Void Monarch, Slaughter Predator's struggles ceased. He knew it was over the moment Lee Sungmin drove the White Fang into his own chest. There was nothing he could do now.

"Do you even know what you've done?" he muttered, his voice hollow.

"I don't know." Slumped on the ground, Lee Sungmin could only watch his world fall apart.

This place was his subconscious. Did its collapse signify the end of his mind? He had no idea. Still, he knew one thing for certain.

Slaughter Predator had given up his rampage.

"The White Fang… the mistletoe," Slaughter Predator began. "It is an absolute spear that ignores all resistance, pierces, and destroys." He let out a dry chuckle. "Though its shape is more fitting for an awl than a spear."

He explained how it was an object, like the Grimoire and Gries, unbound by the laws of this world. The Spirit had given it to the Martial God, whose role was to use the mistletoe to kill Wiji Hoyeon, the Demonic Spirit's refined Absolute Being.

However, fate, as Slaughter Predator had said, was a great current, one that could not perfectly control every variable or human choice. The Martial God had named the mistletoe 'White Fang' and, as a sign of affection and trust, entrusted it to his loyal confidant, the Dark Supreme, and then the Dark Supreme had been killed by Lee Sungmin, placing the White Fang into his hands.

"You've committed suicide," Slaughter Predator spat. "I don't know why the White Fang is in your hands now. Perhaps… the Demonic Spirit intervened, and you, like a fool, committed this act without even knowing what it meant."

"I had to stop you."

The White Fang, embedded in Lee Sungmin's chest, dissolved into dust and scattered.

"Stop me? What's the point in that? Don't you get it? I *am* you. You *are* Slaughter Predator. From the moment you were reincarnated and returned to this world, you were destined to become me."

He knew that, ordinarily, his consciousness would have been completely annihilated. The moment he awakened as Slaughter Predator, his original self should have vanished, allowing the new entity to take over his body, but the repeated variables had prevented that. Lee Sungmin's consciousness had not vanished.

"You said there are no coincidences," Lee Sungmin countered, even if this wasn't the original fate.

"I… I chose for myself. Because I didn't disappear. I… don't want to become The End. I don't want to become Slaughter Predator."

"So you kill yourself?" Slaughter Predator sneered, his body beginning to slowly turn to dust. "Haha… what meaning is there in your own death?"

"It's better that I end it myself than become something that isn't me," Lee Sungmin answered.

Slaughter Predator's sneer only widened. "The Demonic Spirit succeeded, to some extent," he muttered. "The repeated variables made your consciousness too strong. At the very least, he succeeded in blocking Slaughter Predator, but what about next time? And the time after that?"

As he scattered into dust, his voice echoed. "The calamities of The End will continue to come. In this world full of variables, even if Slaughter Predator, a calamity that was bound to appear, has vanished, another will emerge to replace him. Soon, the Spirit Queen will awaken."

A sneer was laced in his final words. "And the Vampire Queen will begin to make her move in earnest. In her hands is a Chimera with the potential to become the second Slaughter Predator. Do you really think Wiji Hoyeon can stop all of that?"

"Haha, hahaha," his sneer grew louder. "What you did was nothing but a desperate struggle."

With that, Slaughter Predator's laughter was heard no more.

The Void Monarch slowly got to his feet, a bitter expression on his face as he approached Lee Sungmin. Through Slaughter Predator's cries, he too understood what had happened. He looked at the small hole in Lee Sungmin's chest and shook his head.

"I'm sorry."

"What do you have to be sorry for?"

"Because I couldn't stop you."

"No," Lee Sungmin said. "You helped me enough. If you hadn't been holding him back, I couldn't have done anything."

The Void Monarch gave a bitter smile at those words as the world collapsed completely around them. He moved as if to help Lee Sungmin up, but then slumped down beside him instead. He figured there was no point. Lee Sungmin had committed spiritual suicide and was already breaking down.

"You're not leaving?" Lee Sungmin asked him.

"Leave? Where would I go?"

"Slaughter Predator said it, didn't he? You could leave me and go to the world my Master went to. You could have walked that path anytime you wished."

"Yeah. I first saw that path in Urmuri, and I knew that as I am now, I could cross it in an instant. Even now, I can go if I just decide to."

"Then why don't you go?"

"What's the rush?" the Void Monarch grumbled, scratching the back of his head. He felt so shitty he could have gotten wasted, but there was no alcohol here. His gourd was in the outside world, after all.

Lee Sungmin gave a faint smile as he looked at the Void Monarch slumped beside him. "If you're not in a rush, take your time before you go."

"I'll go when you disappear," the Void Monarch grumbled.

Indeed, just as Slaughter Predator's had, Lee Sungmin's body was turning to dust and scattering into the void. He looked down at his own fading form.

"Even after I disappear, wait a little while before you go."

"……"

"Because there's something I'm hoping for, just in case."

Stabbing himself with the White Fang had been a reckless act, but after hearing Slaughter Predator's cries, a certain possibility had occurred to him. If things went as he hoped…

*If that happens.*

"You son of a bitch. You're leaving without explaining properly?"

He had wanted to disappear leaving a lingering impression, but the Void Monarch's eyes blazed as he snapped at him.

"You're telling me to wait for who knows what before I go? Until when? Why? You fucking bastard, at least say it properly. Are you taking it out on me because all those other bastards have been vague and never told you anything straight?"

"Ah, come on." Lee Sungmin let out a deep sigh at the outburst. Fortunately, his body hadn't completely disappeared yet.

"During Denir's mental training," he explained, "my mind collapsed and died several times, but after some time passed, my mind always recovered and became fine again."

"Ah." The Void Monarch's eyes widened.

"I'm not sure," Lee Sungmin admitted. "I don't know if my mind can recover like that this time, but… just in case. If I do regain my senses… I think it would be very lonely if you didn't talk to me."

"Don't say such cringey things."

"No, I'm serious."

"Damn bastard," the Void Monarch grumbled. He stretched his legs out and then leaned his upper body back, flopping onto the ground. "I'll just take a nap then."

Lee Sungmin gave a faint smile at his words.

"If you take too long, I'm just gonna leave."

"I'll try."

With that, Lee Sungmin's body turned to dust and scattered completely.

The Void Monarch clicked his tongue as he looked up at the scattering dust and the crumbling world.

"Whatever. I've been fine on my own for hundreds of years," he grumbled to himself and closed his eyes.

"You've returned," the Spirit Medium said.

She slowly turned her head toward the Martial God, who was thinner, more haggard, and dirtier than when he had left. Though she could not see his shabby appearance, he felt a renewed sense of shame and gripped his empty left sleeve with his right hand.

Several months had passed since the incident in the North. He could have returned sooner if he had wanted to, but the Martial God had wandered the world without rushing, forgetting to eat, drink, or even wash. The Martial God of now was so filthy he was hardly different from a beggar.

That was how deeply the events in the North had shocked him. The fact that the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, a man he once considered his rival, had obtained a level of martial prowess utterly incomparable to his own. When they had last met, their skills had been so evenly matched that neither could gain the upper hand. The man he met again had become a wall, a mountain he could never overcome. No, he was something higher, more solid, and more immense than that. The divine power the Chairman had shown at the end was something the Martial God could not comprehend, no matter how much he thought about it.

"Have you achieved what you desired?"

"Did the Spirit not tell you?" the Martial God asked in return.

GRIP.

He clutched his left sleeve, a throbbing pain echoing from the arm that was no longer there, violently torn from his body. He had been feeling that phantom pain for months.

"He did. He said that the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, one of the greatest calamities of The End, has fallen. However, Martial God, this world has not yet completely escaped The End."

"I know," the Martial God spat, dragging his feet to stand beside her. He bit his lower lip hard and looked down at the small pond where koi were swimming.

"However… Heaven Beyond Heaven has lost its power. The Fist Honored One, the Sword Saint, and the Dark Supreme are dead. The Spear King and the Black Dragon Knight betrayed us." He had been unable to kill those two. They had fled at a crucial moment, and he had been too exhausted to pursue them. "And… the Moon Queen. Your sister is dead too."

"No." To the words the Martial God had forced out, the Spirit Medium smiled gently and shook her head. "My sister is not dead."

"…What do you mean?" The Martial God's face was a mask of incomprehension. The Moon Queen was definitely dead. He had watched with his own eyes as her body was blown apart by the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, completely shattered into pieces. "The Moon Queen is dead."

"She is not dead. No, she may have died in that spot, but my sister, who received the Spirit's grace, has been resurrected."

"What nonsense…"

Even for the Martial God, her words were impossible to understand. The Spirit Medium, however, didn't seem to care whether he understood or not. He shook his head as he looked at the inscrutable smile on her face. It was something he could not possibly comprehend with his common sense, but that same common sense had already been shattered to pieces in the North.

"Now… what must I do?" the Martial God asked, his voice dazed. "I sought to meet the Demonic Spirit and find an answer about The End, but I did not receive a proper one. I killed the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, one of the calamities of The End. Now… what else must I do? The End is not yet over. To save this world… what must I do now?"

"Wait."

The Spirit Medium's closed eyes opened. Her two eyes emitted a brilliant light, as if they contained all the radiant light in the world. Even with such beautiful and bright eyes, she could see nothing, yet, they held a magical power that could sway one's heart just by meeting their gaze.

"The Spirit has told me to wait. The time is not yet ripe. Martial God, you are not ready either."

"Ready."

The Martial God gripped his sleeve. He knew what she meant. The fight with the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman had broadened his world. He had thought he had reached the pinnacle for a human… but he was wrong. There was still further to go. The Martial God slowly nodded and turned around.

*The End.*

He released the sleeve he was holding. His desire to stop The End was genuine.
