The surge in his inner arts was undeniable. Since receiving a fifth of Wiji Hoyeon's power, Lee Sungmin could now race from Jack's Inn to the North Gate without a single pause, his movement arts flowing effortlessly.

He had finally reached the level of a Second-Rate martial artist. Wiji Hoyeon had filled the void of his lacking inner arts, and with the Violet Dawn Divine Art, he could finally look toward the 'future'. Though the heights he could reach with his limited talent remained uncertain, he now possessed something he had long been without. Hope.

The Violet Dawn Divine Art was not the only prize on the horizon. Once Wiji Hoyeon finished refining and polishing the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, he would inherit that as well. With two divine arts under his belt, he could dare to aim for the title of 'absolute master', yet, he knew such a goal was not guaranteed. That was a realm reserved for a calamity among calamities.

*It's better than my past life.*

The thought was a constant comfort. In his previous life, ten years of practicing a Second-Rate martial art had left him a mere C-Rank Mercenary, a Second-Rate warrior until the very end. This life, however, was brimming with hope. His nonexistent talent remained a hindrance, but he now had a solid foundation for growth. The potential of a Classless, combined with the correction from his low-grade Martial Skeleton, guaranteed he would reach a certain level of strength.

"Young Master."

The voice stopped him just as he was about to pass through the North Gate and into the forest for his usual training. Lee Sungmin paused, turning to find a neatly dressed young man watching him. He tilted his head, his eyes quickly scanning the man's martial arts robes and the sword hanging at his waist. He was clearly a martial artist, a fact his formal manner of speech only confirmed.

"Young Master, you're a martial artist, aren't you?" the young man asked directly, showing a clear understanding of the world.

Lee Sungmin considered the question for a moment. He was Classless. Though he practiced martial arts, his job section on the status window remained unchanged. To add a job, he would need to join a place like a clan or a Guild, neither of which he had done. It made no sense for a Classless to wield such a considerable amount of inner arts, a fact Wiji Hoyeon had already pointed out. He would have to be careful.

So, he lied.

"Oh, so it's true," the young man's face lit up. "I've been watching you. Your movement arts seemed quite skillful. If you don't mind my asking, which clan did you study under?"

The question was a natural compliment that also served to probe for information. Lee Sungmin shook his head.

"I can't tell you that," he replied, his expression intentionally stiff as he gave the man a suspicious glance. "Even if this isn't the Central Plains Murim, I don't wish to carelessly reveal the clan I studied under."

At his words, the young man nodded in understanding. "I see. It seems I've asked too rude a question." He cupped his fist in his other hand in a formal gesture. "My name is Woon Heeryong. Back when I was in the Central Plains, I was known by the title Broken Wave Sword."

"…I am Lee Sungmin. I don't have a title."

"Is that so? For your age, your martial arts are quite excellent…"

"Because I've never left my family…" Lee Sungmin trailed off, cutting the line of questioning short. His experiences from his past life allowed him to imitate a martial artist to a degree, but he knew his cover would be blown if the conversation went too deep. "By the way, Sir Broken Wave Sword, for what reason did you…?"

"Haha! Sir? I'm not great enough to be called that. Just calling me Broken Wave Sword is enough." Woon Heeryong smiled, clearly pleased by the flattery. Few people disliked such courtesies. "It's nothing much, but I approached you because I was hoping to receive your help, if you're willing."

"My help?"

"Hmm, well…" Woon Heeryong stroked his chin, organizing his thoughts for a moment before continuing.

After some idle chatter, he laid out his proposal. He and his companions were planning to subjugate an Orc settlement and were in the middle of recruiting helpful comrades.

"With your level of skill, Young Master, I believe you'd be a great help. That's why I'm asking." Passion filled Woon Heeryong's voice as he saw Lee Sungmin's interest. "Orcs. If we subjugate the settlement of those walking pig-like bastards, we stand to gain a lot. One of my companions is registered with the Mercenary Guild, so this is an official request. They've even promised a large Subspace Pocket so we can collect plenty of spoils. On top of that, we'll get a reward for succeeding, which will also be distributed fairly."

An Orc settlement subjugation request posted by the Mercenary Guild. The promise of a large Subspace Pocket meant the Guild in Zenavis was putting considerable effort into this.

"How many people are there?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"Three, including me. We also have some minor porters. If you lend us your strength, Young Master, that'll make four."

"Are they all martial artists?"

"Three of us are, and one is a mage."

"The porters are…?"

"Classless," Woon Heeryong said with an indifferent expression. "They won't be of much help in a fight. However, they should be quite useful as porters."

His assessment seemed callous, but it was accurate.

"…Hmm…"

Lee Sungmin contemplated the offer. Subjugating an Orc settlement was something he would never attempt alone, but with three martial artists and a mage, the odds of success were high enough. The Orcs of Zenavis were not that strong. While Orcs in other regions had powerful individuals who could match Third or even Second-Rate martial artists, the ones here were different. They were physically stronger than humans, but they were no match for a warrior professionally trained to kill.

And a mage in the mix changed the calculation entirely.

Lee Sungmin had never learned magic, but he knew its power. Even a low-level mage could unleash unimaginable destruction if given enough time and the right conditions. With three martial artists holding the front line, a mage using their power properly could wipe out an entire settlement without much difficulty.

"Can I hear about the reward?"

"The Mercenary Guild has promised two million Eru as the reward for this job."

Having been a mercenary in his past life, Lee Sungmin was familiar with the Guild's nature. Every city had one, but the branch in Zenavis was incomparably weak. It couldn't be helped. The nearby monsters were weak, and most Otherworlders who arrived in Zenavis didn't stay long. The ones who remained for years were mostly the Classless. As a result, the Guild barely functioned, yet that same Guild had posted a two million Eru reward. It might not seem like much for risking one's life, but that was just how the Mercenary Guild operated. Their rewards were always stingy, absurdly so when considering the price of a life.

*Still, if we sell the spoils…*

He didn't know how much of the Orcs' equipment would be salvageable, but like Goblins, Orcs were valuable as magic ingredients. Selling the spoils from an entire settlement could probably fetch at least 1.5 million Eru, even after the Guild's commission. A total of 3.5 million Eru. He wasn't sure about the distribution, but he would get at least 500,000 Eru.

"…Do you know the distribution ratio?"

"We'll divide it by the number of people," Woon Heeryong replied.

Lee Sungmin fell silent again. 500,000 Eru. It was difficult for a Classless to encounter a request this substantial in Zenavis. What should he do? There was risk, entirely centered on whether he could trust these people. He had no intention of trusting Woon Heeryong, a complete stranger.

Was this an opportunity or a risk? Was it worth taking?

"Alright."

After weighing the pros and cons, Lee Sungmin nodded. In truth, he wasn't in urgent need of money. His daily trips into the forest already earned him more than enough to live on, and he was saving every bit of side income he could.

However, more money was always better. That was an immutable truth, in this life as in his last. Lee Sungmin had no intention of staying in Zenavis forever. He planned to win the Classless Tournament in ten months, receive the Star Spirit Pill, and leave immediately. He needed to save up travel funds. After leaving Zenavis, he would have to start seriously acquiring equipment, so he intended to take any opportunity to gather a lump sum of money.

*Besides, I can't join the Mercenary Guild right now.*

To participate in the Classless Tournament, one could not have any job other than Classless. While joining the Guild was a good way to earn money, it was not an option for him.

"An excellent decision." Woon Heeryong's face brightened at Lee Sungmin's answer. "I believe it's best to strike while the iron is hot. We are already prepared. What do you think?"

"Are you saying we should go right now?" Lee Sungmin asked back, surprised by the haste.

Woon Heeryong just laughed heartily. "Haha! They're just pigs walking on two legs. There's nothing for you to worry about, Young Master."

*Then why did you ask for help?* Lee Sungmin found the man's confidence a little disagreeable, but he didn't object strongly.

*If things look bad, I'll just ditch them and run.*

He was confident he could escape on his own.

Lee Sungmin followed Woon Heeryong out of the city gate. On the path leading from outside the gate to the forest, his companions were waiting.

"Isn't he too young?" The speaker was a short man with a weathered face, barely half a head taller than Lee Sungmin.

"I saw him using his movement arts," Woon Heeryong defended. "Though this Young Master is young, he possesses outstanding martial arts, so he will be of sufficient help."

Lee Sungmin stepped forward and cupped his fist. "I am Lee Sungmin. I don't have a title."

"…Hmph. Little Monkey Marquis? The name's Do Sangryang."

A little monkey. The title fit the man's appearance perfectly.

"They call me Black Dagger Wang Pae," said the next man, who was dressed in pitch-black martial arts robes. He wasn't carrying a separate weapon, but judging by his title, it seemed he primarily used hidden weapons like daggers.

"I'm Lenire," a female mage in a purple robe said as she approached him. "I studied at the Bellas School… but you probably don't even know what the Bellas School is, right?"

Of course, he didn't. While martial arts had some common ground even for those from different murim origins, like the Nine Sects One Gang, the schools of magic were simply too diverse.

"…And those people over there?" Lee Sungmin shifted his gaze slightly. He saw two men standing there silently.

"They're just porters, so you don't need to pay them any mind," Woon Heeryong answered.

They bowed their heads deeply toward Lee Sungmin. Although they looked older than him at a glance, age wasn't very important in Eria.

In Eria, the one with power is the law.
