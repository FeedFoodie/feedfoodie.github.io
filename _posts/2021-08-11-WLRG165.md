Just before they left the forest for good, Lee Sungmin contacted Celgerus through Nebul.

The moment she saw him, Celgerus frowned and spat.

"Don't tell me the spear broke again. Please tell me that's not it. Your spear is made of solid Orichalcum!" Her words were a lament.

A bitter smile touched Lee Sungmin's lips as he shook his head. "It's not broken. It's perfectly fine."

"Then what is it? The armor?"

"No. I just wanted to inquire about commissioning something new."

"...How long has it been since I repaired your spear and armor?" Celgerus replied, her face sullen. "There's no material better than Orichalcum."

She was right. Orichalcum was a top-tier material that surpassed common sense. Even for a blacksmith with the title of Meister like Celgerus, the number of times she had crafted a weapon purely from it could be counted on one hand.

Reaching into his Subspace Pocket, Lee Sungmin produced a large box he had brought from the Void Monarch's treasury.

"What's that?" Celgerus asked. "Don't tell me you want me to make something out of that box?"

Lee Sungmin chuckled and opened the lid. Inside lay a mixture of scales and bones, all shimmering with a faint golden light. Celgerus blinked, peering into the box.

"...What is that?"

While Celgerus could not identify the Dragon scales and bones, Nebul could.

"Could it be..."

Nebul's jaw dropped. He wore an expression of utter disbelief, his lips moving silently before he finally managed to force out a question.

"Are those... Dragon bones and scales?"

"......"

In response to Nebul's stammered question, Lee Sungmin simply nodded.

At his confirmation, Celgerus's expression transformed. Though she couldn't leap through space like Nebul, she leaned so close it seemed she wished she could, her eyes wide with disbelief.

"No way," she uttered, her voice trembling. "Dragon bones and scales? My god, no, that can't be possible! Did you... hunt a Dragon?"

"No, I didn't hunt it. I just got lucky and obtained them."

"Lucky... Just how lucky do you have to be to get Dragon bones and scales?" Celgerus blurted out. "Did you run into a dying Dragon? Or did you raid a lair?"

Nebul felt the same astonishment. He glanced at the box, then moved his hand to open the Erebrisa auction window. "Dragon scales and bones... Small quantities do appear in the auction house, but... a quantity like this. How on earth..."

"There's more than this," Lee Sungmin murmured softly.

Listening to the conversation, the Void Monarch let out a pleased chuckle. The bones and scales Lee Sungmin possessed were from a single Dragon. The Void Monarch, who had hunted the creature in the past, had never processed the remains, instead keeping them as trophies in his treasury. They had remained perfectly intact, without any loss.

When Lee Sungmin produced several more boxes and opened them, both Nebul and Celgerus fell silent.

After a stunned silence, Nebul finally found his voice. "...Excluding the Black Dragon Knight, who is said to be of mixed Dragon blood, it has been over three hundred years since a 'real' Dragon has been seen in this world. The existence of Dragons has now become something familiar only in old tales, myths, and legends. Even three hundred years ago, when they were last seen, they reigned as the masters of magic and its absolute nemesis, with almost no precedent of ever being hunted."

He swallowed hard before continuing. "That amount is enough to arm an entire army. Dragon bones and scales... they possess a powerful magic resistance capable of nullifying most magic, and their sensitivity to Mana and inner qi is so exceptional it scoffs at Orichalcum. They are also highly versatile. These are the best alchemy ingredients and equipment materials that exist in this world."

Celgerus let out a long sigh. "I can't do it."

"No, it's not just me. Among the Dwarf blacksmiths with the title of Meister, there's probably no one who can handle Dragon bones and scales. We've never worked with them before. The chieftain might be a different story, though..."

"The chieftain?"

"The chieftain of the Dwarves," Celgerus muttered, her face full of uncertainty. "He's an old man whose life as a Dwarf is nearing its end. Though he's at an age where he could die at any moment, he's still hale and hearty. Maybe the chieftain could handle this...?"

At her words, Lee Sungmin glanced at Nebul, who shook his head with a troubled expression. "If you mean the Dwarf chieftain, that would be Mackendor. Unfortunately, Mackendor is not connected to Erebrisa. He was a member of ours until fifty years ago, but he suddenly gave up his membership and severed all ties with us."

"I received my Meister title from the chieftain, but it's already been decades since I last saw him," Celgerus added. "He's definitely alive. The Sun's Furnace, which only the chieftain can use, is constantly running, and works made by him occasionally appear."

"Couldn't other Dwarves meet with the chieftain?"

"No. Those guys can't meet him either," Celgerus chuckled. "The chieftain has a terrible personality. It's been decades since he holed himself up in the Sun's Furnace, and he hasn't come out once since. Other Dwarves tried to force their way in a few times, supposedly out of worry, but each time they got their heads smashed by the chieftain's hammer and were chased away!"

"...The last thing Mackendor purchased from us was an enormous amount of food," Nebul said. "Since food doesn't spoil in a Subspace Pocket, it was probably enough for a single person to eat comfortably for over a hundred years."

Lee Sungmin was left speechless. According to Nebul, Mackendor had locked himself in the Sun's Furnace with enough food to last a century. Neither Celgerus nor any other Meister could handle the Dragon bones and scales, and they couldn't arrange a meeting with the one person who might.

[So you have good materials but can't use them.] the Void Monarch clicked his tongue and muttered.

It was a pity, but there was nothing to be done. Still, just in case, Lee Sungmin handed over some of the Dragon teeth and scales to Celgerus.

"I'm not confident..."

"Didn't you say you've never worked with them before? So please, give it a try."

"I don't even know how much I should charge..."

"I don't need any money."

Moved by his words, Celgerus bowed her head deeply. Lee Sungmin, surprised, waved his hands dismissively at her. "You don't have to do that."

"No, I must. It's what I want to do."

After realizing there was nothing he could do with the scales and bones for now, Lee Sungmin showed the Dragon Heart to Nebul.

Nebul, who had thought himself beyond further surprise, simply collapsed where he stood when he saw it.

"This is insane..."

"Pardon?"

"Ah, no. I'm sorry. It's just... I'm so shocked," Nebul stammered, letting out a hollow laugh as he shook his head. "A Dragon Heart. A Dragon Heart. Haha...!" He took a moment to compose himself. "There's no precedent for a Dragon Heart ever being listed in the Erebrisa auction. I doubt there's even a mage who could handle it. Even a decent archmage would likely be unable to..."

"Could you look into it for me?"

"Understood. I'll ask around first and let you know."

His business with Erebrisa was finished.

When Lee Sungmin returned to the Buddhist camp, he found Aladur sleeping and snoring loudly. Nearby, Rubia was curled up, covering her ears as she slept. The Crazy Heavenly Demon sat watching the campfire.

"You're back?" he addressed Lee Sungmin, looking up with a bitter smile. "Looking at you... it makes this one feel ashamed of myself."

"...Pardon?"

"It's already been over half a year since this one met you and started traveling with you. Do you remember when we first met, half a year ago?"

"I remember," Lee Sungmin nodded.

His meeting with the Crazy Heavenly Demon in the North had been a coincidence. Or perhaps it was not a coincidence, but fate. Lee Sungmin didn't want to think too deeply about such things, but if fate truly existed, then their meeting could be called just that. The encounter had been sudden, and through it, Lee Sungmin had found a direction, a sense of what he needed to do and where he needed to go.

"You were strong then, but still weaker than this one," the Crazy Heavenly Demon continued.

"That's true." In fact, Lee Sungmin had nearly died in their fight. He would have died if the Void Monarch hadn't intervened.

"And not long after, you improved at an unbelievable speed. By the time you killed the Blood Heavenly Demon... the Martial God, you were already this one's equal. Then in Ruvus, you surpassed this one, and now you've reached a state several steps ahead."

"Are you feeling self-conscious?"

"This one felt that ten years ago," the Crazy Heavenly Demon said, laughing softly. "When this one saw a martial artist of the Transcendent Realm for the first time, this one felt self-conscious. This one learned how wide the world was, and how small a being I am." He stared into the fire. "It's not that this one feels self-conscious. Nor am I discouraged by comparing you and this one. This one has pride in the martial arts I've learned. Even though this one couldn't enter the Transcendent Realm after ten years of dedication, it's alright. In fact, thinking back, this one couldn't properly devote myself to martial arts for the past ten years because of the lunacy, but not anymore."

He looked back at Lee Sungmin. "This one thinks it was fortunate to have met you. You are this one's benefactor. You cured my lunacy and helped me firmly re-establish my passion for martial arts."

Lee Sungmin remained silent. He knew the feeling of comparing oneself to others, the despair that followed. He had felt it several times in the past and knew very well that overcoming such misery was by no means an easy task.

"You should get some sleep first. I'll stand watch."

The Crazy Heavenly Demon smiled. "Shouldn't I reduce my sleeping time as well?" He then sat cross-legged and began his qi circulation.

Lee Sungmin watched him quietly. The Transcendent Realm was a distant state, so much so that even a martial artist of the Crazy Heavenly Demon's caliber couldn't reach it after a decade of effort. Lee Sungmin himself had progressed since his fight with the Dark Supreme, but he still hadn't entered that realm.

*It is far.*

He had already reached the Transcendent Realm in his Mental World, but even with that memory, the path for his physical body was still long. Even after correcting his mind, qi, and body, it remained a long and distant road.

When the morning sun rose, the group packed up and began walking again, guided by the Evil Spirit. Aladur had been reluctant to head towards Urmuri, but he seemed to have had a change of heart after hearing the details.

"A tribe... The South has many jungles, so there are many small tribes. It would be endless if we tried to find them one by one."

"This one remembers the general location," the Crazy Heavenly Demon said.

"I wouldn't trust that location too much," Aladur pointed out. "Unless it's a fairly large tribe, they don't settle in one place. Didn't you say it was ten years ago? That tribe has probably already left the area you remember."

At this, the Crazy Heavenly Demon wore a troubled expression. If that were true, their journey, which relied solely on a memory, was flawed from the start.

Seeing his expression, Aladur crossed his arms. "I'd like to earn my keep, but I can't use a tracking spell with just a memory."

"Do you have anything you received from that tribe back then?" Lee Sungmin asked subtly.

The Crazy Heavenly Demon was lost in thought for a moment. A moment later, he let out a dry cough and began rummaging through his Subspace Pocket. "Well... that is... you see... Ahem!"

The embarrassed martial artist took out a bracelet made of woven string. It was old and faded, but it still retained its shape.

"What's that?"

"It's... a bracelet this one received from that tribe ten years ago."

"You got it from a woman!" Aladur declared with a hearty laugh.

The Crazy Heavenly Demon averted his gaze and kept his mouth shut. Aladur took the bracelet with both hands, his lips moving as he began to chant the words of a spell. The bracelet was immediately enveloped in black smoke.

"Don't burn it!" the Crazy Heavenly Demon shouted urgently.

"I won't, so don't you worry," Aladur replied with a dry laugh.

The billowing smoke drifted up and entered Aladur's nostrils. He took a deep breath and nodded, then opened his closed eyes.

"......"

"I know where it is."

"Where?"

"Let's get out of this forest first." Aladur returned the bracelet to the Crazy Heavenly Demon and grinned, showing all his teeth. "It's not too far from here."
