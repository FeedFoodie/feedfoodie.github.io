Her sleep was brief and, as always, dreamless.

Wiji Hoyeon was a woman faithful to reality, one who lived each day to its fullest and found no allure in the fleeting world of dreams. She never felt a sense of loss for memories that blurred and vanished upon waking, whether her slumber was light or deep. To her, the act of sleeping was nothing more than a tool for relieving fatigue, a way to efficiently pass the time. Pushing herself up from her curled position, she opened her eyes.

A fierce blizzard was raging around her.

This was Triggia, the northernmost part of Eria, a land where every day was winter and the snow never melted. Instead of melting, the fallen snow piled up, layer upon layer, and froze solid. The air was so cold it felt as if it could freeze the lungs, a harsh climate that was difficult to endure without several layers of thick clothing. Wiji Hoyeon's attire, however, showed little concern for the cold. Though she wore a thick, furry coat, she had no heavy clothes on underneath.

Even so, she did not feel the chill. Having already perfected Extreme Temperature Immunity, she was impervious to it, which was precisely why she could do something as mad as curling up to sleep outside in such a storm. The snowflakes clinging to her hair and skin were, however, an annoyance. She reached a hand above her head.

At her gesture, the Black Dragon Armor descended from its position shielding her from the driving snow and wind, wrapping itself around her body. The artifact, which looked as if it would flutter weakly in the breeze, was something she had obtained during her travels and was now her most cherished possession. It could lengthen or shorten, widen or narrow, all according to her inner arts. Brushing the snow from the armor as it settled over her shoulders, Wiji Hoyeon stepped out from under the tree.

"You could have slept longer."

A gaunt, grim-looking man approached and bowed his head deeply. It was the Phantom Sword of Dokgo, the first and greatest master among the small band of fewer than twenty that comprised the Heavenly Demon Army following her.

"I just woke up naturally," Wiji Hoyeon replied.

She neither liked nor disliked the followers who trailed her. She simply let them tag along because she had no reason to get rid of them. They might revere her, but that did not mean she had taken them under her wing. Wiji Hoyeon simply moved toward where she wanted to go, and the Heavenly Demon Army unilaterally followed. Despite this clearly drawn line, they were captivated by her overwhelming power and worshipped her.

"The appointed time has passed, but they haven't arrived," Dokgo reported.

"Maybe they overslept," she said with an unconcerned expression.

While Wiji Hoyeon accepted it calmly, the Phantom Sword of Dokgo did not. His narrow eyes filled with killing intent as he muttered, "How arrogant. Do they know who they're keeping waiting…"

"The Blood Heavenly Demon is making *me* wait. Since I'm the one waiting, why are you the one getting angry?"

"But, my lord…"

"Don't call me 'my lord'," she cut in. "I have never taken you as my subordinates. I won't stop you from following me as you please, but do not call me your lord."

The Phantom Sword of Dokgo's eyebrow twitched at her words, but he did not argue, simply bowing his head again. A group of followers could be useful, but to Wiji Hoyeon, they were unnecessary. Just moments ago, they had stood guard in this blizzard while she slept, yet she had never asked them to. Therefore, she felt no gratitude. There was no need.

Looking back, most of the eight years she had lived in Eria had been like this. She had seen many things and had many encounters, feeling joy in the vastness of this mysterious world but rarely from the connections she made and left behind. Though she had many followers, Wiji Hoyeon considered herself alone. They saw the Little Heavenly Demon, not her.

She didn't mind. What others saw was entirely their perspective, so she never tried to show them her true self or persuade them to change their point of view.

That was the kind of person Wiji Hoyeon was.

*I don't like it.*

The thought came to her as she stood in the blizzard. A short distance behind her, the Heavenly Demon Army stood their ground. Not all of them were masters who had achieved Extreme Temperature Immunity, and some were shivering violently, yet none left their posts. Wiji Hoyeon turned her head to glance at them, and glimpsing the emotion in the dozens of eyes looking her way, she clicked her tongue softly.

*I don't like it.*

She hated the expectation they held. That was what she didn't like. Wiji Hoyeon knew those kinds of eyes well. She had always seen them before being summoned to Eria, back when she lived as the Demonic Cult's Young Cult Leader. Her indifferent father, the Cult Leader himself, had looked at her with those eyes, though he never said it aloud. The many masters who taught her had worn the same look. The world had changed, but the gazes directed at her had not.

The Heavenly Demon Army was anticipating the meeting between the Little Heavenly Demon and the Blood Heavenly Demon, wondering what new rumor might be born here, what legend might spring from this frozen ground. They felt they were witnessing a historic moment, each of them dreaming that someday they would catch her eye and become her confidant.

Such expectations were disgusting. *Should I kill them?* she thought, clenching and unclenching her fist. It wouldn't be difficult. Even if they all attacked at once, she was confident she could tear them to pieces and bury them in the snow with just a few gestures. No, it wasn't confidence. It was a fact.

It was an easy task, but she wouldn't do it. She knew that even if she did, nothing would change. She had already killed people who approached her with such disgusting expectations several times before, and it changed nothing. If anything, her cruelty only made her blind followers more fanatical. Those who walked the unorthodox path rejoiced, declaring that even unforgiving cruelty was a fitting style for the title of 'the Heavenly Demon'.

"They're here," Wiji Hoyeon muttered.

PAPAPAK!

A violent spray of white erupted from the ground. As the flurry settled, eleven figures stood a short distance away. Wiji Hoyeon's eyes fell on the handsome man in a red robe at their center, and the corner of her mouth lifted.

"Did you oversleep?" she asked with a smile.

At her words, the Blood Heavenly Demon Baek Museon shrugged. When Wiji Hoyeon had gone to the headquarters of the Blood Heavenly Alliance to request a meeting, he had turned her away, claiming to be busy. He had arranged to meet here instead, and yet it was Baek Museon, the one who set the time and place, who had arrived late.

"I was to meet the Little Heavenly Demon, whose fame is soaring these days. I was merely delayed by making various preparations."

"Then why didn't you let me into the Blood Heavenly Alliance's headquarters? You wouldn't have needed to prepare if we were there."

"Huhu! I didn't want to be accused of luring the Little Heavenly Demon into my headquarters in case an unfortunate incident were to occur."

"An unfortunate incident?" Wiji Hoyeon laughed. "I don't know what kind of incident you're talking about."

Baek Museon didn't answer, instead raising his right hand slightly. The ten people standing around him, his honor guard known as the Ten Blood Fiends, stepped back.

"I've already heard about your big picture," he said. "Didn't you say you wanted to fight me?"

"I am also one who is called the Heavenly Demon. I was simply curious about the skills of someone with the title of the Blood Heavenly Demon."

"Haha… It sounds as if you intend to test me."

"Think whatever you want," Wiji Hoyeon muttered, taking a step forward. "Or just consider yourself unlucky."

WHOOOSH!

The Black Dragon Armor covering her shoulders fluttered grandly as the snow piled around her shot up all at once. Far behind them, the members of the Heavenly Demon Army let out gasps of admiration. Facing her, Baek Museon laughed under his breath.

"Unlucky… I don't know what you mean by that."

"I needed an opponent," Wiji Hoyeon whispered. "I considered taking on the Nine Sects One Gang, but that would have been quite a hassle. Messing with a well-united group leads to all sorts of annoying consequences."

"Are you saying the Blood Heavenly Alliance was an easy target?"

"I'm not messing with the Blood Heavenly Alliance. I'm messing with you, the Blood Heavenly Demon Baek Museon. Most unorthodox martial artists are like starving beasts. You just happened to be the strongest, so you became the leader, and the Alliance formed around you. If you fall, do you think the Blood Heavenly Alliance will remain?"

At those words, the Ten Blood Fiends erupted in fury. They were about to stride forward when Baek Museon stopped them with a raised hand, his expression still relaxed as he looked at Wiji Hoyeon.

"You're overconfident."

"Because it's the truth. Not letting me into your headquarters was the right choice. If you had used some dirty tricks, there would have just been more corpses."

"Didn't I tell you? I just didn't want to hear any unnecessary accusations. If you had died inside the Blood Heavenly Alliance's headquarters, ignorant people might have gossiped that I used cowardly means. There are few witnesses here, but… well, it's enough. Are you sure about this, though? This place is too insignificant to serve as your grave. If you wish, I will cripple you but not kill you. Or I could bury your corpse somewhere else."

"I may be overconfident, but you're arrogant. Or maybe just crazy. Do you even realize who you're saying that to?"

Snow no longer fell around Wiji Hoyeon. The pouring flakes evaporated the instant they entered the fighting spirit she was emitting.

"You were unlucky," she continued. "If I had known where the Crazy Heavenly Demon was, I would have gone to fight him instead. If you want to blame someone, blame him for wandering around as he pleases."

"Arrogant. Too arrogant…" Baek Museon muttered, rolling up his sleeves.

The fighting spirit he emitted was blood-red, cloaking him in a crimson aura that stained his eyes the same color. The rumors about the Blood Heavenly Demon were an understatement. He was a monster who had clearly surpassed the realm of the Supreme Peak and was already glimpsing the next stage.

"Let me ask one last thing," he said. "Why are you doing this? To bring down the Blood Heavenly Alliance?"

"You don't understand. Didn't I tell you? It's because you're unlucky."

"You're telling me you came all the way to this distant land just to pick a fight with me for that reason alone?"

"If I defeat you, the rumors about me will grow even louder," Wiji Hoyeon muttered. "Then he'll hear about me too. Where I am, what I've done, and how much stronger I've become. I hope he doesn't get discouraged and hide away. He has a bad habit of self-deprecation."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"You don't need to understand. Ah… right. Just as you asked me a question, I have one for you. Can you answer it?"

"If it's something I can answer."

"Which hand do you hold your spoon with?" Wiji Hoyeon raised her own hand, and the languidly flowing Black Dragon Armor swelled up to envelop it.

"You tell me," she said with a bright smile, "and I'll leave that hand alone."

At her words, the smile vanished from Baek Museon's face.

The sudden clash between the Blood Heavenly Demon and the Little Heavenly Demon was decided in less than half a day.

That day, the Blood Heavenly Demon Baek Museon lost his left arm and was crippled.
