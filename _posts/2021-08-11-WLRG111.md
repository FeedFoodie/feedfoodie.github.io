Lee Sungmin's breathing evened out as he sat up, finding Rubia already awake and sitting on the bed. He turned to face her.

"Did you sleep well?" he asked.

"...I think I slept on the floor."

"You were bothering me, so I moved you."

"Such a pretty way with words," she retorted. "I was worried sick, so I stayed right here until you were completely asleep."

"What was there to worry about?"

"Only the person who was on the verge of death, writhing and screaming in agony."

"I didn't scream."

"You let out a silent scream," Rubia snapped. "Anyway, I was watching because I was worried. It looked like you were going to pass out without even washing, so I even cleaned your body with magic."

No wonder. He had been so exhausted that he had collapsed without a thought for cleaning himself, yet his body felt refreshed.

"Your service is excellent."

"You smelled terrible," Rubia said teasingly, pinching her nose.

Lee Sungmin ignored her and picked up the spear that was leaning against the wall.

"Where are you going?"

"My body feels much better. I should go outside and loosen up a bit."

"Why don't you just get some more rest?"

"Are you worried about me?"

"Is that not allowed?" she huffed. "I can't let you die. If that happened, I wouldn't be able to carry out my master's orders."

"A guy whose whereabouts you don't even know." Lee Sungmin grumbled, the thought of Envirus souring his mood.

At his words, Rubia's cat ears drooped as if her spirit had been dampened.

"...He'll definitely come for me. If he didn't want to meet you, he wouldn't have left me behind."

"Isn't it possible that Envirus just abandoned you?"

"That can't be... right?"

The conviction had vanished from Rubia's voice. Before entering the Dungeon, she had been certain that Envirus would come for her. The near-death experiences she had suffered inside, however, were enough to slightly shake that blind faith. She had been created by Envirus and had lived for him. While she had faced danger before, those threats had been trivial to her powerful master.

However, Envirus was not by her side now. She had almost died. She would have died if not for the Void Monarch.

"I was just kidding," Lee Sungmin muttered, seeing the melancholy settle on her face. "If Envirus had abandoned you, he wouldn't have guided you to me."

"...But I almost died."

"'We' almost died," he corrected her. "But in the end, we didn't, and we won't die in the future either."

Rubia mumbled something under her breath, her eyes fixed on Lee Sungmin. He met her gaze, shrugged his shoulders, and headed outside.

The only inn in this sizable village was an old, decrepit building. Though the village was large, it was not a city surrounded by walls and was therefore always adjacent to danger. With no city lord to enforce order, public security was also poor.

The first floor served as a filthy pub and restaurant. It was already crowded with men who looked no different from vagrants, guzzling cheap liquor and food in broad daylight. Many of them appeared to be mercenaries. Lee Sungmin paid them no mind as he ordered food for his breakfast and lunch. Rubia, who had followed him down, sat close beside him.

"...It smells," she muttered in a small voice.

The sanitation was indeed poor, and the food that arrived tasted mediocre. As he was picking at the last of his meal, someone came shuffling towards their table.

"Never seen your face in this village before."

A foul stench wafted from the man's mouth every time he spoke. Rubia grimaced in disgust while Lee Sungmin turned his gaze to the newcomer. An old beggar with dark spots mottling his face was looking down at him.

"Some alms, perhaps...?"

"Are you from the Beggars' Gang?" Lee Sungmin asked abruptly.

The man hadn't intended to hide it. The beggar grinned, his foul-smelling mouth gaping open.

"And are you the Phantom Spear?"

His whispering voice was low, nearly buried by the chatter in the inn. Lee Sungmin stared up at the beggar.

"I've never had any bad blood with the Beggars' Gang."

"Kekeke! Young hero, you seem to be misunderstanding something. I was just curious. It's not every day an extraordinary master appears in this small village, so I just had to ask."

"That's right," Lee Sungmin answered after a slight pause. "I am the Phantom Spear. Is there a problem?"

"I heard a Dungeon opened not far from here, and that the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon was the one who closed it."

"News travels fast."

"Kekeke! Isn't magic convenient?"

"What do you want to ask?"

"A young hero of our Beggars' Gang barely escaped that Dungeon with his left arm severed. The Supreme Peak high beastfolk Yeokbalsan Jang Deuksu and Silent Flash Baek Sogo also escaped. The problem is, those heroes barely managed to escape with their lives. They don't know exactly what happened in the Dungeon or how it ended."

At those words, Lee Sungmin's eyes widened. A wave of relief washed over him. Baek Sogo had made it out of the Dungeon safely.

"What are you curious about?"

"The Phantom Spear," the beggar asked. "How did you make it out alive?"

Lee Sungmin didn't answer, instead staring intently into the beggar's eyes. After a moment, his mouth opened.

"I must have done what was necessary to survive."

"Hehe! Did this old beggar displease you?"

"No, that's not it. I just don't feel like telling you the details."

"For what reason?"

"Stop beating around the bush and ask properly," Lee Sungmin said, his voice sharp. "Ask what Wiji Hoyeon obtained from that Dungeon."

The beggar's mouth snapped shut. A small flicker of irritation sparked within Lee Sungmin. He had never understood the Orthodox Murim Alliance's obsession with checking and controlling Wiji Hoyeon. As far as he knew, she had committed no great evil, yet they arbitrarily labeled her as such and tried to monitor her every move. He had figured that much out from Jang Deuksu's rambling.

"...What did Wiji Hoyeon obtain from that Dungeon?"

"I didn't see."

So he lied.

"The Dungeon was a terrible place, and I barely managed to survive. I never even met Wiji Hoyeon herself. I was just trying to stay alive when the Dungeon closed, and I was forcibly ejected from it. That's all."

The beggar narrowed his eyes and fell silent, as if judging the truth of Lee Sungmin's words. After a long moment, he nodded his head.

"Thank you for your cooperation."

"Ah, and," Lee Sungmin said, reaching into his robes. He took out a few coins and handed them to the beggar. "Alms."

"...Kekeke! May you be blessed."

The beggar let out a hearty laugh and turned away. Lee Sungmin watched his retreating back and swallowed a sigh.

*So he was getting tangled up like this again.*

Connections, whether he sought them or not, always ended up entangling him. His gaze swept across the restaurant. The vagrants huddled in the corner were watching him. They were all probably from the Beggars' Gang. None of them were great masters, but what made the Beggars' Gang tricky wasn't their individual strength but their sheer numbers. Wherever people gathered, there were always beggars. While other sects tended to reject Colored-Eyed Persons, the Beggars' Gang was different. They accepted anyone.

Lee Sungmin stood up without a word. He had planned to stay in the village for a few days to check on his body, but that was no longer possible. The presence of observers was too bothersome. He paid for his lodging and left the inn immediately. He had no luggage to speak of, so there was nothing to pack.

"Where are you trying to go?"

"First, I'll head north."

He planned to organize the information he had gathered on the way and then decide on a proper course of action. At his answer, Rubia changed her form into a small ball of light and hid herself inside his cloak.

He left the village at a steady pace, not rushing. There were no signs of pursuers, yet something bothered his senses. The Void Monarch on his back made a low humming sound.

[It's magic.]

*Did the Beggars' Gang use magic too?*

[How would I know, but it's certain that you're being watched. What will you do?]

*I'll leave it alone. If I provoke them unnecessarily, they'll just become more annoying.*

He couldn't figure out why the Beggars' Gang was watching him. Was simply surviving the Dungeon enough to warrant their attention? Or were they suspicious of his relationship with Wiji Hoyeon? Either way, he didn't like it. Still, shaking off their pursuit would be difficult. Lee Sungmin knew from rumors just how persistent the Beggars' Gang could be.

*Surely it wasn't Chwi Geol. That guy couldn't be acting so petty just because I left him with the Blood Granny.*

When he thought about it, he was the one who had been properly abandoned. Lee Sungmin grumbled to himself as he walked forward.

A lone bird followed Lee Sungmin from the sky. Since he had not yet used his movement arts, it had no great difficulty keeping pace. The bird's eyes tracked his movements as he walked quickly along the ground, and the image it saw was transmitted to the mage who commanded it.

From there, the scenery traveled continuously through a magical link, reaching all the way to the distant city of Kron. There was a slight time delay, but seeing across such a vast distance was a marvel impossible for martial arts, something only magic could achieve.

"So he was alive," Chwi Geol muttered, placing his hand on the crystal ball.

Having formed a partnership with the core of the Mages Guild, the Beggars' Gang had eyes and ears all over Eria. Even so, very few targets were monitored in real-time like this. Chwi Geol had used his authority as the Beggars' Gang Young Sect Leader to confirm Lee Sungmin's location and designate him for surveillance.

*I thought he would be dead.*

Of course, he had. Their opponent had been the Doppelganger of the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon. Although a Doppelganger was significantly weaker than the original, the Little Heavenly Demon's own strength meant her double would still possess overwhelming power.

His judgment, Chwi Geol told himself, had not been wrong. He had suffered a major injury, losing his left arm, and Jang Deuksu was exhausted from his fight with the Blood Granny. The same must have been true for Baek Sogo. The Phantom Spear's skills were impossible to gauge, and a fierce battle would have carried a high probability of annihilation. That was why he chose to flee. In his judgment, it had been the right thing to do.

If the Phantom Spear had died there, Chwi Geol could have been confident that his judgment was correct. He could have faced Baek Sogo with his head held high and told her that he had saved her life. That even though she couldn't accept it and slapped his cheek, in the end, he was the one who saved her.

*It would have been better if he had died.*

Chwi Geol thought with a bitter smile. Baek Sogo had left. She had unilaterally declared her departure from the Murim Alliance and had left the city of Kron without waiting for their acceptance. He had not yet confirmed where she went, because he had not designated her as a surveillance target.

*I don't like it.*

Chwi Geol looked down at the crystal ball. He could see Lee Sungmin walking with the broken spear on his back. The image of Baek Sogo crying out, asking why he had abandoned his Junior Disciple, flashed through his mind. That was why he disliked it even more. The way she had cried out was as if...

"Jealousy is an ugly thing," Chwi Geol muttered with a hollow laugh.

At a point some distance from the village, Lee Sungmin unleashed his movement arts, but it wasn't just his movement arts. At the very peak of his speed, he activated Haste. While casting magic required magic power, magic learned as a skill was incredibly convenient. His inner qi burned, fueling the spell and adding to the speed of his Dancing Shadow Soul Break.

Whoa.

The sudden burst of speed almost threw him off balance, but he adapted in the next instant. Entrusting his body to the incredible velocity, Lee Sungmin dodged or leaped over obstacles in his path.

*Using this for a long time isn't a good idea.*

Although Lee Sungmin possessed a large amount of inner qi, the combined consumption from his movement technique and Haste was immense. He still had some to spare, but he stopped after running for a long time.

*This would be good to use in a real fight.*

The added speed from Haste could provide a powerful element of surprise. It would take a good deal of trial and error to incorporate it into his Spear Technique. If he added the Strength spell on top of that, it would take quite some time to get used to it all.

*Because I have no talent.*

Lee Sungmin thought with a smirk. There was no sign of surveillance. It seemed the magic couldn't keep up with his running speed and had been left far behind.

"You can come out now."

As Lee Sungmin finished speaking, Nebul emerged from the shadows and bowed his head deeply.

"I will deliver the information you requested," Nebul said, taking out a crystal ball.

Lee Sungmin nodded and accepted it.
