"...Hahaha!"

A disbelieving laugh erupted from the innkeeper, Jack. He glanced from Lee Sungmin's face to the items laid out before him, then let out another dry chuckle.

"Are you telling me you really gathered all this by yourself?"

The haul consisted of teeth from three goblins, five full bottles of their blood, two blowgun pipes, and ten poison darts, along with a single bottle of poison. Lee Sungmin had decided to keep the remaining pipes, darts, and poison for himself, figuring they would prove useful.

Jack looked down at the boy, his thoughts a complex tangle. He knew that goblins were not particularly strong monsters. Their use of weapons and poison made them a nuisance, but any skilled adventurer could kill a few with ease. While entering a nest teeming with dozens of them was an incredibly dangerous task, taking down a few on patrol was supposed to be simple.

The one who had done it, however, was a fourteen-year-old kid. What surprised Jack even more was that he was a Classless, with no special abilities.

Running an inn meant Jack had seen many otherworlders arrive in this city for the first time. Those with abilities, the martial artists and mages, tended to adapt to this world with relative speed.

However, the Classless were different. Not only had they never experienced this kind of combat, but they also lacked the skills to keep themselves alive. It usually took a Classless about a month to adapt, a month to grasp their situation, let go of their attachments to their old world, and become capable of killing monsters without a second thought just to survive.

"You... when did you first get to this city?"

"Yesterday," Lee Sungmin answered truthfully.

Jack was so dumbfounded by the response that he let out another laugh. "And you're fourteen?"

"......"

"You haven't... learned anything special?"

"...That's right."

"Hahaha!" Jack laughed again at Lee Sungmin's answers. He was curious about what kind of kid he was and what he had been through, but he did not press the issue. Not asking about one's previous world was an unwritten rule of sorts.

"The price... I'll give you a fair one. Heheh. A strange kid has shown up. For a Classless to adapt to this world in a single day and hunt monsters..."

Honestly, Jack had thought Lee Sungmin would not come back alive. He had seen guys like him before, ones who quickly realized their situation and rushed out to the hunting grounds in a desperate bid to survive.

Usually, those guys did not return. Understanding their situation was one thing, but fighting monsters and surviving were two entirely different matters.

And yet, this kid had returned. Alive.

"I'll cut your lodging fee in half." 

Unfortunately, giving someome free lodging was not an option. That was a creed for Jack. 

"It's good to form a connection with a talented kid. If you manage to keep surviving... haha! Who knows, you might become a big shot whose reputation is heard all throughout Zenavis. Right, when that time comes, don't forget the favor you received from me."

Jack laughed heartily and patted Lee Sungmin's shoulder. Lee Sungmin offered a smile in return before heading up to his room.

His mind was filled with thoughts as he climbed the stairs.

*You seem like you'll become a regular.*

*A talented kid.*

He recalled the street vendor and Jack's words. In his past life, he hadn't heard such things very often, especially not in this city. Here in Zenavis, he had never once received such words or such an evaluation.

As the city where all otherworlders first arrived, Zenavis made one keenly aware of just how unfair this world was. He could see it just from the way Jack and the street vendor treated him.

Talent. One could so easily gain favor just by having it.

*Talent? I've never had anything to do with it.*

Lee Sungmin sighed as he stepped into his room. The thirteen years he had lived in Eria were more than enough time for him to realize his place. He had never mastered the inner arts technique he practiced for nearly a decade, and the same was true for the second-rate martial arts he had learned through extensive travel.

He would be able to quickly regain the skill level of his past life, since he had reached that level once before. That was only possible because the martial arts he had learned had nothing to do with abstract concepts like enlightenment.

Second-Rate martial arts were those whose level increased with simple proficiency alone. The Soul Chasing Spear Art was a martial art that thoroughly contained only forms. It had no strange techniques and held no profound-looking insights. 

The same was true for the One Thunder Movement Arts, Iron Skin Strong Bones, and the Stone Breaking Fist. One Thunder was a movement and footwork art containing only the fundamentals, while Iron Skin Strong Bones and Stone Breaking Fist were external arts. Though aided by inner qi, they too contained no insights.

That was the difference between second-rate and first-rate martial arts. First-rate arts contained insights. If you were lucky, you could grasp those insights and overcome the wall of the First-Rate.

However, second-rate martial arts were different. Since they were martial arts that contained only forms without any insights, you would gain no enlightenment from the art itself, no matter how much you practiced. At best, you would only get close to the First-Rate level.

*My body is too weak right now. I lack strength and inner qi. If I build up my stamina... the levels of my other martial arts, aside from the Heavenly Truth Meditation Art, will rise quickly, but even if I cling to them, they're still just second-rate martial arts in the end...*

That thought depressed him. To break the limits of a second-rate martial art, one needed the talent to transcend the art's inherent limitations.

However, Lee Sungmin had no such talent. He did not have the aptitude of a grandmaster who could create something from nothing, so he had no choice but to rely on martial arts created by others who had imbued them with insights.

His only hope was the Heavenly Truth Meditation Art. It was a first-rate inner arts technique. If he devoted himself to it, perhaps he might be able to achieve something. 

An inner arts technique increased the absolute amount of inner qi and made one adept at circulating it. If he trained in the Heavenly Truth Meditation Art and made progress, the amount of inner qi he could handle would greatly increase, and circulating it would become easier.

An inner arts technique, however, was in the end just an inner arts technique. Its limits were clear.

*There's a chance, but... it's still a long way off. First, I need to get the Star Spirit Pill.*

Lee Sungmin tossed his dirt-covered clothes into a corner and took a deep breath. His body was a little stiff, but he paid it no mind and got down on the floor.

He decided to start by doing push-ups every day.

{sep}

After he started staying at Jack's Inn, Lee Sungmin headed to the hunting grounds every single day. On the way, he used One Thunder Movement Arts as much as his inner qi allowed. He bought necessary items from Hans, the street vendor who had shown him kindness, and sold any spoils that Jack didn't take through him.

He mainly targeted goblins. By going in and out of the forest several times, Lee Sungmin figured out the territory of the goblin tribe and hunted the ones patrolling its outskirts. He continued to collect blood and teeth. 

With his lodging fee cut in half, the spoils he brought to Jack were more than enough to pay for his room. Jack agreed to take a small commission and gave the remaining money to Lee Sungmin.

"Do you have a Subspace Pocket?"

Two weeks after his return to Zenavis, Lee Sungmin visited Hans's stall with the question. Hans, shivering in the morning air, let out a big yawn and looked at him.

"Got 'em, sure."

A Subspace Pocket was an essential item for otherworlders and adventurers who roamed the hunting grounds. Enchanted with spatial distortion magic, the pocket could hold many items despite its small size.

"But the one I have is secondhand, and the distortion magic on it isn't all that great. Let's see..."

Hans opened a large bundle he had between his legs. Its inside was filled with a pitch-black darkness. 

Lee Sungmin realized the bundle itself was a Subspace Pocket. Hans's hand plunged into it, and after rummaging for a moment, he pulled out a fist-sized pouch.

"It's a used item. Picked it up while passing through a forest a little ways from Zenavis. From a corpse, that is. It can hold about as much as one large bag. Wanna buy it?"

"How much is it?"

"Six hundred thousand Eru," Hans said with a grin.

Subspace Pockets were traded at a high price commensurate with their convenience. They felt almost weightless since they held items with spatial distortion magic, and it was also possible to store food since time did not flow inside the subspace. For an adventurer who collected a lot of spoils, it was an essential item.

"That's expensive."

"You wanna haggle? I'll knock it down to five hundred thousand Eru for you," Hans said with a hearty laugh.

Lee Sungmin, however, shook his head. His entire fortune was currently less than one hundred thousand Eru. He had been scraping together everything worth selling from the forest and selling it to Hans or Jack, but after paying for lodging and necessary items, the money was not accumulating well.

"I'll buy it next time. I really don't have the money."

"So you're not... trying to haggle, I see. Then why'd you ask?"

"Because, to me, you're the one who sells things the cheapest."

A Subspace Pocket was necessary, but he could not afford it yet. He had wanted to find out how much Hans sold them for.

Hans looked stunned, as if someone had just smacked him on the back of the head "...Puhaha! I've been a street vendor for years, but this is the first time I've received this kind of trust. Alright, then let's do this. I'll test you."

"...Yes?" Lee Sungmin's eyes widened at the sudden proposal.

Hans chuckled and continued. "Kill five orcs and bring me their eyes. If you succeed, I'll give you this Subspace Pocket."

Lee Sungmin swallowed hard at those words. Two weeks had passed since he returned to Zenavis, but he had not yet attempted to hunt orcs. They were trickier opponents than goblins, aggressive and possessing tough bodies. Although their intelligence was a bit lower than a goblin's, orcs had a toughness that more than made up for their lack of it.

"...By when?"

"By the end of today," Hans said, a mischievous expression on his face. "It's been two weeks since you came to Zenavis, right? You headed to the hunting grounds the very next day, and you've survived until now... you must have quite a bit of talent. Even if your opponents were just rabbits or goblins. If you can kill orcs too, then I'll believe that the talent you have is real."

*It's not really talent. It's just experience,* Lee Sungmin thought to himself, but he did not let it show on his face.

"Understood. I accept."

He nodded his head firmly.
