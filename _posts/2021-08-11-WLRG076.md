He had never heard the name Jihak before, yet the words of Grandmaster Bulyeong, the Shaolin Abbot, echoed in his mind. *The future of Shaolin.* The duel was sudden, but Lee Sungmin did not complain. There was always something to be gained from defeat, even if he lost. His match with Namgung Heewon had taught him that much, for in that loss, Lee Sungmin had been forced to confront his own shortcomings.

Now, under the Grandmaster's watchful eye, Lee Sungmin stood facing Jihak. The monk held no weapon, a stark contrast to the spear gripped tightly in Lee Sungmin's hands. He shot a questioning glance at Grandmaster Bulyeong, wondering if it was truly alright to use a real, sharpened spear, but the old monk simply met his gaze and burst into laughter.

"Can your spear pierce diamond?"

The words shocked Lee Sungmin. If what Grandmaster Bulyeong said was true, it meant Jihak had achieved the Vajra Body. He gulped, his eyes fixed on the young monk. Jihak had a build similar to his own, and they did not seem far apart in age.

The duel began.

It was over in about one hundred seconds. The exchanges were a blur that ended in Lee Sungmin's complete defeat. He slumped to the ground and looked up with a dejected expression at Jihak, who stood completely unscathed. In contrast, Lee Sungmin was in a sorry state. Grandmaster Bulyeong, watching him collapsed on the ground, laughed loudly.

"Indeed. Now I see why that girl asked you to teach her martial arts."

Lee Sungmin gritted his teeth as the Grandmaster laughed and even clapped his hands. *Did this even make sense?* He had surpassed the realm of a peak master and was knocking on the door of the Supreme Peak, yet Jihak had played with him as if he were a child. He had lost to Namgung Heewon, but he had never been toyed with so thoroughly.

"Jihak. How was it?"

Jihak, who had been bowing with his palms pressed together, raised his gaze. He glanced at Grandmaster Bulyeong and tilted his head.

"How should I answer?"

"Answer honestly, just as you felt." The Grandmaster's prodding gave no impression of a virtuous high monk.

After a moment of thought, Jihak finally spoke. "He is lacking in many areas."

The words pierced Lee Sungmin's heart like a dagger.

"His greatest weakness is experience," Jihak continued. "The spearmanship he has learned seems to be peerless, but he can't execute it properly. It's not that he's unskilled in handling it. It's more that he doesn't yet know 'how' to unleash it."

He had heard the same thing from Namgung Heewon.

"His handling of his inner qi is clumsy. It doesn't seem like he lacks inner qi, but there are many disruptions in the overall flow."

"That's because his mind, qi, and body are out of balance," Grandmaster Bulyeong added. "His Conception and Governing Vessels haven't even been opened, yet his martial realm has risen bizarrely high due to insights. His mind and qi have moved forward, but his body is lagging behind. It's a dangerous situation."

The Grandmaster looked down at Lee Sungmin's confused face. "Child, you don't seem to understand what we're saying. So let me put it like this. If both your legs are the same length, you'll have no problem walking or running. However, if one leg is excessively long or short, both walking and running become difficult. Even if you force yourself to move, you're bound to fall before long. The problem is that while the analogy is simple, your condition is not so simple or easy."

"What should I do?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"The first thing you must do is open the Conception and Governing Vessels. If you're lucky, that might solve the problem. The flow of qi will become smooth, and the disruptions will disappear. Actually, the easiest way is to undergo Body Reformation. Hehe, but Body Reformation isn't something you can do so easily."

With that, Grandmaster Bulyeong rose from his seated position. "Jihak. For the time being, take him and look after his martial arts."

"I understand." Jihak pressed his palms together toward the Grandmaster as he returned to the cave.

Lee Sungmin let out a deep sigh and pushed himself to his feet. He had reached the peak realm on Mount Mushii and awakened to enhanced qi during his fight with Aine. He had thought he had gotten stronger.

In truth, he was not wrong. Lee Sungmin had grown stronger, far surpassing the realm he had reached in his past life. It was not that he was weak. Among martial artists of a similar age, he would be considered one of the very best. It was just that his opponents had been exceptional. The Sword Dragon Namgung Heewon was the next head of the Namgung Clan, a peerless genius praised as being even more talented than the current head, the Heavenly Venerable Sword King, was in his youth.

"Please don't be too discouraged," Jihak said, his voice calm. The same was true for him. "Objectively speaking, your skills are by no means lacking. It's rare for a martial artist to reach such a level at the age of eighteen."

"But didn't you treat me like a child?" Lee Sungmin countered.

"I don't like to say this, as it sounds like I'm praising myself," Jihak began, his expression unchanging. "I am said to be the greatest genius to exist since Shaolin was founded in this land. I have been here since before I could even walk. As I grew up, not only Great Monk Bulyeong but all of Shaolin's greatest warrior monks taught me martial arts. I shared their enlightenment and was raised on all of Shaolin's techniques and miracle herbs."

Lee Sungmin was dumbfounded. *The future of Shaolin.* He now understood exactly what Grandmaster Bulyeong had meant.

"Even so," Jihak muttered in a small voice, "I couldn't defeat the Little Heavenly Demon."

At those words, Lee Sungmin's mind snapped to attention. "You've fought her?"

"......"

"The Little Heavenly Demon. Why did Wiji Hoyeon come here?"

"Her reason was that she wanted to see Shaolin," Jihak replied with an indifferent expression. "It was a sudden visit. She came over Shaolin's mountain gate all by herself and then she said it. That she wanted to see Shaolin."

It was an incredibly ignorant act, Lee Sungmin thought.

"'I want to see Shaolin.' Those were the words of a seventeen-year-old girl who had not yet even come of age," Jihak recounted. "However, not a single warrior monk present could rebuke or laugh at her. I was there as well. It was a very mystical, humiliating experience. I had never imagined that a single person's presence could overwhelm a space to that extent."

"And after that?"

"Grandmaster Bulyeong himself came out, saying a nobleman had visited. Then, Wiji Hoyeon and the Grandmaster spoke, just the two of them. I don't know what they talked about. After that, I had a duel with her. She said she wanted to see Shaolin, and Grandmaster Bulyeong pointed to me and called me the future of Shaolin."

Lee Sungmin already knew the result. Jihak had already said it.

"The world is a wide place," Jihak muttered, closing his eyes. "Did it last a little over one hundred seconds? No, it was only that long because it was a duel. If it had been a battle to the death, I wouldn't have lasted fifty seconds. She called herself the Little Heavenly Demon, but I couldn't understand that title. If she is the Little Heavenly Demon, then who in the world is the Heavenly Demon?"

Lee Sungmin did not know the Wiji Hoyeon of today. He had never even met her. He had assumed she would have gotten stronger, but he could not have faintly imagined just how much. The strength Jihak described was far beyond his imagination. Wiji Hoyeon had apparently played with Jihak, the same Jihak who had just played with him as if he were a child.

"Wiji Hoyeon... just what level has she reached?"

"I don't know. However, one thing is certain. She is already beyond the Supreme Peak and has reached a higher realm. I don't know if she is knocking on the door or if she has already passed through it and is advancing to a new stage. What is your relationship with her?"

"A friend."

"Is that so," Jihak muttered, nodding his head.

Lee Sungmin staggered to his feet and picked up his spear. Seeing this, Jihak spoke again. "What you lack most is experience. From what I've seen, you're not using your skills properly. That can be supplemented by experiencing various real battles."

"I understand."

"It might be a little funny for me to say this, but please don't be too frustrated by defeat."

"You don't have to worry about that," Lee Sungmin said with a bitter smile. "I'm used to frustration, and I'm used to getting back up."

He was just used to it. It was not easy.

After leaving Grandmaster Bulyeong's residence, Lee Sungmin headed down toward Shaolin. He felt the distance, but what felt so far was not the road ahead. He had vaguely thought he was getting closer to her. He believed that even if she was not right in front of him, he could run just a little more, reach his hand out a little further, and maybe touch her.

It was a delusion. Wiji Hoyeon was farther, farther, farther away than he had ever thought. Or perhaps it was not that she was far away, but that he had been approaching too slowly. Either way, she was too far to reach.

*Not enough.*

He felt that he himself was not enough. Lee Sungmin looked down at the spear in his hand. It was not heavy, but he missed the weight he had felt on Mount Mushii, those days when he could not see, when the stench never faded, when the noise never ceased. Come to think of it, though his daily life back then had been so difficult it felt like he would go mad, was it not more fulfilling than any other moment he had ever lived?

*What have I done since coming down from the mountain?*

He walked down the mountain path, not running, contemplating himself as he listened to the cries of the mountain insects. After Mount Mushii, what had he done? He went to the Mercenary Guild and became an S-Rank Mercenary. He had felt satisfied after receiving that high rank, something he could not even get close to in his past life. He thought the ascetic training on the mountain had been validated, and he felt joy in the fact that the 'current' him was different from the him of his past life.

Several months passed. He earned money by completing requests and had a spear crafted with his earnings. He was satisfied. He felt pleasure in holding a fine weapon he could never have held before. For months, it was like that. He acted for his own satisfaction, wanting to be acknowledged and happy when he was. He enjoyed the power he never had, pleased to see the people he knew from the Mercenary Guild in Behenger treat him differently.

From a certain point on, he was not so diligent in his training. He had memories of his past life and knew about a tool called Magic Armor. Without even knowing it was a trap, he relied on those memories, trying to overcome the walls he faced by using a shortcut. Effort. *Effort?* Lee Sungmin let out a hollow laugh. The few years on Mount Mushii were the sum of all his effort. What had he worked so hard on since then? In the end, the current Lee Sungmin was not much different from his past life. He was no different from when he was a C-Rank Mercenary, living day to day, thinking it was okay to play around because he had risked his life on requests. Drinking, holding women. What was the difference between then and now, living each day drunk on the need for compensation? Thinking it was okay to rest a little because he had trained so hard on Mount Mushii.

Thinking this was enough. That he had worked hard. When he did not even have such great skills. Lee Sungmin chuckled. The road to Shaolin seemed long. To see the end of martial arts. That grand thought he had held like a seeker on Mount Mushii was, in the end, nothing but shabby self-satisfaction and hypocrisy.

He now knew reality.

He thought he did not want to become a cicada, and he thought he had shed his larval skin.

It was a delusion.

Lee Sungmin was still a larva.
