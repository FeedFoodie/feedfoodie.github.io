"So it's true."

Hans murmured the words in disbelief. He was the one who had given the order, but he had never truly expected the boy to succeed. It had been a ridiculous command, to send a fourteen-year-old kid to hunt five orcs. This wasn't some prodigy trained in martial arts or magic, just a Classless boy.

And that boy had returned with ten orc eyeballs.

"I almost died," Lee Sungmin rasped, and he certainly looked the part. The dirt from his ambush had caked his skin, now streaked with grime where sweat had cut through. A foul stench of blood, sweat, and earth rolled off him in waves.

"You didn't just find these lying around, did you?" Hans asked, his eyes narrowed with suspicion.

"Find them where? On the side of the road?"

"Maybe you stumbled on some orc corpses."

"Or maybe you just don't want to give me the Subspace Pocket," Lee Sungmin accused, his glare sharp.

The accusation made Hans burst out laughing. He laughed for a long moment before shaking his head.

"No, that's not it. A promise is a promise. I have to give it to you."

Muttering to himself, Hans rummaged through his bundle and produced the Subspace Pocket.

"Here. It's worth six hundred thousand Eru."

Lee Sungmin's hand shot out like a bolt of lightning, snatching the pocket from Hans's grasp. A bright smile spread across his face as he shoved it securely into his own pocket.

"Thank you!"

Hans chuckled, a sneer playing on his lips. "You look like a kid when you smile."

"What do you mean, 'like a kid'? I am a kid."

"Hardly. Hey, I've known you for almost two weeks now, and I've never seen an old soul like you. What the hell did you do before coming here to make you so jaded?"

It was an accurate assessment. Lee Sungmin pouted and grumbled, "I think I'm pretty normal."

"PUHAHA!"

Hans clutched his stomach, roaring with laughter. *It's hard pretending to be a kid,* Lee Sungmin thought. He pulled the five axes from his bag and set them down on the ground in front of Hans.

"Buy these, please."

"You shameless bastard. What am I supposed to do with these? The blades are all dull, and the handles are about to snap."

"You can just take the metal and sell it to a blacksmith."

"What kind of idiot blacksmith would buy poorly maintained iron?"

"Aw, come on. They were heavy, and I went through a lot of trouble to bring them here."

"That's your problem. Anyway, I'm not buying them."

He had hoped Hans's apparent fondness for him might be enough, but it seemed their bond wasn't strong enough for him to buy trash. Feigning disappointment, Lee Sungmin let his shoulders slump.

"Stop with the act," Hans grumbled. "By the way, you. Have you ever thought about becoming a mercenary?"

The sudden question came just as Lee Sungmin was lifting his slumped shoulders.

"...A mercenary?"

"Yeah."

A mercenary. The word was familiar to Lee Sungmin. Of course it was. In his previous life, he had been a member of the Mercenary Guild himself. It was one of the easiest paths for a Classless to take, offering several conveniences. You could easily trade spoils through the guild's counters, though they took a commission, and a mercenary tag granted entry into most cities.

It also made receiving 'requests' simple. Guilds existed in nearly every city, offering jobs that allowed a mercenary to earn money and build a reputation, though the available requests depended on one's rank.

However, Lee Sungmin shook his head. "...I don't plan on becoming one just yet."

In his past life, he had been a C-Rank mercenary. It wasn't a particularly high position. In fact, most mercenaries in the guild were C-Rank. That rank was also the limit a Classless could reach. Unless you had some miraculous encounter, an ordinary Classless like the past Lee Sungmin could never climb higher, no matter how hard they struggled.

He figured that even if he joined right now, he would probably be F-Rank at best. The lowest mercenary rank was G. He had started there in his past life and had barely managed to claw his way up to C-Rank. Even within the same rank, there was a significant gap. If one divided it into C+, C, and C-, he had been exactly a C-minus.

Having already gone through that process, Lee Sungmin knew he wouldn't gain much by joining the Mercenary Guild now. It was easy to get into but hard to climb up, and falling down was dangerously easy. A few botched requests and your rank would drop a level.

It was far better to accumulate enough strength before joining. Starting at D-Rank was a world away from starting at G-Rank. The lowest rank he was currently aiming for was D. He had no intention of becoming a mercenary until he possessed the skills to match.

"Hmm. Well, if you don't want to, I won't push it," Hans muttered, glancing at Lee Sungmin. "Becoming a mercenary isn't a bad idea, though. It comes with all sorts of conveniences."

"I'm sure it does."

He already knew. Lee Sungmin listened half-heartedly, sniffing the air around himself. The stench coming from his body was awful.

"Um. Can I go now?"

"Taking what you need and leaving just like that. You charmless little brat."

"Do you like getting cutesy acts from a boy?"

"Of course not. Go on, get out of here."

Hans waved his hand dismissively. Lee Sungmin gave him a deep bow, picked up the bag he had set down, and scurried away.

"Hey! Take your trash with you!" Hans yelled from behind him, but Lee Sungmin ignored it.

*"You stink."*

The jab came the moment he stepped back into the inn.

A girl with pale cheeks and a light dusting of freckles was pinching her nose, her gaze fixed on him. Lee Sungmin was well aware that he reeked, so he offered no excuse for her rebuke.

"I know."

"Hey! You used informal speech again!" the girl snapped in a sharp voice. Her name was Lula, and she was the daughter of Jack, the man who ran the inn.

"We're only a year apart. It's ridiculous to have to call you 'noona' and use formal speech all the time."

"What's so ridiculous? You're younger than me. That makes you my little brother."

"A 'noona' should act like one to be called one," Lee Sungmin grumbled as he set down the bag on his back.

Lula was fifteen, just one year his senior. Still, Lee Sungmin just couldn't bring himself to call her 'noona'. He might look like a fourteen-year-old boy on the outside, but inside, he was a twenty-seven-year-old man. Calling a fifteen-year-old girl 'noona'! That was a matter of pride he absolutely could not compromise on.

"Where's Mister Jack?"

"Dad's in the kitchen. Tonight's dinner is mushroom stew and roasted chicken legs. Sounds delicious, right?" Lula said with a giggle.

Jack's cooking skills were quite excellent. If Lee Sungmin had to name the joys that got him through the day, there were two. One was the progress of his martial arts. The other was the dinner Jack prepared. He always left early in the morning, so he could never eat a fresh, warm breakfast. For two weeks, his breakfast had been fixed as hard rye bread, and his lunch was the same rye bread he packed when leaving the inn.

"What was for breakfast this morning?"

"Toasted bread, fried eggs, and steamed potatoes."

"Sounds like it was good."

"My dad's a great cook. If you left a little later, you could eat breakfast with us," Lula grumbled, swinging her legs from her perch on the table.

Lee Sungmin gave a bitter smile at her words. There was a reason he left so early. The earlier it was, the fewer competitors there were in the forest. As the closest hunting ground to Zenavis, the forest was frequented daily by otherworlders who had decided to live in this world. If he left late, he would run into them, and there was always a chance something troublesome could happen. It was better to get his business done quickly while subsisting on dry rye bread for breakfast and lunch.

*Still,* he thought, *starting tomorrow, I'll be able to hunt a little more efficiently.*

Now that he had the Subspace Pocket, he could pack proper food to eat in the forest. He hadn't brought milk before, worried it might spoil in the hot sun, but starting tomorrow, he would be able to drink it. Milk was important. His current body was in its growth period, and if he didn't eat a balanced diet, it might not develop like his body from his past life.

"Why do you go to the forest to fight every single day? Especially when you always come back stinking like that," Lula asked, clearly bored.

Jack's Inn was located far from the forest, so it didn't get many guests. The few regulars who did come were mostly Jack's patrons stopping for a meal, not lodgers. Naturally, none of them were Lula's age. For her, Lee Sungmin was the first friend her age she had made in a long time.

"I'm an otherworlder. To survive, I have to hunt monsters and earn money."

"Who do you take me for? I know that otherworlders can survive without hunting monsters. You could just get a job!" Lula snapped.

Lee Sungmin could only offer a bitter smile in response. Lula was right. There were many ways for an otherworlder to survive in this damn world, Eria. Becoming a mercenary by hunting monsters, like he had in his past life, was just one of countless methods. Many powerless Classless gave up on improving their lot and sought regular employment.

"How about getting a job at our inn? I think my dad's taken quite a liking to you."

"I don't know how to cook or clean."

That was a lie. Lee Sungmin was extremely skilled at menial chores. When he first became a mercenary, his group had saddled him with all sorts of odd jobs, but he didn't want to do it. He had died and been given a new life. He refused to spend his second chance doing menial labor.

"What if you die doing that?"

"I'll have to try my best not to die."

"Hmph. You think it's as easy as it sounds?"

"Of course I know it's not easy. I almost died today."

"I don't get it. Isn't it better to live comfortably learning the inn business than to risk your life?" Lula grumbled, kicking her feet.

Perhaps she was right. Because Lee Sungmin remembered his past life, he was painfully aware, more than anyone, that his current efforts might be futile.

"Call me when dinner's ready."

Leaving her with those words, Lee Sungmin went up the stairs.

He knew it might all be for nothing. Even so, Lee Sungmin was determined to start over, to try and live a life better than the one he had lost.
