Kim Jonghyun.

The name left Abel's lips in a stiff, clipped tone. He began with the tale of the Ghost Ship that had materialized off the South coast. The navy, tasked with coastal defense, had scrambled to sink the phantom vessel, but their efforts were futile.

Long-range interception magic from the mages dissipated before it could ever reach the hull, and the sailing ships dispatched to challenge it were sent to the depths one by one. When the Ghost Ship finally reached the port, hundreds of Death Knights poured from its decks. They swung their swords without mercy in a tide of pure massacre.

The corpses of the fallen rose immediately as Undead, and in a single instant, the port city became a city of death, inhabited only by the walking dead.

"Who else but Kim Jonghyun could do something like this?" Abel spat.

Lee Sungmin stood blankly, absorbing the horrific story.

"...The first calamity," he muttered, the words barely a whisper.

Abel's head whipped around at the phrase, his gaze demanding an explanation. Lee Sungmin met it and recounted the story he had heard from Jeniela. What she had said with a laugh had now become a grim reality. Kim Jonghyun had contacted Vollander, the lord of the Death Knights, and in exchange for granting them the reversal to humanity they so desperately craved, he had gained the power to command them at will.

*He's fallen. No, is it because he was that desperate?* the Void Monarch mused.

Lee Sungmin couldn't help but agree. The Vollander he had met at sea was a Death Knight, yes, but he was no villain. He had only ever killed pirates, claiming it was merely cleaning up trash, and had never laid a hand on another human.

*It was the same when this Elder was alive. Vollander's goals were different from other inhuman beings and the Black Stars. It must be because he was forced into becoming a Death Knight. Perhaps because he became such a thing, he longed for humanity. That's why... he had no choice but to cling to a way to become human again. Even if it meant slaughtering commoners, something he hadn't done for hundreds of years.*

"What about the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman?" Abel asked, his voice rough after a long silence. It seemed the news of his death had not yet reached him.

"...The Master has passed away."

Abel's expression went slack with disbelief. "...What?"

Lee Sungmin offered no further explanation, simply turning to walk away.

"Where are you going?" Abel called out hurriedly as Lee Sungmin started to pass him.

"I'm going to the South."

"...Do you intend to stop Kim Jonghyun?"

Lee Sungmin didn't answer, but his silence was enough.

"I'll go with you," Abel declared without a moment's hesitation.

At his words, Lee Sungmin looked back at him. "Do you trust me?"

"What's with the sudden bullshit?"

"...I might be a part of The End myself."

"I don't know what you're talking about," Abel shot back. "What does it matter if you might be part of The End? The important thing is that Kim Jonghyun is raising hell right now, and we need to stop him." He glanced toward the Sword Immortal.

"......"

After biting his lower lip, Abel spat. "It's a shame I can't ask for your help."

"This old man thinks it's a shame as well," the Sword Immortal replied with a faint smile.

Abel stared at the Sword Immortal for a moment longer before whipping around to follow Lee Sungmin. As he watched Abel's robes flutter while he strode away, the Void Monarch chuckled.

*He's got quite the fiery personality. Unlike his moronic brother.*

"It'll take months to get to the South from here," Abel said, catching up. "In that time, Kim Jonghyun will have raised even more hell. The entire South might be scorched to the ground by then."

"You don't have to worry about that so much."

Abel frowned at Lee Sungmin's calm tone. Unaware of the Fairy Horse, he heard only the relaxed nonsense of someone who failed to grasp the severity of the situation.

Once they stepped outside the Taoist temple, Lee Sungmin summoned the creature.

"Good heavens." Abel's mouth fell open.

He stared at the ethereal steed, then looked back at Lee Sungmin in utter disbelief. "This is... a Fairy Horse. A horse that can leap between spaces. How can a human have this?"

"I borrowed it from the Fairy Queen."

"And you're worried you might be a part of The End while receiving a favor like this?" Abel spat, his expression one of incredulity. "If you were really part of The End, there's no way the Fairy Queen would show you such kindness. If she did it knowing, then that would mean the Fairy Queen is also part of The End."

"That possibility isn't entirely out of the question," Lee Sungmin replied with a bitter smile, recalling Osla's repeated words. She was friendly to him and respected his choices. If he was part of The End, then Osla, who showed him such favor, might be as well.

"Shove those damn worries aside," Abel snapped, quickly climbing onto the Fairy Horse. "Now's not the time for such thoughts. Even if you might become The End someday, you're not The End right now. Right?"

"I suppose so."

"Then there shouldn't be a problem with going to stop Kim Jonghyun. The city where he is, it's at the southern tip. Germud. Have you been there?"

"No. I've been to Lekbon, though."

Lekbon was the port town Lee Sungmin had visited on his way to Bellador.

"Lekbon... Lekbon," Abel muttered, frowning in thought. "The distance from Germud to Lekbon is quite far. Haven't you been to any other cities in the South?"

"I've been to Urmuri and Devon."

"They're all about the same. Urmuri has the Yokai. Red Ghost, one of the Black Stars, is dead, and Yana is ruling, but... with Kim Jonghyun on a rampage, the atmosphere there is probably tense. I don't want to deal with any trouble, so let's avoid Urmuri. Devon is even farther... Lekbon is the best option."

"Should we go there, then?"

"Please."

Lee Sungmin climbed onto the Fairy Horse behind him. A thought suddenly occurred to him. "Aren't you taking Lloyd with you?"

"That guy's no help. It's better to leave him here. What about you? The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman is dead, sure, but weren't you with the Crimson Archmage?"

"We parted ways in the Fairy Forest. It might have been dangerous."

"We're both the same, then. To think two grown men would end up riding a horse together... It won't take long, will it?"

It was a question that needed no answer. Lee Sungmin pictured Lekbon in his mind, a place he had been before. The Fairy Horse began to snort. Amidst a long sensation of floating, the scenery dissolved and reformed around them.

"Magnificent!" was the first exclamation Abel let out upon their arrival. "To leap such an enormous distance in an instant. It's something absolutely impossible with human magic."

As a mage, he expressed his admiration repeatedly before finally getting off the horse. Lekbon was a city drowning in fear. The late-night bustle of the port town was not one of lively commerce but of frantic escape. People clamored to board the docked ships, their faces etched with panic.

Lee Sungmin reached out and grabbed one of the men rushing by. "What's going on?"

"You crazy bastard!" the man cursed, a large bundle of luggage slung over his back.

Lee Sungmin silently tightened his grip on the man's wrist. The man's expression changed instantly. "Wh-what are you curious about?"

"Why is it so chaotic?"

"Well... that is. Don't you know what happened in Germud?"

That was answer enough. Lee Sungmin released him. Beside him, Abel pulled out his pipe with an annoyed expression.

"News travels fast. It seems the tragedy Kim Jonghyun caused in Germud has already spread here. It's better to evacuate. Staying behind to defend the place would be a foolish thing to do."

Lee Sungmin knew all too well how powerful the Death Knight Legion led by Vollander was. Though he had never fought them, they were stronger than any army or martial sect in this world. Every Death Knight possessed skills that surpassed the realm of the Supreme Peak, and Vollander himself was a monster whose power ranked among the top of the Predators, not far inferior to the Spear King or the Black Dragon Knight.

Beyond them, however, was the problem of Kim Jonghyun himself. Having obtained the power of a Quasi-Demon King, he was only allowed to exist in this world because he had not fully awakened as a Demon Lord. Even so, he wielded a limited version of that power, and on top of that, he was an outstanding Black Mage in his own right. When Abel called defending the city foolish, he had Kim Jonghyun in mind. Needlessly increasing the number of corpses would only make Kim Jonghyun's army that much stronger.

"It takes about a week to get to Germud from here," Abel stated.

"If we hurry, we'll arrive sooner than that."

"No," Abel said, shaking his head. "We shouldn't be in too much of a hurry."

"You need to know your limits. Do you think the two of us can stop Kim Jonghyun and his army of Death Knights?"

"It would be difficult."

"If the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman were here, it might be a different story," Abel grumbled. "It's true we can't give Kim Jonghyun too much time, and throwing surplus forces at a necromancer is a meaningless act."

"Then what are you suggesting we do?"

"In a week, reinforcements to stop Kim Jonghyun will arrive," Abel said, exhaling a long plume of smoke.

Lee Sungmin couldn't quite understand. Abel had just said that throwing more bodies at a necromancer was pointless.

"Kim Jonghyun is more skilled than any other Black Mage in this world. He has the Grimoire, and through a ritual in the forest, he became a being comparable to a Demon Lord. Even if it's only half."

"I know."

This place was too noisy to talk. Abel led Lee Sungmin away from the port, their search for a quiet spot leading them to a dirty back alley. Ignoring Lee Sungmin's watchful eyes, Abel summoned a Broker from Erebrisa.

"Since you gave me a ride on the Fairy Horse, I'll get the carriage."

"Is there really a need to take a carriage?"

"You might be able to run for a long time with your so-called movement arts, but a mage can't. Mages are more delicate beings than you think."

"Honestly, I don't understand," Lee Sungmin said.

"What is it?"

"Wasting unnecessary time."

"It's to avoid a pointless death," Abel retorted. After making his request of the Broker, he turned his pipe upside down and tapped out the ash. "Look, if the two of us charge in there fueled by some overflowing sense of justice, we'll just be throwing our lives away. Of course, we're not some common trash, so if we struggle with our lives on the line, we could probably deal a significant blow to Kim Jonghyun's army. Yes, that's all we could accomplish by throwing away our precious lives."

"So?"

"This is different from the massacre in the North. Kim Jonghyun used an army of Death Knights to annihilate a 'city'. Tens of thousands must have died. In the North... there were many different problems. It was the Vampire Queen's territory, and they also underestimated Kim Jonghyun's true power."

"Are you saying it's different this time?"

"It has to be different. This isn't the Vampire Queen's territory, and Kim Jonghyun has a precedent. Besides... I don't know if it was intentional, but there is a Saint from the 'Church' in the South."

"A Saint?"

"You probably don't know. He's not a very well-known figure."

As they spoke, the Broker from Erebrisa returned with a large carriage. Abel stared at Lee Sungmin. "Do you know how to drive a carriage?"

"Do I have to drive it?"

"How old are you?"

"Are you talking about my physical age, or my mental age?"

"Damn it." Abel grumbled and flicked his fingertips. A small light burst forth, coalescing into a faint human figure. As Abel traced a formula in the air, the summoned familiar climbed into the driver's seat.

"I heard the Church couldn't do much during the subjugation in the North, but not this time," Abel continued. "There's a precedent, and tens of thousands of people have died, so the Church's Saint will definitely make a move. With Death Knights involved, they have no choice but to act. They have a reputation to uphold."

"Do you think they can threaten Kim Jonghyun?"

"They'll at least buy us enough time to crush him," Abel muttered as he entered the carriage.

He settled onto the seat opposite Lee Sungmin, took a deep breath, and shook his head. The familiar cracked the reins, and the carriage began to move.

"Just tell me," Abel said, his voice low. "What on earth happened for the... the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman to die?"
