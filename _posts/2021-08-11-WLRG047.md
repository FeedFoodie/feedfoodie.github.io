"A Chimera?"

Bern's eyes widened as he absorbed the report. He listened intently while Rudd explained what had transpired in the forest, his gaze fixed on Lee Sungmin the entire time. Despite having subjugated an Ogre Chimera alone, Lee Sungmin's body was without a single scratch.

Truthfully, Bern had not thought Lee Sungmin could handle an Ogre by himself. A peak-level master from the Classless was one thing, but even if his talent was genuine, Bern had assumed he would lack practical combat experience. It was precisely why he had sent Rudd along as a precaution, yet, from what he was hearing, Rudd had not even needed to intervene. Lee Sungmin had subdued the Ogre Chimera all by himself.

After staring blankly at Lee Sungmin's face for a long moment, Bern finally spoke.

"...This has become complicated."

"Pardon?" Lee Sungmin tilted his head.

Bern rubbed the bridge of his nose and furrowed his brow. "Originally, I was planning to give you an S-Rank when you returned from the Ogre subjugation, but an Ogre Chimera..."

"Can't you just give him an SS-Rank?" Rudd, who had been listening quietly, offered his opinion.

At his words, Bern sighed. "I can't just hand out an SS-Rank so easily."

"I think subjugating an Ogre Chimera alone is more than enough to warrant it," Rudd countered.

"It's not a decision I can make alone," Bern said, glancing at Lee Sungmin. "Of course, the achievement itself is sufficient for an SS-Rank. However, assigning someone to that rank immediately after they join the Mercenary Guild is unprecedented. It's not a call I can make on my own."

"I don't mind," Lee Sungmin stated plainly. "I have no complaints about being assigned to S-Rank as you originally planned. I can just build up my achievements and raise my rank later."

"...I appreciate you saying that." Bern nodded. An SS-Rank would grant him various conveniences, but Lee Sungmin saw no need to insist. He understood that raising a mercenary's rank required a record of achievements. No matter how powerful the Ogre Chimera was, Bern would likely face intense scrutiny for promoting a newcomer to SS-Rank so suddenly.

"In that case, I'll proceed with the S-Rank as planned," Bern decided, pausing in thought for a moment before nodding again. "And also... as Rudd said, subjugating an Ogre Chimera is an achievement worthy of an SS-Rank. It's just that I can't promote you right away for various reasons."

"I understand."

"It's because I feel bad about it. So... you. You don't have a place to stay, do you? You're lodging on the fourth floor, but that must come with its own inconveniences. I'll give you a house."

"Pardon?"

"A house. There happens to be an empty one."

"Why are you acting so generous?" Rudd sneered. "You're just stuck with a property that won't sell from that time you dabbled in real estate."

Bern let out a dry cough. "Why do you say things that don't need to be said?"

"To get on your nerves."

"Ahem!" Bern coughed again and turned back to Lee Sungmin. "It's... a pretty decent house. The location is good, too. It hasn't sold, mind you. In terms of market price, it's quite expensive... but you're welcome to use it."

"Thank you." Lee Sungmin was not the type to refuse a gift. He had just been thinking that he needed a house. Staying at the Mercenary Guild was not bad, but he wanted a personal space where he could train.

"You're not planning to cover the quest reward with the house, are you?" Rudd asked.

"Do I look like such an unreasonable person?" Bern scowled at him before opening his desk drawer. "The reward offered by the town for the Ogre subjugation was thirty million Eru. Normally, a monster like an Ogre starts at forty million... but we took the request since we needed to verify your skills. We would have ignored it otherwise."

That was just as Lee Sungmin had thought. In his past life, he had never heard of an Ogre Chimera. It seemed that in the timeline he had experienced, the Guild had simply ignored the subjugation request.

"For an S-Rank Mercenary's request, the Guild takes a ten percent commission," Bern continued, "and as for Rudd..."

"I don't need to take any," Rudd said with a laugh, waving his hand. "I didn't do anything. Let's just say I got to see a good show."

And so, the reward that came back to Lee Sungmin was twenty-seven million Eru. Bern immediately wrote out a check for the amount and handed it over.

"It's a check you can use anywhere in Eria, but don't carry the check itself around. It'll be more convenient to deposit it at a bank and get a card made."

"Thank you." Lee Sungmin looked down at the check, a mix of emotions swirling within him. Twenty-seven million Eru. It was an unrealistic sum. When he was a C-Rank Mercenary, the requests he received were, at most, worth around one million Eru, and it was not as if he always got requests like that. Mercenaries were plentiful, but quests were few. The group he belonged to had a great number of C-Rank Mercenaries, which meant he was not always able to secure work.

Those quests were never easy or comfortable, and the money he earned was always squandered on drinking and buying women. He had once thought about saving money, harboring a faint hope that if he saved up, learned better martial arts, and perhaps even magic, he might be able to change his circumstances. Of course, he never saw it through. Things like saving and frugality were completely unsuited to a mercenary's life.

*The money I struggled for a whole year to earn... an S-Rank gets it all at once.*

It felt unreal, but this was his new reality. Lee Sungmin tucked the check securely into his clothes.

"The location of the mansion is..."

"If you draw me a map, I'll find my way there myself."

"...Are you sure? The roads are quite complicated. Behenger is a large city."

"I'll be fine." It was the city he had lived in the longest in his past life. It had barely changed, so a map was all he needed.

Scratching his cheek, Bern took out a piece of paper and a pen. A short while later, he finished drawing the map and handed it to Lee Sungmin. "This is the place. Here's the key."

Lee Sungmin took the key and the map, then left the room. Bern stared blankly at the closed door before shifting his gaze to Rudd.

"How was he?"

"It would be better for you to see for yourself rather than have me tell you."

"Then show me."

At those words, Rudd pulled up a chair and sat in front of Bern. Bern picked up the staff that had been propped up behind him, his lips moving silently. At the same time, he reached out his other hand and placed it on Rudd's head.

"...Mmm." A small groan escaped Bern as he peeked into Rudd's memories. He put down the staff and let out a heavy sigh. "Indeed. He's not someone who should be kept at S-Rank."

"Didn't I tell you? It would be better to just promote him to SS-Rank."

"I'll propose it," Bern replied. Although he was the branch manager of the Behenger Mercenary Guild, that did not mean he was at the pinnacle of power here. Behenger was home to many mercenaries, and the Guild existed to manage them, not rule over them. In fact, the leaders of the large mercenary groups held more influence than he did.

"Zeon will covet him," Bern muttered in a low voice.

{sep}

The house Bern gave him was excessively large for one person. It was a spacious one-story home surrounded by high walls, located not too far from the Mercenary Guild. After unlocking the main gate and stepping inside, the first thing Lee Sungmin did was check the garden.

It was not well-maintained, with weeds growing wild, but he did not care. Whether weeds grew or not was none of his business, as he had no intention of tending to a garden.

*It's spacious.*

He had never lived in a house like this before. The life of an S-Rank Mercenary felt unreal in many ways, but he knew he would get used to it soon enough. The neglect was a bit of a shame, but Lee Sungmin was satisfied just to have his own personal space. From now on, he could practice his Spear Technique here every day.

Before that, however, he opened his Subspace Pocket. He had used up most of the spare spears he carried during the Ogre subjugation. It was not a great loss, since they were just cheap weapons that could be found anywhere.

*I have money now, too.*

It was not as if he needed to spend it right away. He considered buying miracle herbs or martial arts manuals, but he did not have enough for the former and was not keen on the latter. He still had not properly mastered the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, the Violet Dawn Divine Art, or Dancing Shadow Soul Break. Since he already possessed these three crucial martial arts, a spear technique, an inner arts technique, and a movement art, it was natural that he did not covet any others.

"Nebul."

Lee Sungmin quietly called the name. In response, Nebul rose from his shadow, bowing his head deeply.

"What can I do for you?"

"I need a weapon."

"Do you wish to purchase one? Or have one crafted?"

"Which is better?" Lee Sungmin asked.

Nebul smiled faintly. "Each has its pros and cons. If you wish to purchase, I can offer you the best item for your price range, but there's the matter of preference, isn't there? The weapon you use must also fit your hand. No matter how good a weapon is, it's no use if it doesn't suit your taste or feel right in your hands."

"In that case, let's look into having one crafted."

"What is your budget?"

"Twenty-seven million Eru."

"That's not a lot," Nebul answered.

Lee Sungmin was inwardly dumbfounded. Twenty-seven million Eru was hardly pocket change.

"The blacksmiths we broker for all possess the finest skills," Nebul explained. "As such, their prices are bound to be high."

"Is there no way to make it work?"

"It's not difficult. You could lower your standards a bit, or... you could lower your pride."

"...Pride?" Lee Sungmin tilted his head.

Nebul chuckled. "That's how artisans are, isn't it? They're stubborn and have strong pride. As a result, many of them have terrible personalities. As I said before, the blacksmiths we broker for all have the best skills, but skill and character are not necessarily proportional."

"As long as their skill is certain, I don't mind."

"Please wait a moment."

With those words, Nebul disappeared back into Lee Sungmin's shadow. He was about to use up all the money he had just received, but he did not feel it was a waste.

In his past life, Lee Sungmin had also used a spear, but the weapons he wielded were always cheap. For a C-Rank Mercenary, weapons were disposable tools. Whether a weapon was good or bad did not matter, so long as it could be swung and thrust. He had always believed it was more worthwhile to spend money on better alcohol and more expensive women after a quest than on a weapon.

However, now, things were different. A desire he had not felt in a long, long time stirred within him.

He wanted to try holding a good weapon, just once.
