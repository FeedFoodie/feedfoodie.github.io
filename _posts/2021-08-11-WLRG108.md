"...Impressive..."

The voice was dying. Wiji Hoyeon looked down at its source with a blank expression, her gaze falling upon a creature that was no longer human. Its arms were twisted into grotesque shapes, and its entire lower body was gone.

It had been strong. She acknowledged this, her face an impassive mask. In the nine years she had lived in Eria, no opponent had challenged her as much as this monster now expiring at her feet. The nameless creature had possessed power akin to a natural disaster, forcing even her, who had surpassed the might of her father the Heavenly Demon years ago, to fight with all her heart and strength.

"To think a mortal would defeat me," the creature rasped. "A human, one who lives such a short and fleeting life..."

"You were strong," Wiji Hoyeon muttered, a slight fatigue settling over her as she wiped a bead of sweat from her forehead. He had been a truly formidable foe, yet after their long and arduous battle, all she had shed was sweat, not blood. All she felt was a faint weariness, not pain.

Knowing this, the monster let out a dry chuckle.

"I can see it."

Its eyes, which had been leaking a crimson glow, fluttered half-open and stared at Wiji Hoyeon with an unfathomable depth.

"Your destiny. You will be human, yet not human. I see a fate born to be a warlord, yet destined to transcend it and reign."

"What do you mean?"

"Even a calamity has its own path. Your innate talent has twisted fate itself... Hehe! No wonder I couldn't handle a single human. I have no choice but to accept it. In the end, I am just a phantom bound to this place... Now. Take me. To be reaped by you would be an honor."

At its words, Wiji Hoyeon raised her hand, and a great power rose at her gesture. The monster humbly accepted its end as death blossomed without a sound. The creature that had existed at the dungeon's end dispersed into a fine mist, which swirled in the air for a moment before seeping into Wiji Hoyeon. She stood blankly, feeling the power settle inside her. It was a mass of will-less energy, and it naturally followed the flow of her Heavenly Demon Divine Art, becoming another part of her strength.

With the path clear, Wiji Hoyeon moved forward. A massive door stood where the monster had once blocked the way. As she reached out, the Black Dragon Armor flew forward in her stead and pushed the great doors open.

Inside, mountains of gold and treasure were piled high, enough to buy an entire city. Wiji Hoyeon opened the Subspace Pocket tied to her waist, and the vast hoard was sucked entirely inside. Soon, she reached the very end of the treasury, where a single piece of black cloth floated in the air. As Wiji Hoyeon reached for it, the twitching fabric approached her on its own. It soon covered her entire body, molding itself into a form-fitting garment. Though she was clearly clothed, it felt as if she wore nothing at all. She realized then that this garment was the greatest reward the dungeon had to offer.

"That was quite fun," Wiji Hoyeon said, raising a hand. It passed through the empty air before touching her chest.

"It was my first time in a dungeon. More fun than difficult, really. That last guy was incredibly strong. I probably couldn't have won if it were the me from before."

*Was that really true?*

"I'm not an idiot. I have a decent idea of what this dungeon is. I... met a you that wasn't you. One who looked exactly like you and used the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear. You were an interesting opponent. It was a martial art I taught, but facing it directly revealed so many unexpected things. It was just that well-crafted, after all."

Lee Sungmin offered no answer.

He was leaning on his broken spear, watching her back. He kept his mouth shut, but Wiji Hoyeon knew it was him standing there.

"Did you come for this dungeon too?" she asked with a chuckle. "You're a little late, though. I'm the one who defeated the final monster." Her tone made it clear she had no intention of yielding what she had won. She had conquered this dungeon, and everything here was hers to take.

"But if you ask, there's nothing I can't give you. We're friends, aren't we?"

"...No. It's fine, you don't have to."

"Finally speaking, are we?" Wiji Hoyeon laughed loudly and spun around to face him. She was completely unscathed, a stark contrast to Lee Sungmin, who pushed back hair soaked with blood and sweat.

"...I didn't know what to say. I was thinking."

"Haha! You fool. Nine years have passed. We're meeting after nine whole years, and in all that time, you didn't think of a single thing to say to me?"

"I did... think about it."

Just because he had thought about it did not mean the words would come easily. Wiji Hoyeon simply stared at him, a smile playing on her lips.

"Who did you meet?"

"...You."

"Me? Yes, I thought so. I heard a lot of noise from behind me. Still, you survived. I take it you defeated me?"

"It was tough. I almost died several times."

"But you didn't die. Nine years... I think it was a meaningful time for both of us. What about you?"

"It was very meaningful," he said. "And I went through a lot."

"You've gotten stronger."

"I'm not satisfied," Lee Sungmin replied, his gaze falling to his broken spear. He recalled his fight with her doppelganger. He never would have won without the Void Monarch's help. It was only because of the support from the Demonic Energy that he could reproduce the martial prowess he had reached in the Mental World.

"Why not?"

"Because you're stronger than me."

"Haha! You're quite greedy, you know. Are you aiming to become stronger than me?"

"For now."

"For now... does that mean it could change?"

"The world is a wide place."

"That's a good thing. You're certainly different from nine years ago. Back then, you didn't even have a proper goal. You were just trying to survive, and even after coming back from the dead, you had no intention of living some grand life."

"I hated that."

"So you changed?"

"I think I'm in the process of changing."

"Still not there yet, I see." Wiji Hoyeon let out another laugh. Having turned completely, she strode toward him. Lee Sungmin said nothing, watching as her face drew closer. There was so much he wanted to say, so much more than this, but now was not the time. Not yet. The man he was now did not stand on equal footing with her, nor was he at a level where he could proudly speak of his achievements.

*I hate this,* Lee Sungmin thought. It was the first time he had felt this way. He had wanted to show Baek Sogo how much stronger he had become compared to his time on Mount Mushii, how much more outstanding he was. In the end, he could not show her everything, but he was satisfied because he had prevented her death.

Then what about Wiji Hoyeon?

Not yet.

To be honest, that was it. He was afraid of how she would react if he showed her everything right now. He was not looking for simple praise. He wanted to be acknowledged. She had called him a friend, and he thought of her as a friend too.

That was why now was not the time. Not yet. Because they were friends, he wanted to stand on equal footing. To reach that position, he could not remain someone who would be a source of shame to her. He had to be acknowledged by her.

"Is there still a year left until the day we promised?"

"Yeah."

"I remember the place we promised. The date, too. I kept... thinking about it. When I met you again, what would I be like? What should I talk to you about? What would you have become, and what would you say to me?"

"...You've changed a lot."

"So have you. Oh, right. I've been wanting to ask you this if I got the chance. What about my chest?"

"What?"

It was just like nine years ago, a sudden question completely out of the blue. For a moment, Lee Sungmin tilted his head, not understanding what she meant. Wiji Hoyeon, who had closed the distance until she was right in front of him, placed a hand on her chest.

"I don't think they've gotten much bigger compared to the past life you know. What's your preference? Do you like them a bit bigger?"

"...Uh... is my answer important?"

"Well, if possible, I'd prefer what you like."

"I don't think it really matters..."

"Really? That's a relief then. I was debating whether or not to take a Breast Enlargement Pill. Ah, you probably don't know what that is, do you? They say if you take one consistently, your chest will definitely get bigger. No side effects, either, but I thought if I actually made them bigger, it would be inconvenient in many ways..."

"I don't think you need to go that far."

Lee Sungmin genuinely did not care whether her chest was big or not. What did breast size matter anyway? Hearing his answer, Wiji Hoyeon grinned.

"Will you come with me?" she asked.

At her question, Lee Sungmin's expression hardened slightly.

"There are a lot of guys who follow me around, claiming to be my followers. They're annoying. They do things I never asked them to, treating me with expectations they've made up on their own. I... don't like it."

He recalled the death of the Phantom Sword of Dokgo, and the roar of the Demon Wolf Iron Fist, who had charged in, never knowing the one who killed him was Wiji Hoyeon's doppelganger.

"I hate having others' expectations placed on me," she continued. "It was the same when I was in the Demonic Cult. It's disgusting how they arbitrarily expect things, consciously or unconsciously, wanting me to do 'something' for them."

"I won't be any different."

"I know. I know what I'm saying doesn't make sense. In the end, people are bound to place their own expectations on others, but... it's okay for you to expect things from me. Because I have expectations for you, too."

Her eyes sparkled as she spoke. They were the same eyes he had seen in Zenavis nine years ago, eyes that seemed serious but held a hint of mischief.

"I think it would be fun to travel with you. I've done a lot of things, but I feel like I could do even more with you. Yes, I have a feeling. It will definitely be that way."

"...I'm sorry," Lee Sungmin replied after a brief silence.

"I can't do that yet."

"You don't want to travel with me?"

"No, that's not it. It's just... yes. I'm not satisfied with myself."

"You're so greedy." Hearing his answer, Wiji Hoyeon did not press him further. She chuckled and reached out, placing a hand on his shoulder.

"I like you well enough as you are now. You've become stronger than I ever imagined."

"I'm the one who's not satisfied. I can do more. I've seen a place farther away, so I can go further."

"Haha!"

Wiji Hoyeon laughed heartily at his words. She could not fully understand everything he said, but she could feel the strong conviction behind them, and that made her happy. The Lee Sungmin from nine years ago could never have said such things. Yes, people change. She smiled warmly as she looked at the changed man up close.

"Then let's meet again in a year," Wiji Hoyeon added. "At the place we promised."

"I'm going to keep moving forward. Just as you can go further, so can I. I guarantee you, the me in a year will be far more outstanding and stronger than the me of today, and my standards will be higher than they are now. Because I've seen the you of today, here."

After saying that, she lowered the hand from his shoulder and slowly walked past him. Lee Sungmin turned to watch her go.

"See you in a year."

"Yeah."

Hearing his reply, Wiji Hoyeon gave a bright smile that he could not see. She did not want to show it to him. She happily accepted the subtle tremor in her heart, a memory from nine years ago coming to mind. She remembered being summoned in front of the fountain in Zenavis, standing there blankly, not knowing what was happening, and the gaze she had felt upon her. It was a meeting disguised as a coincidence, and she liked that. Her first friend of the same age. For that reason alone, she held more nostalgia for the short time she lived in Zenavis than for any other time in her life.

*I want to be greedy, but I'll endure,* Wiji Hoyeon thought, clenching and unclenching her lowered fist.

If she had her way, she would have taken him by force, but she held back. She might be hated for it, and she wanted to respect the wishes of her one and only friend. There was still so much she wanted to say, so many conversations she wanted to have. This was not the right place, however, and it was not the right time.

*Let's talk more in a year. Let's eat together, drink together. Let's talk about everything openly.*

As she was leaving through the door, a belated thought occurred to her. *Come to think of it, I didn't get to say this.*

The words, 'I missed you'.

She buried them in her heart as well.

A year would not be that long, after all.
