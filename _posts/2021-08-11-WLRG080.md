"Brother."

The voice cut through the morning air just as Lee Sungmin turned twenty-one. He was still at Shaolin, practicing his spear techniques as he did every dawn, when the familiar call made him turn. Namgung Heewon, whom he had not seen in three years, stood there smiling at him. A flicker of surprise crossed Lee Sungmin's face as he lowered his spear.

"Hyung-nim."

The word, spoken for the first time in three years, felt awkward and unfamiliar on his tongue. His agreement to call Namgung Heewon 'Hyung-nim' had been sudden, and he had sequestered himself in Shaolin to train immediately after. Still, that did not mean he had forgotten the promise he had made.

Namgung Heewon's hair was slightly longer than before, at least on the surface. Lee Sungmin studied his face intently, taking in the changes three years had wrought.

"Congratulations," Lee Sungmin said, bowing his head slightly.

At his words, Namgung Heewon let out a hearty laugh and shook the bottle of liquor he was holding. "You haven't gone and become a monk after being cooped up on this dreary mountain for three years, have you?"

"...I still have my hair, don't I?" Lee Sungmin replied with a bitter smile. It occurred to him then that he had not touched a drop of alcohol since regressing to the age of fourteen.

Namgung Heewon plopped down on a nearby rock. "Then let's have a drink. It's been a while. Our connection may be short and shallow, but sworn brothers are sworn brothers, and a little brother is a little brother."

Lee Sungmin settled on a rock near him. Namgung Heewon pulled a cup from his robes and tossed it over. As Lee Sungmin raised a hand to snatch the small cup from the air, he felt the considerable amount of internal energy imbued within it.

"Your jokes are a bit much," Lee Sungmin remarked, examining the fragile cup. The fact that it had not cracked despite the power behind the throw was proof that Namgung Heewon's inner qi had reached a significant level.

"Huhu!" Namgung Heewon laughed pleasantly. "You've improved a lot." He chuckled under his breath, impressed that Lee Sungmin had caught such a cup without relying on any formal martial arts theory.

Lee Sungmin fiddled with the cup as he replied, "You've completed Origin Reversal. Reaching the realm of the Supreme Peak... congratulations."

"I had a sudden enlightenment," Namgung Heewon said. "No, should I even call it that? I just woke up one day and it had happened naturally. The Body Reformation felt like shit, though."

Looking at him now, there was no trace of inner qi about Namgung Heewon. His physique was sturdy from years of wielding a sword, but beyond that physical toughness, there was no sign he had ever practiced martial arts. To have completed both Origin Reversal and Body Reformation meant he had become a transcendent being of the Supreme Peak.

"You've become strange, little brother," Namgung Heewon noted, tossing the liquor bottle to him. This time, it was not thrown with any internal energy. Lee Sungmin filled his cup halfway before returning the bottle.

"Your Taoist energy has stabilized," Namgung Heewon continued. "It's not Origin Reversal, but... it's different from before, when your energy was so wild you couldn't even properly refine it. Seems your training at Shaolin was quite helpful. Or was it the bland temple food?"

"Is that so?"

"Yeah. Your body has matured... and there's something else that's strange. Before, whenever I saw your face, I'd get all excited for no reason, but not anymore." Namgung Heewon tilted his head with a puzzled expression. "Was I just going through a rebellious phase back then? Had a thing for men?"

Lee Sungmin let out a small chuckle at his words. It was thanks to Grandmaster Bulyeong, who had erased the part of the blessing that had lingered on his body. The strange compulsion that had stirred the emotions of others no longer affected him.

"But it's true that I'm happy to see you after so long," Namgung Heewon admitted, "and it's delightful that your skills have improved."

"You didn't visit before," Lee Sungmin pointed out. "Weren't you supposed to come to Shaolin once a year?"

"I'm not shameless enough to visit someone every year when they're focused on their training. I came because I thought you might have achieved something by now... and it seems I was right. You've become incomparably better than you were three years ago, little brother."

"I'm still no match for you, Hyung-nim."

"Of course not," Namgung Heewon grumbled as he filled his own cup. "I've reached the realm of the Supreme Peak, but in this world right now, that's nothing to brag about. There are just too many masters."

They raised their cups. The taste of the Demon God liquor, which he had not had in so long, did not feel as sweet as it once had.

"What do you mean?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"Ah, right. You've been cooped up here, so you must be out of touch with the rumors. Did you know? Scarlet, who came to Shaolin with you back then, has become the Crimson Archmage. I don't know much about magic, but... haha! I know that's amazing. I heard she won an overwhelming victory in a magic duel against the previous one. They say she's the youngest Magic Tower Master in history."

*Ah. Is it already that time?* Lee Sungmin glanced down at the bracelet on his wrist. He had suspected the time was approaching. In the original future, Scarlet was also destined to establish the Reshir School and ascend to her position. For the past three years, the bracelet had remained completely inert.

"The Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon has begun to make her move in earnest," Namgung Heewon continued. "Followers are gathering under her. Is she twenty now? Huhu, I hear she's a great beauty... and with her Chojeol-level skills, it's only natural for people to flock to her. I also hear that the Blood Heavenly Demon of the north is keeping an eye on her."

Lee Sungmin remembered the Little Heavenly Demon and her followers, but the present of this life was quite different from his past. In his previous life, rumors had persisted that Wiji Hoyeon was a man, her gender unconfirmed. The timing of her gathering followers also seemed a bit early. As he recalled, followers had only started gathering around her after she massacred other top masters in a Dungeon and emerged alone.

"Besides them, many other new masters are making a name for themselves. There's the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun, who's on a dueling tour, and Silent Flash Baek Sogo, who's been gaining fame recently for subjugating monsters that terrorize small villages without any reward."

Hearing those names, Lee Sungmin felt his chest tighten. It had been a very long time since he had heard the name Baek Sogo. He stared blankly at Namgung Heewon's face for a moment before asking cautiously, "...Do you know where... Baek Sogo is?"

"Hm? What's this? Little brother, are you interested in the Silent Flash? Rumor has it she's a white-haired beauty." Namgung Heewon shrugged. "I don't know where she is either. Like Dok Bijun, Baek Sogo is said to wander around a lot. The last rumor I heard placed her near the Namgung Clan, so that's a month's travel from here."

Lee Sungmin nodded slowly. Two years remained. When he turned twenty-three, Silent Flash Baek Sogo and the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun would be killed by Wiji Hoyeon in a Dungeon. He had to prevent their deaths. The most ideal solution would be to stop Baek Sogo from ever going to that Dungeon. There was still time before it was discovered, but if possible, he needed to find her location in advance and meet her.

*I can't stop Wiji Hoyeon right now.*

That would likely be true even two years from now. Jihak had called her a monster who had transcended the realm of the Supreme Peak. From the very first day of their duels, Jihak had been a Chojeol master who had completed Origin Reversal, and Wiji Hoyeon had supposedly dealt with him with ease. Lee Sungmin could not even begin to imagine her level.

During his three years at Shaolin, Lee Sungmin had corrected most of his shortcomings. He was still in an unstable state, the imbalance between his mind, energy, and body not yet fixed. However, his methods for coordinating and handling his inner qi had greatly improved, and his dulled senses had been sharpened. In the process, the sixth sense he had obtained on Mount Mushii also grew stronger as his existing senses reawakened. He became accustomed to the martial arts he knew but could not properly execute through repeated duels with Jihak. He even mastered the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear and Martial Soul Detachment, techniques he had half-given up on. Since this was not Mount Mushii, where achievement was guaranteed through ascetic practice, his martial arts had not improved dramatically. He had simply become able to properly execute what he already knew.

Even with just that, his skills had improved immensely. He still could not seize victory against Jihak, but he was no longer defeated as one-sidedly as he had been at the beginning.

"I just came to see your face, and seeing it... it's nice," Namgung Heewon said, refilling his cup. "Even if our connection is short and shallow, there's a certain kind of happiness you can feel because of it."

Lee Sungmin stared intently at Namgung Heewon's face before asking, "Is something troubling you?"

"You're not completely clueless, are you? Thanks for asking."

"I can't carelessly say I'll be of help, but I can listen."

"That's fine. I just wanted to vent anyway." Namgung Heewon placed the liquor bottle in mid-air. It floated up and slowly drifted toward Lee Sungmin. "The Murong Young Lady got married."

Lee Sungmin's expression, which had been one of admiration as he caught the bottle that flew over via Space Manipulation, stiffened slightly. The Murong Young Lady. Snow Flower Moyong Seojin, of the Moyong Clan. He had been introduced to her via Thought Transmission three years ago, when Namgung Heewon had called her the woman he adored.

"...I see."

"Not even going to offer some clumsy words of comfort?"

"Who is the other party?"

"Silver Flying Dragon Jaegal Taeryeong," Namgung Heewon answered, bringing the cup to his lips. It was another person he knew by introduction.

What was he supposed to say? Lee Sungmin stroked the bottle, lost in thought. He searched his memories from his past life, but he had never been in a situation where he had to give advice on such matters. If he had known beforehand, he might have given some sort of hint three years ago, but he could not remember who Moyong Seojin had married in his past life.

"...Uh... um..."

"Huhu! I didn't say it hoping for comfort," Namgung Heewon said, looking down at his empty cup. "Didn't I tell you? I just wanted to vent. Of course, I'm not an idiot. Nor am I clueless. I did confess my feelings to the Murong Young Lady before things turned out this way. I was rejected, though. She said she disliked me for being too arrogant and a thoughtless martial artist."

Indeed. Namgung Heewon was severe in that regard. It was not the arrogance of an heir to a prestigious clan, but an arrogance inherent to him as a person, born from his talent and skill.

"So I tried to change, but a person can't change so easily. Especially an inborn nature. You can't change it even if you want to. I was born and raised as this kind of person, and I only became more so. It's... impossible to change. That's why I tried to act. Wearing clothes that didn't fit." Namgung Heewon muttered as he stood up. "I'm not a clown. I don't have the talent for it. So I wasn't good at acting. In the end, the Murong Young Lady didn't accept me, and she married the Silver Flying Dragon, who is less arrogant than I am. That's the whole story. Huhuhu!"

The laugh he added at the end was laced with thick self-mockery. *People can't change.* Lee Sungmin felt the weight behind those words and watched him with complicated eyes. He too knew very well how hard it was for a person to change.

"So I quit. The clown act. I needed an escape, something to immerse myself in. What I saw in that moment was the sword, and when I came to my senses, I was gripping it and swinging it. Do you understand what I'm trying to say?"

"I don't."

"My sword is hideous," Namgung Heewon's face twisted into a smile. "It's a sword I reached by swinging it as an escape, to soothe the sting of unrequited love. So... I'm not satisfied. It wasn't a sword I swung with joy. How about you, little brother?"

"Pardon?"

"The three years you spent here," Namgung Heewon asked, looking into his eyes. "Your training... was it enjoyable?"

The Namgung Heewon that Lee Sungmin remembered had eyes full of confidence and pride. He had not disliked that arrogant man. Though their acquaintance was brief, their conversations, the short duel, the defeat, and the advice he had received were all deeply engraved in his mind.

"You're asking a question that doesn't suit you."

"It's because of this wound in my heart. It hasn't healed yet. It might even be festering."

"...I... enjoyed it," Lee Sungmin said after a moment. "I think I did. On this mountain, I learned of my own shortcomings."

"Was it enjoyable to learn of your shortcomings?"

"Knowing them and moving forward was enjoyable."

"I'll have to postpone it," Namgung Heewon muttered. "Seeing your refined Taoist energy, I thought I'd like to have a duel. The one three years ago was less fun than I expected, but... I'll postpone it. I don't like my sword right now, and it seems you can go even further, little brother. It might be good to save the enjoyment for later."

Leaving those words behind, Namgung Heewon departed. They had not talked for long, nor had they spent much time together. Not long after he left, Jihak appeared.

"The Sword Dragon Namgung Heewon," Jihak said. "I've heard many stories, but he's an even more outstanding individual than I thought."

"Why are you only coming out now?"

"If we had met, he would have requested a duel. If that happened, I would not have been able to refuse. Whatever the outcome, I myself could accept and enjoy it, but as the future of Shaolin, I must not choose to duel on my own." Jihak said this with a bitter smile. He was proud to be called the future of Shaolin, but that title also strongly restrained him. That was the kind of person Jihak was.

Suddenly, a thought occurred to Lee Sungmin.

*What kind of person am I?*

It was a difficult question to answer. The Lee Sungmin of his past life and the Lee Sungmin of now. They were people with the same name, but they were too different.

"I will use a spear today," Jihak announced.

"Not 'today', shouldn't you say 'today as well'? You used a spear yesterday too."

"It's because I enjoy sparring with you while you hold a spear, Lee Sungmin. The more I face your Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, the more fun it is. It is a well-made, outstanding Spear Technique. There's a large gap between us, yet it's still enjoyable for me. To think she revised such a technique at the mere age of thirteen... the calamity of the Little Heavenly Demon is more wondrous the more I think about it."

During those three years, Lee Sungmin had many conversations with Jihak. He did not talk about regressing from death, but he did tell him about his connection with Wiji Hoyeon and the martial arts he had learned.

The duel this time also ended in Jihak's victory and Lee Sungmin's defeat. It was a defeat he had experienced every day for three years, but Lee Sungmin knew that there was something to be learned even in defeat.

And so, another year of tireless training passed, and Lee Sungmin turned twenty-two.

He descended from Shaolin.
