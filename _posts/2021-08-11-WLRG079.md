He turned twenty.

The years had added height to his frame and a robust strength to his body. He had once worried that the irregular meals and harsh lifestyle on Mount Mushii would leave him smaller than he had been in his past life, but his time in Shaolin had done the opposite, forging his body into something even sturdier than before.

Lee Sungmin had been eighteen when he first arrived, which meant a year and a half had passed under the monastery's watch.

About three years remained until he had to prevent Baek Sogo's death. He wanted to visit Dreamoor and meet the God of Time, Denir, before then if possible, but he felt no desire to leave Shaolin just yet. His daily life here was monotonous, yet it was deeply fulfilling. The time he spent had not allowed him to reach a new level of mastery.

Instead, it forced him to realize anew all the things he had forgotten, the fundamentals he had neglected. It reawakened senses that had grown dull with familiarity and accustomed him to the Spear Technique he had known of but lacked the experience to truly handle. Through his duels with Jihak, he filled the gaping holes in his practical combat sense.

"Am I not a nuisance to you?"

Lee Sungmin had asked Jihak that question once, after one of their usual duels while they were both catching their breath. By then, Jihak no longer treated him like a child, though that certainly did not mean he could win.

"Why would you think I find you a nuisance?"

"I'm… weak, and you're strong, Jihak. Dueling with me probably isn't very helpful for you."

"That's not necessarily true."

Lee Sungmin still remembered the answer Jihak had given him.

"While dueling with you, Sungmin, I am re-examining the martial arts I have learned. Just as you gain new insights into what you already knew through our duels, so do I."

"Does a master like you really need something like that?"

"The martial arts of Shaolin are profound and complex. I've learned seventy-two of them, so I must continue to learn and advance. The path of martial arts is endless, and I want to go even further."

Those words.

"The world is vast," Jihak had continued, his eyes sparkling. "This vast world… The Otherworlders, summoned randomly from all dimensions, have made this world called Eria too wide, and it will continue to expand. As long as Otherworlders continue to be summoned, it has no choice but to grow. The same goes for martial arts."

In that moment, Jihak was less a martial monk of Shaolin and more a young man burning with a dream.

"I'm grateful that the world is vast. I'm grateful that I'm not foolish enough to lament that it's small. In this wide world, there are many people more skilled and outstanding than I am. We only live once, don't we? I'm curious to see just how far a person like me can go."

Lee Sungmin had listened to him blankly. In his eyes, Jihak was already a great figure, a man who possessed talent and capacity that couldn't be compared to his own, yet even a man like Jihak said the world was vast and that he himself was small.

"Lee Sungmin, do you have a dream?"

Ah.

*How small I am.*

The inferiority complex he had harbored for so long suddenly seemed laughable. His despair was pathetic. The version of himself mired in that inferiority, who despaired and collapsed, who thought that while he was used to getting back up, it was never easy…

It was all utterly ridiculous.

He had lived in a delusion. *I have no talent. My starting line was different. If I were given another chance, I would live a different life.* In reality, he was given that chance. So, did he live a different life? He could say that he did, but was it a fulfilling one?

He couldn't answer that question. Six years had passed since he was summoned in Zenavis. In those six years, how hard had he truly tried? How dedicated had he been? How desperate?

Had he tried harder than the calamities who possessed the very talent he so desperately wanted? Had he been more dedicated? More desperate?

Was he fulfilled?

No.

He realized it with a keen, cutting clarity. He had no choice but to. He had been defeated by the Sword Dragon, Namgung Heewon. He had been defeated by Jihak of Shaolin. The sense of defeat he felt from them, the frustration, the self-loathing for his own weakness… and it wasn't just them. He had trembled at his own powerlessness when Preskan subdued him in the Dungeon.

And he had felt all this when he hadn't even lived a fulfilling life.

Jihak, who had lived in Shaolin since childhood, was undoubtedly a calamity. He was the one who had consumed all sorts of miracle herbs and been taught by every martial monk in the monastery. However, Jihak was not arrogant. He wasn't intoxicated by his own power, talent, or possessions. Jihak's life, born a calamity and raised in Shaolin, was more fulfilling, more diligent, more dedicated, and more desperate than Lee Sungmin's had ever been.

The same was likely true for Namgung Heewon. The life he had lived, born into the Namgung Clan, was probably more profound than the one Lee Sungmin had lived. The same went for the Lich, Preskan, who seemed like a madman. His desperate wish was steeped in madness, but Preskan's entire life—abandoning his body to become a Lich, breaking taboos, and being pursued by the Magic Tower—had more depth than his own.

His starting point was different.

He didn't have their kind of talent.

He hadn't learned a superior martial art.

He hadn't eaten any miracle herbs.

They were just excuses. Pathetic excuses. More than anyone, Lee Sungmin himself knew this well. It might have been different if he hadn't returned to the past, but for the Lee Sungmin who had, they were nothing but empty justifications.

He hadn't done it. That was the simple, damning truth. He *had* learned a superior martial art. He *had* eaten miracle herbs. He didn't have talent? What a joke, for someone who had been given the once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to return to the past. All he had done was run away.

Yes.

That's how it had been until now.

"What is your dream?"

Grandmaster Bulyeong, who rarely emerged from his cave, had been appearing outside quite often lately. Lee Sungmin, who had been watching the fading twilight, stared at him blankly for a moment before opening his mouth.

"I don't know."

Just a year ago, he would have had an answer. He would have said he wanted to catch up to Wiji Hoyeon, or that he wanted to see the end of martial arts, but not anymore.

"A year ago, you answered that you wanted to have a dream. You also said you wanted to fly, and that you had learned to crawl. Have you learned how to walk?"

"I have… learned."

"Kekeke! Child, you have only taken a single step. Do you finally understand now? How vague your answer of 'wanting to fly' was?"

*Did I ever make an effort to fly?* The question popped into his head, and Lee Sungmin shook his head. He couldn't honestly say that he had.

"…Grandmaster Bulyeong. Did you… know about my contradiction?"

"Was your twisted state so grand as to be called a contradiction?" Grandmaster Bulyeong laughed loudly. "Child, I do not know everything about you. Even this old monk with divine powers cannot know everything unless you tell me. It is impossible. However, I have seen the martial arts you've learned. Through them, I came to know what kind of inner demons you harbor."

Inner demons. That was what Grandmaster Bulyeong had called it. Lee Sungmin listened to the words blankly. The inferiority complex and all the other rotten emotions he harbored were certainly enough to be called that.

"Child, you knew a Spear Technique that was close to the best, yet you gave up on learning it. You mastered the common basics to a high level, but you turned a blind eye to a truly outstanding Spear Technique. The same was true for most of the martial arts you learned. That is why I told Jihak to guide you."

"…Why didn't you tell me first? If I had known sooner…"

"How long do you intend to rely on others?" Grandmaster Bulyeong's words made Lee Sungmin's expression twitch. "You are loved by a mysterious phenomena. That love of unknown origin acts as a blessing, giving you an advantage. Most humans will feel an inexplicable favor towards you. Are you aware of this?"

Lee Sungmin listened with his mouth half-open. Grandmaster Bulyeong glanced at his expression and continued.

"This favor is a truly ambiguous thing. It may not work on everyone, but most humans will feel favorable towards you. However, Jihak and I, who have properly learned the martial arts of a Buddhist, are not deceived by your blessing."

The words surprised him. He had vaguely thought that Jihak and Grandmaster Bulyeong simply felt a natural favor towards him.

"Through that blessing, you were able to grow close to others more easily and win their hearts. That is what made you arrogant. You stopped doing things for yourself and started expecting others to do them for you."

"I, I…"

"You ask why I didn't tell you? What would have changed if I had? If you couldn't even realize it on your own, would you have tried to change even if I told you?"

Lee Sungmin couldn't answer. As he hesitated, Grandmaster Bulyeong spoke again.

"By comparing yourself to Jihak, you came to know the inner demons you possessed, and you sought to drive them out. Not everyone can change just because they wish for it, but what's important is to decide for yourself that you 'want to change', and you are trying to change. The eighteen-year-old you has become twenty. I'll ask again. What is your dream?"

"…I want to see the end of martial arts."

"That is just a pleasant lie you whisper to yourself. What is your dream?"

"I want to be… acknowledged by Wiji Hoyeon."

"That is also a lie. It's just a plausible-looking goal you hastily tacked on. What is your dream?"

"…I want to change, to transform."

"What?"

"Myself."

When he finally answered, Grandmaster Bulyeong smiled with a pleased expression.

"That is a difficult dream. More difficult than wanting to see the end of martial arts, more difficult than wanting to be acknowledged by the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon. Changing and transforming yourself will be the hardest of all."

Saying that, Grandmaster Bulyeong turned to look at the twilight Lee Sungmin had been watching. After a moment, he spoke again.

"If you wish, I can remove a part of the blessing that envelops your body."

Lee Sungmin's shoulders flinched.

"The choice is yours, child. The blessing itself does you no harm. The baseless favor you receive from others is an emotion akin to an impulse for them. If you leave it as it is, you will be able to receive favor from many people."

"…Please do it."

Lee Sungmin bowed his head deeply. If it could be removed, he wanted it gone. He meant it sincerely. From Baek Sogo, he had learned the preciousness of bonds formed when people truly connect. He had learned about kindness and repayment, but this unknown blessing forcibly tore down the walls between himself and others. In the encounters he'd had so far, he had probably benefited a great deal from it.

He hated that. *Are bonds formed through a blessing not false?* The thought had always lingered in a corner of his mind.

"Very well."

Grandmaster Bulyeong smiled and nodded.

The next morning, Lee Sungmin sat facing him. The process didn't take very long, but when the Grandmaster said, "It is done," his face looked utterly fatigued. It was the first time Lee Sungmin had seen the old monk, who always smiled like a mischievous child, look so exhausted.

"Are you alright?" he asked with concern.

"How could I be? If my spiritual power had been even a little insufficient, I would have died the moment I touched it. It was only because this blessing was not engraved too deeply that I was barely able to erase it."

As he spoke, Grandmaster Bulyeong coughed violently. After a long fit of coughing, he fell flat on his back, panting heavily.

"It's too late for regrets. A blessing that has been erased cannot be put back."

"…Regrets aside, I don't feel any different."

"Of course, you wouldn't. Your blessing was something that acted on others."

After saying that, Grandmaster Bulyeong claimed he was tired and chased Lee Sungmin out. As Lee Sungmin left the cave, he saw the spear and armor stuck in the ground near the entrance. After a year and a half, they had become filthy.

He looked at them for a moment, his gaze settling on the old spear he had tossed aside.

He picked it up. It was time to practice the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear until Jihak arrived.
