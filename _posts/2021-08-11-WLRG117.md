"I believe I told you to get lost, you monster," Baek Museon spat, his breath coming in ragged gasps.

His words did nothing to deter the Vampire Queen. Jeniela simply offered a bewitching smile and drew closer, the long hem of her kimono swaying with each step.

"How cute," she murmured, her voice laced with amusement as she met his murderous gaze.

Even with a severed arm, Baek Museon was a master of the Supreme Peak, his strength on a different plane from his peers. Had his opponent not been the absurd monster Wiji Hoyeon, the man once known as the Blood Heavenly Demon could have easily handled most masters of his rank, but the creature before him now was something else entirely. She was an inhuman monster who had stalked this mad world for centuries, the pinnacle of the Vampire race, Jeniella of Blood Charm herself.

"Is this what you call living?" she purred, closing the distance between them. "Enduring the bitter taste of defeat and the phantom pain of a severed arm, all through a drunken haze? The Blood Heavenly Alliance that once ruled this city has scattered without a trace, and even the loyal followers who remained have grown tired of your broken state and abandoned you."

Baek Museon remained silent, his stillness only seeming to encourage her.

"I like you more now," she whispered, "than the Baek Museon who ran wild as the Blood Heavenly Demon at the height of his power."

A harsh laugh escaped him. "...Haha!" He gripped the empty, limp sleeve of his left arm with his right hand.

"The Blood Heavenly Alliance? The Blood Heavenly Demon? I was nothing but a puppet in the end. Those memories aren't glorious to me."

"A puppet, you say."

"Am I wrong, you monster?" His voice was thick with regret and resentment. He fumbled for a liquor bottle rolling on the floor, uncorked it with his teeth, and drank the strong spirit down like water before letting out a ragged breath. "...Back when the Alliance was intact, when they called me the Blood Heavenly Demon, I didn't even know a monster like you existed."

"I've never liked showing myself on the surface."

"You left me alone," he snarled, "even though you could've gotten rid of me whenever you wanted. Hehe! That's why I was a puppet. Acting all high and mighty, thinking I was so great, without a clue who was pulling the strings from above!"

He drank again. A delighted smile played on Jeniela's lips as she watched him. The inhuman, once human themselves, possessed an inevitable madness born from their long ages, and the Vampire Queen was no different. She found a perverse love in his hostility, in his slow decay.

"You should think about your body," she said, her face now inches from his. Her hand reached out and took the liquor bottle from his grasp.

He let it go without a fight, but a murderous glint flashed in his eyes. In the same instant, his hand shot forward, piercing straight through her chest.

Jeniela made no move to avoid it. She simply looked down at the hand buried in her torso and smiled faintly.

"Are you satisfied?"

"...You monster..."

His attack was an instantaneous killing blow. He clenched his fist, which had protruded from her back, and felt her heart burst in his grasp with a dull thud.

"Always drinking," she chided gently, her voice unwavering. "Not even eating properly. If you keep this up, your body is bound to break down, no matter how outstanding a martial artist you are."

She took his hand, and at her touch, it was slowly drawn from her body. The gaping hole in her chest sealed itself in an instant. With a bright smile, Jeniela wrapped her arms around his shoulders.

"You want revenge, don't you?"

The voice whispering in his ear was a demon's song. His shoulders trembled. It wasn't the first time he had heard this temptation. Jeniela came to him every day, whispering the same words.

"You're getting weaker because you've given up on getting stronger. You know it too, don't you? No matter what you try, you can't possibly defeat that wench, the Little Heavenly Demon."

He offered no reply. He knew it better than anyone. The Little Heavenly Demon he had faced two years ago was a monster, a nemesis. If it were only that, he might not have fallen into such despair. After all, he too had been hailed as a calamity since childhood.

However, she was different. That monster, the Little Heavenly Demon, was different. He had realized it the moment their first blows met two years ago. The very density of the time they experienced was on another level. She was a true calamity, the kind whose absurd talent could inflict despair on an opponent just by existing.

Despair had consumed him. It wasn't that he hadn't thought of revenge. He thought of it even now, but no matter how much liquor he drank, the nightmares never changed. No matter how he struggled, he was powerless against that monster. Even in his dreams, where anything should have been possible, every battle ended in his own miserable defeat. His subconscious had already surrendered, convinced he could 'never' defeat Wiji Hoyeon.

"Your severed arm," Jeniela's hand traced his back, two long fingers walking up his spine to his shoulder. "It'll grow back if you become a Vampire. Your weakness is the weakness of being human. If you become a Vampire, you won't be bound by such things. I like you, Baek Museon. Become one of my vampires, and I promise you this. I can give you strength greater than the Little Heavenly Demon's."

"...I refuse," he finally said.

Jeniela swallowed a laugh. He refused her every day, but she could tell the difference. There was a vast chasm between his first refusal and this one. He was breaking, consumed by despair, and it wouldn't be long before he gave up on everything.

*I love that so much.*

A heat built inside her as she fought back another laugh. She loved watching humans refuse her offer. She loved watching them break as they struggled with their inner conflict, and she savored the process as they chose to abandon their humanity and become blood-sucking monsters. She could never bear to miss the sight of their final choice, the grief and resolve warring in their eyes.

*That's as far as it goes.*

She licked his ear.

*After that, it's no fun.*

It had been the same with the old man she took in a few years ago. The Sword Phantom, was it? She had put in so much effort to make him one of her own, but once he actually turned, the fun was gone. So she had left him alone. Jeniela was always like that. She enjoyed the chase, the time before she got her hands on a toy she coveted. Once she possessed it, the thrill vanished, and boredom set in. She would just leave it be.

*Still, it felt bad.*

Jeniela's eyes narrowed. Even if she had grown tired of him, he was still a toy she had acquired, and now he was broken. She had heard the one who broke him was coming here, to Trabia. Ju Won's concern about the man also weighed on her mind.

*Should I go see what kind of person he is?*

The carriage entered the Central District. Crouched in the cargo bed, Lee Sungmin's thoughts turned to a particular sect.

*The Rogues' Guild.*

They were a representative information sect, often mentioned in the same breath as the Beggars' Gang, but while the Beggars' Gang used beggars for their eyes and ears, the Rogues' Guild employed a variety of professions like coachmen, courtesans, and petty thieves, taking in practitioners of despised trades as its disciples. The Beggars' Gang was part of the Nine Sects One Gang, but the Rogues' Guild was not. By the standards of murim, they belonged to the unorthodox sects.

He couldn't be certain the coachman was a member, but it was a possibility. The man knew martial arts and seemed to be hiding skills that belied his appearance, which made him suspicious. Lee Sungmin didn't believe the man's constant chatter or his invitation to stay at his house were simple acts of kindness. This was Trabia, a city of the rotten Inhuman Demonic Realm. Anyone who approached him here surely had a motive.

"We're here."

The carriage stopped before a high wall that separated the Central District, though it wasn't as tall as the city's outer wall. Originally, this district was where nobles, wealthy merchants, and the middle class lived. This barren northern land had always had a severe gap between the rich and poor, and the treatment of the haves and have-nots was vastly different. The have-nots lived outside the central wall where security and welfare were poor, while the haves lived safely inside.

However, that was a story from the past. Since the Blood Heavenly Demon's defeat two years ago, the definition of a 'have' in Trabia had changed from money to 'power'. Before, money could control violence and purchase protection, but that was no longer the case. While not everything was decided by power alone, having a lot of money was no longer enough to protect it. The Trabia Central District of today was filled with those who had the power to protect what they owned, a place of inhuman monsters, unorthodox martial artists, and practitioners of Dark Magic.

"It'd be better to stay at my place than some inn," the coachman offered again.

"I'm fine."

Lee Sungmin hopped out of the carriage. The gate to the Central District stood open, and like the outer gate, there were no guards in sight. Unlike the outer gate, however, this one wasn't smashed to pieces.

"Well, if you say so," the coachman said with a loud laugh. "Be careful not to die."

The man and his carriage passed through the gate first. Lee Sungmin let him go ahead before starting toward the gate himself.

[What brings you here?]

The voice echoed directly in his mind. Unsurprised, Lee Sungmin scanned his surroundings. He spotted someone hiding in the shadows of the ceiling. It was a high-level concealment technique, but it couldn't fool the sixth sense he now possessed.

"Who are you?"

[Hk.]

The hidden figure flinched at his question, then dropped the concealment technique and landed softly in front of him.

[A vampire, I see,] the Void Monarch muttered.

The man before Lee Sungmin had bright red eyes and pale skin.

"I asked who you are," Lee Sungmin repeated, staring at the man.

"...You're a sharp one."

"I take it you have no intention of answering?"

"Seems like we're both not going to answer," the vampire replied, taking a step back. "Don't be so wary. I was just curious since I've never seen you before."

Lee Sungmin didn't bother trying to gauge the truth in his words. The ancient monster of Predator, the Vampire Queen Jeniela, resided in Trabia. He couldn't tell if this man was one of her subordinates, but he had no intention of asking that here.

"...Can I just go in then?"

"Be my guest," the vampire said with a shrug, giving Lee Sungmin a peculiar look. "You're not a vampire, are you?"

"I'm not."

"A Lycanthrope then? You don't smell like a beast..."

"I'm just a human."

"Hahaha!" The vampire burst out laughing at his answer. "You don't seem to know it yourself, but there's a strange feeling about you. A fellow human might not notice it, but an inhuman would. A feeling like that."

"I don't know what you're talking about."

"Well, if you say you're human, I'll have to take your word for it," the vampire grinned. "Welcome to the city of the Inhuman Demonic Realm."

With that, his body dissolved back into the shadows. Lee Sungmin stared at the spot where the vampire had been for a moment before resuming his steps and passing through the gate.

*The Sword Phantom was a vampire, but he never told me I had such a feeling. That means...*

He sighed. The not-quite-human feeling that Ju Won and now this vampire had sensed wasn't because of the Black Heart.

*Is it you?*

[It seems to be because of my Demonic Energy pooled at the bottom of your qi center,] the Void Monarch confirmed. [Indeed, a human possessing Demonic Energy is bound to give off a strange feeling. Someone who didn't know better might mistake you for a half-demon.]

*This just got annoying,* Lee Sungmin spat inwardly, full of irritation.

The Void Monarch chuckled heartily at his reaction. [It can't be helped, can it? A human using Demonic Energy is something that should never happen. Think of it as the price for using a power that defies reason.]

"Damn it," Lee Sungmin cursed aloud. He still couldn't fully trust the Void Monarch. Though the entity's help had saved him from several fatal crises, its cryptic attitude was more than enough to keep him confused.

[Now, let's keep going,] the Void Monarch said, his voice cheerful despite sensing Lee Sungmin's frustration.

"Son of a bitch."

The sound of the Void Monarch's laughter was infuriating.
