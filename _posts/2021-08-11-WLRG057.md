Something was terribly wrong, a realization that dawned on everyone far too quickly. The Chimeras at the cave's entrance had been mere gatekeepers, their presence a pale prelude to the Dungeon's true danger. Jung Chul's death had been a sudden, unlucky one, as most such deaths were.

Perhaps that quick end would have been better. The suddenness of it would have meant less fear, less pain.

Screams tore through the pitch-black darkness, a chaotic symphony of things colliding, breaking, falling, and spilling. Each cacophony usually ended with a final, piercing shriek, followed by the nauseating stench of blood and the sound of weeping. Neither the light from magic nor the flames of torches could drive away the oppressive gloom.

'Black Mist.' The word Bern had shouted just before the magic bloomed echoed in his mind. Lee Sungmin had no idea what power such a spell possessed, but he was learning with every passing second.

This fog was a suffocating blanket that stole his vision. Attacks lunged from the darkness, and his sense of direction was so scrambled that he could not even tell where the other mercenaries were. Lee Sungmin steadied his breathing, his grip tightening on the shaft of his spear.

Wielding a spear in the dark was a familiar exercise. The sixth sense he had honed in the mountains allowed him to move even when his sight was useless. He reflexively dodged an attack that came from an unseen direction. Was it a monster or a Chimera? Or perhaps it could have been another mercenary, lost and panicked just like him.

Even so, he could not afford hesitation or mercy. Though he could not see, he knew instinctively from the jarring feedback in his hands that he had pierced an opponent's body. As he exhaled and wrenched his spear free, he felt a staggering tremor and heard the heavy sound of something collapsing to the ground.

How many times had he swung his spear, thrust it, and twisted his body to dodge? He had lost count before a sudden flash of light banished the darkness.

Dispelling the Black Mist had taken Bern far longer than expected. He gasped for breath, his face contorting at the horrific scene the light revealed. He had managed to cast the dispel while maintaining a shield magic, but the Black Mist had already served its purpose. The floor was a tangled mess of mercenary and monster corpses.

"The damage is too great," Zeon spat. Despite his words, he and the Corona Mercenary Group appeared completely unharmed.

"Should we retreat then?" Bern asked, his voice laced with irritation.

"We've already taken losses. Wouldn't retreatin' now be an even greater loss?" Zeon countered with a shrug.

The mercenaries had already suffered more than ten casualties. The Black Mist had wiped out all the A-Rank Mercenaries from the other groups, leaving only those from Corona. The remaining force now consisted of two SS-Ranks, Bern and Zeon, eight S-Ranks, including Lee Sungmin, and a single A-Rank freelance mercenary, Tang Misook.

"I, I..." Tang Misook stammered, her face a deathly pale as she sat collapsed on the ground. The repeated tragedies had shattered her spirit. The crotch of her pants was wet, but no one laughed at her.

"...If you're not confident, you can go back," Bern muttered toward her. "But I can't protect you. That's true whether you press forward or run away. We don't have that luxury."

"...Ugh..."

Tang Misook's shoulders trembled violently. She cautiously turned her head, looking back the way they had come. The cave entrance was no longer visible at the end of the long trail of uncleared monster corpses. They had already ventured quite a long way in. They had killed every monster that blocked their path to this point, but would the way back to the entrance truly be safe? After a moment of hesitation, Tang Misook staggered to her feet.

"I-I'll go with you."

No one tried to stop her. The situation was not good. Lee Sungmin chewed on his lower lip. More than ten mercenaries were already dead, and the Dungeon's master, the Black Mage, was undoubtedly lurking deeper inside.

This was a complete departure from the flow of his past life. Back then, they had suffered little damage while subjugating the Black Mage's Dungeon. The place had already been empty, and while a few magic traps remained, none of them had been fatal. The raid had felt more like robbing an empty house, and in the process, the Magic Armor...

The Magic Armor...?

*Wait.*

A strong sense of unease washed over him. Something felt strange. In his past life, the Black Mage had not remained in this Dungeon. So why were various artifacts, including the Magic Armor, left behind? Common sense dictated that one should take valuable items with them when abandoning a Dungeon.

The pieces did not fit. They had discovered the Black Mage's Dungeon three months earlier than in his past life and had set out to subjugate it. Because of that time difference, the Black Mage, who should have already left, was still here. Right. That much was understandable.

However, did something else happen in the following three months? Was there something so urgent that the Black Mage had to leave the Dungeon without his artifacts? No. This was all just speculation.

Amidst his swirling suspicions, the subjugation party continued deeper into the cave. Magic traps kept appearing after the Black Mist, but perhaps because of their harsh experience, Bern now took the lead, carefully detecting and disarming them. Thanks to him, the party's advance was slow, which gave Lee Sungmin the leisure to sort through the thoughts drifting in his mind.

He could not think of a reason to abandon the Magic Armor or the other artifacts. A situation where he had no choice, where he had to leave in a hurry. Was it the subjugation party? In his past life, did the Black Mage choose to flee rather than face them? No, that did not seem right. If anything, the subjugation party in his past life had been much weaker than the current one.

Did he flee because of something else? Or...

"What are you thinkin' so hard about?"

The question came from Zeon. Lee Sungmin flinched, his shoulders trembling as he turned to look at the man. Seeing Zeon's indifferent expression, Lee Sungmin shook his head.

"...It's nothing."

"You don't seem nervous," Zeon observed, staring intently at Lee Sungmin's face before letting out a laugh. "And you don't seem to be drowning in guilt, either."

"...Guilt?"

"Didn't you kill your comrades?" Zeon asked with another laugh.

At those words, Lee Sungmin's eyes grew cold. Killed his comrades. It took no thought to understand what he meant. When the Black Mist had lifted, the mercenary corpses scattered on the floor had told the story. Several of them had been near Lee Sungmin, and what had killed them were spear wounds that pierced through their bodies.

"That's a cruel thing to say," Lee Sungmin replied, looking at Zeon's face.

Weren't they both the same? In a battle where vision was blocked by the Black Mist, mercenaries had attacked other mercenaries, mistaking them for monsters. In the chaos, the weaker ones had died. Zeon himself must have beaten a few of them to death.

"You're cold. Or are you just pretending to be?"

"Isn't it better than being terrified?"

"I quite like you," Zeon said, baring his teeth in a grin. "Seeing you in person like this, I like you even more."

After saying that, Zeon walked past him. They were suspicious words, and Lee Sungmin glared at Zeon's back but said nothing in response. For now, what mattered was breaking through the Dungeon and subjugating the Black Mage.

No more Chimeras appeared. From a certain point on, even the magic traps stopped activating. It was a clearly strange development.

"Were the monsters and traps that poured out at the beginning just a way to buy time?" one of the S-Rank Mercenaries muttered.

His words sounded quite plausible. It was possible the Black Mage had bought time to escape and then fled, giving up on any further resistance. The other mercenaries nodded in agreement, their expressions showing they believed it, but Bern and Zeon did not.

Neither did Lee Sungmin.

The winding path led them underground. There were no stairs, but everyone knew they were descending. In the depths of the windless cave, the torches did not flicker. Only their shadows moved in unison on the torch-lit walls. There were no traps and no monsters. The smell of blood and corpses no longer lingered.

The silence was terribly tedious and frightening. If only something would appear, if only something would happen. One mercenary, who hated the silence, started humming a tune. His skill was not good enough to warrant even forced praise, but no one shouted at him to shut up.

The slope eventually leveled out. When they came face to face with a large, closed door, the humming stopped. Zeon, who had been silent, glanced at Bern.

"Is it a trap?"

Without answering, Bern closed his eyes. He waved his staff lightly, casting a detection magic.

"No. It's just a door."

"I hope we can finish this quickly and get out," Zeon grumbled as he approached the door. "I don't want to set foot in a cave for a while."

The door opened.

Beyond it lay a carpeted corridor. It was a place where someone lived, or had lived, the traces of it strong. Zeon glanced down the hallway and commented, "There are no signs of life."

"I see," Bern said, moving his staff. A widely expanded detection magic scanned the corridor but caught nothing. No traps, nothing moving. Bern furrowed his brow. "Did he really run away? Why?"

"Well, fighting us head-on would've been a burden. Two SS-Rank Mercenaries, and the S-Ranks are still going strong," Zeon grumbled as he walked down the corridor.

There were many doors in the hallway. Lee Sungmin could hear his heart pounding in his chest.

*This is the place.* He recalled the words of the mercenary who had obtained the Magic Armor in his past life, spoken while drunk. A carpeted corridor. A painting hanging on the wall for decoration. The door right next to it. Inside that door...

"Let's search for now," Bern was the first to suggest, though his wariness had not completely faded.

Zeon and the members of the Corona Mercenary Group did not listen. They were already opening nearby doors and rummaging inside. With no signs of life and the detection magic catching nothing, everyone naturally accepted that this place was safe.

Lee Sungmin quietly moved forward. Tang Misook, who had been terrified just moments before, was now running down the corridor as if that had never happened, throwing open doors the others had not yet touched.

He crossed the corridor. *Isn't this too easy? It must be a trap. It's almost certainly a trap. No. Even if I know it's a trap.*

Lee Sungmin opened a door. The other mercenaries were busy with their own searches and paid him no attention. The same went for Zeon, the one Lee Sungmin was most wary of.

The room looked like a study, with nothing particularly noteworthy in sight. Lee Sungmin approached a bookshelf packed tightly with books. None of the titles were eye-catching, and they were not magic books. He desperately wracked his memory as he ran his hand along the spines.

A red book.

The title was 'That Night.'

He did not know what it was about, probably a novel. The content was not important. When he pulled the book from the shelf, the entire bookcase slid to the side, revealing a hidden room. *Will it be there?* Lee Sungmin swallowed hard and stepped into the secret space.

It was there.

A suit of jet-black armor was hanging on the wall.

The Magic Armor, Devnak.
