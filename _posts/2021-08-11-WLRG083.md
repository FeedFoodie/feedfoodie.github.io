At the very time the Blood Heavenly Demon Baek Wooseon lost his left arm and became a cripple, Lee Sungmin arrived in the city of Dreamoor. Where Bredon had been steeped in a Buddhist atmosphere under Shaolin's influence, Dreamoor was a city of glamour. It did not exude an Eastern ambiance as overtly, yet its streets were filled with people in Eastern-style clothing moving between buildings of similar design.

This was a direct reflection of the Mount Hua Sect, which had established itself here. It stood to reason that even if martial artists from various sects were summoned from different dimensions to Eria, they would not follow the traditions of the Central Plains exactly. This was not the Central Plains, after all. Even disciples of sects bearing the same name possessed subtle differences in character and atmosphere, having originated from disparate worlds.

This divergence was even more pronounced among the Nine Sects One Gang, which so valued tradition. While Shaolin had preserved its lineage by maintaining its common traditions without change, Mount Hua was different. The main branch in Dreamoor had not entirely abandoned the path of Taoism, but its influence had been greatly diluted. Over hundreds of years in Eria, the swordsmanship of Mount Hua, gathered from countless dimensions, had been reforged into something new and powerful by collecting only its finest essence. In truth, the Mount Hua Sect of Eria was Mount Hua in name only and had to be seen as an entirely different entity from its Central Plains counterpart.

This became increasingly apparent as he made his way toward the mountain. Lee Sungmin, of course, had never known the Mount Hua of the Central Plains. Still, he could not shake a strange sense of dissonance as he watched Taoists in martial uniforms, embroidered with the symbolic plum blossom, strolling the streets in a manner that seemed utterly unbecoming of their station. The feeling was all the more jarring because he had spent the last fortnight immersed in the quiet tranquility of Shaolin.

A young Taoist walked with his arm wrapped around a woman's waist, his hands roaming freely. A glance into a roadside tavern revealed a group of his brethren drinking and laughing raucously in the middle of the day. These were scenes unimaginable in Shaolin, and Lee Sungmin could only stare, taken aback.

A snatch of conversation from that very tavern snagged his attention.

"That crazy old man. Is he still standing out front of the gate?"

"I saw him there just before I came down."

"The old fool just doesn't get it. Did he really think Mount Hua would grant a duel to someone who just shows up out of the blue? We're not some neighborhood Third-Rate sect."

The words were punctuated by mocking laughter, and Lee Sungmin's steps faltered. He turned his head toward the sound, his eyes landing on the young Taoists in their Mount Hua uniforms, drinking and laughing. One of them, who was just raising his cup, seemed to feel Lee Sungmin's gaze and paused, meeting his eyes. A moment later, the young man rose and cupped his fist.

"I am Woopoong of Mount Hua. May I ask why you're looking at us, Great Warrior?"

*Great Warrior.* The title felt awkward. Lee Sungmin was now twenty-two, and the Taoist addressing him appeared to be around the same age, perhaps slightly older. He hesitated for a moment before shaking his head.

"My apologies if I made you uncomfortable. I'm on my way to Mount Hua, and I happened to overhear your conversation. I looked over without thinking."

"Ah... is that so," the young Taoist said, a hint of tension in his face. "If you don't mind my asking, what business do you have at Mount Hua?"

Lee Sungmin had not yet reached the Supreme Peak or completed Origin Reversal, nor had he corrected the distortions in his mind, ki, and body, but as his martial arts stabilized, his presence had become so well-refined that he carried the air of a master. It was only natural that even a Taoist of the Mount Hua Sect could not treat him lightly.

"...I'm here on an errand from Shaolin."

"Shaolin!" Woopoong cried out in surprise.

He was not alone. The other Taoists also widened their eyes, their gazes fixed on Lee Sungmin. It was not something he needed to hide. Grandmaster Bulyeong had in fact advised him when entrusting him with the letter. Should he encounter any trouble before reaching the sect, he was to mention his connection to Shaolin before resorting to force.

"Are you a secular disciple of Shaolin, Great Warrior?" Woopoong asked, his voice still tight with tension.

Lee Sungmin wore monk's robes but had not shaved his head. He shook his head in response.

"I'm not a disciple. However, I am receiving some teachings from the Abbot, Grandmaster Bulyeong."

"With Grandmaster Bulyeong..." Woopoong let out a low groan. He exchanged a look with the other Taoists before slowly rising to his feet. "You are an honored guest. If it's alright with you, we'll guide you to Mount Hua."

"Ah... there's no need for that."

"No, no. It would be one thing if we didn't know, but now that we do, we can't just stand by."

Woopoong and the others would not take no for an answer. In the end, Lee Sungmin found himself crossing the city with them, heading for Mount Hua. They employed their movement arts, so it did not take long to traverse Dreamoor.

*I had planned to visit Denir before stopping by Mount Hua, but since it's turned out this way, it's probably better to deliver the letter first. I can find Denir at my leisure afterward.*

"...That old man you were talking about earlier," Lee Sungmin asked as they began their ascent. "Who is he?"

They had just arrived at the entrance to Mount Hua and had ceased their movement arts to walk the rest of the way.

"Ah... so you heard that," Woopoong replied. "Great Warrior, have you heard of 'the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun'?"

"Isn't he the swordsman who goes around challenging people to duels?"

"That's the one. Four days ago, that Dok Bijun came to the Mount Hua Sect. He said he wanted to see Mount Hua's sword and abruptly requested a duel with the Sect Leader, but Mount Hua isn't some common, minor sect, and the Sect Leader is a busy man, isn't he?" Woopoong sighed. "Honestly, this isn't the first time. It's common for barbarians confident in their skills to go on dueling pilgrimages, and the Mount Hua Sect has received a few such visits. If we accepted every single request, what would become of the sect's prestige?"

A sense of unease settled over Lee Sungmin. He searched his memories of his past life, and the image of the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun came to mind. The man's dueling pilgrimage was so famous that it remained quite clear in his memory, yet, among those memories, there was no story of him dueling the Mount Hua Sect Leader. If such a fight had occurred, it would have become a massive rumor regardless of the outcome. It seemed that in his past life, the Sword Phantom had also come to Mount Hua seeking a duel, only to be turned away at the gate.

The mountain was more rugged than Mount Song in Bredon. After climbing a high, steep set of stairs, a large gate came into view. Lee Sungmin froze when he saw the white-haired old man standing before it.

"He's still here," Woopoong muttered under his breath.

The Sword Phantom Dok Bijun. Lee Sungmin watched the man's back, hesitating. *Should I pretend to know him?* While he wavered, Woopoong strode toward the gate. At his approach, Dok Bijun spun around, his gaze sweeping past Woopoong to land on Lee Sungmin.

"Hm."

Dok Bijun had not changed much from when Lee Sungmin had last seen him on Mount Mushii. In fact, he looked better. Back then, he had been unkempt, unable to wash properly. Now, his long white hair was tied back neatly and his beard was trimmed, making him look clean and even younger. Lee Sungmin was inwardly impressed. The man's perfectly refined presence was proof of Origin Reversal, yet the blade-like sharpness in his eyes proclaimed him a swordsman of incredible skill.

"You're..." Dok Bijun's mouth opened, his expression surprised as he looked at Lee Sungmin standing among the Taoists. "Aren't you the kid from Mount Mushii?"

Lee Sungmin was stunned. At the time, Dok Bijun had been blind and had never seen his face. Not only that, but seven years had passed since their meeting. His body had grown and his voice had changed, leaving almost no trace of the boy he had been.

"You remember me?"

"Of course, I remember. I could never forget my time on that mountain. I've never had such difficult days in my life."

"But how? You couldn't see back then, Dok Bijun..."

"I remember the inner qi you exuded," Dok Bijun said, nodding in admiration. "You've grown tremendously. You're incomparable to how you were back then."

As they spoke, Woopoong and the other Taoists looked back and forth between them, startled. Dok Bijun stared at Lee Sungmin's face for a moment before glancing at the closed gate.

"Are you trying to go inside?"

"Ah... yes."

"Don't mind me," Dok Bijun said, turning his back to Lee Sungmin. "I'm here on business as well, but it's different from yours." With his back still turned, he spoke again to the hesitating Lee Sungmin. "Go on in."

His words were the push Lee Sungmin needed. He left Dok Bijun behind and entered the gates of Mount Hua with Woopoong and the others. As the gate closed behind them, Woopoong quickly approached him.

"You knew the Sword Phantom?" he asked.

"We... had a connection in the past."

"Ahem. I see," Woopoong muttered with a reluctant expression.

Lee Sungmin ignored him and looked around at the buildings of Mount Hua. It was something he had felt on the streets, but the atmosphere of the main branch was completely different from the tranquility of Shaolin. It was less a difference between Taoist and Buddhist principles and more a result of the unique way the Mount Hua Sect had adapted to life in Eria.

When news arrived that he carried a letter from Shaolin, Lee Sungmin was immediately granted a private audience with the Sect Leader. He knew by rumor that the leader of Mount Hua, the Sword Immortal Daoist Seongha, was one of the foremost sword masters in the Nine Sects One Gang. Meeting him in person, however, Daoist Seongha felt more like a kind neighborhood grandfather.

"I thought Jihak would be the one to come," Daoist Seongha muttered, sitting across from him.

Normally, Jihak would have come. It was only because Lee Sungmin had other business in Dreamoor that he had come instead. As Lee Sungmin opened his mouth to explain, Daoist Seongha shook his head.

"Ah. I'm not trying to blame you. These things happen. Even though Shaolin and Mount Hua are relatively close, a round trip would still take a full month. In truth, it doesn't really matter who comes, as long as I receive the letter."

With that, Daoist Seongha took the letter from Lee Sungmin. He glanced up as he broke the seal.

"You've reached an incredible level for your age, but you don't seem to be a disciple of Shaolin."

"Yes, that's correct."

"But you must have a connection to them. Heh heh! Shaolin is quite fortunate to have a peerless talent like Jihak. I'm just asking, but have you read the contents of this letter?"

"I have not."

"It wouldn't have mattered if you had. It's not like it contains any great secrets. The current Murim Alliance Chairman, Sage Myeongwol of Wudang, announced he's stepping down. This is just a reply to the request for Grandmaster Bulyeong to temporarily take over as Chairman until the next one is chosen."

At those words, Lee Sungmin nodded in understanding.

"Just as I expected," Daoist Seongha said, a deep sigh escaping him. "Grandmaster Bulyeong refused. That stubborn old man."

Lee Sungmin had thought as much. The Grandmaster Bulyeong he knew would never have accepted such an offer. Daoist Seongha burned the letter with a flicker of True Samadhi Fire.

"I heard from the young Taoists who brought you here," he said, his tone shifting. "They said you're acquainted with the Sword Phantom at the gate."

"Ah... yes. We had a chance encounter."

"It's a nuisance. Do you know why I'm avoiding a fight with him?" Daoist Seongha muttered, crossing his arms.

Lee Sungmin recalled what Woopoong had said and nodded. "I heard it's because there would be no end to it if you started accepting duel requests."

"That's true, in a way," Daoist Seongha said, brushing the ash from his hands and frowning. "But that's not the only reason."

He paused, his expression turning serious.

"It's just a rumor, but the Sword Phantom is dirty."

"...What?"

"No. Not dirty, but rather..." Daoist Seongha clicked his tongue softly.

"Dangerous."
