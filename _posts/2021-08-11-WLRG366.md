For you.

The words left me speechless. My mind went completely blank, unable to form a single thought, and so nothing came out.

Wiji Hoyeon chuckled at my frozen gaze. She lowered the hand that had failed to touch me and muttered, her voice like a lament.

"Do you have any idea?" she asked. "What I've come to know? Learning truths I never wanted to learn, realizing my very existence in this world wasn't for my own sake but for someone high above."

"...I know."

"I was angry and frustrated," she continued, "but you know, in the end, I had no choice but to accept it. Well... there was nothing I could do. I just had to."

Once more, Wiji Hoyeon raised her hand to caress my face, and I lifted my own to meet it. Our hands could not touch, passing through each other like smoke. A pain tore through my chest, sharp as any blade.

"There's no one else but me."

The weight of a responsibility he never wanted. I could not truly know Wiji Hoyeon's heart. Our situations were entirely different and had been from the very beginning. I was a disposable pawn, a piece arranged by the Demonic Spirit simply to deceive the eyes of the Spirit. The many gifts I had received were never meant for me. They were for the Slaughter Predator who was supposed to devour my personality and Advent at the time of The End.

The Demonic Spirit's true aim had always been Wiji Hoyeon. Her talent was so absurd that it was the subject of constant talk, and she was destined to reign as a warlord, all under the direct protection of the Demonic Spirit himself. Though his true intentions could not be fully trusted, her life after being summoned to this world had likely flowed according to his will. It was similar to her past life, at least to some extent, but also a little different. Those small changes had compounded over time until now everything was completely different. The role of the Wiji Hoyeon from the past was not the role she played now.

In this world, Wiji Hoyeon's purpose was to end The End. She was the sword forged by the Demonic Spirit, meant to cut down and kill everything that brought about the world's ruin.

*Just what kind of feelings has she lived with until now?*

She said she had no choice but to accept it, that there was nothing she could do because there was no one else. I had never been pushed that far. At least, my decision to stop The End had been my own choice, but had Wiji Hoyeon ever had a choice at all?

Desperation. The word Abel had used now weighed heavily on my chest.

"I've done a lot," Wiji Hoyeon said, a new power entering her voice. "Ten years ago, after I learned everything, after I learned the role I had to play… the role only I could play. I had to do so many things. It was a terrible feeling. Didn't I tell you? I learned too much. At that point, you weren't a monster, but I knew that someday you would become one, and I knew that I would have to do something about you with my own hands."

"...I didn't become a monster."

"That's what I'm most thankful for in this damn world," she said with genuine sincerity.

WHOOSH.

The darkness spreading behind her stirred. Standing in a different space, I couldn't properly see what moved within its depths.

"When you were becoming a monster, I was too far away from you. I asked the Demonic Spirit to do something, anything. I even threatened to kill myself if he didn't. Was I helpful?"

"...Of course," I answered. So that was it. Obtaining the White Fang back then had been thanks to the Demonic Spirit's help. I had annihilated the Slaughter Predator only because I could wield the White Fang at that crucial moment.

"It's later than I thought," Wiji Hoyeon muttered. "To be honest, I… I wanted to finish everything before you woke up, but it wasn't that easy. It took ten years just to wander the Dungeons and annihilate the monsters inside. Right after that, the Spirit Queen tried to Advent. I debated whether to kill the Vampire Queen first or deal with the Spirit Queen, but I decided the Spirit Queen was more urgent and entered the Elemental Realm."

"How were you able to enter the Elemental Realm?"

"This forest is a complex and mysterious place where dimensions are intertwined. It wasn't easy, but I found a path." She glanced behind her. "They have no sense," she mumbled in a small voice. "There's no time."

Wiji Hoyeon turned her body away from me as the Black Dragon Armor covering her shoulders slowly lifted into the air.

"I was going to take it step by step, but I suppose that was just a martial arts theory. Right now, I can't escape from this place."

I didn't know what she was looking at. I clenched my fists and tried to get closer, but I couldn't reach her. Wiji Hoyeon was right before my eyes, yet I could not go to where she stood.

"What should I do?"

She didn't show me her face. Around her shoulders, the pieces of the Black Dragon Armor moved ceaselessly, shooting into the darkness. The sound of destruction rang out as the armor continuously struck down monsters that were invisible to my eyes.

"I failed to kill the Spirit Queen. I should have succeeded. No, even if I failed, it shouldn't have been like this." Her voice trembled. I looked at her lowered hand and saw her tightly clenched fist shaking. "My existence has become bound to this space. Escaping on my own right now is impossible. That's… a very difficult situation. While I'm tied up here, the Spirit Queen will find a way to escape and Advent in this world, and it's not just her. The Vampire Queen, that monstrous wench, is in some ways trickier than the Spirit Queen."

She tried not to show it, but her situation forced her feelings to the surface.

"What should I do?"

Wiji Hoyeon was not made of iron. I knew that fact better than anyone in this world. I knew her well. I knew that while she might seem cold at first glance, her inner self was nothing of the sort. Even if that true self was only visible to me. I couldn't help but feel the suffocating despair that gripped her in this situation. Her role was to stop The End, and she had lived her life that way until now. Failing to kill the Spirit Queen and getting trapped in this unknown space by a sudden spatial erosion was likely the only failure among all her actions so far.

"...I'm here." I reached my hand toward her back, knowing full well it could not touch her. I saw her shoulders, crushed by anger and frustration, by despair and helplessness, by a responsibility forced upon her. "You don't have to despair over your failure. I'm here. Even if you're over there, I'm here in this world."

Wiji Hoyeon looked back at me.

"You said it was for me," I continued. "I want to stop The End just as much as you do. If you can't do it, then I will."

Her eyes went blank. She just stood there dazedly, looking at my face. I tightened my grip on the spear resting on my shoulder.

"I might not be as strong as you, but I'm not weak."

"...Haha!" Wiji Hoyeon burst into laughter at my words. "Right. I know very well that you're not weak. You've even fought and won against me before," she muttered, the corners of her lips turning up in a smile.

"I'll be back before it's too late."

"I'll be waiting."

"If it gets too late… could you come and get me?"

"Absolutely."

She chuckled at my answer. "It's a shame I can't touch you." Turning her back again, she gazed at the writhing darkness and muttered, "I want to touch you. I want to hold you, kiss you, and do many things that are too embarrassing to say out loud."

"...We can."

"The time I spent with you was good." Her laughter deepened. That time was my happiest memory as well. Back then, the Fairy Forest had been peaceful. The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman was still alive, and I had spent all my time with her.

"Can we go back?"

"...Returning to those days would be impossible." The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman was dead. "But we can be together again. We definitely can."

"...Yeah," she replied with a bitter smile. That time was a precious memory for her too. The years she had spent wandering to stop The End spanned a full decade, and what had sustained her during that time were the memories of the days we spent together and the thought of the days we would spend together in the future.

"That's why I'm doing all this." Wiji Hoyeon glared into the darkness. The ancient monsters Sealinged in this place, the Sleeping Forest, were beckoning to her. A chill ran down her entire body. In a way, it might have been called fear, but at this moment, Wiji Hoyeon did not consider it so. "So you're calling for me," she muttered and shrugged.

"What if," I said, sensing a slight possibility. "...If the place you're wandering in exists because of this forest's Sealing. If I break the forest's Sealing… wouldn't you be able to escape?"

"No." She shook her head. "It seems you can't see it." She raised her hand and pointed into the darkness. "Deep in there, a powerful being whose strength even I can't fully grasp is crouching. It seems to be in a very deep sleep, so it hasn't opened its eyes even though I'm running rampant. The Sealing maintained by Kazuya and his clan is to hold that thing back."

"A monster…?"

"It's a being called Greed," she said. *The Demonic Spirit told me so.* She let out a smirk. "If the Slaughter Predator kills and devours everything living in this world, Greed swallows the world itself. Inside its stomach, the world returns to nothing, and everything begins anew."

"...The forest's Sealing is weakening. Everyone except Kazuya is dead."

"But Kazuya isn't dead, and probably… for the time being, the day Kazuya dies won't come. It's not ready yet."

"Ready?"

"It means the harvest isn't done yet."

Wiji Hoyeon said nothing more. She began to walk toward the darkness.

"I hope this is our last farewell," I spat out toward her retreating back.

At my words, she chuckled and turned her head to look at me. As the darkness swallowed her, Wiji Hoyeon gave a bright smile.

"Me too."

Her figure was no longer visible.

Nevertheless, I couldn't leave the spot. I slowly raised my spear. The gaze I had felt upon entering this space was gone, but I could feel a stirring. An inexplicable tremor shook the space little by little. It was the sound of Wiji Hoyeon fighting monsters in the overlapping dimension.

I didn't think about her defeat. I *wanted* to think that way. I couldn't afford to tremble with anxiety. I trusted Wiji Hoyeon, even though I knew nothing for sure about the fight she was in or her situation. I had no choice but to trust her, just as she trusted me. She had no doubt that I would do the things she could no longer do.

I gripped my spear tightly. Still, if it was possible, I wanted to go to where she was by any means necessary. I wanted to fight alongside her and return with her.

I knew it was impossible.

Slowly, I turned around. I left the stirring forest behind me and passed the rock where I had met the Void Monarch.

At the entrance of the forest, Kazuya was sitting slumped over. He raised his head at the sound of my footsteps.

"You're back?"

"......"

"Did you achieve what you wanted?" Kazuya asked, staggering to his feet. I silently reached out and supported his body.

"...I've achieved half of it."

"Is that so…" Kazuya muttered, nodding his head.

I checked his pulse. It was faint, but there was no sign of it stopping. The time had not yet come. I thought about the words Wiji Hoyeon had muttered.

*The harvest isn't done yet.*

This world had already entered the fate of The End. Nevertheless, it had not yet perished. It was just steadily advancing toward its ruin.

It was an unwelcome reprieve.
