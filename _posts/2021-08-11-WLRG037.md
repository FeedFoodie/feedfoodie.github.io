"You're strange," Mushii muttered.

His voice was the one exception that cut through the static binding Lee Sungmin's ears. At the sound, Lee Sungmin halted the motion of his spear and turned. Mushii was watching him, crouched low on the ground.

"It's already been a year and a half since you came to this mountain. You'll have to receive a new restriction soon."

Had it really been that long? Lee Sungmin carelessly brushed aside the bangs that fell across his eyes. What restriction would it be this time? He already carried six, an unprecedented number. Many ascetics visited this mountain, but very few ever left having achieved what they came for.

"You're the first Classless I've seen go this far."

That was to be expected. Most Classless could not even endure the mountain's presence, let alone its trials, but what of martial artists or mages?

"They don't go this far," Mushii chuckled. One had to be exceptionally tenacious to even attempt training here. No, even if they tried, they could not endure it. That was true for both martial artists and mages alike. The one thing a person needed to survive on this mountain was 'desperation'. Without it, they would not last.

"To be honest, I didn't think you'd last even half a year."

That was probably true. A bitter smile touched Lee Sungmin's lips. Without Baek Sogo's help, he would have fled long before six months had passed.

"Is it because you've experienced death once? What do you desire in this life that makes you push yourself so hard?"

Mushii was genuinely curious. As the God of Trials and Asceticism who ruled this mountain, he had witnessed countless ascetics, but he had never encountered a human like Lee Sungmin. To die and return to the past was a rare feat, the mark of a being whose causality had been twisted by the defiance of death.

A better life.

Lee Sungmin mouthed the words in response.

A goal I want to surpass.

He answered next.

And I want to see the end.

That was his final answer. Each one was less a clear goal and more a desperate wish.

"The end," Mushii repeated, his mouth hanging slightly agape. "The end of what?"

Martial arts.

"A mere mortal wishes to see the end of martial arts?" Mushii said with a chuckle. "The time allotted to humans is too short to see the end of anything. If you truly wish to see the end of martial arts, you'd have to cast off the causality of a mortal and become a transcendent being." In other words, it was impossible. The causality of mortality was not something a person could shed simply by wishing for it. Of course, that was not to say there was no precedent.

"All beings possess potential. However, potential is just potential. The thing that makes it bloom is talent. Those with such talent are called a calamity, and even among those few, a being who casts off mortal causality to attain immortality might appear only once every few hundred, or even few thousand, years. Do you think you can do that?"

I don't know.

He did not say he could not do it.

He said he did not know.

Lee Sungmin had changed. People could change, just as Wiji Hoyeon had told him. He knew he had no talent, but he refused to be defeated by that fact any longer.

He used to hate the word 'effort'.

Now, he had grown quite fond of it.

"...You are strange," Mushii muttered again. He slowly rose to his feet and walked toward Lee Sungmin.

"Gods are fickle. All gods with personalities are. Yes. This is purely my whim. A whim, not a revelation," Mushii murmured, his words incomprehensible. Lee Sungmin, who had been swinging his spear, paused as a strange sense of unease crept over him.

"And I'm just talking to myself."

It sounded as if he were making an excuse to someone else. Mushii narrowed his eyes, his gaze fixed on Lee Sungmin.

"There are no coincidences in the world. At least, not in this world. Not in Eria."

What does that mean?

Lee Sungmin mouthed the question, but Mushii spun around as if to avoid seeing it.

"Eria summons humans from all dimensions for no reason. The humans it summons are diverse, and Eria asks nothing of them. Just to live. That's all."

Lee Sungmin knew that much. He knew how unkind this damn world was, but he wondered why Mushii was suddenly bringing it up. He tilted his head, staring at the god's back.

"And yet, all Otherworlders receive the same special privilege. 'Skills.' A convenient privilege that makes learning something very easy. It exists because there is a good reason for it."

What is he talking about? A strong sense of doubt washed over Lee Sungmin, but he could not press Mushii for answers. The god's attitude made it clear he would not reply even if asked. More than that, a gut feeling told Lee Sungmin he should not ask.

"Yes. There's a reason for everything. Just as there's a reason for a twisted causality, there must be a reason why you had to return to the past. Even if it looks like a well-packaged coincidence."

There's something more to this. Lee Sungmin kept his mouth shut. Mushii still had his back to him, just talking to himself as he had said.

"If a return has begun, it must mean something is about to happen that necessitated it. Since you have returned, you must prepare. You must become a being worthy of the reason for your return."

"Alright, that's enough," Mushii muttered, his monologue ceasing. He did not turn back. Instead, he narrowed his eyes and glared up at the sky.

"Stop it," Mushii warned in a low voice. "This is my domain. If you don't wish for mutual destruction, it would be best not to come."

A sudden chill filled the air. Lee Sungmin gasped. When Mushii finally turned back around, the oppressive cold vanished as if it had never been there.

"Leave this mountain, and if you ever get the chance, go to 'Dreamoor'. Tell the 'God of Time' Denir there that Mushii sent you."

God of Time? What's that?

Lee Sungmin mouthed the question. Though he had memories of his past life, he had never heard of a being called the God of Time. Mushii chuckled and reached a hand toward him.

"I suppose a human wouldn't know."

As Mushii spoke, Lee Sungmin's mind suddenly lit up. He staggered, clutching his head as a memory of something he had never experienced embedded itself firmly in his mind. He had never known of a city called Dreamoor, but now he could recall exactly what kind of place it was. He was also now aware of the God of Time, Denir, who lived there in hiding.

Why are you giving this to me?

Lee Sungmin asked. Mushii just giggled in response.

"Didn't I tell you? Gods with personalities are fickle. This is just a simple whim. Just like how I rambled on because I felt like talking to myself," Mushii said, crouching down in his spot again. "You're quite an amusing and interesting being. You don't seem to have any potential... so why did you return? I wondered if there might have been some mistake, but as a god, I know well that there are no such things as coincidences in this world. ...Also. You have overcome my trial."

A trial?

Does he mean living on this mountain? Lee Sungmin tilted his head. Life here was certainly harsh enough to be called a trial. Each day grew more difficult, especially as the number of restrictions increased.

"Flam," Mushii began. "The existence of Flam is the trial of Mushii."

The moment Lee Sungmin heard it, he did not understand.

Flam. The ascetic who had lived on this mountain the longest. Both Baek Sogo and Dok Bijun had warned him not to meet Flam, for to do so was to fall into Despair. They had been right. When Lee Sungmin first met Flam, he had Despaired. He had Despaired at the difference in their innate gifts, at the brilliant talent possessed by a true calamity.

"Flam is the trial of Mushii itself, existing to make the ascetics of this mountain Despair," Mushii explained. "Those who do not Despair because of him struggle and move forward for the sake of their potential. You... did better than I expected. Despite not being a calamity, you threw yourself into even harsher trials so as not to fall behind one. It was an ignorant method, not the right answer, but that doesn't mean it was the wrong one either. In fact, I rather liked your ignorant answer."

Something clicked into place.

The name Flam had never appeared in the memories of his past life. Now that he thought about it, that was strange. Given Flam's nature as a calamity and his arrogant personality, he could have easily made a name for himself after leaving the mountain. So why had Lee Sungmin never heard any rumors about him? For what purpose was Flam training here? And why, despite living here for so long, did he appear to have no restrictions at all?

The answer was simple. Flam was never a real person. He was a trial created by Mushii, existing only to make the ascetics on this mountain Despair. That was the truth of his existence. It had always been that way. Every time Flam had sought him out, he had tried to make Lee Sungmin Despair.

He had mocked Lee Sungmin's efforts and sneered at his training. He had told him to stop because he had no talent. He had scoffed that no matter how hard he tried, he could never catch up to a calamity. His incredibly arrogant way of speaking seemed to desire nothing but Lee Sungmin's Despair.

So... that's it.

Lee Sungmin mouthed. I... understand. Flam had existed for that very purpose from the beginning. He was a trial with the sole objective of making ascetics Despair. Now that he thought about it, Flam had stopped appearing at some point.

It was from the time Lee Sungmin no longer felt Despair.

"You have done well," Mushii said with a smile. "Consider what I've told you... a reward for overcoming the trial. Of course, what you gain from this reward is entirely up to you."

Mushii stood up.

"Baek Sogo. Do not tell that mute girl about Flam's true identity. If you do, I will have to expel you both from this mountain."

With those final words, Mushii disappeared. Lee Sungmin stood still for a long time, organizing the god's words in his mind. There were no such things as coincidences. The Otherworlders were brought to this world for a reason. Likewise, if he had returned to the past, it was because there was a reason he had to.

*For me?*

Lee Sungmin blankly looked down at his own hands.

A Second-Rate martial artist. A C-Rank Mercenary. A Classless who started with nothing. Thirteen years spent living in the gutter.

For someone like me, who lived such a life, there was a reason to return?

One thing stood out amidst the questions.

If you have returned, you must prepare. To become a being worthy of the reason for your return.

Mushii's words echoed in his mind. Lee Sungmin silently raised his spear.

Even if it was not to become a worthy being.

There was an end that Lee Sungmin wanted to see.

the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear unfolded.
