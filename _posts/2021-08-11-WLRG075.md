At his sudden request for a meal, the Shaolin monks readily agreed. Scarlet shot him a smug grin, and Lee Sungmin, feeling needlessly awkward, smacked his lips.

They were guided to a dining hall that was nearly empty, perhaps because it was still too early for lunch. The only other occupants were a handful of monks with shaved heads. Scarlet found herself staring at their completely hairless heads as if they were some strange spectacle until Lee Sungmin nudged her sharply in the side with his elbow.

"That's rude."

"I didn't say anything," Scarlet grumbled.

They found an empty table, and soon a short young monk brought them their food. It was simple temple fare, a little bland, but Scarlet actually preferred it to overly stimulating dishes.

The other monks paid them no mind, allowing Lee Sungmin and Scarlet to eat in a comfortable silence. Their quiet meal was just coming to an end when a voice broke the peace.

"Lee Sungmin."

A young monk approached their table. Lee Sungmin, who had just emptied his plate and was sipping tea, looked up with wide eyes. The monk pressed his palms together and bowed his head deeply.

"The Abbot is calling for you."

"...Me?"

The young monk remained silent.

The Abbot had to be Gwebeol Buryeong Daesa. It seemed Namgung Heewon had already delivered the message. When Lee Sungmin glanced at Scarlet, she was sucking on the end of her chopsticks and gave him a nod.

"Go on."

"What about you, Scarlet?"

"I'll just look around the area. It won't take long, will it?"

"I guess I won't know until we talk."

"Just go and see," Scarlet said, setting her chopsticks down.

Lee Sungmin gave a slight nod and rose to his feet. The moment he stood, the young monk turned and led the way out of the dining hall.

Once outside, his guide immediately set off, but 'walking' was hardly the right word for it. The young monk employed a strange form of movement art, his small body zipping forward with each step. Where normal techniques were about moving the body quickly through space, this one seemed to fold the very space between his feet.

Even for Lee Sungmin, whose inner arts were nearly flawless and who had mastered the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, a technique bordering on the divine, keeping pace was a struggle. The monk led him out through Shaolin's back gate and onto a mountain path.

Lee Sungmin wondered where on earth they were going. He was certain that Namgung Heewon and the other heirs had not used this route to meet the Grandmaster.

"Are we really going to see Grandmaster Bulyeong?" he called out loudly.

"Yes," the young monk replied.

Soon, the path vanished entirely. The young monk darted between trees like a small animal, with Lee Sungmin following close behind. They ran for what felt like a long time, their movement arts carrying them a great distance from Shaolin before the monk finally halted before a small cave, its entrance barely wide enough for one person to squeeze through.

"This is the place."

The young monk turned around.

"Grandmaster Bulyeong is inside."

As he finished speaking, the young monk pressed his palms together and simply vanished, dissolving into a mist before Lee Sungmin's eyes. Lee Sungmin flinched, staring at the empty space where the monk had stood. The mist was already gone, dispersed as if it had never been.

[What are you doing? Not coming inside.]

The voice that rang in his head was not Thought Transmission. Lee Sungmin looked around, but the sound echoed from all directions, making its source impossible to pinpoint.

[Child. It's inside here.]

The voice clicked its tongue. *Inside the cave.* Tense, Lee Sungmin swallowed dryly and slowly walked into the mouth of the cave.

He had to duck to squeeze inside. A faint light shone from deeper within, and Lee Sungmin used it as a guide. The ceiling gradually rose as he walked, and soon he could stand upright.

"Indeed."

There, at the end of the cave, an old monk sat in a meditative posture. His eyes narrowed as Lee Sungmin approached, the deep wrinkles etched into his face and his long beard speaking of the immense passage of time.

"You are loved by the mysterious phenomena," the old monk muttered, a statement whose meaning Lee Sungmin could not begin to grasp.

"...Who are you?"

"You ask even though you know, child. Are you asking because you truly don't know who I am?" the old monk chuckled.

Lee Sungmin hesitated before speaking. "...Gwebeol Buryeong Daesa."

"Correct. I am the Eccentric Buddha, Bulyeong."

Grandmaster Bulyeong let out a laugh. Lee Sungmin had imagined the Abbot of Shaolin to be a high priest with profound spiritual power, but the man before him gave off the impression of an ancient, wizened Yokai.

"What was that just now?"

"It is a Buddhist art," Grandmaster Bulyeong said.

That answer was concerning, but something else the Grandmaster had muttered bothered Lee Sungmin more.

"Mysterious phenomena... what is that?"

"Strange, valor, pervert, Ghost Spirit. It's a term for all the mysterious beings and phenomena that lie beyond human comprehension."

"What do you mean, I'm loved by the mysterious phenomena?"

"It means exactly what it sounds like, child. You embrace the strange, receive the care of the valorous and the protection of the perverse, and are cherished by the Ghost Spirit."

Grandmaster Bulyeong looked pleased as he said this, but Lee Sungmin felt anything but. He had no idea what these mysterious phenomena were, and he had a feeling that pressing for details would get him nowhere.

"Can't you just tell me straight instead of talking in circles?"

"Child, if I were capable of that, I would have shed this mortal body long ago. My soul would have ascended to the heavens, and I'd be sitting on a divine throne. I've lived for a long time, but I still have a long way to go."

So, in the end, he wouldn't get a straight answer this time either. Lee Sungmin's expression soured, and he plopped down right where he stood.

"How did you know?"

"Know what?"

"That mysterious phenomena thing. Don't tell me you can see that my causality is twisted too, Grandmaster Bulyeong?"

"Causality? I don't know what you're talking about, child. The mysterious phenomena I speak of... are just as I said. They are beings and all corresponding phenomena that humans can't understand, things beyond the scope of comprehension."

As he spoke, Grandmaster Bulyeong flashed a mischievous smile. Coming from an old man with such deep wrinkles and a full beard, the expression was strangely fitting.

"...I... have many questions."

"I cannot give you the answers to them. No human within the Cycle of Mortality will be able to give you an answer."

"...Cycle of Mortality... Can a god answer them?"

"If the gods you're talking about are the ones that exist in this world, then no, they won't be able to answer you."

Grandmaster Bulyeong cackled, his laughter and words holding a strange resonance. Lee Sungmin's expression hardened as he asked his next question.

"......"

"Because they're no different from humans. If you want to know what these mysterious phenomena that love you are... well, it'd be faster to try dying once. Then you might get a little closer to them."

"What kind of nonsense is that..." Lee Sungmin muttered.

Grandmaster Bulyeong's eyes fluttered shut. When they opened a moment later, they were pure, milky white, devoid of pupils. Lee Sungmin gasped as their gazes met.

"Go to the North."

The words Grandmaster Bulyeong whispered echoed inside Lee Sungmin's head. His breath caught in his throat. His heart stopped beating. He tried to blink, but his eyelids refused to move.

"The North. Where the eternal snow never melts. If fate allows, you will have a meeting with a nobleman. No. It will certainly happen."

He couldn't make a sound. *Has time... stopped?* No, it was just flowing with excruciating slowness.

"Five years from now. When winter freezes even deeper. At that time."

Those were the last words. The light vanished from the Grandmaster's eyes. He tilted his head for a moment, and just like that, his eyes returned to normal. Time snapped back to its proper speed. Lee Sungmin blinked rapidly, finally closing his gaping mouth.

"What was that just..."

"Do not ask."

Grandmaster Bulyeong shook his head.

"I don't remember what I just said to you. What possessed my body wasn't me, but a Spirit. Keke! It's a bit funny, isn't it? A bald monk like me talking about a Spirit instead of the Buddha."

Lee Sungmin's mind was in chaos. Grandmaster Bulyeong was, without a doubt, the most bizarre person he had ever met. He replayed the words spoken while the man was possessed by that 'Spirit'. *The North, where the eternal snow never melts. Five years from now, when winter freezes even deeper.* Lee Sungmin frowned, trying to make sense of it.

"...Why are you telling me this?"

"Keke! Child, you keep asking things I cannot answer. It's not that I won't answer, it's that I can't. Most beings would find it impossible to answer your questions."

Grandmaster Bulyeong said this and held out his hand.

"The Sword Dragon. I heard the story from that arrogant brat. You have martial arts manuals from Shaolin, don't you? What do you have?"

"...I have the Hundred Steps Divine Fist, Arhat Movement Arts, and Dharma Meditation Art."

"They aren't such great manuals. The representative fist technique of Shaolin is the Hundred Steps Divine Fist, the representative movement arts is the Arhat Movement Arts, and the representative inner arts technique is the Dharma Meditation Art. Do you know what that means? It means they're common as dirt."

Listening to the Grandmaster, Lee Sungmin retrieved the three martial arts manuals from his robes.

"How did you obtain these common things?"

"...A corpse had them."

"That corpse. Who was it?"

"...Zeon."

"Zeon. Right. There was a fellow like that among Shaolin's secular disciples."

That was all the Abbot of Shaolin had to say about Zeon, a secular disciple who had made a name for himself in Behenger. Grandmaster Bulyeong casually tossed the manuals he received over his shoulder. Lee Sungmin could only stare, dumbfounded that the techniques he had carried all this way were treated like trash.

"Here. Take this."

Grandmaster Bulyeong rummaged in his robes and threw something wrapped in paper to Lee Sungmin, who awkwardly caught it with both hands.

"It's a Great Cycle Pill. Eat it," the Grandmaster said, baring his teeth.

At his words, Lee Sungmin flinched and opened the paper. Just as the old monk had said, a Great Cycle Pill sat inside, emitting a refreshing fragrance.

"You said the martial arts were common. Why give me a Great Cycle Pill?"

"The child complains even when I give him something. What, you don't want it? Should I trade it for a Digestion Pill?"

"No, it's not that I don't want it..." Lee Sungmin flinched and shook his head.

Grandmaster Bulyeong laughed loudly at his reaction.

"Wiji Hoyeon."

Lee Sungmin's shoulders twitched at the sudden name.

"About a month ago, a girl named Wiji Hoyeon came to see me."

"...Yes?"

"Wiji Hoyeon. She was called by the title of the Little Heavenly Demon. It's a strange thing. The child I saw, Wiji Hoyeon, was not a child who should be called the Little Heavenly Demon. There are currently three children in this world called 'the Heavenly Demon', including that girl, but in my eyes, the child named Wiji Hoyeon was the one closest to the title of 'the Heavenly Demon'."

Grandmaster Bulyeong said this with a smile. Lee Sungmin recalled what the Sword Dragon, Namgung Heewon, had said. He believed the Crazy Heavenly Demon, Byeong Wonpae, was the one truly suited for the title, yet here was the Abbot of Shaolin claiming that the Little Heavenly Demon, Wiji Hoyeon, was the closest of all.

"That child has the destiny of a warlord. As long as she isn't caught up in some transcendent scheme, that child will rise to become an absolute being. I have lived for many long years, but I have never seen a child with such a powerful heavenly mandate and talent."

"...Why are you telling me this?"

"That child mentioned the name Lee Sungmin."

Grandmaster Bulyeong's eyes curved into crescents.

"I spoke a great deal with that child, Wiji Hoyeon. Her destiny as a warlord and her talent will soon make her the Heavenly Demon who subjugates the myriad demons and reigns over them. It was simple curiosity. What kind of life has a child destined to become a giant lived in this world? What kind of encounters has she had?"

Lee Sungmin remained silent, curious about what the Grandmaster would say next, and even more curious about what Wiji Hoyeon had answered. The fact that she had mentioned his name intrigued him deeply.

"The first friend she ever made. Her only friend. That's what she said. That's why I became interested in the name Lee Sungmin. Though I never imagined that the child named Lee Sungmin would be a being loved by the mysterious phenomena."

Grandmaster Bulyeong chuckled. The old man's laughter and his words made Lee Sungmin's heart feel a little heavy. *Her only friend... Does that mean that even now, almost four years after we parted, she still hasn't made another?*

"I have lived for a long time. My own martial arts are already complete, but with my talent, I could not see the end of martial arts and escape the Cycle of Mortality. So I dabbled in all sorts of trash skills. That's how I got the title Eccentric Buddha. Keke! How could it not be strange for an old monk who is the Abbot of Shaolin to eat meat, drink alcohol, and dabble in Buddhist arts and evil arts? Well, I'm not dissatisfied with my talent that couldn't see the end of martial arts. Thanks to it, I've learned to perform a few wondrous tricks."

Grandmaster Bulyeong raised a hand and stroked his bald head.

"I already knew you would come here. Yes. From a month ago, when Wiji Hoyeon came here... I knew a child named 'Lee Sungmin' would come. So I told that child, Wiji Hoyeon. That in a month, Lee Sungmin would come here."

"...What did... Wiji Hoyeon say in response?"

"She asked what it had to do with her!"

Grandmaster Bulyeong laughed heartily.

"She said it wasn't time to meet you yet, but she still made a request of me. She asked that if you came here, and your skills were nothing special... I should look after your martial arts a little."

There were still six years left until his promised meeting with Wiji Hoyeon. The promise was made when he was fourteen, and they were to meet when he turned twenty-four. He was still only eighteen.

"You are strange. Setting aside being loved by the mysterious phenomena... you are very strange. Your mind, energy, and body are a tangled mess. Child, let me warn you out of concern. If you continue like this, you won't last ten years before you lose everything."

"...Is it qi deviation?"

"Yes. Right now, you are like an explosive that will detonate someday. Even for me, who has lived for so long, I cannot touch your tangled body and soul."

"Then... what should I do?"

"I don't know," Grandmaster Bulyeong said with a shameless face. "Wiji Hoyeon. I took a liking to that child, so I agreed to her request to look after your martial arts. If you wish, you may stay in Shaolin. If you're lucky, you might gain something."

"Are you saying you'll guide me?"

"Fixing your distorted mind, energy, and body is impossible, but I can look over your martial arts. Yes... but I can't lead this old body to guide you one-on-one. So."

"......"

The Grandmaster's Omnidirectional Telepathy echoed far into the distance. Before long, a handsome young man entered the cave.

"That child is the future of Shaolin."

He was handsome, but he was still bald without a single strand of hair on his head. The young man in monk's robes, called 'Jihak', pressed his palms together and bowed his head deeply toward Lee Sungmin and Grandmaster Bulyeong.

"Have a light duel."

*Yesterday and today,* Lee Sungmin thought. *So many people want to duel.*
