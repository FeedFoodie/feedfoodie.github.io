This was Germud, the city where tens of thousands of citizens had been massacred a decade ago. It was the ground where Demon Lord Kim Jonghyun and his Death Knight Legion had once walked, and later, where the maddened Phantom Spear had run rampant, devouring the zombies of those who were once human. The city had endured a horror of unimaginable scale, but that was now ten years in the past.

Once a splendid giant of the South, Germud was now a gloomy, rotting ruin. The tragedy had left behind a dense miasma of poison and demonic energy, rendering the city uninhabitable. It was a barren wasteland of death, a place so thoroughly poisoned that not even a single weed could grow.

As they stepped into such a place, every warrior was on high alert. The tension was made worse by the possibility that some unknown conspiracy by the Blood Alliance was unfolding within its decaying walls. They knew far too little, having come here on the barest of whispers from the Beggars' Gang, and their own attempts to gather more information had yielded nothing.

For a time, the demonic energy and poison had been so potent that not even common refugees dared to settle there. Many Yokai, coveting the residual power, had sought out Germud, but even those known as great yokai could not withstand the city's toxic embrace. Now, the miasma had subsided considerably, making it possible to enter, but this was true for more than just humans. As the poison thinned, Yokai began to filter in, and the number of them roaming near the city grew quite large. This presence drove people even further away, and as a result, rumors about Germud were scarce and unreliable.

What was here? What were they trying to do? Who was behind it all?

"Kuaaak!"

A sudden scream shattered the silence, and the attack began.

*Blood Alliance… The Blood Alliance? These bastards?*

Moyong Chan's eyes trembled as he swung his sword to fight back. Shapes blurred past him, leaving faint afterimages that his eyes, even honed by the Supreme Peak, struggled to track. He caught glimpses of pale skin, crimson eyes, and sharp fangs peeking from behind their lips. They were not human. Yokai?

*Vampire…! Why are there Vampires?*

Had the Blood Alliance joined forces with Vampires? Moyong Chan gritted his teeth and unleashed the Moyong Clan's Secret Sword Art. The Vampires, however, met his blade with effortless grace. They skillfully parried his strikes or danced around his swordsmanship, their own counterattacks lashing out to exploit every momentary gap in his defense. Moyong Chan sucked in a sharp breath, using his movement arts to evade their deadly lunges.

Vampires possessed physical abilities that were overwhelmingly superior to humans. If that were all, they might have been manageable, but these were not ignorant brutes who relied solely on their physical prowess. They fought with a chilling, intelligent precision, and Moyong Chan was not the only one struggling to survive.

KEOHEOONG!

With a great roar, dozens more figures joined the enemy ranks. They were Lycanthropes, and following close behind them were Black Mages clad in dark robes.

The fight was a slaughter. The Vampires and Lycanthropes displayed an overwhelming difference in power, tearing through the hundreds of warriors from the Murim Alliance.

A voice cut through the din, repeating an offer. "We won't kill you if you surrender. We'll treat you as prisoners." Faced with annihilation, the Murim Alliance warriors eventually gave up their resistance.

And so, it came to this.

"How many died?"

"Twenty-three dead and twelve seriously injured. The rest have minor injuries that aren't a concern."

The one reporting was a martial artist from the Blood Alliance, a man known in the unorthodox sects as the Asura Flame Blade. Despite his reputation, he bowed his head deeply as he gave his report to the Vampire who had been listening. The Vampire nodded and approached the Black Mages.

"Is it possible?"

"I can't give you a definite answer," the Black Mage said, sweating coldly. "The Sealing engraved here is extremely complex. It's a Sealing engraved with sorcery, not magic."

The Vampire's expression soured at the Black Mage's lack of confidence. "Do your best."

He could not reprimand the man, however. The Black Mages he had brought were the only ones who could even attempt to undo the Sealing.

*Sealing?*

Moyong Chan and the other prisoners from the Murim Alliance were all bound, their acupoints sealed so they could not even properly circulate their inner arts, but their ears were not blocked. He could not understand what this talk of a Sealing meant.

Most had surrendered at the promise of being treated as prisoners. A few had resisted to the end, but strangely, the Vampires and Lycanthropes had focused on subduing even them rather than killing them. Because of that, the number of dead was not as high as it could have been.

*Why didn't they kill us? And prisoners…*

As Moyong Chan pondered this, Tang Ahee sat slumped nearby, her head bowed low. She cursed her terrible luck. She had planned to return to the Tang Clan after this, to marry into a suitable Noble Clan or a merchant family and live comfortably. To think she would end up in this damn situation on her very last mission.

"......"

A battered Hwangbo Myeongun, who had fought to the very end, shouted, his body soaked in blood and sweat. He glared at a nearby Vampire, his eyes bloodshot.

"What in the world are you trying to do here? Why did you bring us here!"

The Murim Alliance warriors were forced to kneel in the most devastated part of the ruined city. Jegal Young, bound and huddled next to Hwangbo Myeongun, glanced around at her surroundings. In one corner, Lycanthropes sat drinking, while human martial artists bustled around the robed Black Mages. The Vampires stood watch over the bound prisoners.

"And what would you do with that information?" a female Vampire with a cold expression sneered. "Did you really think we'd treat you as prisoners?"

"What?!" Tang Ahee shrieked at the Vampire's mocking words. Her eyes flew wide as she turned to face her. "You said you'd treat us as prisoners if we surrendered!"

"What a naive human," the Vampire said with a laugh. "You're all going to die anyway. Just think of it as bad luck."

"Waaaaah!"

At those words, Tang Ahee began to sob, tears streaming down her face as she realized she might actually die. Moyong Chan ground his teeth and twisted his bound body. He had not struggled to learn martial arts just to die in a place like this, but with his acupoints sealed and his inner arts suppressed, breaking free was nearly impossible.

"...Before we die... let us ask something."

Jegal Young's lips parted. She did her best to steady the tremor in her voice as she looked at the smirking Vampire.

"Isn't it unfair to die without knowing why? What... what are you people doing here? Why didn't you kill us sooner, and instead tie us up like this?"

"You are sacrifices," the Vampire stated simply. The suggestion to surrender had only been to avoid a hassle. "Sacrifices to be used to undo the Sealing on this land."

"Sealing… you say Sealing. What in the world is Sealed here?"

"Rebecca."

The male Vampire who had been talking with the Black Mages called out. The Vampire named Rebecca turned her head.

"It's ready."

"He says it's ready," Rebecca chuckled, looking down at Jegal Young. "Kid. Like I said, you were just unlucky."

She left them with those teasing words and returned to the group of Vampires.

Uwaaaaah!

Hwangbo Myeongun roared and tried to charge at Rebecca's back, but she sent him tumbling to the ground with a light swing of her foot.

*Sealing… Sealing…*

She might die soon, yet, Jegal Young felt more curiosity about what on earth was Sealed in this land than fear of her impending death.

The Black Mages approached. As they raised their hands, the magic circle they had carved into the ground floated up into the air. The glowing circle crossed the space above them and stopped over the heads of the gathered Murim Alliance prisoners. It was a brute-force method, but it was worth a try. If it failed, they could just use another method. To them, the lives of several hundred prisoners were of little value. It was fine if they were there, but it would not be a great loss if they were not.

"Will it work?" Rebecca asked, looking at the Vampire standing beside her.

He was Rao Xian, the fifth vampire of the great Vampire Queen. He held the fifth-highest rank and power among all those she had taken as her own.

"I don't know," Rao Xian muttered, frowning.

Just as the Black Mages lacked conviction, he too was not certain. He had to undo the Sealing on this land one way or another, however. It was for the sake of the wish of his master, the Vampire Queen.

"If it fails, it can't be helped," Rebecca said. "It'll be a pain, but we'll just have to prepare more sacrifices."

"There's no proof that human souls are the right sacrifice to undo this Sealing, is there?"

"Then should we go find the Grand Sorcerer who cast it?" Rao Xian replied in an annoyed tone.

Rebecca shrugged her shoulders in response.

"...If it were the Queen's Eye of Futuresight..."

"Enough," Rao Xian cut in sharply. "The Queen's Eye of Futuresight has lost its power. It's impossible to find the Grand Sorcerer who cast the Sealing with an eye that can no longer see the future."

At his sharp tone, Rebecca asked no more questions. Success or failure, they would find out soon enough.

The Black Mages began to chant. The magic circle floating in the air began to glow, and the bodies of the kneeling prisoners trembled. It was impossible to damage a Sealing engraved on the land itself with physical force, so the solution the Black Mages had devised was to use the souls of sacrifices to deal a spiritual blow to the Sealing itself.

Slowly at first, without rushing, the light faded from the eyes of the prisoners who were already dying from their severe injuries. Though invisible to everyone, the souls of the dead left their bodies, flew toward the Sealing engraved on the land, and struck it.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD, THUD.

THUD, THUD, THUD, THUD…

It was like a persistent knocking on an invisible door.

The sound was irritating, annoying, like being woken from a deep sleep late in the morning. Waking up to that sound was not at all refreshing. His head throbbed and his body felt heavy. He had slept without a single thought, without even a dream, yet he felt as if he had overslept rather than rested. It would have been more refreshing to wake to the chirping of morning birds and the sun streaming through a window, not this unpleasant knocking.

However, even if it was not birdsong and sunlight that woke him, he ultimately had to open his eyes to stop the repeated knocking. He had to open his eyes and throw off the heavy blanket pressing down on his whole body. He had to pull himself out of the deep, sticky, swamp-like bed and answer the impatient visitor who kept knocking on the door.

"You bastard."

THUD, THUD. Amidst the ceaseless knocking, a voice he had not heard in a long time bled through.

"You finally up?"

"Ah."

It was the same sight he had seen just before he completely lost consciousness. The Void Monarch was perched in the middle of the world. Before, the world had been collapsing from mental breakdown and death, but now, it had no cracks whatsoever.

"I thought you'd be getting up soon," the Void Monarch said as he stood. "Because your shattered mind was slowly recovering. It turned out just like you said it would."

"Did you wait for me?"

"I was about to give up and leave, but I got stubborn," the Void Monarch grumbled. "Honestly, I was worried. I thought that when your mind recovered, that damn bastard, the Slaughter Predator, might come back to life too."

However, there was no sign of the Slaughter Predator. He had been like a second personality, a parasite on his consciousness. It might have been different if he were dormant in the subconscious, but he had been struck by the White Fang while fully awakened. Because of that, the Slaughter Predator had been completely annihilated.

"How long are you going to just sit there? Still not fully awake?" the Void Monarch asked. "Want me to smack you one?"

At his words, a small laugh escaped him, and he shook his head.

"No, I'm fine."

Lee Sungmin.

"Let's go."

Stood up.
