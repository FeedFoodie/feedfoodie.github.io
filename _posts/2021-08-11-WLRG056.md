The subjugation force came together with startling speed. Its formation marked the biggest case the Behenger Mercenary Guild had taken on in years, a fact cemented by the direct participation of Bern, the guild's branch manager, and even Zeon, the formidable leader of the Corona Mercenary Group.

Though he had retired from active duty upon becoming the branch manager, Bern was an SS-rank mercenary. He had stagnated, unable to break through the final wall, but his long years of experience and skill were not to be underestimated. The same could be said for Zeon, a deep master of Shaolin martial arts, who arrived with three S-Rank and five A-Rank mercenaries from his own Corona Mercenary Group. They were joined by four S-Rank and ten A-Rank mercenaries from other guilds, as well as two A-Rank freelance mercenaries.

And Lee Sungmin.

As Lee Sungmin surveyed the assembled warriors, he recognized a stark departure from his past life. There were no support mercenaries of B-Rank or lower. Back then, when they had subjugated the Dark Mage's dungeon, the main force had been composed almost entirely of such lower-ranked fighters. That force had been assembled under the assumption that the dungeon was not particularly dangerous.

This time was different. The revelation of the Ogre Chimera had exposed the dungeon's master, the Black Mage, as a truly formidable figure. Consequently, no riff-raff had even bothered applying to join the subjugation.

This was for the better, Lee Sungmin thought. With the threat the Black Mage posed, a stronger force was a clear advantage. His only concern was that with so many powerful individuals present, it would be much harder for him to slip away and claim the benefits for himself.

*No. I still remember the location of the Magic Armor clearly.*

He had never obtained the armor himself, but he knew precisely where it was hidden. If he acted on that memory, he could get his hands on it before anyone else.

Two SS-Ranks. Eight S-Ranks. Seventeen A-Ranks.

The subjugation force, twenty-seven members in total, set out for the Black Mage's dungeon.

The dungeon had reportedly been discovered in a mountain near the village where the Ogre Chimera was first sighted. A dozen carriages rumbled along the road, carrying the mercenaries. The Corona Mercenary Group traveled in their own carriages, so Lee Sungmin could not see Zeon or his subordinates. Instead, he found himself riding with Bern and the two freelance mercenaries. He considered it fortunate that he had built a decent rapport with the branch manager. Whether that connection was due to his strange ability or not, he couldn't say, but friendship always bred goodwill.

"Well, I'll be. Didn't expect to meet the celebrity of Behenger like this," one of the freelancers muttered from across the carriage. He was a giant of a man with broad shoulders and bulging muscles. Jung Chul. Lee Sungmin remembered the name. He was an A-Rank mercenary, a practitioner of external arts who belonged to no guild.

"Really. You're younger than I thought," the other freelancer added. "How old are you?"

Tang Misook. She was also an A-Rank freelancer and claimed to be from the prestigious Tang Clan of the murim world, though no one but her knew if it was true. Most suspected it was a lie. If she were truly a member of the Tang Clan, she would have no need to do dirty mercenary work. Still, she insisted her name was Tang Misook and that she was from the Tang Clan, and since she skillfully used hidden weapons, everyone simply went along with it.

"I'm eighteen," Lee Sungmin replied.

"I heard you're Classless," Jung Chul mused with a chuckle. "I thought only a monster like Zeon could break through the wall of the peak realm as a Classless. I guess a guy who's destined for success will make it no matter what."

Lee Sungmin offered no particular reaction to his words. Beside him, Bern, who had been meditating, narrowed his eyes.

"How about you stop the useless chatter and circulate your inner arts? We'll have to enter the dungeon as soon as we arrive."

"I know, I know," Jung Chul grumbled, closing his eyes. "A Black Mage's dungeon… It'd be nice if I could pick up an artifact or two."

Tang Misook also fell silent and closed her eyes to meditate. Lee Sungmin clenched and unclenched his fists, his gaze drifting out the window.

It was still dawn. They would arrive around noon and enter the dungeon immediately. He had more to consider than just the Black Mage. There was Zeon. He had to remain wary of him. Zeon had made no move for the past three months, but there was no guarantee that would continue.

And then.

By the time his sixth sense screamed that something was wrong, it had already begun.

A dungeon was typically a natural disaster, appearing suddenly and without warning. It was a crystallization of the illogical, something humans could not predict, but this place was different. This dungeon was made by a 'human'. To be precise, it was less a dungeon and more a workshop, created by the Black Mage to conceal his residence and protect his research. To protect himself.

The meaning was clear. This dungeon was designed from a human's perspective, thoroughly engineered to fend off attacks from other humans.

The first to die was Jung Chul.

A master of external arts, Jung Chul was stuck at A-Rank, unable to overcome the wall of the peak realm, but he was a man who took immense pride in his craft. He walked at the forefront of the group, never doubting the toughness of his own body.

His body was torn apart in an instant.

A black arrow materialized from the gloom and tore through Jung Chul's chest. A martial artist of his level could not manifest a Protective Qi Aura. The arrow, fired with incredible speed, pierced straight through the flesh he had spent a lifetime training, and his body staggered violently.

"What the?!" someone cried out in surprise.

Jung Chul had not even hit the ground. The shadows pooled beneath the torches flickered and danced. Before the arrow-pierced man could even utter a word, something shot out from them and lunged.

HUDUDUK!

Jung Chul's body, honed by external arts, was crushed like soft tofu. His entrails spilled out as blood splattered across the stone floor. Heeding the sharp warning of his sixth sense, Lee Sungmin instantly drew his spear, its Black Luster Steel and Mithril gleaming in the darkness.

"Jung Chul!" Bern roared.

He swung his staff wide, unleashing a burst of light that swallowed the torchlight and illuminated the entire cave.

A monster covered in a black carapace stood atop Jung Chul's mangled corpse. It resembled a praying mantis, but its form was incomparably more grotesque.

"A Chimera, is it?" Zeon muttered, his brow furrowed. Many monsters looked similar to a praying mantis, but he had no memory of one with this particular form.

Bern nodded, his face stiff. "The fact that a Chimera is guarding this place means the Black Mage is still here."

As he spoke, Zeon raised both hands. A master of the Hundred Steps Divine Fist, he used no weapons. His two fists, his very body, were his arsenal.

The sphere of light Bern had created floated high against the ceiling, revealing the depths of the cave. Bizarre and varied forms of Chimeras swarmed within.

"A tough spot right from the entrance," Zeon remarked, though his face showed no sign of concern.

Lee Sungmin stood beside Bern, spear in hand, observing the situation. The Chimera that had killed Jung Chul raised the scythes on its arms and brandished them at Zeon in a clear threat.

"You damn bug," Zeon muttered and strode forward. A golden light flashed from his raised left fist as he thrust it forward.

PWAAANG!

An invisible fist strike shot across the distance, slamming into the Chimera and throwing it backward. That was the Hundred Steps Divine Fist of Shaolin, so named because it could strike an enemy a hundred paces away.

Though knocked back, the Chimera did not die. It quickly regained its posture and tried to leap at Zeon, who immediately thrust his right fist forward.

PWAGAK!

The solid crack of impact served as its death cry as the Chimera's body collapsed.

"Let's go," Zeon spat.

The mercenaries, who had been flustered by Jung Chul's sudden death, regained their confidence at Zeon's display. The S-Rank mercenaries leaped forward, followed closely by the A-Ranks. Bern raised his staff at the rear to prepare a spell while Lee Sungmin also began to move.

As he did, he felt a gaze lock onto him. It was a warning from his sixth sense. He glanced in its direction and saw Zeon staring right at him, clenching and unclenching his fists. Lee Sungmin was about to unleash the Dancing Shadow Soul Break but stopped himself.

Instead, he used the One Thunder Movement Arts to dash forward.

*I can't show him everything. I have to hold back.*

It had been a while since he had used the technique, but perhaps due to the improvement in his inner arts, it demonstrated a speed incomparable to before.

Reaching the front line, Lee Sungmin swung his spear wide. He had no intention of revealing his full strength. Just as he concealed the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, he planned to hide the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear as well. He couldn't hide too much, however, lest they notice he was holding back. He decided to appropriately mix the Soul Chasing Spear Art with the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, focusing for now on the basics rather than flashy techniques.

CHAL.

His spear shot out in a straight line, piercing a Chimera's head. He felt some resistance, but it was nothing compared to the toughness of the Ogre Chimera. A look around showed the other S-Rank and A-Rank mercenaries fighting fiercely. This was still just the entrance. The Chimeras guarding this place were mere gatekeepers.

"Get down!" Bern roared from the back.

His casting was complete. Lee Sungmin immediately pulled back and lowered his head, and the other mercenaries did the same. There could be no more ridiculous death than being caught in an ally's magic.

KWAAAAA!

A crimson flame erupted, wrapping around the cave walls and swallowing the Chimeras whole. They trembled and writhed within the inferno. When the blaze, which had raged for a long moment, finally vanished, not a single Chimera was left. The firepower of a mage nearing the level of an archmage had annihilated dozens of them in an instant.

"So much for martial arts theory," Zeon sneered, but Bern showed no reaction. He let out a deep breath and threw back the hood of his robe.

"It seems the Black Mage is still here, but there's nothing to worry about! Let's go!"

At Bern's shout, Zeon shrugged and led the Corona Mercenary Group forward. The mercenaries from other groups also gathered together. Lee Sungmin moved to Bern's side, and the freelancer Tang Misook quickly stuck close to them.

"What should we do about the body?" she asked.

"We'll retrieve it later," Bern replied without looking at Jung Chul's corpse.

The man had been unlucky. That was all there was to his death.

Death was usually like that.
