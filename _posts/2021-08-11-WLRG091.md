Lee Sungmin raised his head, and there she stood not far away. It was Silent Flash Baek Sogo, an armful of flowers cradled against her. The wind stirred her pure white hair, a color that seemed out of place for her age, and her martial arts uniform was immaculate, a stark contrast to the worn-out clothes he remembered from Mount Mushii.

"...Senior Sister...?"

The name escaped him in a breath of surprise. At his call, Baek Sogo said nothing, her gaze simply fixed on his face for a long moment before she finally started forward. With each small footstep, a deeper melancholy seemed to settle over her features.

"Why... why are you here, Senior Sister?"

"I heard a rumor," Baek Sogo answered, her voice small. Her gaze shifted from Lee Sungmin to the fallen corpse of the Sword Phantom. Even as a Vampire, the Sword Phantom could not recover after igniting his life qi and suffering such fatal wounds.

He was truly dead.

Her steps halted before the corpse.

"...Should I lament... that he was a fool?"

The question seemed directed not at Lee Sungmin, but at herself. Lee Sungmin could find no words, lowering his spear in silence.

"...It's been a while, Junior Disciple. Five years... right? I never thought I'd meet you again in a place like this, for a reason like this."

Baek Sogo's melancholic eyes turned back to him. Lee Sungmin hadn't expected this reunion either.

"You've gotten stronger, Junior Disciple," she said, nodding her head.

"This might sound... a little cruel," she continued, "but I've been watching your fight with the Sword Phantom Elder for a while now. I also saw him push you into a corner."

"Why would you find that cruel?"

"Because if you'd kept fighting like that, he would have killed you."

"But I wasn't killed."

"I was standing by and watching," Baek Sogo murmured, her voice barely a whisper.

"I felt I shouldn't interfere. The difference in your skills was clear, but still... I couldn't bring myself to step in. I thought that if I interfered and got in your way, it would be an insult to the battle between you and the Sword Phantom Elder."

"I have no intention of blaming you."

He understood her feelings, for Baek Sogo was a martial artist to her core. Born and raised in the murim and summoned to Eria, she possessed a rigid honor in such matters. She was the type of person who, even if her inaction led to an irreversible outcome, would grieve but never regret her choice.

"Because I didn't die."

*Wouldn't it have been right for me to die?*

The thought flickered through his mind for a moment, but he immediately extinguished it. Baek Sogo looked at his face, then slowly raised the corners of her mouth into a faint smile.

"...So... I think it's a relief. If you had died here, Junior Disciple, I wouldn't have stopped your death... but I would have been terribly sad afterward."

"Would you have avenged me?" Lee Sungmin asked without realizing it. He thought it a mischievous question, but the words had already left his lips. He regretted it after asking, but surprisingly, Baek Sogo didn't look very flustered. Instead, she simply nodded.

"Even if it wasn't to avenge you, Junior Disciple. I came here... to stop the Sword Phantom Elder."

With those words, she bent down and gently placed the flowers she held upon the Sword Phantom's chest.

"The Sword Phantom Elder was not right."

"...What about him wasn't right?" Lee Sungmin couldn't help but ask. The Sword Phantom was not right. He was dirty. He was dangerous. The words of Daoist Seongha echoed in his mind, tangling with the Sword Phantom's own claims that he had no choice but to become what he was.

"Are you saying it was wrong for him to choose the unorthodox path instead of martial arts?"

"Harming innocent people was what was wrong, Junior Disciple." Baek Sogo shook her head and stood up. "I don't know what kind of hardships the Sword Phantom Elder faced. I don't know why he did this. I... I simply came here after hearing that he killed Daoist Seongha, the Sect Leader of Mount Hua, and then cut down the Taoist priests who pursued him."

"...That was what was wrong?"

"Junior Disciple."

It was their first meeting in five years. Since they had parted, Lee Sungmin had imagined their reunion several times. In the life he had lived since regressing to the age of fourteen, Baek Sogo, like Wiji Hoyeon, was one of the people who had influenced him the most.

From Wiji Hoyeon, he had learned martial arts, and at the same time, he had learned the true depths of his own weakness. Through her, he came to know the inferiority complex one feels in the face of a true calamity.

From Baek Sogo, he had learned how to interact with others. He learned that a human can never live alone.

After that, through Jihak and Great Monk Bulyeong, he learned about the contradictions and shortcomings of 'himself'.

To Lee Sungmin, Baek Sogo was a person of great importance, which was why he could not stand by and watch her die. He had trained for that very reason, because he wanted to prevent her death no matter what.

If they were to meet again.

*What was it that 'I' wanted to say to her?*

"Everyone has their own justice that they strive for," Baek Sogo said with a sigh. "My justice and your justice might be different, Junior Disciple. The justice I hold is a measure that determines how I act as a person. I don't know what kind of heart or justice you brought with you to stand in the Sword Phantom Elder's way. I don't know what feelings you held for him. Likewise, you probably don't know why I came here, or what feelings I held for him."

"I... I felt a sense of responsibility."

"You've grown, Junior Disciple." Baek Sogo let out a low laugh and nodded. It wasn't a sarcastic remark, but a sincere one. "When I saw you on Mount Mushii, I always felt like you were acting. It felt like you were wearing clothes that didn't fit, pretending to suit them, and deceiving yourself, but now... you seem a little different. You've become honest with yourself."

"...You said you came to stop the Sword Phantom Elder. If you don't mind, could you tell me why you thought that, Senior Sister?"

"I belong to the Murim Alliance," Baek Sogo answered. "I thought the Murim Alliance was the most fitting organization to uphold my justice, and it actually was. Thanks to that, I was able to get information on the Sword Phantom Elder's movements quickly, and with the Sword Phantom Elder... we had a connection from the mountain. I wanted to at least be the one to deliver his end with my own hands. Though I didn't know you would be here, Junior Disciple."

"...Senior Sister." Lee Sungmin hesitated before calling out to her again. "The me that you saw... what was I like?"

"A lot has changed," Baek Sogo answered, glancing down at the Sword Phantom's corpse once more. "Your martial arts have advanced several steps beyond when you were on Mount Mushii, Junior Disciple. Back then, you were so pessimistic and harsh on yourself. I'm happy that you seem to have been rewarded for it."

*No.*

"Junior Disciple, I'm sorry, but I don't think we can talk for long. I rushed here, leaving my work unfinished. Now that things are settled... I have to go back too."

"...Senior Sister." Lee Sungmin barely managed to force out his choked voice. "The mission you're in charge of. Can you tell me what it is?"

[Right now, I'm chasing Wiji Hoyeon.]

Baek Sogo replied via Thought Transmission.

[I can't give you the details. Next time... let's meet again next time. Junior Disciple. I'm busy too, so I can't promise when that day will be, but we'll be able to meet again someday.]

With those words, Baek Sogo leaped away and vanished, leaving only the lingering scent of flowers on the Sword Phantom's corpse. He wanted to talk more with her, but the current Lee Sungmin had no reason to hold her back.

*She's chasing Wiji Hoyeon...?*

The information from his past life was becoming a tangled mess. The Sword Phantom, who was supposed to enter the Dungeon with Baek Sogo, had died here at his hands. He had not been involved in the Sword Phantom becoming a Vampire, though the one who died at Wiji Hoyeon's hands in his past life must have also been a Vampire.

Then what about Baek Sogo? Was she chasing Wiji Hoyeon under orders from the Murim Alliance, and was that the reason she entered the Dungeon with her and was killed?

If that was true, he couldn't persuade her. Hadn't she said it herself? Everyone has a different justice, different values. To Baek Sogo, that was an unshakable conviction.

Lee Sungmin had no such conviction. He lacked that same absolute faith in what he possessed. Therefore, he could not stop the departing Baek Sogo.

Even so.

He now knew for sure.

He knew what he had to do, what 'I' was lacking, what 'I' had run from, and how 'I' had lived. The fact that he had returned from death was not important. The fact that he had already lived for several decades was not important.

He had to throw it away.

He had already thrown it away.

Returning to the age of fourteen, Lee Sungmin had embraced his past. He had made the experience of living as a mercenary, the personality formed by it, the life of that Third-Rate, no, Second-Rate era steeped in self-deprecation and inferiority, his 'self'.

It was a tiny self. A trivial past self. The personality formed while living like that was narrow-minded, and the world he had considered absolute was small. The Zeon that Lee Sungmin had admired and wanted to become was not such a great being.

The world was vast. He kept learning that. As he learned, his past self disappeared. It had to be that way. He had to throw away the inferiority complex, the self-deprecation, and everything else. The Lee Sungmin who had lived as a 'mercenary'.

The current 'me' had to become a new self, not the 'me' from when I was a mercenary.

Since when was it?

Since when had he tried to throw it away?

Since when had he become the 'me' summoned to Eria at fourteen, not the Lee Sungmin who lived as a mercenary for thirteen years and died?

It was when he first realized the world was vast. When he thought he had to change. Tracing it all back, in the end, Wiji Hoyeon was there.

*I want to meet her,* he thought.

"It's been noisy lately."

Denir, whom he met four days later, had an indifferent expression. Although Lee Sungmin had not made an appointment, Denir played no silly tricks and opened the door to greet him right away.

"So you killed the Sword Phantom. It's quite strange. The Sword Phantom was stronger than you. Even with ten of you, you wouldn't have been able to kill him."

During those four days, Lee Sungmin had thought a great deal. He thought about the self that distorted the flow, the self steeped in contradictory responsibility and self-satisfaction.

Fighting the Sword Phantom, killing the Sword Phantom. What did he feel? The last thing he felt was guilt. It was not guilt from killing the Sword Phantom, but guilt from betraying the man's expectations.

It was guilt felt because he was weak.

"So. What did you come back to tell me after four days? Do you want to talk about trivial daily matters again? You weren't a very enjoyable conversationalist. Though I probably wasn't either."

"I want to receive the trial."

For four days, Lee Sungmin had barely slept. His cheeks were sunken and the area under his eyes was dark. The Mount Hua Sect praised him as the birth of a young hero for killing the Sword Phantom alone. The information Guilds spread rumors that Lee Sungmin had killed the Sword Phantom, the man who slew Daoist Seongha, and a Vampire at that, under the Night of the Full Moon.

"Foolish," Denir opened his mouth. "My trial is not as merciful as Mushii's trial. I should have already told you about that. Ten people. Ten people with the qualities of a calamity challenged my trial, and only three survived..."

"Give it to me," Lee Sungmin cut him off.

Denir pressed his lips together and glared at Lee Sungmin's face. Seeing the fanatical obsession burning in his eyes, Denir let out a hollow laugh.

"Son of a bitch. You don't know your place and want to die," Denir muttered as he stood up. "Mark my words. This death is neither merciful nor quiet. I guarantee, the pain you will feel through this trial will be different from any pain you have ever felt in your life."

"I'm the one who will experience it," Lee Sungmin answered, gritting his teeth. "So I said, give it to me."

"Fine!" Denir roared.

As he raised his hand, the surrounding scenery flipped upside down. Everything that had been flipped scattered, and Lee Sungmin found himself standing in a pure white space.

"This is the world of consciousness," Denir spat out. "Training here has no effect on your physical body in the real world. It's a place where only your consciousness is trained endlessly. Here, you can't even see the effects of the status window and skills that you Otherworlders boast about. Do you understand what that means? You must train in this world purely with your own memories and talent."

Lee Sungmin slowly looked around. There was nothing.

"It's a place where you don't need to eat, drink, sleep, or excrete. No matter how many years you spend in this world, not even a second will pass for your physical body in reality. Your consciousness alone exists in this space, separate from your body, and the time your consciousness experiences is stretched out endlessly. Even if you spend a hundred million years here, not a bit of time will pass in reality."

He felt he understood what Denir meant by 'harsh'. Denir placed a small hourglass in front of Lee Sungmin.

"When all the sand in this hourglass has fallen, you will have spent one hundred years in this world."

"...And after that?"

"You choose. Whether to leave this world, or spend another one hundred years. If you flip the hourglass again after all the sand has fallen, this time it will be two hundred years. The next will be three hundred years."

It was a terrible trial. The world of consciousness. Every hundred years, he could choose whether to escape this world or continue living in it. No matter how hard he trained his martial arts, as long as he couldn't get help from the status window and skills, it was impossible to confirm his achievements or use his martial arts easily.

Even if he endured for a hundred years, the training from the world of consciousness wouldn't be reflected in his real body. He would only... have the memories.

"Even though it's a place where you don't need to eat or drink, the choice is yours. You are the master of this world. Anything is possible. Though you yourself will remain the same. Try to understand that as you live in this world from now on."

Saying that, Denir nudged the hourglass with his foot.

"What you gain from the one hundred years, or more, that you spend here is entirely up to you. Reflecting what you've gained onto your real body is also up to you. Shall I tell you a few more things? The seven out of ten who failed went mad. Living for one hundred years in this world where nothing happens. During those one hundred years, they couldn't meet anyone and just trained like machines, without even gaining the conviction that they would achieve anything from it... They went mad with despair and died. That was quite an enjoyable sight."

Denir laughed.

"Most manage to get through the first one hundred years. Then they gain confidence and flip the hourglass, and then two hundred years... they start to go mad somewhere in the middle of that."

Denir turned his body, leaving behind the sound of his laughter.

"How many years will you last, I wonder?"

Denir's figure vanished. Lee Sungmin blankly stared at the sand pouring down. He wasn't holding anything, but the moment he thought of a 'spear,' a spear appeared in his hand. He could somewhat understand what Denir had said. This was his world of consciousness. Anything was possible.

"...Well... then..."

Lee Sungmin steadied his breathing and gripped the spear tightly.

The first day of one hundred years had begun.
