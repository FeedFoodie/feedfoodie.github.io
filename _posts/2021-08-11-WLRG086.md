His conversation with Denir had yielded several revelations about the End and its Apostle. Lee Sungmin recalled Kim Jonghyun's cryptic warning, *If I speak, I will die,* and the puzzlement it had caused him. He had been unable to fathom who would possibly want Kim Jonghyun dead. Mushii's muttered words had been just as incomprehensible at the time.

Now, however, he understood. It was the 'Apostle of the End' who had threatened both of them. The question of what The End actually was remained unanswered, but his meeting with Denir had given him a clear direction. Unlike before, when he had been adrift with no idea how to proceed, Lee Sungmin now had his next task.

Envirus.

He knew nothing about this person, only that they were a being whose causality was twisted, much like his own. In the vastness of Eria, finding a single person with nothing but a name was a task bordering on impossible. Still, as a member of Erebrisa, Lee Sungmin had access to information far more easily than others.

*The flow is changing because of me...*

A heaviness settled in Lee Sungmin's heart as he left the city's outskirts. He had never been particularly conscious of it. While he had sensed it before, he had never considered the implications to be a serious issue. His mind drifted back over the eight years he had lived since his regression, since starting anew at the age of fourteen. He was a completely different person from the man he had been in his past life, sharing only a name with that former self.

*Is my existence… really great enough to change the flow?*

He did not know. He had never once thought of himself in such a way.

Lost in a tangle of complicated thoughts, Lee Sungmin found a room at a nearby inn. His mind turned to the 'trial' Denir had mentioned, though he had no idea what it entailed. Neither Denir nor Mushii had explained it properly, and if Denir had not kicked him out, he would have pressed for more details. It was a significant regret, but it was too late to dwell on it.

He circulated the Violet Dawn Divine Art through its Great Heavenly Cycle and fell asleep. When he opened his eyes at his usual early hour, he learned the news.

Daoist Seongha was dead.

The words, overheard on the first floor of the inn, were so staggering that Lee Sungmin could not believe his ears. Just yesterday, he had spoken privately with the man, the Sect Leader of Mount Hua. Daoist Seongha had been old, but his spirit was vigorous, his eyes clear and full of life.

And now, only a single day later, that same man was dead.

"Was the Sword Phantom that strong?"

It was not as if he had fallen ill overnight. The Sword Phantom, Dok Bijun, had killed him. Listening to the conversations swirling through the dining hall, Lee Sungmin felt utterly confused.

He dredged up memories of his past life. Back then, he had been a man of no great standing, and there was a limit to the rumors a C-Rank Mercenary could access. He had not been actively gathering information in those days, so he only ever heard the truly 'famous' stories.

The murder of Daoist Seongha, the Sect Leader of the Mount Hua Sect, by the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun was the kind of major news that should have spread throughout Eria, yet, the Lee Sungmin of his past life had never heard of it. In that life, Daoist Seongha did not die. In this one, he was dead.

Lee Sungmin approached the patrons discussing the matter to ask what had happened. Perhaps it was his chillingly cold expression or the extraordinary aura emanating from him, but they explained the events of the previous night without holding anything back.

Late last night, the Sword Phantom Dok Bijun had crossed the gates of Mount Hua. He had infiltrated the private chambers of Daoist Seongha, woken the Sect Leader, and demanded a duel. Daoist Seongha had rebuked Dok Bijun for his insolence and ordered him to leave, but the Sword Phantom refused to listen. He ultimately struck first, and the sudden ambush escalated what should have been a simple duel into a battle to the death. Daoist Seongha was killed. By the time the other Daoists of Mount Hua arrived, it was already over. Their leader was a cold corpse, and Dok Bijun had fled the scene.

*But why?*

Lee Sungmin bolted from the inn, his mind a blank slate of shock riddled with questions. The Dok Bijun of his past life had not killed Daoist Seongha. He had likely visited Mount Hua back then as well, but the duel never happened, and Dok Bijun must have given up. A year later, he would die at Wiji Hoyeon's hands in a Dungeon. That was the original flow of events.

However, that flow had changed. Denir's words now weighed on him with crushing force.

-Your existence has become a variable, changing this world from how it was in your past life. What do you think that means?

-Your very being is tangling the entire flow of this world.

*Is it my fault?*

His thoughts inevitably led him there. He was the only difference between his past life and the present. In his past life, Dok Bijun had not met Lee Sungmin at the gates of Mount Hua, but just yesterday, Lee Sungmin had met him. He had met him and explained why Daoist Seongha was refusing the duel. Could that conversation have changed Dok Bijun's actions?

If so… if that was true… then wasn't he responsible for Daoist Seongha's death?

For the murder of their Sect Leader, the Mount Hua Sect had no intention of forgiving Dok Bijun. From the moment he escaped, they had declared him an irreconcilable enemy. They had already requested cooperation from many of Dreamoor's Guilds and were in fierce pursuit, casting a wide net to catch him.

Lee Sungmin could not identify the emotion churning inside him. It was a feeling he had never experienced with such intensity in his entire life, but he could not hesitate just because it was unfamiliar. When he came to his senses, he was already running.

According to the patrons at the inn, Dok Bijun had passed Mount Hua and was heading north. The ambush and murder had never happened in the original history, which meant Dok Bijun's actions were not part of the predetermined flow. He was not heading north because something was there. He was simply… running away.

Using his movement arts at full power, Lee Sungmin shot through the North Gate in an instant. A breathless strain seized his very core, but he refused to stop.

Responsibility.

It was an unfamiliar feeling. To the mercenary he had once been, responsibility meant 'money'. If you failed a request, you returned the fee. That was the only extent of it. What about now? Now that he had died, returned, and was living again? Had he ever felt a sense of responsibility before?

No. He did not know. He had never even been conscious of it, for it seemed he had never been in a situation that required him to take it. The eight years he had lived had been for himself. He had no clear purpose beyond the distant goal of a reunion with Wiji Hoyeon, and he had simply filled the time in between with 'things to do'.

He had never felt responsibility in a life like that. In truth, he did not even know if this was something he truly needed to feel responsible for. He just knew he could not stand by and do nothing.

Because 'I' met the Sword Phantom, Daoist Seongha, who should not have died, was now dead.

The existence known as 'I' was distorting the original flow.

It was not just this time. The past eight years flashed through his mind. There were surely others whose lives had deviated from their original paths because of him. He could not feel responsible for all of them. That was right. Lee Sungmin was selfish. He only thought of those he knew, those within his reach.

That was why he felt a sense of responsibility toward the Sword Phantom. He felt responsible for this outcome, and at the same time, he wanted to ask the man something.

There was something he had to ask.

"Halt."

Five Daoists blocked his path as he neared Mount Hua, each of them wearing the plum blossom crest on their robes.

"You can't proceed beyond this point," one of them stated.

"…And why's that?"

"A dragnet is about to be cast ahead. I don't know who you are, but if you don't want to get caught up in trouble, I suggest you find another path."

"…Are you chasing the Sword Phantom?" Lee Sungmin asked.

At his question, the Daoist's face turned to ice.

"We are."

What should he do? What was the right course of action? Lee Sungmin stared at them in silence. Seeing that he was not backing down, they gripped the hilts of their swords.

Should he retreat? Even if he met the Sword Phantom… what could he possibly do? No. What did 'I' want to do in the first place? Did he want to save him? That was impossible. The man had already killed Daoist Seongha. Mount Hua would stop at nothing to settle that debt, and there was no defending his actions.

What 'I' wanted to do was…

"…Please step aside," Lee Sungmin said, his voice low.

In response, one of the Daoists drew his sword with a sharp ring of steel, the blade glinting with a pale light. Following his lead, all five drew their weapons in unison. Lee Sungmin bit his lower lip and gripped the spear slung across his back.

The Daoists charged, the one in the center closing the distance fastest. Lee Sungmin had never personally faced the sword of Mount Hua, but that did not mean he was ignorant of it. Jihak, a master of Shaolin's Seventy-Two Ultimate Arts, had also reached the level of a master swordsman, and for four years, Lee Sungmin had dueled him every single day.

Because of that, he knew the 'sword'. He knew how the weapon was used. A sword art was merely a method to wield it in more varied and powerful ways. Ultimately, it could not transcend the nature of the blade itself.

He deflected a horizontal slash with Ran's Technique. He could not use any killing moves. To do so would be to cross a line from which he could never return. That was why he did not use the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear.

In truth, there was no need.

He faced five opponents. They were all disciples properly trained in the sword arts of Mount Hua, but they were not exceptional masters. They were slightly younger than him, and their skills were far inferior. He listened to the whistle of blades swinging from all sides, sensing the attacks without even needing his eyes.

Unlike him, who only intended to subdue them, the attacking Daoists were aiming to kill. He felt a bitter pang at how the situation had turned out. *Perhaps, if I still had that 'blessing'… I might have been able to persuade them.*

He dismissed the thought. Hadn't he asked Great Monk Bulyeong to remove it precisely because he did not want to rely on such things? The spear spun in his hands. He used the changjun, not the spearhead. He swung the spear not as a piercing weapon but as a heavy, blunt instrument, striking the nearest Daoist in the shoulder. The man screamed and dropped his sword. Lee Sungmin immediately switched targets. PABABAK! A series of rapid thrusts from the spearhead knocked the swords from the remaining Daoists' hands.

"Stop!"

A thunderous roar shook Lee Sungmin to his core. Startled, he took a step back, but his sixth sense screamed a warning, and he raised his spear to defend.

KWAAANG!

The impact was strong enough to make his hands go numb, sending his body flying backward. The one who had intervened was a middle-aged man with a short beard, who had thrust a sword at him. Lee Sungmin had managed to block the attack, but he grimaced as he felt his insides churn from the force.

This new Daoist was about half a step above him in skill. Realizing this was a bad turn of events, Lee Sungmin glared at the man. The Daoist who had forced him back glanced at his fallen juniors.

"…Who are you?" he asked.

His tone was more respectful, perhaps in acknowledgment of Lee Sungmin's skill. In truth, it was likely also because the Daoists Lee Sungmin had defeated were almost completely unharmed.

"…Ah… Lee Sungmin?"

An unexpected voice called out at that moment. Standing behind the middle-aged Daoist was a familiar face.

It was Kim Jonghyun.
