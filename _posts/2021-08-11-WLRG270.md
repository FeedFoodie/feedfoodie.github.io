The Chairman was not flustered. Heaven Beyond Heaven had been targeting Wiji Hoyeon openly, and while he did not know what they were after, her connection to their objective was an undeniable fact.

"To be honest, this one had no desire to do anything to that wench, nor did this one want to interfere with Heaven Beyond Heaven," he stated.

His words rang true. If the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman had intended to interfere from the beginning, he would have never let Wiji Hoyeon leave his side. He had instead chosen to be a thorough bystander, letting her roam free. It had been the same in Legro Forest. In truth, Wiji Hoyeon could have left the forest anytime she wanted. The only reason she had stayed was simply because Lee Sungmin had remained there with her.

"This one doesn't know what Heaven Beyond Heaven's objective is," the Chairman continued. "Whatever those bastards were plotting, this one figured it had nothing to do with me. Back then, this one had no intention of leaving the forest and wasn't interested in what they were trying to do."

"Are you saying that's no longer the case?" Lee Sungmin asked.

The Chairman countered his question with one of his own. "Didn't you make the choice?"

Lee Sungmin was left speechless, his mouth shutting abruptly. After a brief silence, he grumbled with a sigh, "I don't understand why you said that to me."

"It's just as this one said. This one respects Osla's words. This one also respects you to some extent, and this one respects your choice."

"Is that all?"

"No. That's not it." A low chuckle escaped the Chairman. "This one... well... needed a trigger. A trigger for this one to act. As this one said, The End is unpleasant, but that wasn't enough of a reason for this one to move."

At those words, Lee Sungmin felt as if he had seen a side of the man he had never known before. He also found it a little hard to accept. The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman he knew was arrogant and self-centered. For such a man to seek a trigger from others seemed like a contradiction.

As if reading his mind, the Chairman answered the unspoken question without hesitation. "The things this one wants to do are limited." He looked directly into Lee Sungmin's eyes. "What this one wants to do is train in martial arts. In fact, when you think about it, there's almost nothing else this one wants to do. You should know too."

"...What are you talking about?"

"Denir's Trial is cruel," the Chairman explained. "The Mental World you reach after accepting it. In that pure white world, only 'I' exists. The time expands, forcing you to endure a long, long period of isolation. No matter how much you immerse yourself in martial arts theory, it's not easy to pass that time. Naturally... your mind starts to go. To hold onto your fading sanity, you desperately think and think again. Afraid of forgetting how, you force yourself to think just to somehow hold onto yourself."

He knew. Lee Sungmin had been through it too, though his case had been special in many ways. Even Denir, the one who gave him the trial, had been astonished. Lee Sungmin's mind had collapsed several times inside that Mental World, yet it had naturally recovered over time. Thanks to that, he had been able to endure for 2100 years. Others were not the same. Not Envirus, not the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, and not Baek Sogo.

"This one only desired martial arts. This one only thought of becoming world number one. Most other desires faded away, and it's not much different now. This one did pay some attention to the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance, where this one is the Coalition Leader, but... this one wasn't deeply engrossed in it. Your senior sister, Silent Flash, also went mad in that way."

Lee Sungmin bit his lower lip, recalling Baek Sogo. He remembered her smiling without hesitation as she declared she would eradicate evil. She had said it herself, that she had become narrow-minded and only stubbornness remained. She was aware of her own distortion. Even knowing, she could not help it. For a long thousand years, Baek Sogo had strived for that one thing. Though it was but a moment in real time, the time she spent in the Mental World was an eternity. Her original personality had faded, and the broken conclusion she reached after a millennium of contemplation had become the conviction that sustained her.

"It's pitiful," the Chairman chuckled. "It's an unattainable wish. Eradicating evil is impossible. Absolute righteousness does not exist in this world. Setting oneself as the standard of justice is merely proof of a madman."

Lee Sungmin recalled the Baek Sogo he first met on Mount Mushii, the one who had said with a faint smile that she was a good person. *Does being good equate to justice?* He couldn't know. He had never paid much attention to things like good and evil in his life.

Pushing aside his thoughts about Baek Sogo, Lee Sungmin asked, "Do you know Wiji Hoyeon's location?"

The Chairman's eyes narrowed behind his mask. "How would this one know?"

"Then how are we supposed to find her?"

"If we ask around, a sighting might turn up somewhere," the Chairman muttered, stroking his chin. "However, it's a pain to just wait around blindly until then. Perhaps we should pay a visit to the Murim Alliance."

"...Pardon?"

"The Murim Alliance," he repeated. "We don't know where the Martial God is, but the Black Dragon Knight, who is connected to Heaven Beyond Heaven, is there. If we capture and interrogate that bastard, wouldn't we be able to learn more about the Martial God or their group?"

"...No, I mean. How do you plan to meet the Black Dragon Knight?"

"What's there to worry about? We just go find him."

"You mean we can just meet him if we go?"

"What's he going to do if he says he doesn't want to meet?" the Chairman scoffed. "Do you think the Murim Alliance can stop two Transcendent Realm masters from barging in?"

"...Why two?"

When Lee Sungmin asked, the Chairman suddenly raised his hand. Lee Sungmin flinched and stepped back without realizing it. The Chairman lowered his hand and let out a deep sigh. "Why is it that I have to explain every single thing for you to understand?"

"I was just asking to be sure."

"This one is going. Are you, the disciple, saying you won't?"

"So... what you're saying, Master, is that you and I, just the two of us, will barge into the Murim Alliance without warning. We'll grab Chairman Black Dragon Knight by the collar, subdue him, and ask him about Heaven Beyond Heaven..."

"That's the plan."

"What about the aftermath?"

"That depends on how they react," the Chairman said in a casual tone, though it was not a matter to be taken so lightly.

Subduing the Black Dragon Knight might be difficult for Lee Sungmin alone, but it would not be a very hard task if the Chairman himself stepped in, and after that?

"I'm not particularly afraid of the Murim Alliance picking a fight," the Chairman mused. "It's possible the Sword Immortal might step in because of this... hmm. We'll think about that when the time comes."

"Isn't that too reckless?"

"Then do you have a plausible plan? The End, which we know nothing about, is approaching. What are you saying we should do to stand against it?"

"What about going to find Osla?"

"I wonder. It's questionable if we could even meet him if we went looking. This one is here because Osla asked me to leave the forest. If he doesn't want to meet us, it's impossible for us to enter Legro Forest, no matter what we do. It's easier and faster to strike the Black Dragon Knight, whose location is certain."

"...I understand." In the end, Lee Sungmin had no choice but to nod.

Since they were not storming the Murim Alliance right away, he parted ways with the Chairman and went to Baek Sogo's residence. They drank together and talked a lot until the morning sun rose. During that time, Lee Sungmin noticed just how much distortion she carried within her. He did not sympathize with her conviction. Her words about eradicating evil were absurd.

He knew. He also knew that for a thousand years, in that Mental World where going mad was only natural, Baek Sogo had endured by holding onto that conviction alone. It was impossible for him to deny her distortion-filled belief to her face. It would not be easily denied, and if he did deny it and somehow managed to make her understand...

*What would become of her then?*

[Aren't you just scared in the end,] Void Monarch sneered. [You're afraid that your senior sister will break even more than she already has. You're also afraid of being hated by her for denying her conviction.]

Lee Sungmin grit his teeth, biting his lower lip.

[Or perhaps you're secretly hoping. Hoping that your senior sister will realize it on her own. Just how absurd her conviction is, and how greatly distorted her desired ideals are, but listen. Do you think your mad senior sister will stop just because she realizes it herself?]

*She would despair.*

[You know it well,] Void Monarch continued. [Admit what you must. The senior sister you remember is no more. What's left is just a madwoman who, after thinking alone for a thousand years, has reached her own conclusion and mistakes it for the absolute answer.]

"Then are you saying I have to stop my senior sister," Lee Sungmin spat out. "I... don't know. Isn't justice such a sweet-sounding word?"

[What about hypocrisy and feigned evil?]

"Don't ask me," Lee Sungmin retorted. He stood before Baek Sogo's door and cleared his throat, knocking once. No answer came from within. He knocked again, but there was still no response. In truth, he already knew. He slowly opened the door.

The room was empty, as if it had been from the start. The bed was neatly made, and there were no signs that someone had used the room. Lee Sungmin walked slowly inside, his eyes scanning the space.

There was a letter on the desk. He picked it up. The ink had dried long ago. *Did she leave right after we parted this morning?* He began to read.

Within the calm sentences, there was anxiety and fear. Baek Sogo, his senior sister, knew well what others called wrong. Narrow-minded and selfish. Stubborn. Self-righteous. She was aware of all that criticism herself. Even knowing, she had no choice. Even if she was anxious, it was what had sustained her all this time.

-I'm sorry for leaving without saying goodbye, Junior Disciple. I wanted to stay with you a little longer. To be honest, I'm scared. I can't help it. I'm human, after all.

1000 years was a long time. It was long and lonely. It seems the thoughts and worries I had to keep from going mad made me mad instead. I might not be right. I know I'm selfish. The things the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman said. The things you didn't say to me, Junior Disciple. I know them well too.

However, you know, Junior Disciple. It can't be helped. I've contemplated for 1000 years and I've reached my own conclusion. So, I... even if I know it's not right, I have to act to find a definite answer for myself. The eradication of evil. It's laughable, but I'm really going to try and act for this cause.

I'm scared. I'm scared, but I have to do it. Sometimes, I hear a voice in my head. Every time I hear it, I realize it. That I've really become strange.

I said I was going to Wudang. I don't know if I'll be able to find the answer I want there or not. Still... I'm going to go. I've never been to Wudang, you see. Actually, I wanted to go with you, Junior Disciple. Junior Disciple, did you know? How beautiful the mountains of Wudang are. They say that when winter passes and spring comes, Mount Wudang is dyed in gold as the bright yellow forsythias come into full bloom. I hope I can go there with you someday, Junior Disciple.

I'm okay, Junior Disciple. I'm scared, and anxious... but. I still believe in myself for now. So, I hope you'll believe in me too, Junior Disciple.

We'll be able to meet again.

The letter ended abruptly. The handwriting was messier than the lines she had written with a tree branch on the mountain. Even after reading the entire letter, Lee Sungmin stood there for a long time.

[Aren't you going to go after her?] Void Monarch asked.

Lee Sungmin carefully folded the letter and put it inside his robes. "...My senior sister said it herself. To believe in her."

[You're going to trust a madman?]

"I believe," Lee Sungmin nodded. "Because my senior sister believes in herself."

Still, he thought as he stroked the bracelet on his wrist, it would have been nice if she had at least said a final goodbye before leaving.
