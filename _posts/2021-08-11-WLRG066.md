Though he had planned to wait for Rudd's return to at least say goodbye, Lee Sungmin could not afford to wait aimlessly in Behenger. He had no idea when Rudd's request would end or when the man might be back, and every passing day felt like a waste of precious time.

After three more days passed with no word, he finally decided to leave.

Leaving Behenger required little preparation. He had no luggage to speak of, as the house he had been staying in was not his, and none of the furniture inside belonged to him either. The only thing Lee Sungmin packed was enough food to last a few days without needing to hunt. He kept the amount small, for even with a Subspace Pocket, he had no desire to carry unnecessary weight.

"Is that all you're bringing?"

Scarlet, whom he met at Behenger's North Gate, sounded displeased by his simple arrangements. She looked every bit the part of a traveler, dressed in a large robe with the hood pulled up and a huge bag slung over her back.

Lee Sungmin's lips parted slightly as he stared at her.

"...Aren't you using a Subspace Pocket?"

"This *is* my Subspace Pocket," Scarlet replied, glancing over her shoulder at the enormous bag. He had never seen one of that size before.

"...Do you have a lot of luggage?"

"I'm not just traveling. I'm leaving to settle down and conduct my research. Of course I'm going to have a lot of luggage," she said, patting the bag.

Lee Sungmin cautiously stepped closer and reached for it. "Let me carry that for..."

"Don't be ridiculous," Scarlet snapped, slapping his hand away. "I never asked you to carry it for me. I'll carry my own things."

He flinched and stepped back, studying her face. Although he had met her a few times over the past three months in Behenger, Lee Sungmin still could not quite grasp what kind of person the woman named Scarlet Reshir was.

"Come on, let's go. I've already rented a carriage."

"We're taking a carriage?"

"Of course we're taking a carriage. Don't tell me you were planning to walk all the way to Bredon?"

As Scarlet questioned him, Lee Sungmin found himself at a loss for words and shut his mouth. In truth, he had been thinking of a horse rather than a carriage.

"I rented it with my own money, so don't worry, I won't ask you to pay. Ah, but... I couldn't find a driver. I'm just asking, but you can drive a carriage, right?"

"Ah... yes. I can."

"That's a relief."

In his past life as a low-rank mercenary, he had learned all sorts of skills just to be included in requests. Driving a carriage was one of them.

To his surprise, Scarlet was well-prepared. The horses hitched to the carriage were healthy and the vehicle itself was in good condition. It would probably need repairs for a long-distance journey, but for now, it seemed they could use it without any major issues.

"So... which direction should I head?" Lee Sungmin asked, taking out a map and a compass. Eria was such a vast continent that the map lacked fine detail, but it was good enough to get their bearings.

"Go wherever you want," Scarlet said, tossing the large bag she had been carrying into the carriage. She turned back to him and pushed the hood of her robe from her head. "I don't have a particular destination in mind."

Her voice carried a strange tone that made Lee Sungmin give her an odd look.

"It's just... I was getting stir-crazy from being cooped up in my room. I don't care where we go."

He wondered if he was imagining it, but the words seemed tinged with a faint bitterness.

"What are you doing? Let's get going."

Scarlet climbed into the carriage and sat beside him in the driver's seat. Lee Sungmin gave a low, dry cough and pulled on the reins.

The carriage began to move slowly.

*

He had barely escaped with his life.

The Lloyd who had attacked the Dungeon was an even greater monster than the man he had faced ten years ago. Preskan had not been idle during that time either, yet the decade that had passed between them had only served to build a higher, more insurmountable wall between him and Lloyd.

*But I survived.*

That monstrous bastard was a human, not even a Lich. If a calamity in the field of magic truly existed, it would be Lloyd. Preskan was certain of it. The man was a monster far superior to any other mage he had ever seen.

And yet, he had survived. That was what mattered. This time, his luck had been terrible. If Preskan had been at his full fighting spirit, he was confident he could have killed Lloyd. Of course, the Mages Guild would have been in an uproar over that, and they would have hunted him down by any means necessary.

*Because my body wasn't in good shape, we were barely evenly matched. No. I was pushed back a little. What an absurd monster.*

If Preskan had been an ordinary human mage instead of a Lich, he would have died in that Dungeon. The fight with Lloyd had pushed him to the very brink of death. He had chosen to flee because dying even once would mean losing an enormous amount of magic power. It was a shame he could not kill Lloyd, but regardless of the outcome, he would have to continue evading the Mages Guild's pursuit anyway.

Preskan felt more pleasure than regret. Before him, a girl with voluminous black hair stood blankly, staring into a mirror. The clothes she wore suited her quite well, but she seemed displeased with them.

"Aine," Preskan called her name cautiously.

She was the culmination of a cherished, decades-long goal to create the ultimate life form. He had researched for years with that single purpose in mind, and the Chimera girl was the result that had finally borne fruit. He named her Aine, but for all that she was the crystallization of his lifelong wish, Aine was utterly incomplete. Both her body and her heart were flawed. He was trying to attune her belatedly, but a complete attunement was an impossible task with his current skills.

"Aine."

Only after he called her name a second time did she look back at him. A disharmony of body and soul. The bigger problem, however, was that Aine did not listen to him very well. Perhaps the obedience seal had not been properly engraved when he had hastily made the soul inhabit the heart and body. There were problems here and there, but he could not attune her right now.

Preskan was severely injured. Even for a Lich unbound by a physical body, the attacks of an archmage like Lloyd had dealt him fatal damage. He would need a great deal of time to recuperate. The fact that Aine did not kill him, even with the obedience seal so loosely engraved, was proof that it was functioning to some extent.

"It's about time you go."

"Okay," Aine replied with a slight nod. Her face showed her reluctance, but she did not completely disobey his words.

Her body vanished from before his eyes. It was not magic. She had simply moved at an incredible speed. Preskan sighed and sank to the floor.

The place he had fled to was not far from the original Dungeon's location. It was not that he was trying to hide in plain sight. He simply had not been able to get very far in his hasty escape.

*I hope the heart is still there.*

Preskan swallowed a sigh and prayed. He had not been able to grab the remaining heart in his rush to escape. Since he had failed to kill Lloyd, he doubted it would still be there, but he sent Aine to check anyway, holding onto a sliver of hope.

Aine rushed out of Preskan's hideout and headed straight for the Dungeon. Partway there, she stopped running. A strange sense of unease emanated from the heart beating in her chest. Aine frowned and pressed a hand firmly against her chest. Amidst the unpleasant, tickling sensation beneath her skin, she turned her head.

Something a short distance away was pulling at her attention. What should she do? Preskan, her father, had asked her to go and check if the heart was still there.

*Later.*

Aine casually postponed his request.

Since the sun would be setting soon, Lee Sungmin stopped driving the carriage. Traveling through the night would only tire the horses and cause the carriage to wear out quickly. He brought them to a halt on a suitable patch of flat ground and tethered the horses.

"Let's camp here for the night."

"Okay."

He had not talked much with Scarlet on the way. While he drove, she sat beside him in the driver's box, reading a magic tome. Her concentration never wavered, even as the rough road shook the carriage. Truly a future archmage.

"...Are you used to camping out?"

"You ask the weirdest questions. I lived on Mount Mushii for almost two years, you know. Without being able to wash or change my clothes. Camping out? I'm used to it. I'm so used to it that I still feel more comfortable sleeping on the bare ground than in a bed."

THWACK. Scarlet snapped the magic tome shut. The book she had been reading was so heavy and thick it could have easily been used as a blunt weapon. She tossed it carelessly into the carriage.

"Well then, I'll get things ready for..."

"Ready? Ready for what?"

"I need to gather some firewood..."

"What do you take a mage for?" Scarlet asked back, a look of disbelief on her face. She glanced around and stretched her hand toward a nearby tree.

Without any incantation, magic manifested instantly. A thick branch high up on the tree snapped off and flew to her feet. With a nonchalant expression, she reached her hand toward the branch on the ground.

POOF!

With a small explosive sound, the branch caught fire.

"Magic is what lets you avoid troublesome chores," she said. "It's not just for killing opponents and things like that."

"Ah... yes."

"Anyway, all you martial arts practitioners are like this. So ignorant you make your bodies suffer. You know a few magic spells too, don't you? You should think about using magic more actively and efficiently."

Scarlet grumbled as she sat down. Lee Sungmin could not think of anything to say in rebuttal and quietly sat down near her.

"Haste, Strength, Fatigue Recovery, Brainwashing. They're basic spells, but the power and potential of magic can change infinitely depending on how you use it. You. Have you ever thought about using magic properly?"

"Ah... no. I've always focused on martial arts."

"Your thinking is too rigid. Just because you like martial arts and only practice martial arts doesn't guarantee you have a talent for it. You never know. Maybe you have more talent for magic."

Scarlet crossed her arms. Lee Sungmin had no reply to that, so he kept his mouth shut. He thought she was not entirely wrong. What a person wanted to be good at and what they could be good at were two different things. For those two to be the same was an extremely lucky case, a case that could be called a calamity.

In his case, the two did not align. He wanted to be good at martial arts, but he could not be. He had heard that he had no talent countless times, so recalling it now did not stir any feelings of self-loathing.

"Anyway, it'll take a few months to get to Bredon from here. In the meantime, I could teach you a few useful magic spells..."

Scarlet's chattering expression suddenly turned to ice. A moment later, Lee Sungmin sensed it too. His sixth sense. No, this was not a warning from his sixth sense.

The sensation came from inside his left chest. The heart was radiating a palpable sense of displeasure.
