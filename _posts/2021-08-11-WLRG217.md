When he saw Wiji Hoyeon again after a week, she looked remarkably well. Her complexion was healthy, and a gentle smile graced her lips as she stood under a tree. The sight made Lee Sungmin freeze, but he was not the only one. Even the Void Monarch, residing in his head, was at a loss for words.

[...My god,] the Void Monarch groaned.

The Wiji Hoyeon that Lee Sungmin knew, the one he remembered and had always seen, was a woman who wore nothing but martial arts attire. This Wiji Hoyeon was different. She wore a flowing dress of a style he had never seen before, layered beneath her Black Dragon Armor. The image of her white wrists peeking from beneath the large sleeves and her calves extending below the hem of her skirt made Lee Sungmin unknowingly take a few steps back.

"What a funny expression," Wiji Hoyeon chuckled, her eyes fixed on his face.

Lee Sungmin opened his mouth to say something, but the words would not come, and he clamped it shut again. He had weathered many strange encounters and shocking incidents in his life, yet none had stunned him quite like this moment.

"Is it really so surprising that I'm wearing clothes like this?" she asked.

"Uh... yeah," Lee Sungmin answered in a dazed voice.

Her laughter rang out at his reply. She lifted the hem of her skirt, which rested on her thigh, and spoke again. "This is the first time I've ever worn anything like this, too."

It was also the first time she had ever bought such a thing. For a week, while avoiding Lee Sungmin, Wiji Hoyeon had been deep in thought, wrestling with her feelings. Her feelings had changed with the situation... no, that wasn't right. They hadn't changed at all. They had been there at the bottom all along. It was just that the way she expressed them had shifted as the situation did.

"I thought you would like it," Wiji Hoyeon said with a smile.

*Ah.* Lee Sungmin stared blankly at her face. That smile, the one he had thought looked so good on her. Behind that faint smile... he quietly clenched his fist. What was she so anxious about?

"It's a kind of reward," she explained. "You won the duel, didn't you? So..."

"It looks," Lee Sungmin barely managed to speak. "It... suits you."

They were simple words, but saying them had been incredibly difficult. At his compliment, Wiji Hoyeon's expression transformed. The smile that had been hiding her anxiety vanished, and her eyes, which had concealed her agitation, widened. She stared at him, her mouth half-open.

"It does, right?" she asked, and soon a new smile bloomed on her face. This one was different. Unlike the previous one that hid her unease, the one she wore now was genuine.

Though it was not directed at him, the Void Monarch's heart pounded with anticipation, and he remained silent. If he had followed his usual impulses, he would have shoved Lee Sungmin's back and urged him to hug her, bite her, kiss her, but he could not act on his nature. If he had, Lee Sungmin might have genuinely dunked him in a cesspool.

"That is... I mean..." Lee Sungmin started, keenly feeling how terribly unskilled he was at this kind of conversation. He might have even less talent for it than he did for martial arts. "Just now... about the fighting spirit martial art. The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, I mean, the Master..."

[Idiot,] the Void Monarch spat.

"Let's not talk about that right now," Wiji Hoyeon said, slowly shaking her head before he could finish.

*Yes.* Lee Sungmin immediately shut his mouth.

Wiji Hoyeon lowered the hem of her skirt she had been fiddling with and bowed her head. She toyed with a strand of her unadorned hair, hesitating for a moment. Lee Sungmin quietly set down his spear.

Wiji Hoyeon was the first to speak again. "...Can I come closer?"

Was the question even necessary? Before Lee Sungmin could answer, the Void Monarch hastily interjected.

[Hey. You go.]

*What?*

[You idiot eunuch bastard,] the Void Monarch roared, unable to bear his frustration. [The Little Heavenly Demon is mustering her courage, and you're just going to sit there and passively accept what she gives you?]

[I don't know what kind of change of heart she's had, but that girl is trying hard, doing something that doesn't suit her, but what about you? In the middle of this complex contradiction, all you're doing is shutting up and waiting! Is that all? Can you be satisfied with this? Are you confident you won't regret this later?!]

At the furious shouts, Lee Sungmin unclenched his fist. Wiji Hoyeon was still waiting for his answer, her head bowed.

*Are you confident you won't regret it? Can you be satisfied?*

The Void Monarch's words echoed in his head. The image of Wiji Hoyeon in clothes other than her martial arts uniform, the sight of her bowing her head so unlike herself. That he was just shutting up and waiting, doing nothing. He could not refute it. Every word was true. The situation was unfamiliar, and it was frightening. He had no experience with this kind of thing. His past life was not even worth mentioning. The Lee Sungmin of his past life had been just a mediocre mercenary, and the only women he had known back then were the cheap prostitutes befitting a C-Rank Mercenary.

Lee Sungmin slowly took a step.

"......"

At the sound of his footstep, Wiji Hoyeon's shoulders flinched. It was not a great distance, but to Lee Sungmin, it felt impossibly far. Seeing him finally move, the Void Monarch sighed in relief. This was enough. He had no intention of interfering any further.

"...I'm here," Lee Sungmin said hesitantly.

At his words, Wiji Hoyeon's head slowly lifted. It had been a long time since he had seen her face this close. She hesitated for a moment before raising her hand. Her fingertips, trembling uncharacteristically, touched his cheek.

"...A lot of time has passed," Wiji Hoyeon muttered, her thoughts drifting back eleven years. She remembered being thirteen, first summoned to Zenavis, standing blankly in the middle of the square with no idea what was happening. By chance... no, it was not chance. She chuckled softly at the memory.

"Shall we walk a bit?" Her hand, which had been caressing his cheek, lowered.

Lee Sungmin nodded. Wiji Hoyeon turned first and began to walk.

"It's been eleven years," she said.

He followed her, watching her back as she walked ahead. He did not want to look at her back. Lee Sungmin took a large stride and came up beside her. Wiji Hoyeon glanced at him, now standing at her side.

"Back then, we... no, I was young. You had died and returned to the past. You were fourteen then, but your real age was much older."

"Not really," Lee Sungmin said with a bitter smile. "I was young back then, too."

Perhaps he still was. Even if he had lived for two thousand one hundred years in the Mental World, that long stretch of time was too vast to even be aware of, especially since he had focused solely on martial arts.

"I made you my first friend," Wiji Hoyeon continued. "In many ways... you were an interesting person. For me, who had lived as the Demonic Cult's Young Cult Leader, there was no one like you. In my world, the only people around me were those oppressed by my background and my father's authority."

"I probably wasn't much different," Lee Sungmin added in an indifferent voice. "You said it yourself. That you hated being expected of by others, but I had expectations of you, too. I went to meet you with those expectations."

"Maybe so, but... huhu! You know, back then, I didn't care about such things. I was alone in the Demonic Cult, and I was alone in this world too. When my background and my father's presence disappeared, I gained freedom for the first time, but I was still alone and lonely. Think about it. I was only thirteen years old."

At those words, Lee Sungmin let out a small laugh. That was right. She had only been thirteen, and that young Wiji Hoyeon had thoroughly reprimanded him.

"Just half a year... after spending that time with you, we parted. That half a year wasn't a long time, but it was very meaningful to me."

"Meaningful in what way?"

"Because it was the first time I spent as myself, not as the Demonic Cult's Young Cult Leader, and it was also the only time I spent with a friend."

Friend. The word, repeated over and over, weighed strangely on his heart.

"We had a duel when I left Zenavis, didn't we? Promising to meet in ten years. Though that promise ended up being anticlimactic because I was cursed."

"It couldn't be helped."

"That's true. Still... when I met you in Ruvus, you had become incredibly strong. I felt good about it. However," Wiji Hoyeon paused. Before they knew it, they were in the middle of the forest. The sun had set, and the night was dark. Perhaps because he was with her, the chatty Fairy Realm did not come close, though its lights could be seen in the distance.

"I was also relieved," she muttered in a low voice. "You see... I wanted to always be stronger than you. Because I had become the purpose of your life, so... I didn't want to be caught. Maybe, while calling you a friend, I took it for granted that I stood above you."

Lee Sungmin did not answer. He had thought of her in the same way, and that was what had created the contradiction between them.

"Not anymore," Wiji Hoyeon said, stopping her walk. She slowly turned to look at him. "In the week-long fighting spirit duel, I lost to you. There's no need to say it was because I was weakened by the curse. The result is the result. With that, I no longer stand above you."

"...That's right."

"I am no longer your goal."

"That's not true," Lee Sungmin shook his head, denying her words. "I've only won once. I... I put in a lot of effort to reach this level. It's a bit much for me to say it myself, but I also had many miraculous encounters."

The training at Mount Mushii, Shaolin, and with Denir. Obtaining the Black Heart was a miracle, as was meeting the Void Monarch.

"Maybe... I might not be able to get any stronger than I am now," Lee Sungmin looked straight at her. "But you're different. You'll keep getting stronger. You're outstanding, on a level that can't be compared to me. So... just because I won this one time, it doesn't mean you're no longer my goal. You'll be stronger than me in the future."

"...I appreciate you saying that," Wiji Hoyeon chuckled after listening to him. "However," she added, shaking her head, "I don't want to remain just a goal."

She paused, then muttered, "There's something I want to ask you."

When she spoke, she did not look at him. Her gaze was fixed on her feet, her hands now tightly gripping the hem of her skirt.

"What am I... to you?"

It was a question she barely managed to ask.

"A friend? A goal? An ideal? An object of admiration? Is that... all?"

Lee Sungmin could not answer immediately.

"The things I said before we parted. Do you remember?"

"...I remember."

"Things about copulation, and stuff like that. Thinking back now, I don't know what I was thinking when I said those things. I also said my breasts might get bigger in ten years. Actually, there was a chance. There still is."

"I don't think size matters."

"That's a relief, then. I don't want to force them to grow either," Wiji Hoyeon giggled. "I was young back then. I didn't even know what my feelings were. Maybe I was ignoring them. It hasn't been much different since then. Still... even when I tried not to think about it, you kept coming to mind. The day I would meet you again."

Though they had met several times since then.

"I'm not used to this," she muttered. "These kinds of feelings. Showing my emotions, acting on them. It's so... awkward. I'm not used to it, so it's scary. It was the same this time. I asked for a duel as soon as I met you. I was embarrassed, and for me, that was easier and more comfortable."

However, now, even that had become difficult. Wiji Hoyeon chuckled softly.

"I don't think of you as just a friend."

She finally lifted her head, her gaze meeting his directly.

"That's why I'm curious. About what you think of me."
