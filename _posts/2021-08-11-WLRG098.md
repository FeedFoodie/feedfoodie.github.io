"They say a Dungeon has been Beggars' Gang'd."

The Phantom Sword of Dokgo approached with caution, his voice low as he delivered the news to Wiji Hoyeon. Her eyes opened, and she looked toward him. In the darkness, the Black Dragon Armor that enveloped her seemed to stir.

"A Dungeon?"

"Yes. The location is near here," the Phantom Sword of Dokgo said, bowing his head.

Wiji Hoyeon simply stared at him with an indifferent expression. A tremor ran through the Phantom Sword of Dokgo under her cold gaze, but he did not look away. A deep sense of awe rooted him to the spot before he bowed his head once more.

"What do you think about seizing it?" he advised with sincere earnestness.

Dungeons were like natural disasters, appearing suddenly and without warning. They were places filled with all sorts of mysteries that defied human common sense. The only certainty was that within a Dungeon, danger and opportunity coexisted.

"Is there a need to bother?" Wiji Hoyeon muttered.

The words seemed meant for herself, yet they were also a question directed at him. The Phantom Sword of Dokgo was inwardly pleased that she had asked.

A year had passed since Wiji Hoyeon clashed with the Blood Heavenly Demon Baek Museon in the North and emerged victorious. That defeat had dealt a major blow to Baek Museon and the Blood Heavenly Alliance, which had ruled the northern city of Trabia. Baek Museon's left arm was severed, leaving him one-armed, and the alliance that had been growing around him was cut in half.

As a result, Wiji Hoyeon's fame had grown even greater, and the Heavenly Demon Army, which had numbered only twenty at the time, increased significantly in size. Of course, she was not pleased with this development and had abandoned her adoring followers to act alone. Nevertheless, a few exceptional masters, the Phantom Sword of Dokgo among them, continued to follow her.

He considered himself an absolutely essential and loyal subject. He was captivated by her overwhelming strength and sincerely believed that she would soon become the center of this unknown world. What the Phantom Sword of Dokgo felt for Wiji Hoyeon was nothing short of faith.

"I believe this is fate," he said, his voice trembling with excitement. "The Beggars' Gang'd Dungeon is not far, and you are here. The master of that Dungeon can be no one but you."

From a normal perspective, his words were difficult to understand, driven only by zeal, but the Phantom Sword of Dokgo believed them with all his heart.

"......"

Wiji Hoyeon was lost in thought for a moment. It was not that she had no interest in something like a Dungeon. She had wandered Eria for a long time but had never experienced one before. It was not what she could gain from it that stirred her heart, but rather what she might experience inside.

"Maybe I'll go."

Curiosity spurred her into action. With a nod, she rose to her feet. At her first step, the followers who had been crouched nearby began to stir and move as one. The Phantom Sword of Dokgo watched her back as she walked away, then glanced toward a distant spot. His gaze, staring into a deep darkness where nothing should have been visible, was filled with blatant hostility.

"......"

Perched on a high tree branch, the man on the receiving end of that look simply shook his head with a wry joke.

"That's one murderous gaze."

Among the many prodigies of the Nine Sects One Gang, if one were asked to pick the single most outstanding, people would hesitate, but if asked to name three, those with any knowledge would answer without a second thought.

Shaolin's Jihak.

Wudang's Cheongmyeong.

Beggars' Gang's Chwi Geol.

These three had mastered all the famous ultimate techniques of their respective sects and had been trained by all their elders, prepared to become the future of their factions. Jihak and Cheongmyeong were individuals who did not engage in external activities, focusing only on their martial arts training at their sect's headquarters.

However, Chwi Geol was different.

"What will you do?"

Chwi Geol turned his head. Five people were concealing their presence where his gaze landed.

"The Little Heavenly Demon. That monster has started to move," Chwi Geol muttered. "I don't know what they were talking about, but they're probably heading to the Dungeon."

A woman who had been silent until then finally opened her mouth.

"We should go too."

It was Silent Flash Baek Sogo.

"You never know what might happen in a Dungeon. Entering one without proper preparation is a dangerous thing."

"If Wiji Hoyeon conquers the Dungeon and obtains great power, that would be even more dangerous."

The one who spoke was not Baek Sogo. A middle-aged man with a bushy beard looked at Chwi Geol with serious eyes. Under that gaze, Chwi Geol could find no suitable reply and clicked his tongue.

*I have a bad feeling about this.*

A feeling was an uncertain thing, but Chwi Geol was the type to trust his intuition. In truth, it was not as if he had a history of gaining great benefits or avoiding misfortune by trusting that feeling, but it was only natural to not want to do something he was reluctant about.

"The Phantom Sword of Dokgo looked this way. If even he saw us, it must mean Wiji Hoyeon was also aware of our presence."

"Until now, Wiji Hoyeon hasn't caused any friction with us, despite being aware of our presence. She may seem arrogant and ruthless, but she isn't foolish. In fact, she has never once had a real conflict with the Nine Sects One Gang."

"I heard Shaolin was humiliated by Wiji Hoyeon."

"It seems Shaolin doesn't consider it a humiliation. On the contrary, Grandmaster Bulyeong expressed goodwill when speaking about her."

"Shaolin has really gone off the deep end."

The five of them, including Chwi Geol and Baek Sogo, continued their conversation.

"The reason Wiji Hoyeon has ignored us until now is because we belong to the Murim Alliance," Chwi Geol said, his voice firm. "But inside a Dungeon, our background won't be able to protect us. It's a place where anything can happen."

It was dangerous. The intuition he trusted was insisting on it. However, the other four did not listen to him.

"We can't just leave Wiji Hoyeon alone."

"I know the Dungeon is dangerous, and I know that little monster possesses terrifying power."

"There's no time."

"Calling for reinforcements is one option, but what if she conquers the Dungeon before they arrive?"

"We don't know what's inside. There could be divine arts or incredible miracle herbs. There might even be great magic or an artifact."

"What's certain is this. The power one obtains from a Dungeon is incomparably stronger than the powers that exist in this world."

"We can't let Wiji Hoyeon obtain that power."

While the conversation went back and forth, Baek Sogo remained silent. Chwi Geol swallowed a sigh and scratched his head roughly. Persuading them was impossible. They had volunteered for this dangerous mission of monitoring Wiji Hoyeon themselves. They were all masters with power worthy of recognition even within the Murim Alliance, but they were also madmen who had bet their lives on the ambiguous concept of 'justice'.

*The one who falls in love loses.*

Chwi Geol thought, glancing at Baek Sogo. If she had no intention of backing down, then he had no choice but to go with her.

"...There's no helping it," he finally said.

While Wiji Hoyeon and the Heavenly Demon Army moved toward the Dungeon, and Baek Sogo and the Murim Alliance followed, Lee Sungmin was also making his way there.

He did not know the exact location. All he could do was wait near the vicinity for it to be Beggars' Gang'd and for the information to circulate.

He bought the information. The place where the Dungeon had opened was not far from his current position.

[Are you really going to go?]

Rubia asked, her anxious voice coming from the small orb of light floating around him. Instead of her beastfolk form, which was a result of Envirus's preferences, she had reverted to this state.

"I have to go."

Lee Sungmin pushed his hand into his Subspace Pocket. His fingers wrapped around the spear Celgerus had made for him, and he pulled it out. He had not used this spear since his training at Shaolin, but now he had to. Regardless of whether he was qualified, the things he was about to face required him to use everything he had.

[...Phew. Fine. I get it. Since it's come to this, it can't be helped, but you've never been inside a Dungeon before, have you?]

"I haven't," Lee Sungmin replied, though that was not entirely correct. He had entered a Dungeon exactly once in his past life.

*And I died.*

He did not want to remember it. It had been his first time, and he had not been properly prepared. This time, however, he had made his own preparations, spending his entire fortune to purchase a variety of potions. Lee Sungmin checked his equipment. Various daggers were fastened to his belt, complementing the spear on his back. He draped a cloak over it all and took a slow, deep breath.

He had considered several methods. Persuading Wiji Hoyeon. Persuading Baek Sogo. Subduing Wiji Hoyeon by force was impossible. Then what about Baek Sogo? Was she possible? He had not even been able to confirm her location. Eria was vast. He had bought information that both of them were in this vicinity, but finding and meeting them in this wide area was close to impossible.

[You're going into a Dungeon with no experience! What's with all this confidence?]

"Because I have to."

Lee Sungmin ran toward the location he had heard about. Rubia, in her orb form, hummed as she hovered beside his head.

[First, it's important to grasp the Dungeon's form. Every Dungeon has a definite end, and it's conquered when you reach that end and fulfill the conditions. The problem is how to get to the 'end'! A Dungeon could be a maze or a forest. It might even be an ocean.]

Lee Sungmin searched his memory. The Dungeon his past self had entered and died in was an underground maze.

[Inside a Dungeon, there are various means that threaten intruders, like monsters or traps. Outside, killing a monster only leaves a corpse, but if a monster dies inside a Dungeon...]

"I know," Lee Sungmin cut in. "Monsters in a Dungeon, unlike those outside, leave behind definite spoils. Things like money, miracle herbs, magic, martial arts manuals, potions, and weapons."

That was why all sorts of people went crazy and rushed to a Dungeon when it was Beggars' Gang'd. Considering that, it was understandable why the Murim Alliance would follow Wiji Hoyeon inside. If she succeeded in conquering it, she, who already possessed great power, would become a monster that no one could even touch.

"This is the place."

Lee Sungmin stopped walking. He could see a giant, red-glowing crystal embedded in the rocky ground. The Dungeon he had entered in his past life had looked just like that. He strode toward the crystal.

When he reached out and touched it.

[Do you wish to enter the Dungeon?]

A voice echoed in his head. Lee Sungmin slowly nodded. A feeling of levitation, as if his whole body was floating, enveloped him.

The scenery changed.

He was not even given a chance to examine his new surroundings. The moment his feet touched the ground, a blade of killing intent sliced through the air toward him. The sudden attack was enough to startle him, but he reacted instantly, planting a foot back and whipping his spear up to block.

KRAANG!

The resounding clang of metal on metal sent a shockwave up his arms, and the force threw him backward. Both hands gripping the spear tingled. It was the first time he had received such a heavy attack since becoming a peak master. Lee Sungmin narrowed his eyes and looked at the opponent who had attacked him.

"...Who are you?"

A large man with a bushy beard asked, glaring at Lee Sungmin. Lee Sungmin glanced at the giant axe the man was holding. The weapon was heavy, but the immense strength that had pushed him back was not due to the axe's weight alone.

"...I am called Lee Sungmin."

"Lee Sungmin?"

At those words, the large man's eyes widened.

"No way. Are you the Phantom Spear who killed the Sword Phantom?"

Lee Sungmin disliked the moniker. People, thrilled by the rumors, had dubbed him the Phantom Spear simply because he had killed the Sword Phantom who had killed Daoist Seongha. It was an unfitting name, as his own skill had not been the deciding factor in that fight. It was probably thanks to the help of the Black Heart.

"...That's right."

Although he disliked the name, Lee Sungmin nodded his head.

"Why are you here?"

Though he asked the question, the large man did not swing his axe again.
