"This... crazy bastard," Denir muttered, his gaze fixed on the vacant figure of Lee Sungmin.

In the real world, barely five minutes had ticked by since the trial began, but in the separate reality of the conscious mind, Lee Sungmin had just endured two thousand years. The very idea was absurd, unbelievable.

Denir had offered this trial to ten others before. Seven of them, all individuals recognized as calamities, had their minds shatter before the five-hundred-year mark. They were unable to overcome the Despair in a world where growth was forcibly suppressed. A mind that collapsed in that place was a mind that died, leaving behind a vegetative shell that no magic or potion could ever revive.

Of the three who had succeeded, the most resilient had only managed a thousand years. He had confessed that the solitude and madness beyond that point were simply unendurable. That was what the man who endured a millennium had said.

And yet, this man was still training, now in his two-thousandth year. This was beyond the realm of talent. Was it some innate obsession then? Or grit? A desperate will to survive? Denir could not fathom it. The Lee Sungmin he had observed simply did not possess that kind of capacity. In fact, his divine power was weak compared to other humans.

So how was he still enduring after two thousand years?

*His mind collapsed several times, yet he recovered. That's... impossible. How can a shattered mind just... mend itself?*

Over those two millennia, Lee Sungmin's mind had broken multiple times, only to somehow reform and continue the training. Denir knew this was impossible. The time spent in the world of consciousness could never be forgotten, which was precisely what made the trial so terrifying. One had to possess memories spanning centuries without being able to forget a single thing, moving alone in a world of nothing while repeating the same training over and over. Since growth was suppressed, there was little to gain from the endless practice itself. Denir's Trial was about forging mental accomplishments over a perceived period of hundreds of years, a slow and grueling advancement. It was entirely different from Mushii's trial, which accelerated overall growth through a series of restrictions.

"Ah."

A soft sound escaped Lee Sungmin's closed mouth. Denir flinched, instinctively taking a few steps back as the man's eyes fluttered open. Lee Sungmin blinked, his gaze vacant as he slowly took in his surroundings.

"Ah... Aah. Ah?"

The sounds were clumsy and unpracticed as he moved his lips. He tried to stand, staggering before his legs buckled and he collapsed on the spot.

DRIP.

Blood trickled from his nose. Lee Sungmin looked down at the dripping red and tilted his head.

"It's only natural," Denir said, his voice tight. "Your mind just lived through two thousand, no, two thousand one hundred years. You should be grateful your brain didn't just explode."

"Grateful... yeah. Grateful," Lee Sungmin muttered, his lips twitching. "I'm grateful. Thank you."

He roughly wiped his nose with the back of his hand and staggered to his feet. Lee Sungmin looked down at his own body, blinking slowly.

"My body feels heavy."

"You've lost your manners."

"Well... I have lived twenty-one hundred more years," Lee Sungmin countered, pressing his temples. "This isn't a dream, is it? The memories are just too clear."

He trailed off. "From a certain point, I didn't even think. I just repeated the motions, doing and not doing. I wonder if I felt like a machine. No, a machine might have had it better."

"You went mad several times in the middle of it," Denir stated.

"I know. There were moments when I'd just lose consciousness. When I came to, a lot more sand had fallen."

"You're a monster," Denir said with a fed-up expression. "I still don't know how you managed this. A collapsed mind means the death of the spirit..."

"I'm not sure either," Lee Sungmin replied, his face blank. "But I did it. I thought about going longer... but I felt it was my limit. If I had gone on, I would have really died from madness."

Was that really true? He had already gone mad and died several times over.

"It seems your personality has changed a bit."

"Because I've lived for twenty-one hundred years."

"That's quite mild, all things considered. The guy who came out after a thousand years couldn't even think straight. The fact that you're maintaining your personality to this extent is abnormal."

"Is... that so?" Lee Sungmin moved his body as he replied. He cracked his joints as if stretching and looked around with wide eyes. As he clenched and unclenched his hand, he drew up the inner arts within his qi center.

Seeing this, a startled Denir asked, "Wait, wait! Don't tell me you're planning to perform your martial arts here?"

"Ah... right. This isn't the world of consciousness. I shouldn't swing it around here."

With that, Lee Sungmin shuffled his feet and walked out of the room. Denir followed behind him, his face a mask of anxiety.

As he stepped into the courtyard, Lee Sungmin felt a powerful sense of disharmony with his own body. It was a natural feeling. The Lee Sungmin of the mental world had trained for twenty-one hundred years. In contrast, his physical body had only been sitting still for five minutes. A gap of two thousand one hundred years existed between the Astral Body he had used and his real one.

"Spear... spear..." Lee Sungmin muttered in a small voice, clenching and unclenching his hand. Normally, a spear would have appeared in his grasp the moment he thought of it.

Denir sighed heavily at the sight and raised his hand. "This isn't the world of consciousness. First, you need to understand that."

"The sense of disharmony is strong."

"I know."

As Denir moved his hand, a pure white orb formed above his palm. "This is a backup of your memories. While you were in the Mental World, I extracted only the memories you had before. Take it."

Denir tossed the orb. Lee Sungmin stared blankly as it floated toward his head.

"What are you doing? Catch it."

"What'll happen if I catch it?"

"Your memories of the twenty-one hundred years and the fighting spirit from your training will mix. It should help lessen the disharmony."

"Really?" Lee Sungmin twisted the corner of his mouth and chuckled. "Memories of my pathetic days. Still, I need them. There are things I must not forget."

Saying that, Lee Sungmin caught the orb. His mind cleared with a flash. A moment later, he nodded his head.

The addition of memories did not reduce the physical disharmony. It did, however, ground him with the clear understanding that this world was not the mental one. Lee Sungmin looked down at his empty hand before taking out a spear from his Subspace Pocket.

Right there, he performed his Spear Technique. He flowed from the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear into the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, and after that, he put down the spear and entered qi circulation. Lee Sungmin, who was checking his body in this way, laughed under his breath.

"I don't like my body."

"Of course you don't. The body that trained for twenty-one hundred years is different from your body now. You may have the memories, but it won't be easy to reach the level you experienced with this body."

"I felt my lack of talent thousands, tens of thousands of times in that world. It can't be helped. That's just who I am."

That was the end of his reflection. Lee Sungmin turned to Denir. "I'd like to stay here for a while."

"What? Why?"

"I don't really have anywhere else to go. You don't have to let me in the house. I'll sleep in the yard if I have to."

"This... Ugh... Hooo!" Denir, who was about to protest, eventually squeezed his eyes shut and let out a loud sigh. "Do as you please."

{sep}

He organized the things he needed to do.

Preventing Baek Sogo's death was something he absolutely had to accomplish. He had one year left. In that year, Lee Sungmin had to engrave what he had gained from his training in the Mental World onto his physical body.

*My mind, ki, and body are all tangled up.*

He now understood the reason clearly. The insights gained from his fight with Aine had led him to a higher realm, but his body could not fully accept those insights. The same was true for his fight with the Sword Phantom. His body moved in the direction his insights led, but that was its limit.

Currently, in Lee Sungmin's body, his mind was far ahead of everything else. His ki and body lagged far behind. The hard-won balance of mind, ki, and body he had achieved at Shaolin was once again a tangled mess thanks to Denir's Trial.

*I have to undergo Body Reformation.*

The first goal was to open the Conception and Governing Vessels and achieve Body Reformation. He had the 'experience', even if it was only experience from the mental world. Still, that was better than nothing.

*In that world, it took about fifteen hundred years to enter the realm of the Supreme Peak.*

However, that did not mean it would take fifteen hundred years to reach the realm of the Supreme Peak in reality. He already had the memories of reaching it. He knew how to use the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, how to use the Dancing Shadow Soul Break, and how to operate the Violet Dawn Divine Art.

Lee Sungmin put down his spear and sat down in a cross-legged position. Once more. A faint smile touched his lips at the thought. His mind felt surprisingly refreshed. The disharmony with his body was still unfamiliar, but the process of aligning this mismatched feeling seemed like it would be enjoyable.

*One year from now. How far can I go?*

He had to be able to go anywhere. That was what Lee Sungmin thought.

Spring came.

The young martial monk at the mountain gate tilted his head as he watched the man climbing the stairs. He had been standing there just a moment ago, guarding the bottom of the stairs, but at some point, that man had suddenly appeared in his vision. It was such a natural appearance, as if he had been there from the very beginning, that the monk was slow to be surprised even after spotting him.

*A master...!*

He realized it as the man drew closer. The man showed no aura, as if he had never learned martial arts, but the martial monk knew well that this was, in fact, the sign of a true master.

"Who are you?" the monk asked in a wary voice.

At his words, the man lifted the bamboo hat he was wearing and looked up at the monk.

"Seunjeo."

"...Huh..."

As the man smiled and called out his Buddhist name, Seunjeo's jaw dropped.

"Lee Sungmin?"

He had returned to Shaolin after a year.
