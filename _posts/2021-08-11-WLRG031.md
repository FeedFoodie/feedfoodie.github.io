A young man named Flam approached, appearing to be around Lee Sungmin's age with a similar height and build. His eyes, a dark blue that seemed to hold the starry sky itself, fixed on him.

"Lee Sungmin," Flam muttered, his hands empty.

Unlike the others on this mountain, he carried no visible curse. He didn't reek of Scarlet's foul stench, nor was he blind like Dok Bijun. He wasn't missing the use of an arm like Baek Sogo, nor was he unable to speak.

"How long have you been on this mountain?" Flam asked.

A question about Flam's own restriction surfaced in Lee Sungmin's mind, but he pushed it aside for now and stared blankly at the other boy's face.

"About… three months now?" he answered. He had marked every passing night since his arrival, and today was exactly the ninetieth day.

"Three months!" Flam exclaimed dramatically. "To last that long on this mountain, that's amazing!"

"...Didn't you say you've lasted over a year?" Lee Sungmin asked, dumbfounded.

Flam's eyes widened in surprise. "You know me?" he asked, tilting his head.

"I heard from Baek Sogo."

"Ahh, so the mute woman told you." Flam chuckled, stepping a little closer. "Then you must know my name too. No need for introductions, then." He beamed. "You should've come to say hello. Everyone on this mountain is older than me. I've been needing a friend my age."

The phrase 'a friend his age' brought an unwelcome image of Wiji Hoyeon to Lee Sungmin's mind, and he quickly forced it away.

Flam's eyes suddenly shone with a new light. "By the way, do you use martial arts?"

Lee Sungmin gave a reluctant nod. He was not pleased about this meeting, not at all. He knew of Flam's ability to imitate any martial art or magic just by seeing it. The thought alone was unsettling. A martial artist's techniques were their property, their very everything. The same was true for a mage's magic, yet Flam could steal it all from another person with a simple glance.

"...Yeah."

"I can tell by your expression," Flam said with a small laugh, twirling a lock of shimmering blond hair around his finger. "Baek Sogo must have told you that I can imitate martial arts just by seeing them."

Lee Sungmin said nothing, his wariness deepening. Having spent months with Wiji Hoyeon, he knew all too well what kind of people the so-called 'calamities' were. They surpassed common understanding, their minds far beyond their years, and they were invariably arrogant and self-righteous.

"Let's see... so..." Flam raised his empty hands, his fingers curling around an invisible shaft, his posture perfectly mimicking a spearman's stance. He shot a grin at Lee Sungmin, baring his teeth, and then he moved.

He flowed through the Rannachal, the very same form Lee Sungmin had been practicing, before transitioning seamlessly into the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear. He couldn't perform the entire technique, of course, as Lee Sungmin himself had only mastered up to the third form, but what Flam executed was a perfect, flawless mirror of what he had seen. It was exactly as Baek Sogo had described, and it left Lee Sungmin utterly astonished.

"It's a good Spear Technique," Flam muttered, lowering his hands with the air of a critic. He turned back to Lee Sungmin, his eyes sparkling. "What do you think?"

The question held no discernible malice, yet Lee Sungmin knew exactly what Flam wanted to hear. He wanted shock. He wanted amazement.

After a moment's hesitation, Lee Sungmin forced the word out. "...Amazing."

A bright smile bloomed on Flam's face. "I'm better at it than you, right?"

The casual remark sent a chill through Lee Sungmin's heart. He couldn't detect any malice, but perhaps that was just Flam's personality. It didn't matter. A child could kill a frog with a carelessly thrown stone, and right now, Lee Sungmin was that frog.

"It's the same with Baek Sogo and old man Dok Bijun," Flam continued with a giggle. "Isn't it strange? They must've practiced way more than me, but when we actually try it, I'm better."

In that moment, Lee Sungmin understood everything. He understood why Baek Sogo had warned him away, why Dok Bijun had said a meeting would be bad for them both.

*This is what it feels like.*

It was nothing like his time with Wiji Hoyeon. She had called herself a calamity, but she never flaunted it at him. He had never even considered her a rival, but a master and a goal to strive for. Flam was different. This incomparably cruel boy knew his own talent all too well and understood exactly how his heaven-sent gifts crushed others.

Now, with perfect clarity, Lee Sungmin grasped the meaning behind Baek Sogo's warning. *Do not Despair*. A true calamity didn't need to fight to win. Merely facing one was enough to make a person despair.

"So, what kind of restriction did you get?" Flam asked, apparently bored by the silence. "I have four of them. Aren't you curious what they are?"

"No. I'm not," Lee Sungmin replied defiantly.

In truth, he was curious. What kind of restrictions could Flam possibly have? Baek Sogo had three after half a year. Scarlet and Dok Bijun likely had two. Baek Sogo's extra restriction was because she had started with two. If a new one was added every six months, it made sense that Flam, after a year, would have four.

"Liar. You're curious," Flam giggled. "I'll tell you something good. On this mountain, the stronger the restriction you receive, the more you're favored by Mushii, the god of penance and trials. In other words, the more you make things difficult for yourself, the greater the reward." He hummed. "Not that I need anything like that, though."

His gaze sharpened. "The Spear Technique you perform is an outstanding martial art. However, you, the one performing it, are not outstanding."

He already knew that. Wiji Hoyeon had told him he had no talent. In the eyes of a true calamity, his aptitude was so trivial it was practically invisible.

"So, listen," Flam said, his tone changing. "How about this instead? Just give up and leave the mountain. I mean, with your skill level, you could probably make a living in some small city. Don't waste your time clinging to a martial art that doesn't even suit you..."

"Get lost."

"......"

The sound of his own grip tightening on his spear was sharp in the quiet air. Flam's eyes widened as Lee Sungmin cut him off.

"I wasn't finished talking."

"I said, get lost."

Flam put on an aggrieved expression. "Why would you say that? I'm saying this for your own good. That's so harsh. I was even trying to look out for you since we're the same age. It's a waste of time."

"It's my time," Lee Sungmin spat out.

He wanted to kill him. It had been a very long time since he had felt such intense killing intent, but he couldn't act on it. Mushii would surely intervene, and even if the god didn't, winning against Flam was impossible.

Because Flam was a calamity.

"You're so mean," Flam muttered, the smile gone from his face. He took a few steps back and narrowed his eyes, glaring at Lee Sungmin. "I was just trying to help you. Why do you hate me?"

"I never asked you to say that crap to me."

"So I can't even speak my mind without your permission?"

"Just like you have the freedom to spout whatever you want at me, I have the freedom to spout whatever I want back at you."

Flam was left speechless, his mouth opening and closing. After a moment, his shoulders began to tremble as if he was about to say something more.

WHOOSH.

A sudden gust of wind was the only warning before Baek Sogo appeared between them, her expression hard as she stared at Flam. While Lee Sungmin was startled by her sudden arrival, Flam seemed entirely unsurprised, having clearly sensed her approach.

"I thought you'd just keep hiding and eavesdropping," he sneered, the corners of his mouth turning up.

Baek Sogo only shook her head silently. As Flam opened his mouth to say more, she shot her hand out, blocking his face.

"You take care of him so much. It makes me jealous," Flam grumbled, spinning on his heel. He took a few steps, then glanced back at Lee Sungmin. "See you next time."

Without waiting for a reply that would never come, Flam shot away in a blur.

Once he was gone, Baek Sogo turned and used a wooden stick from her waist to write in the dirt.

[You did well to endure.]

"I have to endure," Lee Sungmin replied, turning away. "I don't plan on leaving this mountain right now."

His steps felt heavier than usual, a weight that had nothing to do with his restrictions. His heart itself was heavy.

"...Thanks for coming to stop him."

No reply came, for she could not speak. Lee Sungmin lifted his spear and began to swing, the heavy weapon moving by sheer physical strength alone, without any inner arts.

His chest felt tight, as if something was caught in his throat. Baek Sogo's words echoed in his mind, telling him not to despair. He thought of his promise to meet Wiji Hoyeon in ten years. He thought of his past life, of the thirteen empty years that led to an equally empty death. He thought of himself, here and now.

He lost track of time, and when he finally came to his senses, the sun had set. Baek Sogo was gone, but in her place lay the carcass of a large deer. His qi center was empty, his body was drenched in sweat, and every muscle ached.

*It wasn't enough.*

Talent.

To chase a calamity, an ordinary person had to put in several, no, tens of times more effort. To chase a calamity who also put in effort, it might still be impossible to catch up.

*Talent, talent.*

Lee Sungmin had no talent. Wiji Hoyeon had told him so, and Flam had just confirmed it. None of that mattered. He himself already knew it well. It was why he had sought other things, the Martial Skeleton, the Heavenly Truth Meditation Art, the Holy Spirit Pill. He had planned to seize other opportunities to make up for what he lacked.

*Talent.*

The word itself was a crushing weight. It made Lee Sungmin despair. No, what he truly feared was the day, ten years from now, when he would meet Wiji Hoyeon again.

He was afraid his level would be pathetic.

He was afraid Wiji Hoyeon would be disappointed.

That was his true fear.

"Mushii," Lee Sungmin whispered, his hands trembling as he clasped them together. The despair had curdled into fear, a terror that this new life he had fought so hard for would end just as pathetically as the last.

"Mushii...!"

His call became a sob. He cried, the first tears he had shed since returning from death.

[Human.]

A voice echoed in his mind.

[Why do you call upon a god?]

A heat haze began to shimmer in the air before him.
