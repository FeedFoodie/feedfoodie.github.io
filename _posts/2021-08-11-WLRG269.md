Lost in thought, the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman processed Abel's story. He now understood the shape of things to come, or rather, the shape of things that had already come to pass, all of it centered on his disciple.

Having dedicated his entire life to martial arts, a path he had walked for the sole purpose of reaching a higher, more complete state than any other, the words 'The End' were utterly foreign to him. It was a hundred years ago that he had first secluded himself in Legro Forest, and in all that time, such a concept had never crossed his mind.

"......"

His fingers traced the contours of his mask. The End. Such an unconditional finality did not sit well with him.

"It's unpleasant," he muttered, his voice low, "to think I have to die for something I don't even understand." He turned his gaze to Abel, who sat directly across from him.

"So, I understand the gist of it. The End. An unknown force that brings an unconditional end to this world, and my incompetent disciple here is the one who brought it about."

He shook his head. "However, I cannot agree that my disciple is no longer useful. I'm willing to show you a great deal of respect, but do not belittle my student."

"...That's not what I meant."

"That boy is a mysterious one," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman said, glancing at Lee Sungmin. "The reason I took him as my disciple... hmm. I thought it was a coincidence, but hearing this grand story of yours, I suppose it couldn't have been. Fate must have protected him. He survived when he should have died, and by chance, he caught my eye, which saved his life and made him my student. That must be it."

"I don't know about your connection with him, nor am I curious. What is certain, however, is that The End is near. Fate has already begun to flow…"

"......"

Lee Sungmin, who had been silent until now, finally spoke, his eyes narrowing as he looked at Abel. "You're not much different from your brother."

"...What do you mean by that?"

"My brother also tried to kill me, spouting nonsense about fate."

"I know," Abel sighed, leaning back in his seat and scratching his head vigorously. After a moment of thought, he rummaged through his clothes and produced a large pipe.

"Do you mind if I smoke?"

"Be my guest."

At the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's permission, Abel immediately put the pipe to his lips. As he puffed away, drawing in smoke, Lloyd, who had accompanied him, felt as if he were sitting on pins and needles. He knew nothing of The End and had simply followed his Guildmaster here.

"...Alright, fine. I'll admit it," Abel said. "I'll concede that some of my behavior resembles my damn fool of a brother, but you know, my brother was afraid of his own death. He abandoned his fate and ran away. I'm not like that."

"What is it you want to do?"

"To be blunt, I don't care if I die," Abel spoke from behind a cloud of hazy smoke, his eyes holding a light distinctly different from Envirus's listless gaze. "I'm not particularly attached to my life."

"But if it's not just me, but the entire world that's going to die, then I, for one, don't want to stand by and do nothing, 'knowing' that it's going to happen." Waving away the smoke with his hand, Abel let out a low laugh. "I won't say my brother did nothing. It's true he struggled in his own way to stop The End. However, his top priority wasn't stopping The End, it was his own life. Not that it matters much now. Breaking a Mana oath is a fatal mistake for a mage."

"...Ahem."

Lloyd coughed uncomfortably. He didn't know the full story, and he couldn't just sit by and smile while insults were hurled at Envirus, the master who had taught him magic.

Abel shot Lloyd a glare and spat. "Your master was a fucking idiot. He had outstanding talent as a mage, but in the end, he was just a fool who only cared about himself."

"Are you saying you're different?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"I'm trying to be," Abel laughed loudly. "The End was predetermined, and there was no way to stop it before it began, but now the time has come, so we have to try. Right, I should apologize for what needs apologizing. I'm sorry for saying you had no value. If the Fairy Queen told you something, there must have been a reason, and that key, too. I don't know what it is, but since it was transformed from the Stone of Reincarnation, there must be some use for it."

"You said we should join forces," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman interjected. "Join forces to fight some unknown thing called The End. The enemy isn't even clear, and we have no idea when this 'End' is supposed to start. How exactly are we supposed to prepare?"

"By being aware," Abel answered. "Chairman, as you said, we don't know how The End will arrive. It might have already begun without us even knowing. This world is damn big, isn't it?"

"I can't get a grasp on it."

"It's the same for me. It's not like a meteor is suddenly going to fall and destroy the world, or a Demon Lord is going to appear. Nothing has been confirmed. That's why we must first be aware. The End is definitely coming. We must absolutely…"

"Stop it?" the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman asked with a smirk. "You're mistaken about something. I've listened to your entire grand story, but I never once said I would help you stop The End."

"...What?"

"I've lived long enough. It's not that I have no regrets, but if death is unavoidable, then I must accept it. Especially a predetermined end."

"Didn't you just say it would be unpleasant to end like that?"

"Unpleasant is unpleasant," he retorted. "How can anyone accept everything in this world with a happy heart? What can't be helped, can't be helped. I'm not a child. I understand that much."

"Are you saying you don't care if everyone in the world dies?!"

"What have they ever done for me?" the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman shot back, leaving Abel at a loss for words. Seeing the anxious tremor in the mage's eyes, he pressed on. "Don't be mistaken. My title is the Demon Emperor, and I am the leader of the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance, a union of unorthodox sects. Our very name implies wickedness and injustice. The 'Demon' in my title refers to devils and evil spirits, and 'Emperor' is self-explanatory. I am the emperor of devils and evil spirits, standing at the pinnacle of all wicked and unjust trash. Do not force the 'right choice' upon such a person. Do not compel me with appeals to save the world for humanity's sake."

"But…!"

"Enough," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman said, shaking his head. "You cannot persuade me. The only ones who can... hmm... yes. My incompetent disciple, and the tiny fairies living with their queen in that distant forest."

His words were suddenly directed at Lee Sungmin, who flinched in surprise and met his master's gaze. From behind the mask, he could see the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's eyes smiling.

"My foolish disciple. What would you have your Master do?"

"...Why are you asking me that?"

"Because I believe your choice is important," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman answered without hesitation. "Didn't Osla say it? That you would one day have to make a choice. I don't know when that time will be. The End? Fate? I know nothing of such things. However, I respect Osla, and you, just a little."

"This much," he said, holding up his thumb and index finger toward Lee Sungmin, leaving only a tiny gap between them. "I respect you this much. So you choose. What should I do?"

"...Don't you already know what my answer will be?"

"Say that obvious choice with your own mouth."

"I don't want to die," Lee Sungmin said with a sigh. "Why did this happen to me? Why did I get caught up in this? Was it a mistake to grab the Stone of Reincarnation and return to the past? I don't know. I just don't know, but I don't want to die. I went through all that trouble to come back, and I've finally started living a life more valuable than my last one. I don't want to let a world like that be destroyed."

"So he says," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman laughed heartily, his voice booming. He had, of course, known what Lee Sungmin would choose. To Lee Sungmin, it felt mischievous of his master to force an answer he already knew so well. The Chairman turned back to Abel. "If this thing called The End arrives, I shall endeavor to stop it. So. What will you do now? Will you remain here and wait for it to come?"

"...I'm thinking of going to Wudang," Abel replied, his expression soured by the turn of events. "The Sword Immortal's power will be helpful."

"Why not go to the Murim Alliance?" Lee Sungmin asked. Though they were hostile to Heaven Beyond Heaven, that group likely held the most power in the current murim. Even with three of the Six Honored Ones killed by Lee Sungmin, they still had five masters of the Transcendent Realm, including the Martial God and the Black Dragon Knight.

Abel shook his head. "I know about the group called Heaven Beyond Heaven, but I can't trust them."

"Why not?"

"Because I have no way of knowing what kind of future they're predicting and hoping for." Abel pointed a hand at Gries. "I've been offering my lifespan to Gries to glimpse things I cannot see. It's how I figured out the identity of The End and about you, the Observer, but even with Gries's magic, I cannot glimpse the future."

"...Are you saying Heaven Beyond Heaven is seeing the future?"

"I'm not certain, but their movements make it clear they are acting with some purpose, even if they aren't seeing the future, and their purpose is focused on someone."

"The Little Heavenly Demon," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman nodded. "I don't know why Heaven Beyond Heaven is so obsessed with that girl."

"As long as I don't know their purpose, I'm not eager to contact them," Abel continued. "Likewise, the Vampire Queen of Predator is also a difficult opponent." He furrowed his brow. "She possesses the All-Seeing Eye. I don't know what future she has seen, but I doubt it's one favorable to us. For a monster with that much power to remain silent in the North for so long... there must be a very good reason."

"So you're going to see the Sword Immortal because he's an easier opponent to deal with?"

"Since he's from the orthodox sects, it might be easier than persuading someone from the unorthodox sects," Abel remarked sarcastically.

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman just chuckled. "I won't see you out, but I'll give you a piece of advice. The road to Wudang is long, so be careful, and don't underestimate the Sword Immortal."

"Thanks for the advice."

There was no longer a need to maintain the barrier. He had said everything about The End, and as long as they avoided the topic, the Apostle would not intervene. Gries emitted light once more, and the space it had enveloped shimmered before disappearing.

"I need to rest a bit." Abel picked up the pipe he had set down and put it back in his mouth.

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman rose from his seat and looked at Lee Sungmin. "Follow me."

Lee Sungmin did as he was told, following his master out of the room. The moment they were outside, a hand clamped down on his shoulder, and a voice echoed directly in his mind.

[the Little Heavenly Demon.]

Lee Sungmin's shoulders trembled at the mention of her title.

[We must find that girl.]
