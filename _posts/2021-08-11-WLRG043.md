Ogre.

Among the countless monsters that roamed Eria, the Ogre stood as one of the most powerful bipedal creatures. They boasted a natural resistance to magic, and their hides were so tough that most blades simply could not cut them. Furthermore, they possessed a rather high intelligence that made them tricky to deal with. While it varied by individual, some Ogres were even capable of using magic.

The Mercenary Guild assigned ranks to the monsters of Eria, a system based on whether a mercenary of a corresponding rank could subjugate them. A common Orc was rank E. An Ogre started at a minimum of A.

In his past life, Lee Sungmin had never once accepted a request to hunt an Ogre. They were dangerous monsters, and he knew the value of his own life.

*An Ogre, huh.*

Lee Sungmin stroked his chin. It was this very monster that he now had to face. The request Bern had brought for his skill verification was to subjugate an Ogre that had been spotted in the forest on Behenger's outskirts, a plea submitted to the Mercenary Guild by a nearby village.

The carriage rattled along the road.

"Do you need to come along?" Lee Sungmin asked Rudd, who was sitting across from him.

At the question, Rudd grinned, baring his teeth. "I was free, and the branch manager asked me to tag along. Said he wanted me to see for myself."

"You think I'm going to pull some kind of trick?"

"Nah, it's not like that. The sword energy you showed yesterday was real. Let's just say the branch manager and I are both curious about a guy like you." Rudd chuckled as he spoke. Since he was a freelance mercenary unaffiliated with any group, time was all he had when he was between requests. Lee Sungmin felt a little awkward about how friendly Rudd was being.

Lee Sungmin already knew of Rudd, of course. He had seen the man several times in his past life, though they had never spoken. An S-Rank freelance mercenary was an unusual existence even within the Behenger Mercenary Guild, a figure from a world entirely different from Lee Sungmin's own C-Rank life on the bottom rungs.

He remembered the tavern on the guild's first floor, how he and the other low-rankers would drink with prostitutes while watching men like Rudd sip expensive liquor at the bar. He had harbored jealousy and envy then, feelings he never dared to voice. Now, a person from that different world was showing him a friendly attitude. Of course, he couldn't know what ulterior motives Rudd might have. Lee Sungmin remained wary and suspicious, but he did not push the man away. A person could not live alone. He had learned that from Baek Sogo on Mount Mushii.

"I'm not doubting your skill," Rudd said, "but you're young. You said you're a Classless and have only been in Eria for four years, right?"

"You think I'll make a mistake because I'm inexperienced?"

"That's the gist of it. Don't get too offended. Both the branch manager and I just want to make sure you're safe. It's not every day a master of your caliber comes looking to be a mercenary."

It seemed Rudd wasn't just following along out of simple curiosity, though it wasn't simple goodwill or sympathy either. Lee Sungmin stared at the man's face for a moment before glancing out the window at the rushing scenery.

"You're quiet for your age. Are you always this reserved?"

"......"

That wasn't actually the case. It was merely a side effect of the silent training he had endured on Mount Mushii. Lee Sungmin was simply used to silence.

The training on the mountain had given him much, but it had not all been beneficial. A dulling of his senses was the most pressing problem. He constantly heard a faint static and smelled a foul odor. By restricting even his sight, Lee Sungmin had trained his ability to 'feel' with his skin to a sharp and sensitive edge, but he had not been able to train his sense of smell or hearing in the same way.

He was also mindful of his lack of real fighting spirit. His training on the mountain had been solitary, and the rule that practitioners could not harm each other meant he could not even spar. Although he had real combat experience from the memories of his past life, the Lee Sungmin of then and the Lee Sungmin of now were different enough to be considered two separate people.

Moreover, an opponent like an Ogre was a behemoth his past self had never faced. He could not rely heavily on his old experiences for this fight.

"Any experience fighting an Ogre?" Rudd asked.

"I've never even seen one."

Around sunset, the carriage passed through the castle gates. From what he had heard, the forest where the Ogre had appeared was still another half-day's ride away.

"However, I believe I have the basic knowledge," Lee Sungmin added. "If we keep on like this, we'll arrive late. Is that alright?"

"We'll stay the night in the village that sent the request. If the Ogre decides to attack tonight... well, we'll just have to deal with it then." Rudd didn't seem tense at all. Unlike Lee Sungmin, he was already an S-Rank Mercenary and had likely subjugated Ogres before. "We lump them together as 'Ogres', but monsters aren't all identical even if they're the same species. Some Ogres can even use magic."

"I know."

"Good, then. There was no information that this Ogre uses magic, but it's best to keep it in mind. I may be your observer and protector, but I have no intention of helping you from the start. I'll only step in if you're about to die."

"I understand," Lee Sungmin replied.

The sun had already set, but the carriage continued on its way. Rudd let his body sway with the carriage's movements and opened the bag slung over his shoulder. It must have been a Subspace Pocket, as he pulled a large loaf of bread from inside. Lee Sungmin accepted the piece Rudd offered him. It was just ordinary rye bread, but to Lee Sungmin, whose sense of taste was no longer restricted, it was delicious enough to be a heavenly meal.

"You said you learned martial arts, right? Do you use a weapon?"

"Yes. I use a spear."

"A spear! That's a good weapon. I use a sword. Personally, I'd rather not fight a spear user. They're tricky weapons to deal with."

Rudd talked to Lee Sungmin ceaselessly, though the topics he brought up were of little interest. After all, Lee Sungmin already knew that Rudd used a sword.

"You seem like a good kid, but you're no fun to talk to," Rudd grumbled after a while of talking by himself.

Lee Sungmin gave a bitter smile at his words. "I'm on the quiet side."

"I've never met a guy who calls himself quiet who actually is. Most of the time, it just means they don't want to talk to you."

"That's not it."

"I'm kidding," Rudd said, bursting out laughing by himself.

Late that night, the carriage finally arrived at the village. Rudd hopped down first and led the way, introducing Lee Sungmin to the village chief who had come out to greet them. Hearing the introduction, the village chief furrowed his brow.

"He looks so young..."

"Age and skill don't have much to do with each other," Rudd said with a grin, patting Lee Sungmin's shoulder. "This kid could probably kill everyone in this village if they all came at him."

The village chief, an old man with a messy beard, flinched at Rudd's words and met Lee Sungmin's eyes. Lee Sungmin simply looked back with an indifferent expression before giving a slight nod.

"...Ahem. I've prepared a room. If you wish, I can bring you a meal."

"Thank you."

The place the village chief led them to was a spare room in his own house. After quickly unpacking, Rudd sat on the floor and looked at Lee Sungmin.

"How about a drink?"

"No."

"Why not? 'Cause you're young? Don't be ridiculous. What's age got to do with drinking? And where in the world is a mercenary who doesn't drink?" Rudd grumbled as he pulled a large bottle of liquor from his bag. "Liquor is good. It helps you relax..."

He hummed, drinking straight from the bottle. Watching him, Lee Sungmin sat down cross-legged. He prodded the inner qi in his qi center once but did not circulate the Great Heavenly Cycle. He didn't fully trust Rudd.

"Is it okay if we don't stand guard?"

"Why should we go that far?" Rudd set the bottle down after a long gulp. "The request we received is for the Ogre's subjugation, not the village's protection. Of course, if they offered more money and asked us to stand guard, I'd be willing to do it, but we can't go doing things we weren't asked to. We're not pushovers."

It was a point Lee Sungmin understood perfectly. He was a mercenary, after all.

"What moves a mercenary isn't compassion or sympathy," Rudd chuckled, taking another swig. "It's money."

Lee Sungmin quietly nodded. He knew. The Lee Sungmin of a few years ago would never have even asked such a question.

However, now.

Baek Sogo's words, the things she had told him not to forget, weighed slightly on his mind. He wasn't greatly bound by them, however. Lee Sungmin and Baek Sogo were different people. Just because she was a good person didn't mean he had to be one too. Baek Sogo had told him not to forget his debts of gratitude. He owed no such debt to these villagers. He was a mercenary who had accepted a request. That was the only reason he was here.

That was all.

There was no attack during the night. Lee Sungmin woke early in the morning, a force of habit. His sleep was peaceful now that the nightmares were gone, but years of barely sleeping at all had conditioned him to wake before the sun.

In one corner, Rudd was curled up fast asleep, his arms wrapped around his shoulders. It was a strange sight, seeing an S-Rank Mercenary sleeping in such a way. Lee Sungmin glanced at the empty liquor bottle rolling on the floor before quietly leaving the room.

He breathed in the cold dawn air. Stepping out into the backyard, he saw chickens huddled in their coop. They just stared at him, not making a sound. Lee Sungmin slowly raised the spear he had brought with him.

Rannachal.

He performed Rannachal, not the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear. Slowly, deliberately, he tensed the muscles of his entire body, conscious of every single movement. Though they were basic forms, he did not perform them with anything less than his full attention.

"Diligent, aren't you."

Rudd came out after the dawn had passed and the morning sun was high in the sky. He approached Lee Sungmin with a big yawn.

"You should've slept a little longer. You'll get tired wandering around the forest."

"I'm not the type to sleep deeply."

"It's not because I was snoring loudly during the night, is it?"

"That's not it."

Rudd chuckled at Lee Sungmin's reply and glanced at the spear in his hands. It was an unremarkable weapon, but it was severely worn out.

"You should get a new weapon too."

"I have a spare."

"That's a relief, then. Come on, let's go inside." Rudd patted Lee Sungmin's shoulder. "We need to eat before we go to the forest."

"......"

Lee Sungmin lowered his spear.
