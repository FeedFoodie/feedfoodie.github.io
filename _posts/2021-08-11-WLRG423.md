The master of the Battle God Chronicle patted Lee Sungmin's shoulder a few times before stepping back, a satisfied expression settling on his face. It was only natural. The Battle God Chronicle, which had until now lacked a clear physical form, had just acquired its first domain.

"What happens now?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"What about?"

"You… no, the Battle God Chronicle has taken Eria as its domain. Does that mean you'll all be living in this world now?"

The master of the Battle God Chronicle asked with a grin, "Why? You don't like that?"

Lee Sungmin did not answer right away. In the end, Eria had escaped the fate of The End, but the problem was what came next.

"…I'm not sure."

"Don't worry," the master replied in a laughing voice. "I have my own world to live in. I have no intention of needlessly living here." He elaborated, "I've made it my domain, but I don't plan on living in this world. My goal is to make the Battle God Chronicle a true divine realm, and this land has merely become its first Sanctuary. I don't know how much faith I'll receive from here yet, but well, let's just think of it as a type of investment."

"An investment…"

"Don't think too hard about it. In the end, it turned out just as you wished. The destruction known as The End no longer exists in this world. Perhaps in the distant future, this world might meet a natural end, just as several other dimensions have. That's not my concern, though." The master of the Battle God Chronicle's gaze drifted to the records filling the library as he spoke.

"There's no need to worry too much about the Fairy Realm and the Elemental Realm either. The events that unfold in this world from now on will be recorded here, and those who have been interfering will share those records, just as they always have. Are you dissatisfied with that?"

"No, I'm not."

"There's no reason to be. It won't have a negative impact on this world. Look at it positively. Now that this world has become a Sanctuary of the Battle God Chronicle, you all have the backing of the most violent divinities in existence." He added, "And as I said, I also intended to pick a fight directly with the Great Demon World. That will be a good stimulus for the eternity the Battle God Chronicle will spend from now on."

With his words, the records that had filled the library vanished into thin air. The master of the Battle God Chronicle stretched widely.

"Work hard on proselytizing. To be blunt, if this place is the Battle God Chronicle's first Sanctuary, then you are my first unorthodox. Not that you'll be able to receive any power from me, though."

"Because you're not a god?"

"That's right. Not every Absolute Being is a god. To become one, you need a Sanctuary, you need the unorthodox, and you need faith."

Their interests had aligned perfectly. Lee Sungmin had wanted to free Eria from the fate of The End and make it a real world, not a Slaughterhouse. All of that was resolved the moment the Battle God Chronicle directly Advented in the library.

"I'm leaving." The master of the Battle God Chronicle turned around. The beings who walked the single path, who had Advented alongside him, all looked to their master. "The next time I see you, it'll be in the Battle God Chronicle, I suppose. Don't come too soon. Work as much as you can as an unorthodox and build up some faith. Come after that. If you come after doing a half-assed job, you'll die."

Without waiting for a reply, the master of the Battle God Chronicle disappeared. Starting with him, the beings of the Battle God Chronicle vanished one by one. Lee Sungmin watched the single path connected to the library. Its master was already at the far end, and the walkers of the path followed dutifully behind him.

"Let me ask you something." The Spear King, who had been glaring at the master's back, finally spoke. He turned his head to look at Lee Sungmin. "Was the Martial God strong?"

"So-so."

"……"

The Spear King's brow furrowed at the answer. He glanced toward the single path and grumbled, "I should have fought the Martial God too… That moron. To think he'd die without even making it here."

A bitter smile touched Lee Sungmin's lips.

"Black Dragon Knight," the Spear King muttered. "Tell that guy to work hard. I came, so that bastard has to come too."

"He's busy with his love life."

"What kind of bullshit is that?"

"He's fallen for the Saintess."

"Rip his dick off," the Spear King replied without a moment's hesitation. His consistent attitude sent a chill down Lee Sungmin's spine rather than prompting an empty laugh. "That bastard, what love life? What's the age difference… Tell him to stop doing useless things. If he doesn't listen, you rip his dick off for me. A martial artist doesn't need a woman."

"Then what about female martial artists?" Lee Sungmin asked, curious what he would say.

The Spear King frowned. "There's nothing to rip off."

"That's… true."

"You crazy bastard. What are you thinking?"

The question, coming from the Spear King of all people, made him feel strangely awkward. The Spear King snorted, turned, and looked up at the single path. Without another word of farewell, he headed for it. Now, the only ones left in the space, aside from the Spirit and the Demonic Spirit, were the Void Monarch and the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman.

"Have you been well?" the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman asked casually.

Lee Sungmin let out an empty laugh and looked at him. He needlessly rubbed the corner of his eye with a fingertip, where the tears he had shed earlier were now gone without a trace.

"You're asking that a bit late."

"Seeing as you're still alive, you must have been doing well enough," the Chairman chuckled. "I even gave you this one's corpse and told you not to die, to survive."

The contents of the will he had seen several times came to mind. The will left by the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman was still with him.

"And you'll continue to do well in the future."

"I'll have to." Lee Sungmin turned his head, and the Chairman met his gaze with a faint smile.

"…Master, are you doing well?"

"I am. It's more enjoyable than when I lived here. Over there, the boredom that used to drive this one mad doesn't exist."

Sungmin understood completely, for he carried the Chairman's memories within him. He could empathize with his words unconditionally. Most of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman's life had been steeped in boredom, a long existence feeling almost no emotion for the secular world. In previous worlds, his death had always been by his own hand, and this world had not been much different.

"It's not that I don't feel a sense of longing," the Chairman admitted, looking up the single path. "I also think about how fleeting it was. In this world, this one was history's strongest."

"I was stronger," the Void Monarch, who had been listening silently, interjected.

The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman completely ignored him. "But over there, I'm not. That's what made it more enjoyable for this one."

"The longing… what is it for?"

"The time when this one was human." Those words also served as proof that the man before him was no longer human. "Even when I was human… to be honest, I wasn't very human-like. Still, I do miss those days a little. I miss the quiet life I spent in the Fairy Forest. In truth, life in that forest wasn't very interesting. However… this one liked that tranquility. That place over there is far from tranquil. One must always struggle and move forward."

Even as he said it, there was not the slightest sign that the Chairman wanted to go back. A being with such a lax heart would find it impossible to escape the Cycle of Mortality, let alone walk that path. The Chairman reached out and patted Lee Sungmin's shoulder.

"You were the only disciple this one ever taught. This one is beyond comparison, but even so, I think if I had taken a snot-nosed kid from a country village and raised him, he would have had more talent than you."

"I'm not that bad."

"That's what you think. In any case, having a disciple like you brought me joy in my later years. This one has never raised a dog in my life, but it felt like raising a dog."

"Don't lie." He had been demoted from a snot-nosed kid to a pet dog. Lee Sungmin furrowed his brow and denied the Chairman's words. He knew better. The feelings he had inherited, the emotions the Chairman had held for him, were not the affection one held for a dog.

"Since you have this one's memories, I can't even lie, can I," the Chairman let out a hearty laugh. "I'm glad that everything of this one's was a help to you. I was glad you were this one's disciple, and I was glad this one was your Master. It's good to see you again like this."

Hearing his words, Lee Sungmin bowed his head deeply. The Chairman's memories mixed with the words he was speaking now, intensifying the feelings he had held for his student. Feeling them wash over him, Lee Sungmin closed his eyes.

"Take your time coming," the Chairman's presence grew faint. "It's a long road over there. Once you ascend, it's hard to come back down or turn back. No, you can do it. It's just that if you do, you can never enter that place again."

"I know."

"Come without leaving any regrets. This one left a few regrets. That's why… I sometimes feel that longing."

That was the last shred of humanity the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman possessed. Lee Sungmin raised his head. He looked at the fading figure and said, "I was glad that you were my Master."

"Give my regards to Osla and Yehwa," the Chairman replied with a smile, and then his form vanished.

A variety of emotions washed over Lee Sungmin. Bitterness, sorrow, longing. The memories of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman that had stirred his heart faded, and his own memories took their place.

"Well now." The Void Monarch, now left alone, scratched the back of his head. He looked at Lee Sungmin with an awkward expression. "We're seeing each other again much sooner than I thought, so I don't really have anything to say."

Lee Sungmin chuckled. It had not even been a full week since the Void Monarch, who had always occupied a corner of his consciousness, had left him for the Battle God Chronicle.

"Did you cry because you missed this Elder?"

"I did," he answered honestly.

The Void Monarch blinked a few times, then burst out laughing. "You disgusting bastard."

"I kept a promise, too."

"What promise?"

"The one about going into the cesspool."

At that, the Void Monarch laughed even louder. Clutching his stomach, he choked out a question between gasps. "How did it feel?"

"It was horrible."

"As long as you know. You son of a bitch, I should have dunked you in a cesspool long ago. Do you finally understand this Elder's suffering?"

"But back then, you were the one who did something deserving of being thrown in a cesspool."

"You're the trash one for getting all serious and threatening to dunk me in a cesspool over a few jokes," the Void Monarch clicked his tongue. "Just think of it as a good experience. When else in your life will you get to go into a cesspool?"

"Probably not."

"Kekeke! I didn't think you'd actually keep a promise made in jest. I didn't know you were so obedient. I guess I have nothing to worry about in the future then."

The Void Monarch reached out his hand, his large palm coming to rest on top of Lee Sungmin's head. He smiled fondly as he roughly ruffled Lee Sungmin's hair.

"You are alone," the Void Monarch said, his tone softening. "But from now on, there will always be someone else by your side. I think that's… a relief. Even if this Elder can't see it for myself, and I can't settle in your head and feel it with you like before. Still… I think it's a relief." He smiled warmly. "I said it back then, but being with you was fun. Life in 'that place' is also fun, but sometimes… yes. Just like that guy, Yang Ilcheon, said. The past becomes a source of longing."

Even if you thought you had no regrets right before you died, it was truly impossible to have none at all. Everyone died with regrets, whether they were many or few.

"My regret was you." The Void Monarch's hand stilled on his hair. "Because you were such a moronic idiot. I was so worried about you. Still, I believed in you. When I decided to leave you, and as I was leaving… I believed. I believed that you wouldn't Despair, that you wouldn't break, and that you wouldn't die."

That belief was what had allowed the Void Monarch to leave him. His hand left Lee Sungmin's head.

"You said you'd never been happy. Be happy from now on. This shitty world is over. It might still be shitty, but not like before. You ultimately achieved what you wanted. You saved the world from The End."

A torrent of emotions surfaced. He thought of the people he had met and parted with until now. The Crazy Heavenly Demon, who knew nothing of The End but traveled with him anyway, only to be blocked by the world's limits and die, unable to overcome his own destiny. Abel, who sacrificed his life with the sole intention of stopping The End, who banished the unkillable Kim Jonghyun from spacetime before his own lifespan ran out. The Sword Immortal, who lost his cherished disciple Cheongmyeong and blocked Jeniela out of vengeance, allowing Lee Sungmin to escape. Besides them, countless other events flashed through his mind.

"You can feel relieved," the Void Monarch said. "You have repaid the regrets and expectations placed upon you. So… from now on, live as you wish. You said you've never been happy, so live happily now. Enjoy everything you want to enjoy, and as much as possible… without any regrets."

"…Yeah."

"This Elder's regret was you." The Void Monarch looked toward the single path. "If you live a satisfying life, my regret will disappear."

"…Wait." Lee Sungmin felt the Void Monarch's presence fading and hurriedly tried to stop him.

The Void Monarch, who had been wearing a satisfied expression, furrowed his brow. "What is it? I was all set to leave."

"Don't you have anything to say to Yana?"

At those words, the Void Monarch looked as if he had been struck in the head with a hammer. Seeing that expression, Lee Sungmin let out a deep sigh. He recalled the image of Yana in the Demonic Spirit Pavilion, crying sorrowfully while desperately suppressing her emotions.

"That… um…"

"You son of a bitch…" Until that very moment, the Void Monarch had not given Yana a second thought.

As Lee Sungmin gave him a resentful look, the Void Monarch hesitated before squeezing his eyes shut. "Tell her I'm sorry."

"……"

"Uh… tell her to live happily."

"……"

"Forget a guy like me… um… find a decent Yokai or… a human, and if she can, have some kids…"

"Are you serious?"

"Ah, fuck, then what the hell am I supposed to say?" the Void Monarch's face twisted as he snapped. "I'm sorry, and I'm sorry I couldn't return her feelings. Live well. That's it. There's nothing more to say."

"You're not even going to say you'll see her again someday?"

"Dammit, this isn't some neighborhood restaurant anyone can just drop by whenever they want…" he grumbled. "Also tell her, 'Let's see each other if we can'."

"Fine."

Only after hearing that did Lee Sungmin nod in satisfaction. The fact that he now had something encouraging to say to Yana put his mind at ease.

"You meddling bastard…"

"There's no one else to pass on the message. If you had just left a few words for Yana before you left last time, this wouldn't have happened."

"Did I have time to think about that then?" the Void Monarch shot back. "Anyway. I've said all I have to say. Dammit, you brought up something unnecessary and ruined the mood for leaving."

"Thanks," Lee Sungmin said with a small laugh. "I said it back then, but thank you. For everything… all of it."

"As long as you know." The Void Monarch smiled back, and his body disappeared.

The library was still connected to the Battle God Chronicle, but none of the walkers of the single path remained. For them, reaching their master at the end of that path was more important than anything else.

"…Hoo."

Lee Sungmin looked at the single path visible in the distance. He watched the master of the Battle God Chronicle at its end and the people walking toward him, suppressing a powerful impulse that rose in his chest. It was the urge to walk that path right this instant. He saw the Void Monarch and the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, already far ahead, and the Spear King, slowly advancing from the entrance. He imagined a life of moving forward with a clear goal, of striving to surpass it. He felt more anticipation than vague fear.

*No.*

Not yet. Lee Sungmin slowly turned around.

"Now, then."

The Demonic Spirit was looking at him with a slightly nervous expression. Lee Sungmin gave the Demonic Spirit a warm smile.

"What should we do now?"

The Spirit, who was slumped on the floor, trembled like an aspen leaf.
