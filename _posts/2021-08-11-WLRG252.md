The Demonic Spirit's single, floating eye narrowed. Undeterred, the Martial God stared into it and pressed his question.

"Don't avoid the question. I know this world will face The End soon enough."

[If you know that, then you know how meaningless your question is.]

"My question was whether it's set in stone."

[All things must come to an end. This world is no exception.]

"Is there a way to avoid it?"

[You have your role,] the Demonic Spirit whispered. [Every being has its own role, and those roles are unequal. I won't say the role and destiny you possess are small, but they aren't that great either.]

"I am the one who decides that."

[You are, among the swarm of ants crawling on the ground, an especially large ant,] the Demonic Spirit continued. [That is what you are. That ant has a role, and even if it insists otherwise, it can never stop being an ant.]

At those words, the Martial God's expression hardened.

RUMBLE...!

Power surged from the Martial God's body, swelling as if to blot out the sky.

"If you are one of the unorthodox who bring about The End, then perhaps I can delay it, even for a moment, by extinguishing your existence right here."

[Wrong.]

The Demonic Spirit's reply to the Martial God's determined words was a small yet powerful voice.

[I am not an apostle who carries out The End. I merely interfere and present the path, and as I said, you have your role.]

"What is my role?!"

[I will not present your path to you.]

The Demonic Spirit's eye slowly closed.

[However, I will tell you this. Your role does not end here. This is the end of my courtesy to you. Go back.]

The Martial God's eyebrow twitched at those words. He kicked off the ground, lunging toward the Demonic Spirit in an instant.

However, the Demonic Spirit's eye, which had been closed, snapped open.

"......"

The Martial God's airborne body vanished on the spot.

Wiji Hoyeon had watched it all happen. She had heard everything, yet she remained unshaken.

A mysterious sense of disharmony washed over her, a feeling that she was not quite herself, or perhaps that she had just realized something profound. Staring at the Demonic Spirit, Wiji Hoyeon asked her own question.

"What is my role?"

Lloyd received a summons from the Council of Elders before he could even return to Ruvus.

He looked down at the crystal ball, a knot of nervousness in his stomach. He had much to say, much to report. There was the failure of the subjugation, the matter of Kim Jonghyun, and the issue with the Murim Alliance, which had yet to announce its position on the Moyong Clan Head's rampage. Not much time had passed since the subjugation ended, so their silence was expected for now.

*This incident can't just be overlooked.*

It could not be covered up as the mere personal rampage of the Moyong Clan Head. To condone it would set a dangerous precedent. Even if it was a personal act, the Murim Alliance had to be held responsible, as the Moyong Clan served as one of its pillars.

*It's obvious what those old fools from the Council of Elders will blabber on about.*

They would likely try to smooth things over by talking about mutual interests. Lloyd sighed deeply and placed his hand on the crystal ball.

The Council of Elders were supposedly retired mages, but they held undeniable power within the Mages Guild. Lloyd did not respect them. Those old men, having achieved their own ambitions, made no effort to communicate with the younger generation. They reigned from on high as if they deserved reverence, all while jealously guarding the knowledge and status they had gained.

[Why don't you relax your face?]

The moment he grasped the crystal ball and infused it with magic, a voice echoed in his mind. Lloyd's shoulders flinched.

*This voice...*

His eyebrows trembled. He had heard this voice only once before, at the ceremony appointing him as the Golden Magic Tower Master.

"...Is that you, Guildmaster?"

[Looks like you haven't forgotten, kid,] the Guildmaster chuckled.

Lloyd immediately bowed his head. The Mages Guild Master was the pinnacle of their organization, existing behind the Tower Masters and the Council of Elders. To Lloyd, he was also more than just a title. He was the younger brother of Envirus, the Master who had taught Lloyd general magic.

"Why... are you here in person, Guildmaster?"

[Because this is important. Or would you rather deal with the idiots on the Council?]

"No... sir."

Even though he was his Master's brother, Lloyd had never met the Guildmaster in person. He had only ever heard his voice, even during his own appointment. Lloyd knew of their family connection, but the Guildmaster had never once mentioned it or given any indication he knew.

[Start with your report. What happened during the subjugation?]

At the Guildmaster's question, Lloyd hesitated, glancing at the documents spread across his table. They detailed the events of the subjugation, and he began to read their contents aloud.

[So that kid, Kim Jonghyun, was trying to pull off something very interesting,] the Guildmaster said with a smirk. [Do you know what he was trying to do?]

"...I don't know."

[That bastard was trying to become a Demon Lord.]

"What?"

Lloyd's eyes widened. Trying to become a Demon Lord. He couldn't take the words at face value. By his standards, a human becoming a Demon Lord was an impossible feat.

[But he failed. It was his destiny to do so.]

"Does failing mean... Kim Jonghyun is dead?"

[No. He's not dead. His name is still written in the Grace, which means he's still the owner of the Grimoire.]

The Guildmaster spoke as if it were no big deal, and Lloyd could not understand his casual attitude. He was certain this was not a matter to be taken so lightly.

[The Murim Alliance attacked the Mages Guild?]

"Yes... The Green and White Tower Masters, myself included, have agreed that this incident must not be overlooked."

[Then let's sort out our relationship with them,] the Guildmaster said briskly.

Lloyd could only stand there with his mouth agape. "...Just like that?"

[I'm not planning to sever ties completely, but I do intend to make it clear we're offended.]

"How... do you mean?"

[We'll think about that later. More importantly, a lot has happened. It seems Predator has started to move as well.] Hmm, hmmm. The Guildmaster, lost in thought on the other side of the crystal ball, finally spoke again. [Is the Crimson Archmage with the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance?]

"It seems likely."

[Good. Don't go back to the Golden Magic Tower. I'll head there myself and meet you when you arrive.]

"What?" Lloyd let out a surprised sound.

The heat was so intense it felt as if it could cook the flesh from his bones. Lee Sungmin, who had perfected Extreme Temperature Immunity, normally didn't feel heat or cold, but the waves radiating from this place made even him gasp for air.

"What's with all the cursing?" he muttered, watching the back of the Dwarf hammering away before the blazing furnace.

"Wouldn't you be cursing too, kid!" the Dwarf chieftain, Mackendor, roared, whipping his head around with a contorted face.

Even among the long-lived Dwarves, Mackendor was one of the oldest, yet he possessed a sturdy, muscular body that belied his age. Sweating profusely, he paused his hammering to take a ragged breath.

"You show up out of nowhere with a letter from the Fairy Queen and order me to start hammering!"

"You seemed happy enough at first, getting to work with a dragon's scales and bones."

"I didn't think it'd be such a damn pain in the ass!" Mackendor bellowed. "Dammit, I thought it would be a few scale fragments and some bone shards. Who would've thought it'd be whole bones and thousands of unstripped scales?!"

"You were happy about that, too," Lee Sungmin pointed out.

He really had been. Just a few hours ago, Mackendor had shown great passion, declaring that his craftsman's spirit was burning for the first time in decades as he placed the scales into the Sun's Furnace, a forge only the chieftain could handle. Three hours later, however, Mackendor's passion had cooled completely, a stark contrast to the scorching heat spewing from the furnace.

"I'm old," Mackendor muttered, picking up his hammer again. "If I were thirty years younger, I could hammer for dozens of hours without a problem, but not anymore... I'm an old man. You should've come thirty years ago. Why show up now?"

"I wasn't even born thirty years ago."

"You little brat!" Mackendor spat another curse at the reply and brought his hammer down. "Beer... I need beer. A nice, cold beer..."

"I can't trust the hammering of someone who's drinking."

"Every Dwarf needs to drink for their artistic soul to burn!"

"What I'm asking for isn't a work of art full of artistic soul. I need sturdy armor and a sharp spear."

"What do you know about art, you son of a bitch?"

Despite his outburst, Mackendor didn't stop hammering. Lee Sungmin wiped the sweat trickling down his forehead with the back of his hand. It had taken him two months to reach the Dwarf Village after leaving the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance. After a brief greeting with Celgerus, who also lived there, he had come straight to the Sun's Furnace, shown Mackendor the letter from Osla, and requested the creation of his new equipment.

"Are you going to keep watching? At this rate, it looks like it'll take a month to make!"

"I feel like you won't do it properly if I'm not watching."

"What do you take me for! Now that I've received a letter from the Fairy Queen, I'll do my best, so you don't have to worry." Mackendor pulled the Dragon's bone from the furnace. "Damned Dragon bone. It's been stuck in a furnace that can melt Mithril or Orichalcum in an instant for hours, and it's still fine."

"Can it even be processed?"

"If it were impossible, I would've said so from the start. Why don't you stop bothering me and get lost?"

"That's the plan."

He had stayed because he felt a little sorry for Mackendor, who was suffering in the heat. It seemed unreasonable to stay any longer. To think there was a heat that could even penetrate Extreme Temperature Immunity. Lee Sungmin pushed his sweat-soaked hair back and turned around.

"The bastard, I tell him to go and he actually goes."

"What do you want me to do?"

"It's boring hammering all by myself."

[He's a senile old fool,] the Void Monarch muttered in disbelief at Mackendor's grumbling. [They say he's been cooped up in this furnace for decades. Maybe that's why he's lost his mind?]

*I think so too,* Lee Sungmin somewhat agreed.

"...I heard you were originally a member of Erebrisa. Why did you give up your membership?"

"All sorts of bastards came looking for me, nagging me to make them things. It was annoying and irritating. I wanted to retire and rest, you know? Just making what I want to make once in a while."

"Is that why you holed yourself up in this furnace?"

"Right now it's hot as hell because the fire's lit, but normally it's not this hot. It's a very cozy and nice place. I intend to die here. In the end, after creating my final masterpiece, I will throw myself into the furnace and burn to death."

"That's a death that would be a nuisance to those left behind."

"I think it's cool," Mackendor said without hesitation.

Lee Sungmin, of course, didn't sympathize in the slightest.

"I need an assistant," Mackendor gasped, having been hammering repeatedly. "I'm old. You. Do you have any experience with hammering?"

"I know how to do simple weapon maintenance."

"Even the mutts in this village know how to do that."

"Are you saying the dogs here know how to hammer?"

"Can't you take a joke?"

"That was a joke, too."

"You son of a bitch." Mackendor's shoulders trembled. "Right. The spear and armor you were using. You said the guy who made them lives in this village, right?"

"......"

"Go get him. Tell him he's my new assistant."

"Are you sure about that?"

"What's not to be sure about?"

"He's a Dark Elf."

"As long as a craftsman can hammer well, what does race matter?" Mackendor replied in an annoyed voice.

Lee Sungmin let out a small laugh. He was well aware that Celgerus, despite holding the title of a blacksmith Meister, was not treated properly because he was a Dark Elf. He had received help from Celgerus several times before. The spear and armor he had made had been very useful, and he had only asked for a pittance in return. While it was partly for Celgerus's own satisfaction, Lee Sungmin still felt grateful to him.

"I understand."

Pleased that he might be able to help Celgerus unintentionally, Lee Sungmin turned and walked out of the furnace.
