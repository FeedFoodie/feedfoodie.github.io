As the closest city to Zenavis, Behenger was the first destination for most otherworlders upon their departure. This influx had made the city large and well-developed, a place where those summoned to the world of Eria could truly begin their lives. If Zenavis was the cradle where they acclimated, Behenger was the foundation upon which they built their futures.

That foundation was often laid within a Guild, a place where an otherworlder decided the direction their new life would take. Those with a mind for commerce sought out the Merchants' Guild, while aspiring smiths went to the Blacksmith Guild. Though many different Guilds existed, an especially large number of otherworlders knocked on the door of the Mercenary Guild. The requirements for joining were not particularly strict, and once a person became a mercenary, a path to survival was guaranteed.

The opening of the Mercenary Guild's door was therefore hardly a major event. Even the sight of a boy in shabby clothes stepping inside was common enough at the Behenger branch. Only the mercenaries seated near the entrance showed any interest in the new face.

"Hey, kid. Think you can earn your keep?"

"Damn, kid, you'd need a pretty face for that. You look like somethin' I wouldn't even take for free."

"Go ask your mommy to make you some food! Assuming you even have one!"

The men cackled, but Lee Sungmin said nothing as he closed the door behind him. Like most of its kind, the Behenger Mercenary Guild's first floor also served as a tavern for its members. A few were already thoroughly drunk in the middle of the day, but that was simply the way of mercenaries.

They earned for the day and enjoyed for the day, for concepts like saving or frugality were foreign to them. Any quest that paid well naturally demanded they risk their lives, and no matter how iron-willed a person was, repeatedly putting their life on the line would wear them down. The desire for self-reward was only natural. They had risked their lives and survived. They had worked hard. What was the point of saving money when a mercenary never knew when they might die? So they spent it. They played.

Lee Sungmin had been a mercenary in his past life, too. He was intimately familiar with the lifestyle of chugging from a bottle in broad daylight and the atmosphere that simultaneously welcomed and ostracized newcomers. A new guy turning out to be a nobody was a relief, but if he was skilled, they grew envious. Before they were mercenaries, they were human, and that was just how humans were.

Ignoring the jeers, Lee Sungmin crossed the hall. The stench of alcohol grew stronger the deeper he went. A few mercenaries had prostitutes at their sides, their hands casually groping breasts. They moved aggressively, as if showing off the lewd gestures, while the women skillfully accepted their advances and let out pleasing moans.

Lee Sungmin didn't spare them a single glance. He finally stopped in front of the bar where mercenaries in more respectable attire were seated. They paid him no mind either, focused on the drinks the bartender served or their own conversations.

The bartender was no different, paying Lee Sungmin no attention. Lee Sungmin knew exactly who he was. Though he had arrived a year later than in his past life, the man's face was unchanged.

"I'm here to become a mercenary," Lee Sungmin spoke up.

At his words, the bartender, who had been wiping a glass, finally glanced at him. He was a middle-aged man with a clean-cut face, tall but not stoutly built, with narrow, sharp eyes.

"Did you get a letter of introduction from somewhere?" he asked.

Lee Sungmin shook his head. The bartender made a face that said he had expected as much.

"If you want to become a mercenary, go up to the second floor. They're taking applications there," the bartender said, clearly having nothing more to say on the matter.

Lee Sungmin already knew applications were handled on the second floor. He had spoken to the bartender regardless because he wanted to make a distinct impression.

Bern.

That was the bartender's name, and he was also the Guildmaster of the Behenger Mercenary Guild. Bern was a highly skilled mage.

"Pardon me," Lee Sungmin muttered, reaching out to pick up a fork from the table. The fork's owner glanced at him but did not intervene.

WHOOSH.

A violet light enveloped the fork in Lee Sungmin's hand. He held it out with a deadpan expression, and Bern's face instantly changed.

"Looks like the master got one-upped. He must've underestimated you 'cause you're a kid," the mercenary who owned the fork chuckled lowly.

Lee Sungmin knew who he was, too. Rudd. He was a free mercenary belonging to the Behenger Guild, not part of any corps. Though not from the murim, he was an S-Rank Mercenary with skills comparable to a peak master.

"...So you were a martial artist," Bern muttered with a sigh, putting down the glass he was wiping.

The mercenaries who had been glancing over began to murmur amongst themselves. What Lee Sungmin had coated the fork with was 'sword ki,' proof that he was a peak master who could freely wield inner qi.

"Sit here," Bern said, pointing to an empty chair.

Lee Sungmin put down the fork and sat. Bern stared intently at his face.

"How old are you?" he asked.

"I'm eighteen."

"Sword ki at eighteen. You're a calamity."

Rudd, who was sitting nearby, chuckled at the assessment. It was a common one. Anyone who reached the peak realm before their twenties was worthy of being called a calamity. The words left a bitter taste in Lee Sungmin's mouth. He knew all too well that he was no such thing.

"How long have you been summoned to Eria?"

"It's been about four years as of this year."

"Four years... that's quite a while. It's strange you haven't become a mercenary until now. Did you come straight from Zenavis?"

"No. I left Zenavis three years ago and have been traveling since."

"Is that so?" Bern nodded. Information was exchanged between Mercenary Guilds anyway. If he put his mind to it, he could easily find out what Lee Sungmin had been doing in Zenavis. "Is there a reason you want to become a mercenary?"

"It's the easiest path, and it offers a fair amount of freedom."

"That's right. Mercenaries are free. Especially the higher-ranked ones," Bern replied.

Low-ranked mercenaries had to constantly take quests to make a living, but high-ranked mercenaries were in a different position. The Guild guaranteed their living expenses, and the large rewards from high-level quests meant they rarely suffered from financial hardship, as long as they didn't live too extravagantly.

"I'm late in asking your name."

"It's Lee Sungmin."

"You're a martial artist, I presume?"

"I'm Classless," Lee Sungmin answered without hiding anything. He had not forgotten that Dox, the branch manager in Zenavis, had seen his identity through an artifact. He didn't want to arouse Bern's suspicion by telling a pointless lie.

"Classless?"

Bern couldn't help but be surprised, and he wasn't the only one. Rudd, sitting near Lee Sungmin, and the other mercenaries who had been eavesdropping all wore shocked expressions.

"A Classless reaching the skill of a peak realm master in just four years... you're a real calamity..." Rudd muttered with a look of disbelief.

Lee Sungmin didn't react. Bern, who had been quietly watching him, cleared his throat.

"...Normally, we conduct an evaluation when registering new mercenaries. However, for a peak realm master, something like that is unnecessary," he muttered, pulling a stack of papers from beneath the counter. "First... let's get the contract filled out. It's customary to assign a peak realm master as an S-Rank Mercenary, but we'll need to verify your skills first."

"......"

Lee Sungmin filled out the contract while listening to Bern. The Guildmaster looked down at the boy, who asked no further questions. It wasn't incomprehensible for someone to suddenly show up wanting to be a mercenary, but he couldn't help but feel a little puzzled by Lee Sungmin's complete lack of inquiry.

There was no need to ask. Lee Sungmin already knew exactly what a mercenary did. After signing his name at the bottom, he handed the contract back to Bern.

"...If you wish, I can arrange a meeting with the leader of a decent mercenary corps."

A mercenary corps was an organization of mercenaries belonging to the Guild. The major ones held significant power and sometimes claimed the best quests in advance.

"No, that's alright," he refused.

Joining a mercenary corps wasn't purely beneficial. Most took a commission from the quest fees of their members. That wasn't necessarily a bad thing, of course. As long as they provided services worth the commission, belonging to a corps couldn't be called a disadvantage. It was just that someone with Lee Sungmin's skill had no need to join one. It was far more liberating to be a free mercenary, registered only with the Guild, than to be managed by a corps.

"That's right. Being a free mercenary is better. You don't have to deal with the tiresome politics," Rudd said with a laugh.

When Lee Sungmin refused, Bern didn't press the matter further.

"Have you found a place to stay?"

"No."

"Then you can use an empty room on the fourth floor. I'll give you a quest to verify your skills tomorrow." Bern then asked, "Would you like something to drink?"

Lee Sungmin shook his head and pushed his chair back. "I'm still too young."

"An interesting answer," Bern let out a small laugh.

Lee Sungmin ignored the gazes of the mercenaries following him and went up to the fourth floor, an exclusive level for high-ranking mercenaries. In his past life, he had never stayed here. The only times he had come up were to clean or run errands. The atmosphere of the hallway was so different from the levels below that it felt like another building entirely. He walked down the hall, which was lined with a plush carpet, searching for an empty room. A key left in the lock indicated an available space, and Lee Sungmin pulled one from the nearest door.

He opened it and stepped inside, surveying his new quarters. The room was spacious and came with an attached bathroom where the water, supplied by magic, was always hot. A temperature-control spell kept the main room neither too hot nor too cold. The bed was large, and the space was furnished with an empty wardrobe and other basic pieces.

Lee Sungmin locked the door. Tomorrow, he would receive the quest to verify his skills. He had no idea what it would be, but if he succeeded, he would become an S-Rank Mercenary.

"Nebul," Lee Sungmin said aloud, calling for the Broker from Erebrisa.

In response, a neatly dressed Nebul emerged from his shadow.

"You called?" Nebul asked, bowing his head politely.

Lee Sungmin stared at Nebul's face for a moment before speaking. "I heard that Erebrisa also brokers quests."

"We arrange quests for clients who are registered mercenaries with a Guild. Are you looking for a quest?"

"No. Not yet. Are there quests available for an S-Rank Mercenary?"

"Of course. Quite a few of our contracted clients are mercenaries. S-Rank quests are the lowest tier of quests we can arrange," Nebul replied with a gentle smile.

That made sense. There were qualifications to become a member of Erebrisa, and even a peak master like Lee Sungmin would have found it impossible without Mushii's recommendation. This meant that any mercenary who was a member had to be S-Rank or higher.

*SS-Rank, or SSS-Rank... They're monsters.*

S-Rank was merely the minimum a peak master could receive. From there, one's rank would rise based on skill and performance, but that progression was limited to SS-Rank. An SSS-Rank Mercenary was a title for a transcendent individual who had gone far beyond the peak realm to reach a new level of existence. Across all the experiences of his past life, Lee Sungmin could only remember three such mercenaries.

*Could I possibly reach that level?* Lee Sungmin clenched his fist at the thought.

"Thank you for answering."

"Not at all. Please call me anytime."

Nebul vanished after saying his piece. Lee Sungmin sat on the bed, lost in thought. This was why he hadn't joined a mercenary corps. As a member of Erebrisa, he could receive high-level quests through their brokerage at any time.

*Six months from now.*

Now that he was a mercenary, he intended to work diligently. First, he would successfully complete the skill verification quest tomorrow. After that, he would take on high-level quests through Erebrisa. By building his reputation and achievements this way, he might even be able to reach SS-Rank.

Before that, however, there was something else. In six months, he had to obtain the Magic Armor from the quest to subjugate the Dark Mage Dungeon.
