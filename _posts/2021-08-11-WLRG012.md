Wiji Hoyeon's childhood memories, clear as they were, contained only the education befitting the Demonic Cult's Young Cult Leader. Her first and most vivid memory was of a bookshelf, one that stretched into a vast library packed with martial arts manuals. From the moment she could read, write, and remember, her entire world was that library. Her father, the great Cult Leader who stood at the pinnacle of the Demonic Cult, offered his only child a harsh education instead of affection.

Read, memorize, read, memorize. The cycle was endless. Under the pretext of building a foundation, she was given weapons instead of toys. She was taught to kill things to gain familiarity, starting with insects, then rats and rabbits, and eventually people.

Her father would occasionally visit to check on her martial arts progress and feed her miracle herbs. The Cult Leader did not want a lovely child. He wanted the next Cult Leader of the Demonic Cult, a successor who could reign over the world.

Wiji Hoyeon strove to live up to his wishes, a feat made possible only by the peerless miracle herbs he provided, the peerless divine arts of the Demonic Cult, and her own innate, calamitous talent.

She was finally allowed to leave the library after she turned ten. By then, she was accustomed to killing and had laid the foundation to learn the Heavenly Demon Divine Art, the greatest martial art in the Demonic Cult's history.

Three years passed as she trained in the Heavenly Demon Divine Art to become the next Cult Leader. That was the entirety of her past. There were no people around Wiji Hoyeon, only the god-like Cult Leader and the servants who obeyed his every command.

*Friend.*

The word was completely foreign to her. She had never had a friend in her entire life, for within the great Demonic Cult, no one was qualified to be hers.

*First friend.*

Wiji Hoyeon looked down through the window. In the Central Plains Murim, he was a boy she would not have even glanced at. No, their paths never would have crossed in the first place.

Here, however, things were different. In this world, there was no Demonic Cult and no god-like Cult Leader. This world called Eria was a place free from all the things that had oppressed her. That was why Wiji Hoyeon had no desire to return. To her, this world was full of the freedom she had only cautiously wished for.

She did not want to go back. A month had passed since she was summoned to Eria, and every night as she lay down to sleep, she would worry. She feared that when she fell asleep and opened her eyes, this would all prove to be a dream. Each morning, she was relieved to find that it was not.

"...What a pity."

Wiji Hoyeon's lips parted as she muttered to herself. She sighed, gathering her loose hair and tying it up.

"He has no talent," she murmured, her gaze still fixed out the window.

In the inn's backyard, Lee Sungmin was swinging his spear, dripping with sweat.

The Violet Dawn Divine Art was one of the most peerless divine arts among the countless martial arts the Demonic Cult possessed. It could not compare to the Heavenly Demon Divine Art, but one who properly mastered it would have no trouble reigning as the hegemon of a region.

Like all divine arts, however, the Violet Dawn Divine Art was a martial art for a calamity. Wiji Hoyeon had said it before. The difference between a First-Rate martial art and a peak realm martial art was its kindness. That kindness, however, was not meant for a mortal. Those who mastered divine arts and rose to the ranks of the strong were, without exception, people who were frequently called a calamity in their youth.

A divine art possessed even more of this inherent kindness, and the Violet Dawn Divine Art was no exception. It was a martial art for a calamity, and while a calamity might accept that kindness as such, a mortal could not.

The Lee Sungmin she saw was a human far from being a calamity.

Lee Sungmin was well aware of that himself.

He had been training in the Violet Dawn Divine Art for a month. The incantations he learned as a skill were firmly embedded in his mind, and he recited them with every qi circulation. At first, he had clung to the hope that learning a divine art would allow him to grow stronger quickly. In his past life, he had never learned a divine art, only the Second-Rate inner arts technique, the Spiritual Meditation Art.

It did not take long for that hope to be shattered.

Thinking about it now, it was only natural. He had clung to the Second-Rate Spiritual Meditation Art for thirteen years and only reached the eighth star. The Violet Dawn Divine Art was unmistakably a divine art, but it was a poor fit for him.

*I'm not a calamity.*

Lee Sungmin steadied his ragged breath as the inner qi of the Violet Dawn Divine Art spread throughout his body. His exhausted muscles caught their breath, and his breathing stabilized. His progress was much slower than he had expected. The kindness Wiji Hoyeon spoke of felt like unkindness to him. Since he was not a calamity, he could not understand the kindness intended for one.

While training in the Violet Dawn Divine Art, Lee Sungmin also devoted himself to the Soul Chasing Spear Art. Even if the divine art was a poor fit, the spear art showed great progress when combined with it. Though his inner qi were still lacking, the inner qi regulation of the Violet Dawn Divine Art was incomparably superior to that of the Spiritual Meditation Art or the Heavenly Truth Meditation Art.

"Isn't there a way to increase my inner qi?" Lee Sungmin asked, catching his breath. His question was for Wiji Hoyeon, who had come down from her room and was now sitting in the shade.

"Miracle herbs," she replied. "If you want to explosively increase your inner qi, eating miracle herbs is the fastest way."

Lee Sungmin knew this was the only real answer. He had only asked in the faint hope there might be another way.

"There are no shortcuts to raising inner qi other than miracle herbs," Wiji Hoyeon continued with a chuckle. "The fortunate thing is, this world is incredibly abundant with qi. We usually use the word 'gapja' to describe the amount of inner qi. One gapja of inner qi is, in other words, sixty years' worth."

She paused, a small smile on her face. "To gain one gapja, you have to train for sixty years. Superior inner arts techniques and miracle herbs exist to shorten that time."

In the end, he had to set his sights on obtaining the Star Spirit Pill. He did not know how much it would increase his inner qi, but eating it would significantly improve his current situation.

"Are you going to the forest again today?" Wiji Hoyeon asked.

Lee Sungmin picked up a towel from the wall and roughly wiped the sweat from his forehead. "Yeah."

"To earn money?"

"Yeah. You're not coming, right?"

At first, Wiji Hoyeon had shown great interest in the hunting grounds, but she lost that interest after a week. The grounds were just a forest, and the creatures that appeared were rabbits and wild boars, with the occasional bear. A little deeper lay the territory of goblins and Ogres. Lee Sungmin, however, mainly frequented the goblin territory. He could probably handle the orc area now, but he was not being reckless.

Going to the forest was one of Lee Sungmin's daily routines, but it had nothing to do with Wiji Hoyeon. After her initial interest waned, she grew tired of repeating the same routine. Instead, she picked up another hobby. While Lee Sungmin went hunting, Wiji Hoyeon would wander around Zenavis.

"I'm thinking of going to the library again today," she said, holding out her hand to him.

He let out a deep sigh and took out 10,000 Eru from his wallet. "Did you already spend everything I gave you last time?"

"It's been three days, of course I did," she replied with a smile.

Since Wiji Hoyeon did not hunt monsters, Lee Sungmin gave her money. Honestly, he did not feel it was a waste. The fact that she checked on his martial arts every day was a great help to him.

Leaving Wiji Hoyeon behind, Lee Sungmin left Jack's Inn. He used the One Thunder Movement Arts and ran towards the hunting grounds. Although his progress with the Violet Dawn Divine Art was slow, his inner qi had certainly increased. More importantly, he had become more efficient at using them. A month ago, a ten-minute sprint would have depleted his inner qi, but now he could afford to pace himself. That did not mean he could use his movement arts all the way from the inn to the hunting grounds without stopping.

*A corpse.*

Lee Sungmin stared down at the body. This was not the first time he had seen a corpse in the forest. It was less common near the entrance, but their frequency increased the deeper one went. Most of the bodies belonged to the Classless. Martial artists and mages could protect themselves against goblins and orcs, and they rarely stayed in Zenavis for long. They knew they could only earn pocket change here. As long as there was no way to return to their original world, they had to leave for a larger city to improve their beggar-like situation. The money from beating up goblins or orcs near Zenavis was negligible.

The Classless, however, were different. They were trapped in a cruel, vicious cycle. With almost no way to accumulate the power to leave, they stayed in Zenavis for long periods to prepare for graduation, scraping together money to buy martial arts manuals, learn magic, or simply train their bodies. It was not something that could be done in a short time. In his past life, Lee Sungmin himself had lived in Zenavis for three years.

*This... was done by a human.*

There was still some distance to the goblin territory. Lee Sungmin crouched to examine the corpses. There were two of them. The wounds looked like they were from a sword.

*No weapons. Their pockets have been searched. There's no way a goblin scout would come this far... It was a person.*

Something clicked. Lee Sungmin recalled a memory from his past life, his eyebrows furrowing. Was it around this time? For a short while, 'Classless hunting' had been a trend in Zenavis. It had not lasted long, maybe a week. Someone had hunted the Classless who entered the goblin territory. The motive was their money and equipment. He did not know who the culprit was, but the person had persistently targeted only the Classless, robbing them of their valuables and taking their gear.

"......"

Lee Sungmin clicked his tongue and stood up. In his past life, he had not even been able to advance to the goblin territory when the hunting occurred.

*What bad luck.*

He had to get out of here. The blood on the corpses had not yet dried.

RUSTLE.

Lee Sungmin's shoulders flinched. 'Bright Ear.' It was a skill he had obtained from hunting in the forest for two months, one that enhanced his hearing.

*My luck is...*

gone.

Lee Sungmin bolted forward without hesitation.
