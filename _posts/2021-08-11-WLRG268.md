He understood Abel's words all too well. The truth of his own existence, of a man who had died and returned to the past, was a story he had heard from everyone he met until he was sick of it.

Lee Sungmin remained silent. He had come back, and because of that, he knew how this world would unfold for the next thirteen years. In this world, only he held that knowledge. He had become an Observer of a future that was, for a time, set in stone.

However, Lee Sungmin had not lived the same life. The path he walked was nothing like the one he remembered. He had gone from a third-rate C-rank mercenary to a master of the Transcendent Realm. He had met Wiji Hoyeon. He had saved Baek Sogo, who was supposed to die. Countless other changes, large and small, had rippled out from his interventions, and even beyond his direct actions, many things had likely changed simply because he existed.

The fate that should have flowed according to his memories had been diverted by his hand. Distortions and variables had piled up, and now, the thirteen years of the future he remembered had ended. Lee Sungmin was twenty-seven, and he had no idea what would happen from this moment on.

That was The End.

"...Am I the one who brings about The End?"

"It's not 'you' specifically," Abel corrected. "The regressor, the one who returned to the past, is who brings about The End."

"Then what about the world in my previous life? Are you saying The End existed there too?"

"Perhaps."

There was no way to confirm it. The Stone of Reincarnation appeared in Dungeons. What if someone else had found it, died, and regressed to the world Lee Sungmin had once lived in? If so, another The End might have arrived after the point in time that unknown Observer remembered.

"Was I supposed to have died?" Lee Sungmin asked, his voice dazed. Abel was telling him a truth no one else had ever properly explained. His very existence, his return to the past, had brought The End to this world. Then what would have happened if he had died before? If he had died first, would The End have been averted?

"The Grace of Fate was protecting you," Abel said. "Until now, you were a body that couldn't die even if you wanted to. Does that ring any bells?"

At the question, Lee Sungmin closed his mouth again. Plenty of things came to mind. Before he turned twenty-seven, during the thirteen years he lived from the age of fourteen, he had faced one crisis after another, yet he had never truly faced death. Fate had saved him time and time again. Even when he was on the verge of death at Preskan's hands, the Black Heart had saved him. There was also the time the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman had intervened.

"...I was able to survive all this time because of the Grace of Fate?"

"You were a being who had to survive until the age you were meant to die, until the timeline you remembered was complete. The End begins when you reach a world you do not know, but not anymore. Fate will no longer protect you. Your reason for existing vanished the moment you reached this point in time."

Abel muttered this and then burst into laughter.

"That's why my brother's an idiot. He ran away because he didn't want to face a catastrophic fate of death, and in the end, he got swept up in it anyway. He tried to kill you without knowing anything... but there was no way he could have. The one protecting you was the very fate my brother so desperately wanted to avoid."

Abel spoke as if to himself. After a brief silence, he looked up.

"This world is a strange place," he said, the corners of his eyes crinkling. "Eria. This world summons people from different dimensions every single day. Martial arts, mages, science, but there's something lacking among them. What do you think it is?"

"Science," Lee Sungmin answered.

"Correct. Compared to martial arts or magic, the proportion of scientists among those summoned to Eria is almost nonexistent. Those we call the Classless, summoned from worlds with no Void Path and no magic, they remember the civilizations they lived in, yet they're almost completely ignorant of science."

It was just as he said. Lee Sungmin recalled his own life in twenty-first-century Korea. Though it was long ago, he remembered it clearly. Airplanes that flew in the sky, cars, trains, and even weapons like guns.

"Eria doesn't summon people randomly. Think about it. Martial arts and magic are individual powers. No matter how outstanding the manual or magic book one learns, it's impossible to master it properly if their talent is lacking. Only chosen geniuses can handle them with excellence, but what about science? The technology it creates? It doesn't require great aptitude to use it, that is, if it's already completed."

As if his throat was dry, Abel downed the prepared tea in one gulp.

"Of course, we're completing and developing things to some extent by combining the absurd stories we hear with magic. The train that moves with magic power is one example. The Mages Guild is researching a machine called an 'airplane', which carries numerous people and flies freely through the sky. I believe the balance is being maintained that way, by replacing high-level scientific technology with magic. This world... it's that kind of world. That's what I think."

"Are you saying it was designed as a world where magic, martial arts, and science coexist?"

"That's right. I've heard that in worlds where martial arts exist, magic is almost nonexistent. The same goes for magic. In worlds where magic is dominant, high-level physical techniques like martial arts have barely developed."

"...In the world I lived in, magic and martial arts didn't exist either," Lee Sungmin added.

Abel nodded once more. "It's clear that someone created this world and is summoning suitable individuals from each dimension. It's an undeniable fact that magic, martial arts, and technology are mixing and developing because of it, although the technology side is severely lagging. It's developing in a completely different direction as it combines with magic."

"The martial arts of the Nine Sects One Gang," the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman interjected. "Even with the same martial arts from the Wudang Sect, even with sword forms that share the same name, many differences exist if they came from different dimensions. The martial arts created by the founder, Zhang Sanfeng, have many variations. Such arts are being reinterpreted and merged within Eria's Wudang Sect."

"It's the same with magic," Abel continued. "Even for the same Fireball, the detailed incantations or the arrangement of Mana are different. The reason the Mages Guild constantly recruits newly summoned mages is to compare how different their magic is from what already exists. They want to determine what's different, which is superior, and develop it further. It's as if... they're using us, who live in this world, to create martial arts or magic that is close to 'perfection', don't you think?"

"Are you saying we're being bred?"

"Perhaps."

The air grew heavy.

"...What is The End?" the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman finally asked. "The end of the world? I don't quite understand. The End is fated because that foolish disciple of mine returned to the past? No, that can't be it. It must be that such an End was fated for this world from the very beginning. Why is something like that fated to happen?"

"I don't know what The End is either," Abel admitted. "As long as the 'unorthodox' of The End exists, it might be... perhaps the Apostle will descend and kill everyone, but this is just speculation. I don't know how The End will arrive or why such a thing even exists. However, I have no desire to die."

Abel said this with conviction, and that was the end of his story. Even he did not know everything about The End with any certainty.

"...Do you know about this?" Lee Sungmin reached into his robes and pulled out a golden key. When Abel tilted his head, Lee Sungmin explained that he had entered the Dungeon where he obtained the Stone of Reincarnation, and the moment he grabbed the stone, it had transformed into this key.

"...I don't know what it is," Abel muttered, staring at the object. Having been told beforehand that it was bound by magic, he did not touch it. He tried to examine the key using his own magic, but it was no use.

"It's no use," he repeated in an annoyed voice. "It's certainly no ordinary object, but I can't figure out what it is."

Abel gave up with a speed that left Lee Sungmin flustered. "Aren't you going to try anymore?" he asked.

"I am the greatest mage in this world," Abel declared with incredible arrogance. "If I can't figure it out, then no other mage can. There's no need to waste any more effort."

He turned his attention back to the Chairman. "In any case, Coalition Leader, I do not wish to die. That is why I resist The End. If possible, I would appreciate it if you would lend your strength."

"Are you suggesting we form an alliance against an unknown The End?"

"Isn't it better than just waiting with our hands tied?"

"That's true." The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman nodded. He glanced at Lee Sungmin, who was sitting beside him, nervously chewing on his lower lip. "Don't think such foolish thoughts," the Chairman said to him.

"You don't know what I'm thinking."

"I'm telling you not to blame yourself."

Lee Sungmin was left speechless by the blunt words.

"It may be that you brought The End to this world," the Chairman continued. "However, the responsibility is not yours. You were just swept up in it too, and... your role probably isn't just to survive until now and bring about The End."

"What do you mean by that?"

"Osla must have told you. That you would have to make a choice someday."

Indeed. Osla, whom he had met in the Fairy Forest, had said such a thing. He had called Lee Sungmin a pitiful being and told him to be faithful, to live each day without regret. Osla had referred to the Apostle of the End as an old promise, meaning The End was something decided long ago.

The End is the end.

The Apostle of the End is the one who carries out the end.

*And me?*

Osla had not been able to give him an answer back then. Because of that, Lee Sungmin had wondered if he himself was the Apostle. In a way, that was correct. Because he had survived until now, this world had come to face the fate known as The End.

However.

*Nothing is set in stone yet.*

Osla had said so with certainty, and Mushii had echoed a similar sentiment.

*Why did you come back again?*

*Wasn't there some kind of mistake?*

*There is no such thing as coincidence in this world.*

*If you returned to the past, there must be a reason for returning to the past.*

*If you returned, it means something worthy of your return will happen, and if you returned, you must prepare.*

*To become a being worthy of the reason for your return.*

Abel had said that upon reaching this point in time, Lee Sungmin's reason for existing had vanished. That his entire purpose was to live for thirteen years and observe a world he did not know, but Mushii and Osla had told him otherwise long ago.

*It hasn't vanished.*

Lee Sungmin chewed on his lower lip. Right, it hadn't vanished. He had not yet experienced the event that warranted his return. He had not yet become a being worthy of the reason for it. He recalled Osla's words again, the words that said nothing was set in stone.
