"Hey, you," Lee Sungmin said, his voice cutting through the quiet rhythm of their journey. "What's your name?"

He directed the question to the apprentice mercenary trailing behind them. They had been traveling with Woon Heeryong for half a day, yet Lee Sungmin knew nothing of the boy's name or age. The only sound he had made was the ragged noise of exhausted panting.

"Yes? Me... you're talking to me?"

The apprentice stared at Lee Sungmin, startled. His face was a filthy mask of sweat and dirt, and through the grime, fine wrinkles were visible, lines that spoke more of hardship than of years.

"Who else would I be talking to?" Lee Sungmin asked back.

Woon Heeryong glanced over at the exchange but made no move to stop it. Lee Sungmin watched the apprentice hesitate and let out a grumble.

"It's not like your name is some great secret. Why all the hesitation?"

"Ah... I'm sorry."

"There's nothing to be sorry for. I'm not with the Mercenary Guild."

The boy's utter intimidation told Lee Sungmin all he needed to know. Either Woon Heeryong or Lenire had been berating him constantly. Perhaps both of them had. In fact, he had probably been chewed out nonstop back at the guild as well.

"So. What is your name?"

"It's... Brass," the boy answered, bowing his head.

Lee Sungmin clicked his tongue at Brass's timid demeanor. It was not a pleasant sight, but he understood it. He had been the same way back when he was a G-Rank mercenary.

"Brass," Lee Sungmin began, his tone serious. "I'll be honest. When the orcs attack, I'm not sure I can protect you."

At his words, the color drained from Brass's face.

"Make a choice. Run away, or follow us."

Lee Sungmin was speaking the simple truth. His current martial skill was somewhere between Second-Rate and First-Rate. He was confident he could escape a fight with orcs on his own, but he had no confidence he could fight while also protecting Brass. He knew Woon Heeryong, who was completely ignoring their conversation, would certainly not lift a finger to help. Lee Sungmin's first impression of the man had been a kind and favorable one, but first impressions were often the opposite of reality. Just because Woon Heeryong treated him with kindness did not mean he was a kind person.

"Run... away..." Brass stammered.

Though Lee Sungmin had told him to choose, he knew the boy wouldn't run. Not that he would have stopped him if he did.

However, where could he possibly run? Living in the forest was out of the question, which meant he would have to return to Genomia. The problem was, Brass lacked the skills to make it back alone. Even if he got lucky and survived the journey, what excuse could he possibly give the Mercenary Guild?

"I... I can't run away."

Of course he couldn't. If he returned to Zenavis, he would face all manner of abuse from the guild. By forcing the choice, even a hollow one, Lee Sungmin was creating a justification for himself. *In that case, don't blame me if you die.* It was Brass who had chosen to follow. Lee Sungmin had given him the freedom to choose, even if the answer was obvious from the start.

Their luck held. They encountered no orc patrols on their way and soon arrived at a spot a good distance from the orc village. Lee Sungmin took out the signal flare he had received from Do Sangryang.

He had used one several times in his past life. Scattered across a wide hunting ground, a signal flare was an invaluable tool. It could be used when you found what you were searching for, when you wanted to lure out a monster, when you needed to call for help, and.

When you were dying.

The reason was simple. It was a final plea, a request to have your body recovered. Lee Sungmin had never fired one for that reason. He had died in an instant, with no time for such things. Besides, he had died inside an undiscovered Dungeon, so no one would have come even if he had.

*I hope no other mercenaries show up for no reason.*

It was unlikely. There were few such noble souls in their line of work. Unless they were comrades from the start, mercenaries were not the type to come running just because they saw a signal flare in a vast forest.

Crimson smoke shot upwards.

"Do you really think those orc bastards will come here?" Woon Heeryong asked.

Lee Sungmin, who had been watching the rising smoke, turned his head.

"They... they will come."

He was about to state it as a certainty but caught himself, softening his tone. His past life's experience told him the orcs would definitely come, but the Lee Sungmin in Woon Heeryong's eyes was a different person.

orcs were fiercely territorial. If anything unusual happened within their domain, they would always investigate. Their way of thinking was incredibly simple, so they would never consider that a signal flare might be a trap.

*If we had enough people, we could have set up a proper trap.*

It was not easy for just three people to dig something that could trouble orcs. It would take a long time, and if they dawdled until nightfall, they would be in a difficult position. Even if they had the time and succeeded, what about the stamina they would lose in the process?

"Let's hide," Lee Sungmin said after confirming the red smoke was soaring high into the sky. He produced a Subspace Pocket from his robes.

"Is that...?" Woon Heeryong's expression was one of pure surprise. He clearly never imagined Lee Sungmin would also possess such an item.

"I had a chance to get one by coincidence," Lee Sungmin replied, pulling a large waterskin from the pocket. He poured the water onto the dirt ground, and the soil, thoroughly mixed with water, became mud.

"What are you trying to do?"

"We're going to cover our bodies in mud to hide our scent."

"Ooh. A good idea," Woon Heeryong exclaimed in admiration.

Lee Sungmin went first, rolling in the puddle to cover his entire body. Brass followed his lead, and finally, Woon Heeryong spread mud all over himself.

"Young Master, you seem quite experienced for your age."

"...I learned from my Master."

"You have an excellent Master," Woon Heeryong replied, his eyes flicking once to the Subspace Pocket that Lee Sungmin had put away.

Lee Sungmin filed that look away. Lying flat on the ground, Brass's body trembled with a terror that was only natural. Lee Sungmin ignored him and assembled the spear he had taken from his pocket, placing it beside him.

"Young Master, you use a spear?"

"......"

"I see."

After that question, Woon Heeryong fell silent. Lee Sungmin heightened his vigilance and slowed his breathing. He had positioned Brass between himself and Woon Heeryong, which was exactly as he intended.

Lee Sungmin did not trust Woon Heeryong. He knew that look well. Greed. Woon Heeryong's gaze was thick with it. You should never trust a man with eyes like that.

"KRRK!"

A sound broke the silence.

A dozen or so orcs were cautiously approaching the source of the smoke. Lying prone behind dense bushes, Lee Sungmin assessed their equipment. They all had sturdy builds and carried crude weapons. These were the young orcs, the main fighting force of the tribe.

*They said there were about fifty orcs in the tribe. If half of them are females, the old, and the young... quite a few of them came out.*

orc society was thoroughly male-dominated. Female orcs were not warriors. Their role was to bear children, and they could not even fight properly. The same was true for the old. Orcs grew quickly and maintained a youthful body for a long time, but old age came upon them swiftly. They had no respect for their elders, no concept of filial piety. Old orcs were just surplus bodies that filled the village's headcount, and in harsh times, they served as emergency food. They were a barbaric race that even preyed on their own kind.

*Raiding an empty village would be ideal... but even if only the females and old orcs are left, the job will be much easier.*

A dozen young orcs increased the burden, but luring them out meant the plan was a success. The Mercenary Guild's request was the annihilation of the entire tribe.

"Let's go," Lee Sungmin whispered.

"Wait," Woon Heeryong said, stopping him. "How about we send the porter out first?"

"...What?"

"Wouldn't it be better to send him out to draw their attention? Then we can ambush them."

Woon Heeryong's suggestion was ruthless, but it was rational. If they sent Brass out, the orcs would obviously kill him. In the process, their attention would be divided, and if he and Woon Heeryong attacked in that opening, they could annihilate the orcs quickly.

All color drained from Brass's face. His lips trembled, a silent scream trapped behind the hand Woon Heeryong had clamped over his mouth without him even noticing.

"......"

Lee Sungmin said nothing. He had no obligation to save the boy's life, but that didn't mean he could blindly agree to send him to his death.

After all, Lee Sungmin was also a Classless.

"Is that really necessary?"

"This way is more efficient."

"I'd rather go out first myself," Lee Sungmin said.

At his words, Brass's eyes widened. Woon Heeryong stared intently at Lee Sungmin's face, and in that gaze, Lee Sungmin was once again certain. Woon Heeryong was by no means a good person.

"Do as you wish," Woon Heeryong said.

Lee Sungmin ignored Brass's stare and swallowed hard. The number of orc warriors was thirteen.

*Can I do it?*

*I have to.*

Was it unnecessary meddling? Pointless goodwill? Or was it just pity? They had no grand connection, they weren't even friends. It was only because they were both Classless, that was all. He had no obligation to protect him.

Even so, he knew that if an orc attacked Brass during the fight, he would have helped him.

That was the kind of person Lee Sungmin was.

He burst through the bushes. The Violet Dawn Divine Art ignited his inner qi, sending power surging from his qi center through his entire body. The truth was, if he hadn't received those inner qi from Wiji Hoyeon, he wouldn't have tried to help Brass. This was the kind of favor he could offer, one extended only as far as he could handle, within a range that wouldn't bring harm to himself.

It was not just a favor. There was meaning in stepping forward first, in showing his skills.

Before the orcs could even register the figure that shot from the foliage, Lee Sungmin had already unleashed the One Thunder Movement Arts. His spear lanced through a nearby orc's neck, killing it without a sound. He wrenched the spear free with a flick of his arm and swung the shaft into another orc's head. CRACK! With a dull thud, its skull shattered.

"KYEEK!"

"KUEK! KUAACK!"

The orcs shrieked. Lee Sungmin took a few steps back and settled into his stance. They didn't charge rashly. Even their dim minds knew not to blindly attack the man who had killed two of their own in a heartbeat.

The orcs crept closer, the young and brave ones taking the lead. They bared their fangs and contorted their faces, their killing intent a palpable force. Tightly gripped weapons were thrust forward menacingly.

Lee Sungmin gripped the spear with both hands. A spear is long. Its length gives it the advantage of striking first if there is enough distance. That was the foundation of spearmanship, how to utilize that distance and how to maintain it. The secret, Wiji Hoyeon had told him, was simple. You just have to kill the opponent before they can close the distance.

It was easier said than done. What Wiji Hoyeon called simple was by no means a simple matter for him. The difference in their talent was like heaven and earth.

"KRAAA!"

An orc charged. Lee Sungmin's eyes widened, his grip tightening on the spear as strength flooded his arms.

Soul Chasing Spear Art Third Form, Thunder Splitting Strike.

THWACK! The spear tore through the chests of three charging orcs. His physical strength was lacking. Or perhaps it was his timing. It was the immaturity of his body rather than a lack of inner qi. He had pierced two cleanly, but the spear lodged in the third, unable to pass all the way through.

He had intended this. An immature Spear Technique. The target of that intention was Woon Heeryong, hiding in the bushes. He was showing him just enough to gauge the level of his martial arts, leaving an impression. Whether an opportunity to use that impression would come or not, he was fine with either outcome.

He unleashed the solar eclipse of the Soul Chasing Spear Art, One Strike One Kill. He let his hand slide down the shaft, accelerating his body before striking the butt of the spear with his left palm. SMASH! The half-embedded weapon blasted completely through the orc's body.

*Five dead. What's left is...*

Eight. The bushes behind Lee Sungmin rustled.

"I'll help you!"

It was Woon Heeryong. The detestable bastard shouted, his voice ringing with false gallantry.
