In the heart of Eria lay Ruvus, a great city that served as a nexus of power. It was home to both the Golden and Red Magic Towers of the Mages Guild and the headquarters of the Mercenary Guild. Its proximity to Kron, the seat of the Murim Alliance, ensured its streets were always bustling with a vibrant mix of martial artists and mages.

However, martial artists and mages were not the only souls who walked this world. The Classless, their presence incomparably faint, were also summoned to Eria, living alongside the original inhabitants and the descendants of those summoned long ago. It was not just the powerful who dreamed of a life in Ruvus. It was everyone.

Ruvus was the city of dreams. Many longed to settle within its walls, and those who succeeded enjoyed a life of prosperity. Even the city's lower class lived as well as the middle class of any other. It was a place of high standards, where low-grade C-Rank Mercenaries struggled just to live from one day to the next. Even mercenaries of A-Rank or higher were barely qualified to take on requests here. Ruvus was the most famous and largest city in the land, yet Lee Sungmin had only ever known it by name.

"Lee Sungmin? Hmm... an S-Rank Mercenary, I see."

He had not performed a single mercenary request since leaving Behenger, but his badge was far from useless. The city's renown was matched only by its strict security. A long line of people snaked from the gate, and entry was forbidden to anyone without proper identification.

"And you?" the guard asked, turning his attention to the next person.

After a long wait, Lee Sungmin's turn had finally come. He presented his mercenary badge and passed through the gate without issue. Tucked quietly in his arms, Rubia went completely unnoticed during the inspection.

"This one is the Crazy Heavenly Demon, Byeong Wonpae."

When his turn came, the Crazy Heavenly Demon produced an identification tag from his robes and showed it to the guard. The guard examined the tag and nodded.

"I'm saying this just in case, but don't even think about causing trouble here," the guard warned, his tone weary. "I don't care if you're from the orthodox or unorthodox sects. This city is under the protection of the Murim Alliance, the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance, and the Mages Guild."

"Do you take this one for a foolish country bumpkin?" the Crazy Heavenly Demon grumbled. In a city as vast as Ruvus, the powers of various factions were deeply intertwined. No matter how great a martial artist he was, he could not afford to act recklessly here.

"You may enter."

As they passed through the gate without any further trouble, the Crazy Heavenly Demon muttered under his breath.

"The Murim Alliance, the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance. Hmph! If this one just put his mind to it..."

"Don't be ridiculous," Lee Sungmin cut in, having overheard the grumbling. The Crazy Heavenly Demon flinched at his words and promptly shut his mouth.

His promised meeting with Wiji Hoyeon was tomorrow. They had hurried their journey and arrived just in the nick of time. On the long road from Trabia at the edge of the North, Lee Sungmin had dueled the Crazy Heavenly Demon almost every day. They had carefully controlled their strength so as not to delay their travels, but in that time, the old master had come to fully acknowledge Lee Sungmin's martial prowess.

"Don't worry, I won't cause any trouble."

"If you do, then you and I are strangers, Elder."

"How harsh! Have you forgotten the grace this one showed you by teaching you the Blood Ring Divine Demonic Art?"

"And didn't I agree to every single one of your demands for a duel on the way here, even when I didn't want to?" Lee Sungmin countered. "Since I've put up with all your stubbornness, Elder, I consider the debt for learning your art fully repaid."

"Keuk!" the Crazy Heavenly Demon groaned.

Though he said as much, Lee Sungmin knew he had received a great deal of help from the old man during their journey. The constant duels had allowed him to thoroughly test his own abilities and make great progress with the Blood Ring Divine Demonic Art. What was certain was that he had not yet entered the Transcendent Realm. At the same time, he grew convinced that the state he had reached in the Mental World was indeed that very realm. He was still weaker than that version of himself. A great chasm still existed between his current self, who stood on the verge of transcendence, and the self from his mind, who had already crossed it.

Lee Sungmin was not the only one who had gained something. Constantly sparring with a martial artist of a similar level had also helped the Crazy Heavenly Demon grow. In the end, they had both benefited.

"It's been a while, Ruvus."

Rubia emerged from his arms, stretching like a cat that had been curled up for too long. She placed her hands on her hips and looked around.

"Have you been here before?"

"Of course. The Mages Guild headquarters is here, and so are the Golden and Red Magic Towers."

The Golden Magic Tower. The name stirred a memory from several years ago in Preskan's Dungeon, where he had formed a connection with its master, Lloyd. The man had told Lee Sungmin that if he ever came to visit, he would grant him a favor as thanks for saving his life.

*There's nothing I particularly want to ask for right now.*

Even back then, he had postponed the favor for the same reason. He glanced down at his left wrist, at the bracelet he had received from Scarlet when they parted ways at Shaolin. She had said she would let him know through it if she ever needed help, but the bracelet had not reacted once in all these years. He was secretly relieved. He owed Scarlet a great deal, and its silence was proof that she was safe.

"My heartless master hasn't contacted me once in over a year," Rubia said, her voice filled with the same excitement she had shown throughout their journey. "But maybe she left a message at the Golden Magic Tower."

Lee Sungmin's eyes widened. "What do you mean?"

"Lloyd, the Master of the Golden Magic Tower, is my master's disciple."

"What?" Lee Sungmin's face was a mask of surprise.

Puffing out her chest with pride at his reaction, Rubia declared, "My master is an amazing person, you know. She may not be famous among the general public, but there are few renowned mages who don't know of her!"

Her words decided their plans for the day. Tomorrow was for Wiji Hoyeon, leaving no time for anything else, but today's schedule was empty. They would stop by the Golden Magic Tower and the Red Magic Tower.

As befitting a giant city, Ruvus had many convenient facilities not found elsewhere. Trains ran on railways, with stations placed for the rapid transit of its citizens. Lee Sungmin followed Rubia as she took the lead, heading toward a station near the city gate.

"A train..."

He could not help but be amazed. Having lived in Seoul, he was familiar with railways, trains, and subways, but he had never encountered them in Eria. He had heard rumors that Ruvus had such a system, but this was the first time he was seeing it with his own two eyes.

"It's the pride of Ruvus, created through the cooperation of mages and technicians," Rubia explained excitedly. She pointed a triumphant finger at the train stopped on the tracks. "Not all Classless are like this, but some possess technical skills that mages and martial artists don't have. When you combine that with the dexterity of Dwarves and magic... ta-da!"

She beamed. "It doesn't shake like a carriage, it's faster, and you can just sit still and enjoy the scenery outside the window!"

"Why are you the one acting so proud?" the Crazy Heavenly Demon asked, looking at her with disbelief.

In response, Rubia raised a hand and pinched her nose. "Could you please not talk? You reek of a country bumpkin."

"What?"

"Just hurry up and come on. We need to buy tickets!"

Ignoring the sputtering old man, Rubia skipped toward the ticket gate. Lee Sungmin glanced at the train, lost in thought for a moment before following her. This world was harsh to the Classless, but not to all of them. Depending on the knowledge they possessed, some were treated quite well. Such talented individuals, however, were extremely rare. He knew about railways, but he certainly did not have the knowledge to build one. Most were likely the same. The Classless who possessed no knowledge worthy of respect were forced to live miserable lives. Just like him, in his past life.

Ruvus's Central Plaza, the Golden Magic Tower, and the Red Magic Tower were not far from each other. Lee Sungmin bought a ticket to the Golden Magic Tower first.

"Why don't other cities have such a convenient mode of transport?" the Crazy Heavenly Demon muttered as he boarded. He seemed uncomfortable riding a vehicle made of metal, yet when they found their seats, he insisted on sitting by the window.

"Because it's difficult to maintain," Rubia gloated, magnanimously giving him the window seat. "Ruvus has the most stable security of all the cities in Eria, and it's a city without disturbances because the different factions keep each other in check. Besides, there are many wealthy people, so the amount of taxes collected is enormous. A railway is convenient, but it's not something you can just implement in every city."

Lee Sungmin had to agree. Not every city in Korea had subways or trains. Establishing a railway required time, money, and technology, and not every city could secure all three.

"Ooh... it's moving..."

The train began to move. The Crazy Heavenly Demon gripped the armrests tightly, his eyes fixed on the window. Lee Sungmin also gazed blankly outside. It lurched a little at first, but once the train picked up speed, only a faint vibration remained.

"Short-distance teleportation still carries a lot of risks," Rubia said, swallowing a laugh as she watched the old man, who was mesmerized by the scenery flashing past. "It's one thing if a mage does it themselves, but teleporting a group of people has too many variables. Mages are developing it, but it'll probably take at least a few decades before it can be commercialized."

"Country bumpkin!" she chirped.

"An iron horse made of metal is running..." he murmured, still in awe.

"It's thanks to magic! Isn't it cool? Even people who can't use martial arts can travel long distances quickly on a train. It's not martial arts that make this possible, but magic and technology."

"But... this one is faster running."

"Could you please stop saying such ignorant things?"

"If this one put his mind to it, he could smash an iron horse like this with a single blow."

"What kind of nonsense are you spouting now?"

Rubia and the Crazy Heavenly Demon bickered back and forth. They had grown much closer during their journey, and watching them now was like seeing a grandfather and his granddaughter.

[It has changed a lot.]

The Crazy Heavenly Demon was not the only one who was surprised. The Void Monarch, dwelling within the Magic Armor, let out a cry of admiration.

[There was nothing like this four hundred years ago when I lived.]

*Of course it's different, given how much time has passed.*

[I hadn't properly realized it until now. Yes... four hundred years. That is a long time. I wonder how the South has changed.]

The Void Monarch's muttering felt a little lonely. His time had stopped four hundred years ago when he was sealed in the Sleeping Forest. Though the South was now said to be teeming with Yokai, the South he knew was the one from four centuries past.

The train stopped at the Golden Magic Tower station. The Crazy Heavenly Demon looked as if he wanted to ride longer, so after a stern warning not to cause any trouble, Lee Sungmin left him behind and got off.

Just like the station near the city gate, this one also had many beggars shamelessly sitting together in a corner. They watched Lee Sungmin as he passed, but he ignored their gazes. Kron was home to the Murim Alliance and the Beggars' Gang Main Branch, so it was only natural for there to be many beggars in a city so close by.

"Let's go, quickly!" Rubia urged him on.

He pushed the beggars' stares from his mind and left the station. The Golden Magic Tower was close. Contrary to its name, it did not shine with a golden light, but it was indeed a tall and imposing tower. Not far from it stood another magic tower of a similar height.

"That's the Red Magic Tower," Rubia said.

"After we meet Lloyd, let's stop by there too."

"......"

"There's someone I need to check on," Lee Sungmin said as he walked through the entrance of the Golden Magic Tower.

When he said he was there to see Lloyd, the mage on the first floor simply shook his head and refused. The reason was that they had not made an appointment with the Tower Master. Lee Sungmin was about to say something more, but Rubia stepped forward before he could.

"I can't tell you the details, but we are important guests of Lloyd's," she said coolly. "If you refuse us, he will hold you accountable for your rudeness. Are you sure you're okay with that?"

"Even so..."

"So you're saying you can handle the consequences? Fine. We'll leave," Rubia said, looking the young mage up and down. "What a shame, and you're still so young... Tsk tsk!"

At her words, the mage's face turned pale. He hesitated for a moment before letting out a deep sigh and nodding.

"Understood... What should I tell him?"

"Please connect me to Lloyd through the crystal ball. I'll do the talking."

The mage brought a crystal ball and handed it to Rubia. After a brief signal, its color changed. Rubia stared at it intently. They could not see Lloyd's image, but on the other end, Lloyd was looking at her. A moment later, the crystal ball turned a brilliant gold.

"He's given his permission," Rubia said, returning the crystal with a triumphant smile.

The mage flinched at her expression and nodded. "Please go up."

Lee Sungmin and Rubia went through the door the mage held open. Once it closed behind them, Rubia skillfully operated a device on the wall. The floor then began to rise slowly.

"An elevator."

"You know what it is?"

"Because I'm from another world."

The floating floor continued its smooth ascent. Before long, it stopped, and Rubia immediately opened the door.

"This way."

Lee Sungmin followed her across a hallway. At the end of the hall was an ornate golden door. Before Rubia could even raise her hand to knock, it swung open on its own.

"An unexpected guest," a voice drifted from inside.
