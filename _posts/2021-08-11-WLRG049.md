Lee Sungmin spent the remainder of the day in the backyard, practicing his Spear Technique until night finally fell and drove him inside. The house, untouched for some time, was coated in a thin layer of dust, but a quick tidying was all it took to make the space manageable.

Though a kitchen was available, he did not prepare a meal, opting instead for the food stored in his Subspace Pocket. After he had eaten, he began to circulate the Violet Dawn Divine Art.

As he entered the world of his inner qi, his thoughts naturally drifted back to the fight with the Ogre. It had been his first battle since descending the mountain, no, his first true battle as a peak realm master in either of his lives, past or present.

The experience had stirred many things within him, enough to create a distinct sense of disharmony. The most jarring problem was the chasm between what his body now was and what it remembered as familiar. Three years on the mountain had thoroughly accustomed him to the severe restrictions placed upon him. Because of that, he now felt a profound dissonance with them gone, which was compounded by the dissonance between his abilities before and after reaching the peak realm.

He had to get used to it.

The problems were numerous. His body was currently in a state of severe imbalance. He had tried to maintain a nutritional balance while living on the mountain, but with his sense of taste sealed, every meal had been a terrible ordeal. Naturally, the amount he ate had dwindled.

It was not just his meals. For nearly two years, he had been unable to sleep long or deeply. With his senses of smell, hearing, and sight all restricted, those three had failed to develop much. Only his sense of touch, the sensations he felt through his skin, had developed to an abnormal degree.

This was not entirely a disadvantage, however. By sealing all his other senses, a new, previously non-existent sense had begun to bloom.

Sixth Sense.

It even existed as a skill in his status window.

Sixth Sense.
Allows you to feel what cannot be seen with the eyes, heard with the ears, or smelled with the nose.

The description for the passive skill was as vague as any other, but what he felt was undeniably real. Even if the sensation itself was as indistinct as its description, the Sixth Sense was a tangible part of him.

*But I can't rely solely on my sixth sense and sense of touch.*

He needed to train his other senses. It did not have to be intense, but he had to correct the profound imbalance that plagued him.

The Violet Dawn Divine Art continued its circulation, his inner qi rotating smoothly. Amidst the rhythm of his even breathing, Lee Sungmin's thoughts gradually faded. Before he realized it, his consciousness had submerged into a state of deep immersion. It was only thanks to the Brainwashing magic and the Violet Dawn Divine Art that he had managed to hold onto his ravaged mind during his time on the mountain.

The next day, Lee Sungmin headed to the Mercenary Guild early in the morning. He figured a day was enough time for his rank plaque to be ready, and he planned to pick it up before checking the available requests.

Even at this early hour, the Behenger Mercenary Guild was crowded with mercenaries grabbing breakfast or still drinking from the night before. The moment Lee Sungmin pushed the door open and stepped inside, the clamor came to a sudden halt. Every eye turned to him, staring blankly for a beat before a low murmur rippled through the room.

Lee Sungmin had become something of a celebrity here. On the day he joined, he had demonstrated his sword ki for all to see, and he had followed that by subjugating the Ogre Chimera, a feat worthy of an S-Rank or higher. The fact that he was still only eighteen years old made it only natural for the other mercenaries to take an interest in him.

"You're here?"

Bern was already out from behind the bar. He greeted the approaching Lee Sungmin and reached into his pocket.

"Good timing. Your mercenary plaque is ready."

"That's what I came for," Lee Sungmin said with a smile. Bern handed him the plaque, its surface engraved with a golden 'S'. Fiddling with it, Lee Sungmin couldn't help but recall the one he had carried in his past life.

*Back then, it was a copper C.*

Bern spoke to the smirking Lee Sungmin. "S-Rank Mercenaries get a monthly stipend of five million Eru. It's more than enough to live on."

Even without taking a single request, he would receive five million Eru every month. The mere existence of an S-Rank Mercenary was worth that much. Eria was a vast world, and all sorts of people were summoned from various dimensions, but peak masters or mages who could qualify for S-Rank were anything but common.

"What happened to the Ogre Chimera's corpse?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"Hm?"

Bern's eyes widened, a flicker of surprise crossing his features. He clearly had not expected the question. After studying Lee Sungmin's face for a moment, Bern shrugged.

"We're investigating it. Nothing special's been found so far, but there's a high probability a Black Mage was involved."

"You mean there was a Black Mage in the area?"

"Probably," Bern confirmed. "That forest wasn't a suitable territory for an Ogre in the first place. It's reasonable to assume a Black Mage turned one into a Chimera and just released it there."

"Why would they do that?"

"Are you asking for my opinion as a Guildmaster? Or as a mage?"

"Both," Lee Sungmin replied without hesitation.

He had his reasons for pressing Bern on this. Bern was not only the branch manager of the Behenger Mercenary Guild but also a mage of considerable skill. At Lee Sungmin's request, Bern fell silent for a moment before speaking.

"They probably wanted to test something."

"A test, you say?"

"Its mouth was sewn shut, made unable to eat. Ogres aren't completely savage monsters, you know. They have a fair amount of reason. They won't attack a village because they fear being subjugated."

Lee Sungmin listened intently, recalling that Rudd had told him something similar. Seeing he had his full attention, Bern continued.

"A starving monster becomes extremely ferocious. Humans grow weak on the brink of starvation, but monsters are the opposite. They grow stronger and more violent. Do you know why? To survive. They have to be stronger and more violent to be able to hunt."

"So they deliberately sewed the Ogre's mouth shut?"

"To induce a state of forced starvation, I'd guess. If you'd been a few days later, a whole village might have disappeared. Or, the Black Mage would have retrieved the Ogre."

Lee Sungmin suspected it was the latter. In the memories of his past life, he had never heard a rumor about a village being destroyed by an Ogre. It was just as Bern said. The Black Mage had likely released the Ogre into the forest to observe its condition under forced starvation.

And they would have retrieved it before it caused any real trouble.

*It's changed.*

In his past life, the Ogre Chimera was never subjugated. This time, it was. He worried that this single event might act as a variable. There was a high probability that the Black Mage who released the Ogre was the master of the Dungeon that would be discovered in six months.

"There might have been a Black Mage nearby," Bern warned. "You were lucky, I think. If you'd run into them, you might not have made it back alive."

"Are they that strong?"

"They're skilled enough to subdue an Ogre, turn it into a Chimera, and control it. They must be a very high-level Black Mage, but why are you so curious?"

"Just curious. It's my first time seeing a Chimera," Lee Sungmin replied, tucking the mercenary plaque into his coat. "Well then. I'll see you next time."

"Ah, wait."

Bern stopped him just as he was about to turn away. He wore a troubled expression.

"There's someone who wants to meet you."

"Who is it?"

"Zeon. Do you know him?" Bern asked.

At the name, Lee Sungmin's face stiffened. He forced his expression to relax and shook his head.

"...I don't."

"I suppose not. You just became a mercenary, after all. Zeon is an SS-Rank mercenary. He's the highest-ranked mercenary registered at the Behenger branch."

He knew.

Of course, Lee Sungmin knew Zeon. An SS-Rank mercenary. A secular disciple of Shaolin and a master of fist techniques. The leader of the Corona Mercenary Group.

How could he not know? The Corona Mercenary Group was the very same one Lee Sungmin had belonged to in his past life. Zeon had been his leader, yet there had been no real connection between them. Lee Sungmin had been far too insignificant for a man like Zeon to even notice.

The Corona Mercenary Group was a massive organization with over a hundred affiliated mercenaries. With the SS-Rank mercenary Zeon at its head, there were five S-Rank Mercenaries serving under him. There were also dozens of C-Rank Mercenaries, and among them, Lee Sungmin, a Classless with no notable features, had been just another grunt that the higher-ups never even glanced at. He had endured in the group since he was E-Rank, but even as a C-Rank Mercenary, he was just another run-of-the-mill Classless.

"It wouldn't be a bad idea to meet him," Bern advised. "Many mercenary groups would be greedy for someone with your skills. Zeon and his Corona Mercenary Group are considered the best among Behenger's groups. Of course, I'm sure you don't plan on staying in Behenger forever. The names Zeon and the Corona Mercenary Group are well-known in other cities too."

"...Does he want to meet right now?"

"He's already waiting inside," Bern said with a bitter smile. "I know this is sudden, but..."

"I understand," Lee Sungmin cut him off, nodding slowly. "I'll meet with him."

The choice was his, but refusing now would put Bern in an awkward position. For the sake of the man who had helped him in many ways, it seemed right to at least meet Zeon once.

In the end, that was just an excuse.

Lee Sungmin wanted to meet Zeon.

The room was thick with cigarette smoke. Bern's reception room had, for the moment, become Zeon's private space. Bern, opening the door and entering first, frowned at the sight.

"Couldn't you have at least opened a window?"

At his words, Zeon, who was holding a cigar, merely shrugged from behind the veil of smoke. As he put the cigar down, Lee Sungmin stared intently at him.

This was the first time he had ever met Zeon one-on-one. Despite having been a part of the Corona Mercenary Group, there had never been any point of contact between them. He had not even met Zeon when he first joined. To the group, a person like Lee Sungmin was nothing more than a gofer, in charge of errands and cleaning.

"Not bad," Zeon muttered, his eyes fixed on Lee Sungmin's face. He was a man with broad shoulders and short-cropped hair.

Always, Lee Sungmin had watched Zeon from a distance. To him, the mercenary 'Zeon' had been an object of admiration.

Zeon was a Classless.

He had been summoned to Eria fifteen years ago. As a Classless, he happened to learn Shaolin's Hundred Steps Divine Fist and later traveled to the Shaolin headquarters in Eria, where he had his martial arts acknowledged and became a secular disciple. The details were the stuff of heroic tales, but Lee Sungmin had respected and admired Zeon simply for being a fellow Classless who had achieved so much. He used to vaguely think that he wanted to be like Zeon someday.

"...I'm Lee Sungmin."

"Zeon."

Zeon put the cigar back in his mouth.

The object of his long-held admiration was sitting right in front of him.
