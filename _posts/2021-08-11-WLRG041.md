Behenger.

It was the name of the city Lee Sungmin was heading for, the very city where he had first become a mercenary in his past life. He had been seventeen years old then, not even a Third-Rate warrior.

His original plan had been to train on Mount Mushii for two years, but that plan had gone awry. Three years had passed on the mountain instead, and now, as he finally left it behind for Behenger, Lee Sungmin was eighteen.

His age was not the only thing that had changed. The boy who had arrived in Behenger all those years ago was a far cry from the man he was now. The Lee Sungmin of the past had not even reached the level of a Third-Rate warrior, yet the current Lee Sungmin had already surpassed the peak masters. The Broker from Erebrisa had said as much after seeing him, confirming that his skills had already transcended the peak realm.

Considering his age, anyone would have called him a calamity born of talent. In truth, that assessment was not entirely wrong. Excluding an absurd existence like Wiji Hoyeon, the ability to reach the skill of a peak master by the age of eighteen was more than enough to earn such a title.

However, Lee Sungmin judged himself more harshly. He had always believed it was less painful to assume the worst than to offer himself clumsy comfort. It was an excessive inferiority complex, born from being too self-aware, just as Wiji Hoyeon had pointed out several times, but Lee Sungmin had no intention of discarding it.

He knew that even an inferiority complex could be a source of motivation. The three years he spent on Mount Mushii had changed him, teaching him to use that feeling to drive his body forward rather than letting it crush him with despair.

A peak master at eighteen. On the surface, it sounded like a calamity, but what was the reality? He possessed thirteen years of memories from a past life. Even if they were unremarkable, those memories alone were enough to allow him to quickly rise to the level of a Second-Rate expert.

That was not all. The Violet Dawn Divine Art and the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear he had learned were divine arts. As Wiji Hoyeon had said, they were martial arts that guaranteed a smooth path to the Sword Energy Realm just by learning them. From the moment he acquired them, Lee Sungmin was already destined to reach the peak realm.

On top of that were the growth corrections from being Classless and having a Low-Grade Martial Skeleton, two factors that accelerated the speed at which he learned. As if that were not enough, he had come to Mount Mushii, where he trained for three hellish years with numerous restrictions weighing down his body.

Only after all that was he a peak realm expert.

If he had not come to Mount Mushii, how much longer would it have taken? If he had never received the divine arts, he might never have even dreamed of reaching the peak realm in his entire life.

*Mediocre... I can't be called a calamity.*

Lee Sungmin smiled bitterly as he descended the mountain. He had lived on Mount Mushii for three years but had grown no particular attachment to it. That did not mean he disliked the place. He was grateful for it. The three years he spent there were more fulfilling than the entire life he had lived before.

He unleashed the Dancing Shadow Soul Break.

The acceleration was a revelation, incomparably faster than anything he had ever achieved. He had used the technique many times before, but always under the crushing restriction of 'heaviness'. Now, he was different. He was free and light, unbound by any restraint.

His Dancing Shadow Soul Break had reached the 5th Star. The Violet Dawn Divine Art was at the 6th Star, and the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear had also reached the 6th Star.

The mastery of these arts was now a part of him. The inner qi cultivated by the Violet Dawn Divine Art circulated rapidly through his entire body, while the Dancing Shadow Soul Break made him even more agile and swift. He descended the mountain in an instant, running a considerable distance without rest, his breathing remaining perfectly steady. The divine art he had clung to for three years had recovered most of the inner energy that had dissolved into his blood and qi, returning it to his qi center. He had also steadily accumulated the mountain's clear energy there. Just as his skills had risen to the peak realm, his inner qi had grown to a level befitting them.

*At this speed, I'll get to Behenger in no time.*

The journey from Mount Mushii to Behenger was originally a four-day walk. Using his movement arts, however, it seemed it would take less than two.

There was no need to hurry, though. Lee Sungmin set his direction toward the city and began to walk. Having lived under restrictions on his sight, hearing, and smell, getting his bearings felt a little awkward, but the years his senses had been free were longer than the years they were not, and he managed to correct the awkwardness.

As he walked, Lee Sungmin organized his future plans. First were the things he absolutely must do.

He wanted to prevent Baek Sogo's death. Less than six years remained until she and Wiji Hoyeon would enter the same Dungeon, where Wiji Hoyeon would kill her. Lee Sungmin now possessed the skills of a peak master, but he was certain of one thing. Wherever Wiji Hoyeon was, whatever she was doing, she was stronger than he was now.

It was only natural. She had already surpassed the skills of a peak master the moment she left Zenavis. In the three years since, Wiji Hoyeon would not have been idle, and the same would be true for the next six.

The six years a calamity like her would spend and the six years he would spend were by no means the same. After all, no matter how far a grub crawls for six years, it cannot possibly travel farther than a human walks in one.

He wondered if it would have been better to train longer on Mount Mushii, but he had decided to leave the mountain for now.

There was something he needed to obtain first.

In half a year, the Mercenary Guild in Behenger would receive a subjugation request for a Black Mage's Dungeon on the city's outskirts. Though it was an artificial Dungeon, the skill of its creator was formidable, and the Mercenary Guild would suffer great losses. Of course, they did not only suffer. By succeeding in the subjugation, the guild secured enormous spoils of war.

Among them was one item Lee Sungmin was focused on in particular. The Magic Armor, Devnak. It was an armor that a C-Rank Mercenary, who had participated in the subjugation, had obtained by chance. It was later classified as an A-Rank artifact, and because it formed a contract with the wearer's blood, the Mercenary Guild could not even take it from him.

The real reason they could not take it was simple. The C-Rank Mercenary who obtained Devnak had instantly surpassed the peak realm due to the armor's power. Lee Sungmin did not know the armor's exact capabilities, but he knew he needed to get it.

Fortunately, he remembered exactly how. The mercenary who found Devnak would tell the same story whenever he drank, a story of how his life had changed. At the time, Lee Sungmin had been clearing empty plates and cups in a corner of the tavern, listening intently.

A life-changing opportunity. The Lee Sungmin of that time had desperately wished for one too.

With his current skills, he could receive an S-Rank rating at any city's Mercenary Guild. For a martial artist, an S-Rank was granted to those with skills at or above the peak realm.

If he became an S-Rank Mercenary, he could choose requests as he pleased. He would also receive a huge monthly stipend from the guild and face no penalties even if he took on no work at all. An S-Rank Mercenary held that much value.

Lee Sungmin planned to become an S-Rank Mercenary, carry out other requests for half a year, and then apply for the Dark Mage Dungeon subjugation. His first goal was to obtain the Magic Armor Devnak. If possible, he also wanted to get his hands on the other spoils from that Dungeon.

When night fell, Lee Sungmin prepared to camp. He easily caught two small rabbits and started a fire, placing the prepared meat over the flames and taking a slow breath.

To be honest, he had been looking forward to this moment again and again. The restriction on his sense of taste was gone, as was the one on his smell. Lee Sungmin swallowed hard as the aroma of sizzling meat wafted toward him. How long had it been since he had smelled something like this? After a short yet agonizing wait, the meat was fully cooked. He immediately picked up the skewer and tore into it.

Delicious.

It was meat without any proper seasoning, but it was so delicious that tears streamed down his face. The taste could not even be compared to the terrible meals on the mountain that he had never gotten used to. Lee Sungmin realized just how blissful a meal could be.

And he made a vow. Even if he were to enter Mount Mushii again, he would never accept the restriction on his sense of taste.

The sleep he got without nightmares was peaceful. He woke a few times in the middle of the night out of habit, but he was not afraid of falling back asleep. For the first time in a very long time, Lee Sungmin slept soundly. The headaches he felt every morning were also a little less severe, likely thanks to the good rest.

As soon as he woke, he did a light Energy Circulation Exercise before getting up. He wanted to practice his Spear Technique, but getting to Behenger was the priority.

It was not until the next day that he finally arrived. Having traveled through the forest, his appearance was not very tidy. He smelled of blood, mingled from the monsters he had encountered and the sellable spoils he had taken from them.

"Where you from?" a gate guard asked.

"Zenavis," Lee Sungmin answered, his face expressionless.

The guard frowned at his reply. Coming from Zenavis meant one simple thing. He was an otherworlder.

"Get in. Just don't cause any trouble inside."

The guard moved his spear aside, a displeased look on his face. Lee Sungmin gave a slight nod and entered the city gates. Behenger. Traces of his memories remained here and there in the scenery of the city he had returned to after so long, and he felt a very, very small sense of familiarity.

*Where was the Mercenary Guild...*

Lee Sungmin relied on his memory to head toward it.

He planned to become a mercenary right away. Why not strike while the iron was hot? He wandered a little, but as he walked, his memories began to surface one by one. Since Behenger was the city where he had spent the most time in his past life, finding his way was not difficult.

He did not have many fond memories, however. His life in Behenger was not something that could be called a 'memory'. In this city, he had to do absolutely everything just to survive. As a result, there was nothing worth recalling. To be honest, he did not even want to.

When he stood in front of the Mercenary Guild, an old memory surfaced. A trivial one. It was a memory of the senior mercenaries who had bullied him here, a memory of those bastards.

Lee Sungmin let out a short laugh and grabbed the doorknob.

He realized just how insignificant they truly were.
