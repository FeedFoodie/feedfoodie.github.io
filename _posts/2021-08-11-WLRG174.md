A spray of blood painted the night sky as the large yokai was sent flying. He tore through several stalls before crashing to the ground, his body rolling across the floor in a broken heap. He coughed, spitting a mouthful of blood, and lifted his distorted face.

"Chuseong…!"

Chuseong, the target of the enraged shout, slowly approached. The man he had just attacked was a great yokai, the ruler of this very street. He had once served under Red Ghost, and when Yana became the new master of Urmuri, he had bowed his head once more and lived a quiet life.

"You've gotten soft," Chuseong said, baring his teeth in a grin.

His sharp fangs and ferocious gaze burned with a clear killing intent. The injured yokai could not understand it. He had no idea why Chuseong, who had fled Urmuri, had suddenly returned, nor why he was being attacked. He knew only that Chuseong was genuinely trying to kill him, and with that certainty, he somehow forced himself to his feet.

"Why are you trying to kill me?!" he shouted.

"Yana has left Urmuri," Chuseong spat in reply.

The weirdo who had entered Urmuri with him was also gone from his side. That one had been with Chuseong for his own purposes, and Chuseong had no desire to know what he was up to. There was no reason to waste thought on such things.

A yokai eating another yokai. The act held many meanings.

It was a privilege unique to Vampires among the inhuman species to reliably increase their power by drinking the blood of their own kind. Their bloodsucking was a potent form of Energy Drain, for to a Vampire, blood was the very essence of life and power. A yokai was a being that straddled the line between inhuman and monster, yet they shared this same privilege.

When a yokai ate a human, it was to consume the fear that blossomed in their victim just before death. That fear became their power. The same principle applied when a yokai ate another yokai.

The bloodied great yokai staggered upright. It went without saying that he did not want to die, but he had no desire to face Chuseong head-on either. Even if he had fled from Yana, Chuseong was still one of the most prominent great yokai in all of Urmuri.

In the end, the yokai chose to turn and run. Chuseong laughed heartily, as if he had expected nothing less.

He extended a hand, and a crimson heatwave slammed into the fleeing yokai's back.

"Kuaaak!"

A gruesome scream echoed through the street as the smell of burning flesh spread in all directions. Among the gathered yokai, not a single one moved to stop Chuseong's hunt.

The heatwave completely engulfed the yokai's body, erupting into a crimson flame that began to incinerate him. The yokai screamed and struggled within the inferno. Laughing loudly, Chuseong approached the thrashing figure. He opened his mouth wide and inhaled, pulling the still-burning yokai into his maw.

"Hahaha!"

A feeling of fullness settled in his stomach, and Chuseong laughed again. He had never liked how such a pathetic creature paraded around, calling itself a great yokai. A yokai should be more ferocious, a creature that acts on its desires.

Red Ghost, whose heart had been torn out, had turned Urmuri into something like a tourist city, perhaps a lingering effect of his human life. Yana, his successor, had acted as if she was not in control, but she never ignored the disputes between yokai.

However, now, neither Red Ghost nor Yana was here. There was nothing left to hold him back.

Chuseong looked back at the yokai who were watching him. They were frozen, terrified by the death of a great yokai. He let out another huge laugh at their pathetic faces and stirred up a new heatwave.

"You clowns who do nothing but sell smiles!" Chuseong roared, his voice booming as he swung the wave of heat.

KWAAAAA!

The stalls burst into flames. The crimson blaze flickered and grew, swallowing the surrounding buildings. The yokai cried out in panic. They were not the only ones. The humans who had come to enjoy the night market, knowing nothing, also screamed in terror at the sudden conflagration.

{sep}

Lee Sungmin was completely unaware of the commotion in the night market. He remained in the hidden space within the Fairy Realm's garden, the same place where he had met Yana. He was deep in concentration, attempting the monumental task of combining the breathing technique for handling Demonic Energy with the Violet Dawn Divine Art.

"Damn it."

He finally let out a curse. He was trying to merge two completely different things. A calamity with the talent of a patriarch might have been able to do it, but for Lee Sungmin, it was impossible. He had achieved a level not far behind a clan's patriarch, but that was a result of his dedication to the martial arts he had learned, not because he possessed any outstanding talent.

*I might not need to overhaul the martial art from scratch… but incorporating the breathing technique into the Violet Dawn Divine Art. It's impossible for me.*

Although it had only been a day, he had no confidence that he could succeed even if he spent more time on it. Lee Sungmin sighed and got to his feet.

[I don't know much about martial arts either.]

Even the Void Monarch was of no help in this matter. The Void Monarch was a great yokai with immense power, but that did not mean he excelled at martial arts.

Should he get help from someone else?

The first person who came to mind was Wiji Hoyeon. She possessed the calamitous talent to overhaul her martial arts at the age of thirteen, and the Violet Dawn Divine Art he had learned originated from her. Wiji Hoyeon could likely solve the problem he was struggling with easily.

However, Wiji Hoyeon was not by his side right now.

"Stop."

A voice came from behind him. Lee Sungmin flinched in surprise and spun around. A man in shabby clothes was standing there, looking down at him from behind a monocle.

"I believe I gave you a warning. Not to give up on being human. Was I too indirect?" the man muttered, taking off the hat he was wearing.

From those words, Lee Sungmin realized who the man was.

"Envirus."

"That's right." Envirus nodded.

The haggard-looking man dragged his feet as he moved closer. Lee Sungmin stood up, staring intently at Envirus's face and its patchy beard.

"A little late, aren't we? No, maybe very late?" Envirus muttered, tilting his head. He twirled a large, magician-like hat on his finger. "Well. I suppose this was inevitable once you met the Void Monarch. If it were possible, I would have annihilated the Void Monarch myself…"

Envirus clicked his tongue. Hearing this, the Void Monarch rose up in a flare of Demonic Energy.

Lee Sungmin stared at Envirus and asked, "...We finally meet."

"Indeed," Envirus replied. He dusted off his dirt-covered robes and sat down on the spot. "You and I have quite a lot in common."

He began to speak, pushing up his monocle with his index finger. "You must have undergone Denir's Trial as well. How long did you last?"

"...2100 years."

"......"

Envirus's eyes widened at the answer. He blinked his wide eyes and let out an exclamation. "Amazing. 2100 years. That's a realm beyond just having strong divine power… My limit was a thousand years." He let out a small laugh. "It's not just Denir's Trial. You're also a being whose causality is twisted. Just like me."

"Why did that happen to you?" Lee Sungmin asked directly.

Envirus answered with a calm face, as if he had expected the question. "I didn't return to the past from death like you did."

"Then?"

"I am a very ambiguous being. Human, yet not human, but not inhuman either." His words were incomprehensible. "I know that you are loved by the Apostle of the End. A very blind and devoted love at that. I also know that a powerful Destiny Force is entangled with you. However, 'I' am not included in your Destiny Force."

Envirus wiped his fatigue-filled face with his hand. "But not anymore. I was not part of that great destiny, but by meeting you, I've been swept up in your Destiny Force. The reason I tried to avoid meeting you was for that very reason. That's why I sent Rubia instead."

"What happens if you get swept up in my Destiny Force?"

"Things will go with the flow," Envirus chuckled. "Without knowing what kind of ending there is. Struggling to change a predetermined ending, yet not knowing if it has changed or not… ho ho! You end up living according to the flow set by someone looking down from above."

"...Are you saying it wasn't like that for you until now?"

"Escaping that destiny is the reason my causality became twisted."

"If you went through all that trouble to escape, why appear before me now?" Lee Sungmin asked, his face a mask of confusion.

This thing called Destiny Force. He did not want to understand it. He believed that not knowing what the future held was natural, regardless of any destiny.

"Because I couldn't just leave it alone," Envirus replied. "Do not abandon your humanity. That's what I told you. I said that because the existence of the Void Monarch, who met his death four hundred years ago, is that powerful. If you were to become a complete yokai, you might be influenced by him. If that happened, something terrible would occur. The dead Void Monarch would be resurrected with a new body."

[This son of a bitch, what does he take this Elder for?]

The Void Monarch flared up in anger. Just a few days ago, Yana had tried to offer Lee Sungmin's body to him, and he had refused.

"Even if the Void Monarch has no such intention," Envirus spoke again, "if you become a yokai and are no longer human, no one knows what kind of being you will become. That's what makes it so dangerous. For 'me' to no longer be 'me' is a terrifying and dangerous thing. Especially for you. Can't you inherit the Void Monarch's Demonic Energy as it is? If you become completely consumed by Demonic Energy and go berserk, you will become a yokai as powerful as the Void Monarch, without being the Void Monarch."

"Are you saying you jumped back into the destiny you worked so hard to escape, just to prepare for something that hasn't even happened?"

"I considered it that important," Envirus said, placing the hat he had been holding back on his head. "Even if I get swept up in your Destiny Force and become part of the flow I worked so hard to escape, I thought it was necessary to meet you at least once, and if possible, I wanted to stop you."

"...I haven't done anything. Your worries were in vain. I have absolutely no intention of becoming a yokai."

"Is that so?" Envirus asked back with a chuckle. "Destiny is something you cannot defy. It is the fate of us mortals to struggle within the great flow. Especially you… you are loved by something that governs such a powerful Destiny Force. I was lucky enough to escape it, but you will never be able to."

"...My thoughts and choices. Are you saying all of that is left to the flow of destiny?"

"I can't be certain about that. Because neither I, nor any being in this world, can see the flowing destiny."

"What in the world are you trying to say?" Lee Sungmin could not suppress the irritation welling up in his chest. He clenched his fists and glared at Envirus. "What you're saying is nothing but vague. Destiny or whatever. I've never once thought about such things, and I don't believe the life I've lived so far has flowed according to some bullshit destiny."

"That's because destiny is an ambiguous thing."

"What's ambiguous is the crap you're spouting!" Lee Sungmin yelled. "Don't talk to me about a future that hasn't happened and that no one knows about. Don't assume things will turn out a certain way and try to lecture me. I'm going to live as I please. Not as a plaything of destiny or whatever that crap is!"

"Is that so?" Envirus made a strange expression. "Perhaps even you acting like this right now is…"

"Don't give me that bullshit!" Lee Sungmin couldn't hold back any longer and shouted even louder.

At his words, Envirus looked at him with somber eyes. "...I'm sorry if I offended you. Still, I was worried about you."

"Forget it," Lee Sungmin spat, trying to calm his ragged breathing. What in the world had happened when he returned from death? He had never once received a proper answer to that question. Everyone just talked in circles.

"...Do you know about the destiny I belong to?"

"I do not. Didn't I say? Mortals can never know the end of destiny."

"Then you wouldn't know what will happen to me in the future either."

"That's right." Envirus nodded.

Lee Sungmin let out a deep sigh and asked, "...Let me ask one more thing. Can you handle a Dragon Heart?"

"A Dragon Heart?" Envirus tilted his head. "...I can handle it, but why do you ask?"

The irritation that had filled him upon meeting Envirus seemed to cool down, just a little, at his answer.
