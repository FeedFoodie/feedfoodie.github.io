Teresa stared at Lee Sungmin, her eyes wide with an astonishment that suggested she had never imagined such an answer.

"......"

Her voice was filled with incomprehension as she spoke.

"The things you possess will one day kill you. Or they'll twist you into something that isn't you."

"I know."

Lee Sungmin knew the danger she spoke of all too well, especially the poison spreading through his body. She could only be referring to his Demonic Energy.

Even without considering the Black Heart or Void Monarch, the Demonic Energy itself was a potent poison coursing through his veins. Osla's seal was imperfect, a temporary measure against a tide that would one day erupt. The old dragon had warned him of this several times, urging him not to grow complacent and to find a complete solution.

Purification offered a clean way out, a method to completely erase the Demonic Energy he possessed. Perhaps it was the ideal solution.

However, that was the problem.

"Demonic Energy... so, can you only purify the poison that has spread through my body?"

"That's... impossible. It purifies all the impurities in your body. It's impossible to single out and purify only the Demonic Energy."

"Then I can't accept."

He refused to lose anything else. The Black Heart had granted him immense benefits, allowing him to inherit the power of the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. He would not lose it. It was a potential the Chairman had passed on by sacrificing his own body, a chance to grow stronger and advance further. Without it, his growth would have stagnated.

"I don't want to lose it."

However, that wasn't his only reason. There was also the uninvited guest in his mind, Void Monarch.

Lee Sungmin was acutely conscious of him. While he had once wanted nothing more than to be rid of the entity inhabiting his armor, that thought was now a distant memory. Void Monarch had become his confidant, his comrade, his friend. In some ways, he was even a master.

[And the bastard who knows that is the same one who threatens to dunk me in a cesspool every chance he gets?]

Void Monarch grumbled from within. Teresa slowly nodded her head. She drew back the hand Lee Sungmin had pushed away.

"If you ever change your mind, please let me know," she said.

"I don't think I will."

"You never know what might happen in this world. You are extremely dangerous, Phantom Spear. What resides within you is nothing short of chaos, and if it explodes, your very existence will be erased, and after that?"

"I know that, too." A slow smile touched Lee Sungmin's lips. "Even so, I can't accept the purification."

"Are you saying you'll willingly live with the danger?"

"Because I need it."

And it was the truth. He needed it all. He needed Void Monarch, the Black Heart, and the Demonic Energy. Lee Sungmin couldn't imagine himself without them. He would never have reached his current position with his natural talents alone. This luck, this help from things that were not his own, had all come together to create the man he was now. Even if this was all part of fate's design, he could not discard it.

"Enough of that talk," Abel interjected, his patience clearly worn thin. "What's the plan for Kim Jonghyun?"

{sep}

"What will you do?"

After their conversation with Teresa, Lee Sungmin and Abel returned to their carriage. The course of action was already set. As collaborators with the Church, they had decided to join the attack on Kim Jonghyun, who now occupied Germud.

"They'll be of some help," Abel remarked, putting a pipe to his lips the moment he was back inside.

Teresa might not have known about The End, but he couldn't be certain the entire Church was ignorant. Regardless, they had no choice but to stop Kim Jonghyun.

"What about other reinforcements?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"It'll be difficult. The South has little of murim's power. Do you really think the Yokai of Urmuri will cooperate? There's no need to be pedantic. They're a different species from us."

"What about the Mages Guild?"

"Don't expect much from them either. The distance is too great. Even if I, the Guildmaster, gave the order, it would take months for the Magic Tower Masters or the Mage Army to arrive. Are you suggesting we use a Fairy Horse to ferry them back and forth?"

"Is that possible?"

"Impossible. A Fairy Horse isn't omnipotent, and consecutive Space Leaps would put a strain on you." Abel grumbled, exhaling a long stream of smoke. He had never expected a numerical advantage against a necromancer leading a legion of Death Knights.

He had learned a great deal from Teresa. The Church's position was absolute. They had to kill Kim Jonghyun for the massacre he had committed in Germud and for raising the dead as Undead. For that reason, Teresa, who had never before appeared in public, had taken action herself, mobilizing hundreds from the Holy Knight Regiment and the Priesthood. In addition to the forces already on the move, more Holy Knights were reportedly gathering around the city.

"A few thousand at most," Abel replied coldly. "The best we can hope for is a favorable matchup between dark magic and holy magic, but can that really overturn a difference in forces of hundreds of thousands against a few thousand?"

"No," Lee Sungmin answered, his expression stoic. If the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman were here, perhaps it would be possible. His overwhelming martial prowess could have erased such a disparity, but the Chairman was dead.

Now, Lee Sungmin had to be the one to do it.

"Are you going to run?"

"No." Abel shook his head. "We can stop him now."

There was no wavering in his voice. The difference in forces was several dozen times over. Kim Jonghyun had not only inherited Arbes's power but had also become a Quasi-Demon King, wielding the authority of a Demon Lord. Since the Grimoire he possessed was a magic tome for a Demon Lord, he would likely be able to use its magic as well.

"We can ignore the mass of Undead," Abel laid out. "The Holy Knights will hold back the Death Knights, and you should be able to stop Vollander."

"And you?"

"I'll stop Kim Jonghyun," Abel declared. "I'm different from Hyung-nim."

As he spoke, he rummaged inside his robe and pulled a small crystal ball from his Subspace Pocket.

"Still," he added, "I should ask for help, just in case."

"From whom?"

"An old friend," Abel said, explaining that she happened to be in the South. He placed both hands on the crystal ball.

Four days later, the carriage stopped just outside Germud, where a considerable force had already gathered. At a glance, their numbers reached the thousands, more than Abel had expected. While the news of an annihilated city had sent many fleeing in terror, these were the ones who had chosen not to run. They were the ones willing to throw their lives away to stop the terrible demon named Kim Jonghyun, gathered on the plains before the city.

From the carriage, Lee Sungmin and Abel looked out at the assembly. They saw Priests and Holy Knights dressed in white, alongside mercenaries and natives who had made their homes in the South.

[There are Yokai, too,] Void Monarch muttered.

*Did they also come to stop Kim Jonghyun?*

[That's a rather charitable thought. The Yokai are likely here to feed on the fear. A massacre of this scale is unprecedented. That city is already a hell, swirling with the terror of the dead. Any Yokai craving power would inevitably covet it.] Void Monarch clicked his tongue. [Do you know what this means? This won't be a very good battlefield for you. Even with the seal, breathing the air of that city will stimulate your suppressed Demonic Energy.]

*Even so, I can't run away.*

[Because the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman wouldn't have? As I've said before, you are different from him...]

*That's not why.*

Void Monarch fell silent at Lee Sungmin's sharp retort.

*Because my Master wouldn't have run away. Yes, I'm not him. I'm allowed to run, but I don't want to. It's not because of what he would have done... it's because I, myself, don't want to run. If Kim Jonghyun is The End, then I have to stop him.*

[Do you think that's your role? Do you believe it's right for you to stop The End because you're the one who brought it about?]

*It's because I don't want to die.*

Void Monarch guffawed at the honest answer.

*I've come this far, only to stand by and watch the world end? I went through the trouble of dying and returning to the past, gained power I never dreamed of... and now I'm supposed to just die? Screw that. Why the hell should I?*

[I like your honesty!] Void Monarch's laughter grew louder.

"More than I thought," Abel mused, looking out the window. "They can at least be used as meat shields."

"What about the friend you were supposed to meet?"

"She'll be here soon," Abel said, stroking the crystal ball on his lap. He had said little about this friend, only that she would be helpful.

*Hm?*

Just then, a figure approached the carriage. "She's here," Abel grumbled, tucking the crystal ball back into his robes.

Watching the person stride toward them, Lee Sungmin's eyes widened. He recognized them both instantly, the woman marching forward with an annoyed expression and the man trailing behind her with his head bowed low.

Frau and Aladur.

A long time ago, when he had come to the South to break Wiji Hoyeon's curse, he had met Aladur acting as a guide in the Bewitching Forest. After coincidentally saving the man's life, he had formed a connection with Aladur's master, Frau.

SLAM!

The carriage door flew open with enough force to tear it from its hinges. Frau stuck her head inside, her eyes scanning the interior. The first person she saw was Abel, sitting with his arms crossed.

"You son of a bitch," she spat. After glaring at Abel with murderous eyes, her gaze shifted to Lee Sungmin. "...Why are you here?"

"It's been a while." Nearly two years had passed since he had left Urmuri after the Crazy Heavenly Demon's death. It wasn't a short amount of time, but Frau clearly remembered him.

"If I'd known you were here, I would've told Yana," she muttered, plopping down next to Lee Sungmin. Aladur stood at the carriage entrance, his shoulders slumped. As he tried to step inside, Frau glared at him.

"Where do you think you're going, you bastard? Stand outside the carriage!"

"Yes..." Aladur answered dejectedly. Lee Sungmin felt a pang of pity for him, but the carriage door slammed shut before he could offer a greeting.

"So the friend you mentioned was Frau?"

"I didn't know you two knew each other."

"Friend? Don't make me laugh. What kind of friend invites someone to a death trap?"

"You could've refused if you didn't want to come."

"I must have been crazy," Frau spat, grinding her teeth. "Why did I ever make a promise to you back then?"

Abel just chuckled. It seemed there was some kind of binding promise between them, an oath sworn on Mana that was as good as betting one's life.

"It's not necessarily a death trap," Abel countered. "More people have gathered for the punitive force than I expected, and the Church's Saint is here herself. You, a Grand Sorcerer, have come to help. I've reached the pinnacle of magic, and this kid here... well, you know he's one of the strongest martial artists around."

"Aladur!" Frau suddenly shouted.

The carriage door opened a crack, and Aladur poked his face inside. "What is it, Master... UGH!"

His words were cut short as Frau's fist slammed into his face.

"That's enough. Get out."

Aladur whimpered, clutching his now fist-sized nose.

"What are you doing?" Abel asked in disbelief.

"I was pissed."

"So you hit your disciple?"

"Disciple, my foot. He's the one who ran away because he didn't want to serve me. He should be grateful I didn't kill him," Frau grumbled, adjusting the rings on her wrists. Her expression then turned serious. "Something's wrong." Her eyes narrowed. "I don't know what's happening in that city, but it's not normal. How about we turn back right now?"

"No," Abel replied instantly. "We can't do that." He placed a hand on his staff, Gries. "Kim Jonghyun is the first calamity of The End."

"Oh, damn it." Frau buried her face in her hands.
