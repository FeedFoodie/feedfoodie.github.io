Persuading them proved to be far less difficult than Lee Sungmin had worried. The Church was far more cooperative than he had feared. A group driven by self-interest would have twisted the events in Germud ten years ago to its advantage, but the Church had not. They were not a group that sought personal gain, and their actions had proven it.

The past decade had etched a few more wrinkles onto Theos's face, and time had similarly transformed Teresa. The teenage girl he remembered from ten years ago was now a woman in her mid-twenties. Teresa possessed a sacred beauty, yet her eyes remained as pure as they had been all those years ago. She clasped her hands together and bowed her head deeply to him.

"I'm deeply grateful for what you did ten years ago, Lee Sungmin. We never would've made it out of that terrible city alive without your help."

Teresa opened her eyes.

"You and the Mages Guild Master laid down your lives to defeat the Death Lord and the Demon Lord, and… when the Demon Lord's fatal curse drove you mad, we couldn't help you. We had no choice but to retreat."

"I know you had no choice."

"But I still feel guilty for not being able to do anything. Lee Sungmin, you said your martial sister, Silent Flash Baek Sogo, did what we couldn't. She stopped you, and now she's dying from a deadly poison."

"Yes. So please…"

"Of course, we must help," Teresa declared without a moment's hesitation. "I have ears, so I'm well aware of Silent Flash Baek Sogo's heroic deeds. Now, let's go."

Teresa's response was far more proactive than he had expected. She immediately rose to her feet, placed her hands on her hips, and looked at him.

"Theos, please make a suitable excuse to the Pope."

"Understood."

"Now, let's go," Teresa said again, her eyes shining.

Her enthusiastic attitude flustered Lee Sungmin. Was it really alright to take her just like this? She was, after all, the one and only Saint of the Church.

"By the way," she began, "you've changed a lot from ten years ago."

As Lee Sungmin summoned the Fairy Horse, Teresa added with a light laugh, "Your hair, too."

At her words, he paused and touched the hair he had roughly tied back. He had been so busy running around since waking up that he had not even had a chance to cut it.

"It suits you," she said, before her gaze turned serious. "And… the poison that was killing you ten years ago."

"What about it?"

"…Well," she said, choosing her words carefully, "I'm not sure if it can even be called a poison anymore. It's become a part of you."

It was because his body had completely become a Yokai. Lee Sungmin smiled faintly and mounted the Fairy Horse. Before he could offer a hand, Teresa hopped up and settled behind him.

"By the way, what happens when I ride this?"

Teresa, swept up in the moment, knew nothing about the Fairy Horse she had just mounted. There was no need for an explanation. The experience itself would suffice.

The Fairy Horse leaped through space.

"You really make good use of that."

Behind a flash of light, the Fairy Horse emerged from a rift in space. Osla clicked his tongue as he watched Lee Sungmin dismount.

"What would you have done without the Fairy Horse?"

"I would've had to run my feet off covering a distance that would take months to travel back and forth," Lee Sungmin replied with a bitter smile.

The vigor she had shown moments before was gone. As Teresa dismounted, she looked as if she were on the verge of death. She staggered away from the horse, collapsed to the ground, and covered her face with her hands.

"I feel sick…"

"Some people just aren't cut out for it. It's like motion sickness," Osla said with a chuckle.

Teresa weakly lifted her head to look at Osla. A moment later, her eyes widened in shock.

"A… Fairy Realm…?!"

"People who see us always say that," Osla replied in a singsong voice, whistling.

Teresa stared blankly, her mouth agape, at the other Fairy Realm beings who were chuckling along with Osla. She soon collected herself and asked, "Where is Silent Flash?"

He led Teresa toward Baek Sogo's location. On the way, his eyes met Yana's, who was resting with her back against a tree. Yana gave a slight nod in greeting to them both.

Teresa stuck close to Lee Sungmin's back and whispered, her voice laced with awe. "I… I know her. We passed by in a flash, but I saw. Nine tails… She's the Nine-Tailed Fox, right?"

"……"

"Your connections are… quite extensive," Teresa muttered in admiration.

Lee Sungmin let out a small laugh at her words.

"You're back early."

Scarlet was by Baek Sogo's side. "Thank goodness," she added, stroking the forehead of the unconscious woman. "She suddenly lost consciousness. Baek Sogo told me beforehand that it happens often, so I shouldn't be too surprised. Still, how can a person's heart not worry?"

Scarlet confessed that if he had been any later, she would have used the bracelet to call for him. Grasping the situation, Teresa emerged from behind Lee Sungmin. She took a spot beside Baek Sogo and closed her eyes. A pure white light began to spread from her.

Scarlet flinched in surprise and stepped back. Lee Sungmin also put a reasonable distance between them, for although he had become a complete Yokai, Teresa's hold power was still fatal to him.

For a long while, Teresa held Baek Sogo's wrist, channeling her hold power. Lee Sungmin and Scarlet stood nearby, their worried eyes fixed on Baek Sogo.

"You don't have to worry so much," Osla said, approaching Lee Sungmin's side. "The poison that has accumulated in Baek Sogo's body is being purified. It might take a little more time, but the poison itself can definitely be cleansed."

Osla chuckled. "This is incredible. It was a poison that even I, the Fairy Queen, couldn't do anything about. A poison that engraves itself onto one's very existence, poisoning them to death. To purify it with hold power alone… They say she's the only Saint of the Church, and she certainly deserves the title."

A little more time passed before Teresa slowly opened her eyes.

"Whew."

She let out a short breath and wiped the sweat trickling down her forehead.

"It's done," Teresa said with a bright smile. "It's over."

Beside her, Baek Sogo was breathing peacefully.

She was always watching. She watched everything. The long years of slumber had bound her physical body, but her mind remained free. She was, after all, a transcendent being unbound by such mortal constraints. If she wished, her spirit could leave her sleeping body at any time.

And so, she watched over the lover she adored, the human named Cain. Even after he lost his magical abilities by breaking his contract with Mana. Even after his legs barely moved, confining him to a wheelchair. Even after he had become a wrinkled old man, unable to completely suppress his aging with magic.

The Spirit Queen loved him. Her love for him was like an imprinting, an indelible mark upon her very soul.

Summoning, and a contract.

Countless Elementalists dreamed of contracting with the king of all Elementals, but only Cain had succeeded. He was the first to summon her, the first to form a contract with the Spirit Queen. Out of affection, the queen had told her summoner, the first to ever call her, her name.

However, the Spirit Queen was a being who should not have been summoned to this world. Instead, the countless Elementals she commanded came to follow Cain's orders. That was fine too. The queen developed a deep affection for Cain, the first to summon her. To the queen, who had never been connected to anything in this world on equal terms, Cain was her sole confidant and contractor.

Whether obsession came first, or affection, the queen could no longer tell. The catalyst did not matter. Truly, to the queen, the catalyst was nothing more than a trifle. She loved him and wanted to protect him.

To save him, she had broken her promise. She had wanted to save his destiny. The End? She could not have cared less. As long as she was with Cain, everything would be fine.

And that Cain died.

It was a miserable death. The space created for Cain, the mansion built for him. She had often imagined living there with him once she finally awoke, once her body was free. A demon who came knocking on the mansion's door strangled Cain to death. His younger brother? She thought he had said something like that, but it held no great meaning for her.

What was important was that Cain was dead, and that the human who killed him was also dead.

*What should I do, what should I do.*

Inside her sleeping body, the queen's mind spun in circles. A lost hatred and a burning desire for revenge consumed her spirit.

*Why did Cain have to die? I saved him. I broke my promise and saved his destiny. Why did Cain die?*

To save the world.

Yes, that was it. To stop The End. Cain had died as a sacrifice for it. For such a paltry reason. To the queen, that too was nothing more than a trifle. What did it matter whether the world was destroyed or not? Cain should have lived on happily with her in that mansion.

And that Cain died. He died to save something like the world.

"No."

That was the first thing the queen said upon opening her eyes. Unbearable hatred and anger had forced her awake. Originally, she should have slept for much longer, but now, it did not matter. The queen intended to give up everything. Her promise, her own existence as the Spirit Queen, the new world she would return to after this one ended, and all the Elementals she ruled. Everything.

Cain was dead. He had died like that, but in the end, his death had not changed the world's fate.

That enraged the queen. Having awakened, she intended to become one of the calamities of The End herself. She would ignore all promises and Advent in the world. Since she had given up her promise and accepted ruin, the law forbidding her from Adventing could no longer bind her. In the first place, that was a rule made by Spirits and Demonic Spirits. If the Elemental queen herself wished to become The End, a world fated for The End would not refuse her.

The awakened queen left her room. All the Elementals gathered below her palace, sensing their queen had awoken. They were connected by a single mind. Every Elemental knew of the queen's fury, her hatred, and her desire for revenge, and they sympathized with it. Only Rubia, the artificial Elemental, trembled in fear at the queen's feet. The queen did not spare Rubia a glance. Vicious revenge, hatred, and anger had narrowed her vision.

The queen was about to declare it. That she would accept ruin and become an apostle of destruction. All Elementals would Advent in the world with the will to destroy. Commanding that great destruction, the queen would erase the world. The Water Elementals would make the seas rage, swallowing coastal cities with tidal waves. The Earth Elementals would shake all the lands, and the Fire Elementals would spew lava from the fissures they created. The Wind Elementals would spread the resulting flames across the entire world, and the Spirit Queen would watch it all with joy.

The Spirit Queen raised her hands. Now, she was about to declare it.

She was about to.

For some time, 'it' had been standing behind all the Elementals. Not a single one of them, not even the queen who ruled this realm, had noticed its presence. It had casually ignored the fundamental law that no being other than an Elemental should enter the Elemental Realm. Its very existence placed it in a position that transcended all laws.

A jet-black cloth fluttered, and in that instant, all the Elementals standing between it and the Spirit Queen vanished. Only then did the queen finally notice the presence.

"Spirit Queen."

It.

She muttered in a voice full of weariness.

"Sarahenne."

It was the queen's name. The name that no one but Cain should have known flowed from her lips.

At some point, this had all become a tedious task for her. She wandered through Dungeons, smashing them and absorbing their power, yet the growing strength felt no different than before. She just continued to wander, endlessly. Ten years had already passed this way. Ten years ago, she had spoken up once. 'Save him.' If she had been refused, she would have killed herself.

"It was tough getting here," Wiji Hoyeon said, looking at the Spirit Queen.

She had long since forgotten the number of Dungeons she had conquered and had given up counting. The Dungeons Wiji Hoyeon had conquered so far were calamities of The End that would have, when the time came, all manifested at once to end Eria.

Now, that particular End would not come.

Wiji Hoyeon tilted her head. "What number are you?" she asked. "What number calamity, I mean."
