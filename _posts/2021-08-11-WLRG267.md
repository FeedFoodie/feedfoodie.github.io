By the time I woke in an unused room, the sun was already high in the sky, nearing noon. After I washed, the Void Monarch's nagging became unbearable, forcing me to seek out the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman.

[That liquor bottle was my most cherished treasure! To think I let it get stolen right under my nose!]

*Didn't you say the liquor flows endlessly from it anyway?*

[The problem isn't the liquor, it's that *someone else* has it! Get it back this instant!]

*It's not like you'd drink from it even if you had it.*

[You damn bastard! If you hadn't lost that bottle yesterday, if you'd spent the night drinking with that white-haired senior sister of yours… you really think you'd have woken up alone today?]

*What's that supposed to mean?*

[That liquor is potent enough to get even this Elder drunk if I'm not careful. If you and your senior sister had kept drinking from that gourd until morning, you two would've definitely lost your senses. A drunk man and woman would have inevitably done what they were supposed to do…]

*Spout any more bullshit and I'll dunk you in a cesspool.*

[It's been a while since I've heard that threat.]

Void Monarch sneered at the familiar threat, but the idea of a cesspool bath must have truly repulsed him, because he obediently fell silent.

Lee Sungmin found the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman in his office. He knocked, and after receiving permission, stepped inside.

The Chairman wore a different mask from the one Lee Sungmin had seen at dawn, and resting quietly on his desk was the Void Monarch's gourd.

"A magnificent liquor bottle," the Chairman began, his voice appreciative. "No matter how much I drink, it never runs out. The taste is finer than any liquor I have ever had. Had I been in the Fairy Forest instead of the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance, I would have gladly drunk myself into a stupor."

"Its owner is pestering me to get it back," Lee Sungmin said.

"Owner? Ah, you mean the great yokai possessing you. This was his?"

"That's right."

"I am not so shameless as to steal another's belongings. I will return it."

With that, the Chairman used Space Manipulation, levitating the gourd from his desk and sending it floating gently back to Lee Sungmin. As the gourd settled into his hand, Void Monarch heaved a sigh of relief inside his head. Lee Sungmin found it ironic that the great yokai, once a terrible nightmare hundreds of years ago, was now getting so worked up over a single liquor bottle.

"What do you think of Silent Flash?" the Chairman asked, changing the subject.

"What are you trying to say?"

"She is dangerous," the Chairman muttered. "There's nothing more troublesome than an honest and stubborn fool. There is a saying that anything too rigid is bound to break, isn't there?"

"You mean how a reed bends instead of breaking?"

"Sometimes, it is better to bend. Silent Flash will die an early death. Or she will fall into Despair and break."

"...There's no guarantee that will happen, is there?"

"Anyone who blindly follows an unattainable conviction is bound to be consumed by it and become a monster. Or they will fall into Despair from reality long before that ever happens." The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman chuckled. "Perfect timing. You have guests."

"Pardon?"

"A few days ago, the Mages Guild Master visited. On the surface, he claimed it was to discuss the Crimson Archmage, but that wasn't his only reason."

"What does that mean?"

"The Crimson Archmage will be staying with us for the time being. To be more precise, the Mages Guild and the Unorthodox-Demonic Alliance have decided to work together."

"What about the relationship between the Murim Alliance and the Mages Guild?"

"The Mages Guild is neutral, no different from the Mercenary Guild. They've always had connections with our unorthodox sects, and those ties have simply grown stronger. In any case, the Guild Master's real purpose for coming here wasn't the Crimson Archmage. It was to meet you and me."

The Chairman led Lee Sungmin down to a spacious reception room where two mages were waiting. One of them was a face Lee Sungmin knew well. His eyes widened.

"Lloyd?"

"It's been a while." A look of pleasure lit up Lloyd's face. While their last encounter had put Lloyd in an awkward position, he had suffered no real trouble from it. After all, he had only been fulfilling the promise he made to grant Lee Sungmin one unconditional favor.

"There's no need to call for him anymore."

The voice belonged to the other man, Abel. Lee Sungmin's gaze shifted. He had never met the Mages Guild Master, yet the man's face was strangely familiar. As Lee Sungmin studied him, Abel threw back the hood of his robe and flopped into a chair.

"Envirus, no, Cain. You've met my pathetic older brother, haven't you?"

"...Ah!"

Though his overall aura and the shape of his eyes were different, his facial features were unmistakably similar to Envirus's. Abel chuckled at Lee Sungmin's surprised expression.

"I know what happened between you and my brother. I know he tried to back out of his promise to you, and I know his body collapsed from a foolish decision, forcing him to depart for the Elemental Realm."

"What?" It was Lloyd who gasped, his surprise evident. He had accompanied Abel here without ever hearing this story about his own Master.

Abel ignored him. His attention was focused solely on Lee Sungmin, the very reason he had come.

"The one who returned," Abel began. "In another world, not this one. You died, but for some reason, you returned to the past. I'm not mistaken, am I?"

"Can you see it too?" Lee Sungmin asked. A Black Mage had eyes that could see souls. A high-level one would surely be able to see the twisted causality of his.

However, contrary to his guess, Abel shook his head. "No, I'm not a Black Mage. It's just that they aren't the only ones who can see souls."

Abel muttered this as he reached into his robes, pulling out a magic tome with a pure white cover. The moment he saw it, Lloyd's face went deathly pale. He shot up from his seat, his mouth agape.

"Th-that's, Gries..."

"Why do you keep getting surprised?" Abel asked, annoyed.

"Why on earth is Gries... no, why, Guildmaster. Why do you have Gries?"

"Because I'm the Mages Guild Master."

"Gries belongs to the Mages Guild, not to the Guildmaster! Does the Council of Elders know about this?"

"They're a bunch of bastards who've abandoned any ambition for the arcane arts," Abel snapped in a scathing tone. "The old geezers on the Council of Elders are the kind of pricks who only worry about how to get their limp dicks hard for some kinky sex. They're the motherfuckers who use magic to create more pleasure and stimulation while shoving their own staves up their asses. Why the hell should I ask permission from bastards like them?"

Lloyd could offer no rebuttal, his lips just moving wordlessly. Abel cackled at the sight.

"Well, to be fair, not all the elders are shoving staves up their asses and obsessed with sex, but I'm not entirely wrong, either. You know what the most popular topic is among the Council these days? A sex Chimera designed for ultimate pleasure. You get what I'm saying? It means they're ramming their old dicks into vaginas made of slime."

"Stop... Stop!"

"Can't just kill them all, either," Abel mused, ignoring Lloyd, who was clutching his head in anguish. He turned back to Lee Sungmin, whose own expression had turned grim after being suddenly subjected to such a sordid tale.

"This is a magic tome called Gries. Do you know of it?"

"...I know it as one of the magic tomes the Mages Guild possesses. The twin to the Grimoire that Kim Jonghyun ran away with."

"So you know the basics. Do you know why I took this out?"

"I don't."

"To talk about The End."

Abel said this while flipping through the pages of Gries. Lloyd, who had been lamenting just a moment ago, now stared wide-eyed at the turning pages. Gries, much like the Grimoire, was an artifact kept by the Mages Guild, but one that had remained almost completely unresearched.

"Have you deciphered Gries?" Lloyd asked in disbelief.

"Only in part. Gries is a wicked and incomplete magic tome. I imagine the Grimoire is the same. You can't use it through normal means."

"Then how...?"

"What Gries demands to activate its magic is lifespan." Abel answered, his expression completely unfazed.

At his words, Lloyd's face went white. He stumbled backward. "Don't tell me... Guildmaster. You brought me all the way here to use me as a sacrifice to use Gries...?"

"Don't be an idiot, just sit down. I'm casting the magic for myself, so why would it demand your lifespan?"

"Then...?"

"I'm using my own lifespan, so don't freak out." With that, Abel stopped flipping through the pages.

A palpable stir ran through the mana in the air. Unsure of what he was about to do, Lee Sungmin watched Abel with wary eyes.

"What are you trying to do?"

"I'm casting a barrier," Abel replied. "One to prevent the Apostle of the End from interfering."

He began his incantation, and Gries was immediately enveloped in a pure white light. The Unorthodox Alliance Chairman, standing beside Lee Sungmin with his hands clasped behind his back, watched the glowing tome and the chanting mage.

"Until now, I have never thought much of the kind known as mages," he muttered, "but today, that prejudice has been shattered. My perspective was too narrow."

The Chairman had good reason to say so. The immense magic power flowing from Abel as he controlled the power of Gries was greater than that of any mage Lee Sungmin had ever seen. Envirus had been a figure at the pinnacle of magic, but the power Abel now displayed was far greater than his.

"Using your lifespan... isn't that too dangerous...?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"It's not a problem," Abel answered after finishing his incantation. "I know exactly how much lifespan I have left. Using this much won't kill me."

He had cast a barrier, but nothing in the room seemed to have changed. Abel, however, slumped into his chair, looking slightly exhausted.

"It should last long enough for our talk."

"...The Apostle of the End... you said?"

"You must have felt it a few times yourself," Abel said. "Or perhaps you've seen others try to tell you this story, only to stop when they felt death's presence."

He had seen it many times. It was the very reason he had always been prevented from getting answers.

"This barrier, incomplete as it may be, will block the Apostle of the End's intervention. In other words, I can now tell you what I know."

"Are you saying you know everything?"

"I won't make the arrogant claim that I know everything," Abel muttered, turning to the Unorthodox Alliance Chairman. "Do you know why I called you here, Coalition Leader?"

"You must have thought my power would be helpful."

"That's part of it. You are one of the strongest humans, after all."

"It seems that's not the whole reason."

"I know that you have a relationship with the Fairy Queen," Abel answered, his gaze returning to Lee Sungmin. "The End is near."

He continued, his focus locked on Lee Sungmin. "Coalition Leader, your power and your relationship with the Fairy Queen… I hope all of it is for the sake of stopping The End. I have no proof, of course."

"Is that also destiny?" the Chairman asked.

"As long as it's connected to him," Abel said, gesturing to Lee Sungmin, "I believe it is part of a single destiny."

Lee Sungmin asked the next question, and Abel answered, frowning as he continued.

"You died in a past life and returned to the past by some means. The future you 'remember' and the future that has actually unfolded in this world are quite different, aren't they? What do you think is the reason for that?"

"...Me."

"That's right. You. If you had lived exactly as you did in your past life, nothing would have changed. Things would have progressed just as you remembered, but you didn't do that. Because you were different, the world you remembered became different."

Lee Sungmin was already aware of this. This world had changed in countless ways from the future he knew. It was because he, having returned to the past, had lived a completely different life. Those small changes had rippled outward, creating a massive distortion.

"You are a distortion of this world's predetermined destiny," Abel said, his voice low. "A variable, a singularity. From the moment you returned to the past, this world's fate became entangled with your very existence."

"...What does that have to do with The End?"

"You are an Observer," Abel continued. "From the moment you returned, you became the Observer of this world's destiny. Because you, the Observer, acted differently, the flow of fate changed, and now that you've passed the point in time when you died in your past life, the world that has changed because of you is advancing into a world you 'don't know'."

Abel muttered this, then let out a short, sharp chuckle.

"Yes. You, who returned to the past. The world that exists after the point you died, because you continued to live. A world unknown to you, the Observer."

His laughter grew louder, filling the room with a chilling sound.

"That is The End."
