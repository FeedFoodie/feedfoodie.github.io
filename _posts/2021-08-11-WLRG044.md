A giant dao city like Behenger owed its security to its towering walls, the lord's order of knights, and a host of private soldiers. For those who chose to live beyond such safety, there was usually a reason they could not live within. In extreme cases, they might have been exiled for a crime. For others, the reason was as simple and grand as having no money.

Lee Sungmin had no way of knowing why the villagers who requested the Ogre subjugation lived outside the city, nor did he need to. As Rudd had said, what moved a mercenary was not compassion but coin. They had accepted a request for payment, and they would see it done.

"Still, this is strange," Rudd muttered as they walked through the forest, his brow furrowed as he glanced around. "This forest... it's a decent size, but it's far too small for an Ogre to live in."

Lee Sungmin silently agreed. Although he had never faced an Ogre, he possessed some basic knowledge about them. They were among the most powerful bipedal monsters, the kind of creature that would become the apex predator of any forest it inhabited. So why would one be here? The woods were fairly wide but not particularly large, hardly a suitable habitat for a monster of its scale.

"Ogres are omnivores," Rudd said, striding forward. "They eat everything that moves, and things that don't move too. They aren't picky, but they still have preferences. It's like us, you know? Humans are omnivores, but we prefer meat. Ogres are the same."

He stopped in front of a tree that had been snapped and toppled, his frown deepening as he studied the splintered wood. This was not the work of a woodcutter's axe.

"Ogres are big. They're nearly three meters tall, and some are even bigger depending on the individual. With a body that large, they eat a lot... and there just doesn't seem to be enough food in this forest to fill an Ogre's stomach."

Rudd's expression was troubled. Omnivorous Ogres preferred meat, and they did not discriminate between the flesh of a human or an Orc. They ate common beasts and would even turn on their own kind. That was simply the nature of monsters.

"The subjugation request came in yesterday, didn't it?" Lee Sungmin asked.

"That's right. They said the Ogre was first spotted two days ago," Rudd confirmed with a low mutter. "It's really strange. This forest doesn't have any monster settlements, like for Orcs or Goblins, and you know, Ogres are very... smart. They're ferocious, but they don't usually attack humans."

Rudd was explaining what he knew, seeing Lee Sungmin as a greenhorn who, despite his skill, was still an inexperienced mercenary. His perspective was not wrong. Lee Sungmin knew little about high-level monsters like Ogres, so he listened attentively as they walked.

"Of course," Rudd continued, "if you come face-to-face with one, it's not some merciful beast that'll just ignore you and go on its way. What I mean is, an Ogre won't be the first to attack a village. You know why? Because that would be a major incident. An Ogre knows damn well that if something like that happens, a proper Hunter will come for it. Even though this village is outside Behenger's walls, the people here are subjects of Behenger's lord. If a monster on the level of an Ogre attacks a village, the knight order will be dispatched to kill it. Even monsters don't want to die."

"But we aren't knights," Lee Sungmin pointed out.

"Right. We're mercenaries. We move for money, but the result is the same. We came here to kill the Ogre, after all. Even if it hasn't attacked the village."

"I'm not sure I follow what you mean by 'strange'."

"I mean it's weird," Rudd insisted. "There's not enough food here to fill an Ogre's stomach. There might be animals, but in the long run, this forest isn't a good territory for one to claim. The kind of forest an Ogre claims needs to be bigger than this, with more food it can eat without repercussions."

"Like Orcs or Goblins?"

"Exactly. A monster eating another monster isn't a reason for mercenaries or a knight order to get involved."

"Maybe it's just made this its temporary shelter?"

"That's even stranger," Rudd said with a frown. "There are no forests around here suitable for an Ogre's territory. I thought maybe an old Ogre lost a territorial dispute and fled here... but that's strange too. See, an Ogre that's lost a fight for territory has half given up on living. It attacks and eats anything, villages or whatever, but this one hasn't even touched the village."

"Could there be a reason for it?"

"I don't know the reason, and frankly, it's not our problem. This Ogre is a strange one, like it just sprouted from the ground, but what we need to think about isn't 'why' it's here, but 'how' to catch and kill it."

Lee Sungmin nodded, his hand already resting on the spear slung across his back. This was his first time fighting an Ogre, and it would be a lie to say he was not nervous. It was not a tension born of fear, however. The emotion he felt was closer to a heart-pounding excitement. This subjugation was his first real battle since reaching the peak realm. He had hunted a few beasts and killed monsters on his way to Behenger, but those could hardly be called true fights.

*It's been three years.*

As they tracked the Ogre's trail, following its giant footprints and the path of broken trees, a sense of unease began to creep over Lee Sungmin.

"This is strange," he muttered.

Rudd nodded in agreement. "Right. It's strange."

"This thing... doesn't it shit?"

"It does," Rudd replied, his brow furrowed in thought. "If it's eating. An Ogre is an apex predator in most places. They're bold. They don't hide their droppings. In fact, they use them to mark their territory, but... while there are signs of an Ogre, I don't see any droppings."

It was not just the droppings that were missing. There were no signs of any meals either. Instead, they found another clue.

It was the corpse of a deer, horribly torn to pieces. It looked less like a corpse and more like a dismantled pile of meat, ripped apart by an Ogre's characteristic brute strength. Lee Sungmin and Rudd stared down at the remains.

"He didn't eat it," Rudd said.

"Why not?"

There were no signs of consumption on the mangled corpse. Not a single bite mark. It had simply been ripped apart. They continued on, finding more bodies, the remains of various beasts haphazardly torn to shreds. The grotesque sight of entrails hanging from the stump of a broken tree greeted them.

"Strange," Rudd muttered again. He had subjugated Ogres before, three of them in fact, but none had been this bizarre. "Is it even an Ogre?"

As he spoke, Lee Sungmin felt it on his skin. A sudden shiver ran down his spine. Before his ears heard a sound or his nose caught a scent, he simply *felt* it, a warning from the sixth sense he had honed on Mount Mushii.

Lee Sungmin immediately drew his spear. Rudd had not sensed what he had, but he did not question the action. He instantly stepped back and drew his own sword.

THUD.

Only after the feeling came the sound. It was the heavy thud of something moving, a footstep that grew closer. THUD, THUD.

CRACK, CRASH! A tree collapsed from a sweeping blow. Where could such a giant have been hiding? Rudd and Lee Sungmin swallowed their shock and stared as the Ogre revealed itself. No, could that even be called an Ogre?

"...What is that...?" Rudd breathed.

The creature that appeared was strange, its appearance bizarre. Its build and features were undoubtedly those of an Ogre, but it was different. Its huge mouth was sewn shut with something like a thick thread pulled tightly around its lips, and it was not just the mouth. Marks covered its body, as if it had been patched together like a rag.

Rudd swallowed hard. Looking closer, he saw its body was different from a normal Ogre's. Its right and left hands were different shapes. The right was a thick Ogre's hand, but the left had thin fingers and long nails. Lee Sungmin saw the thread embedded in its left wrist. The shape of the muscles, the color of the skin, it was all slightly different, and its eyes. The Ogre's two eyes were different colors.

"...Chimera?" Lee Sungmin muttered.

At the word, Rudd's shoulders trembled. A Chimera. A monster made by combining other monsters. Such a creature, of course, did not occur naturally. A Chimera was a product of magic research.

"Let's run," Rudd said in a small voice.

A Chimera based on an Ogre. He had never even heard of such a thing. If that creature was truly an Ogre Chimera, their chances of winning were slim. Even as an S-Rank Hunter with the skills of a peak realm master, Rudd had no confidence he could win.

Chimeras underwent various magic treatments. The very goal of the research was to create the 'ultimate of a species'. A single species had clear limits, and the conditions to evolve beyond them were insufficient. That was why Chimera research began, an effort to artificially reach a species' ultimate form by mixing it with others. In other words, all Chimeras were creatures that had undergone procedures to reach that ultimate state. Even a simple Orc Chimera was only based on an Orc. Its strength varied greatly depending on the procedures performed. A Chimera based on an Ogre, a species that was already an apex predator... that was not a monster an S-Rank Mercenary could handle.

"KUUUUUUH!"

The Ogre let out a tremendous roar, but its tightly bound lips muffled the sound into a rumbling growl. Only then did they understand why it had not eaten. It was not a choice. It could not. With its mouth shut, it was unable to eat, and it must have torn its prey apart out of sheer frustration.

Hunger turned to ferocity. The Ogre began to run. They had to escape. Just as Rudd had said, Lee Sungmin took a step back, but the Ogre's charge was faster than either of them could retreat.

Its movements registered in Lee Sungmin's wide eyes as it swung a giant fist. The heavy, violent blow aimed for his body. If it connected, every bone would shatter, his organs would burst, and he would die instantly.

*I'll die.*

*Here?*

The ascetic training on the mountain flashed through his mind. The steps he had taken every single day registered in his consciousness. Was it a conscious act or an unconscious one? *I have to do it.* With that thought, Lee Sungmin moved.

This was not Mount Mushii.

Lee Sungmin's body was not heavy.

Traceless Step.

Lee Sungmin's body vanished. Behind him, Rudd's eyes widened in shock.

And in the space of a heartbeat, Lee Sungmin was inside the Ogre's guard.
