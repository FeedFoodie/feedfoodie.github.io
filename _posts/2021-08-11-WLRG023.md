Lee Sungmin clamped his lips shut, glaring at Wiji Hoyeon as he tried to process her words. A confident expression remained on her face as she continued.

"You told me yourself. In your past life, I survived for thirteen years and made a name for myself. Isn't that enough? It means I won't die for at least the next thirteen years."

"You don't know that."

"No. I won't die," she insisted. "I can't die. I have no intention of enjoying this freedom I've finally gained for only thirteen years. I will live for a long, long time."

Her words were filled with a conviction so strong it felt like a premonition. After speaking, Wiji Hoyeon fixed her gaze on him again.

"You are like a small boat adrift on the sea without a destination. You're just sailing along with the blowing wind and the flowing sea, without a port of call. No, should that even be called sailing? That's just drifting."

He couldn't deny it. Lee Sungmin was well aware of how faint his sense of purpose was. To be precise, he was simply afraid to create one. His previous thirteen years had given him experience, but they had also shackled him with limits. Knowing reality, knowing the future, had become his cage. Lee Sungmin had no talent. He knew that. He knew that even if he practiced martial arts for another ten years, he would remain a Second-Rate martial artist.

"Because you're just drifting aimlessly, you can't move forward properly," she said. "That's why you need a goal. A goal called 'me'."

It was a convincing argument. Wiji Hoyeon was arguably the strongest of all the otherworlders he had ever heard of in his past life. He wondered if he could ever reach the tip of her toes, even if he dedicated his entire life to training. That very thought was what made him so negative.

"Just because I make you my goal, it doesn't mean I can become like you..."

"Have you tried?" Wiji Hoyeon spat out. "Please. Don't talk without even trying."

"You're the one being irresponsible!" Lee Sungmin's words were laced with venom. "I can't. I'm not able to, because I have no talent. You and I have different starting lines. You were born with a calamity-level talent and received all sorts of support. You must have gorged on miracle herbs and lived in a place overflowing with divine arts. Isn't that how you became the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon?"

This was the essence of the person named Wiji Hoyeon, a truth Lee Sungmin had denied, ignored, and wanted to disregard.

"But I'm not," he continued, his words turning into a bitter introspection. "I had nothing. It was the same in my past life. I was summoned to this world with nothing, with bare hands, without any talent. In your past life, you graduated from this city and left in a month. I lived here for three years. I steeled my resolve against rabbits and boars, risked my life against Goblins, and ran from Orcs in fear. I lived like that for three years before leaving, and in the next city, I became a mercenary and did odd jobs to survive."

Wiji Hoyeon did not argue. She simply listened, her expression calm as he laid his story bare.

"That's me. A Second-Rate martial artist after thirteen years. A C-Rank Mercenary. Then I died, and now I've started over. A person like me..."

"The you of now is a Second-Rate martial artist," Wiji Hoyeon finally said, her voice cutting through his despair. "The you who was a Second-Rate martial artist after thirteen years became one in just three months after starting over. You're right. I was born a calamity and received a lot of support, but what about you now? You've learned the Violet Dawn Divine Art. You've received twenty percent of my inner qi. You will also learn spearmanship."

She waved a hand dismissively.

"Is this not enough? If so, I'll give you more. What should I teach you? Do you want external arts? Or movement arts? How about fist arts or palm arts? Tell me. If there's something you want, I will teach you."

"...I don't want it," Lee Sungmin replied. The Spear Technique and the Violet Dawn Divine Art were already more than enough. He lacked the confidence to learn anything more.

"You've gained a miraculous encounter yourself. Even if you can't help your innate talent, you aren't desperate and you don't try because you lack a sense of purpose. Isn't it a waste to spend your second life, which you've painstakingly gotten back, in such a way?"

Wiji Hoyeon sighed and picked up the cup she had set down, gulping the milk inside.

"Well. Maybe this is just me meddling. Unnecessary interference. It's your life, so there's no need for me to arbitrarily create a goal for you. I'm sorry. I said something I shouldn't have..."

"Shut up," Lee Sungmin spat, clenching his fists so tightly his knuckles turned white. Every word she had uttered was a barb stuck in his chest. As he cut her off, Wiji Hoyeon's eyes widened in surprise.

"Are you that angry? I was properly apologizing..."

"Yeah. I'm angry. To think I'd get lectured like this by a thirteen-year-old brat."

His pride was hurt, that much was a given. However, what truly angered him was the thought, *I'll make you my goal.* He glared at Wiji Hoyeon, but his fury was directed inward. What truly angered Lee Sungmin was himself, for being unable to refute most of what she had said. It was himself, trembling with petty pride while agreeing with every word.

"I'll make you my goal, and even if you leave, I'll keep training my martial arts. When I meet you again, I'll stick a spear in your chest..."

"No, you can't do that," Wiji Hoyeon immediately retorted. "I'd die. How could you do that to a friend?"

A reluctant expression crossed Lee Sungmin's face as he changed his words.

"...Then... uh... I'll aim to defeat you..."

"Hmm. A duel should be fine," she said, finally seeming to understand. "I'll be looking forward to it."

A bright smile bloomed on her face. Lee Sungmin stared at it blankly for a moment.

*This is crazy.*

To think his heart had just skipped a beat at a thirteen-year-old girl's smile. *Isn't this a little dangerous?* He quickly turned his gaze away, avoiding the sight of her smiling face.

{sep}

After that day, Lee Sungmin stopped going out to the forest. He had enough money anyway. His wallet was full from the Mercenary Guild's reward, not to mention the money he had taken after killing the Classless Hunter. He also had the funds he had been diligently saving for his future travel expenses.

"Your fundamentals are surprisingly sloppy," Wiji Hoyeon remarked. "It must be because you never had a Master who taught you properly."

When Lee Sungmin stopped his forest excursions, Wiji Hoyeon began to instruct him actively. In the afternoons, she would overhaul the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear, and in the mornings, she would teach him the basics of spearmanship.

"They say the sword is the King of All Weapons, but that's just something sword-wielding bastards spout out of vanity. Most people who've learned a bit of martial arts acknowledge the truth. The spear is the weapon worthy of being the King of All Weapons. It's extremely tricky to face."

"......"

Wiji Hoyeon's spear cut through the air with a light swing.

"So it's good to solidify your fundamentals."

Lee Sungmin had no choice but to agree. It was an unavoidable fact that his fundamentals were sloppy, a natural consequence of having never learned from a teacher. He had mastered spearmanship through the contents of the Soul Chasing Spear Art and the harsh reality of actual combat.

"The spears of the Central Plains are largely divided into four parts," she explained. "The blade at the end is the spearhead. The tassel below it is the spear tassel. The pole is the shear shaft, and the short metal piece on the opposite end is the Changjun."

BWOONG!

She swung the spear in a wide arc. The red spear tassel below the spearhead fluttered along with the trajectory.

"The spear tassel isn't just a simple decoration. It also serves to mesmerize an opponent's eyes. Watch."

Wiji Hoyeon swung the spear repeatedly. The red tassel scattered chaotically, its limp swaying a stark contrast to the spear's straight, deadly line. Its deep red color couldn't help but draw one's gaze.

"If you want to handle a spear properly, you must use all of it. Use the Changjun as you would the spearhead. Mesmerize with the spear tassel. Adjust your distance by changing the position of your hands on the shear shaft."

Lee Sungmin nodded blankly. After a few more swings, Wiji Hoyeon assumed a proper stance.

"The fundamentals of Central Plains spear thrusts are largely divided into three. These are used as the basics in renowned Spear Techniques like the Taiji Spear or the Yang Family Spear, and are even used in Baji Quan. It's not even a matter of all rivers flowing into the sea. This is just the most basic of basics that can be applied to all martial arts. They are Lan, Na, and Chal."

Lan was an outward spin, and Na was an inward spin. The outward spin of Lan deflected an attack, while the inward spin of Na coiled around one.

"The important thing is that you must not use only your arms. Using only your arms won't put proper power into it."

Chal was a thrust.

"The length of the spear creates that much power. Change your grip on the shear shaft. If you only use the middle, you won't get proper power."

"...Yeah."

"However, you can't say that Rannachal are the fundamentals for the entire weapon. This is, after all, only for thrusting. The spear has various methods of attack besides thrusting. A spear is also a blunt weapon."

Wiji Hoyeon demonstrated a few more movements. Then she handed the spear to Lee Sungmin, took a few steps back, and clasped her hands behind her back.

"Try it."

"...What?"

"Didn't I show you? Try it."

"If I could perfectly copy it after seeing it just once, do you think I would've whined about having no talent?"

"When did I ever tell you to do it perfectly?" Wiji Hoyeon grumbled at his response. "Just try it. You won't know until you do."

Lee Sungmin raised the spear. At that moment, Wiji Hoyeon added, "Don't use your inner qi."

Without drawing on his inner qi, Lee Sungmin performed the spear fundamentals she had taught him. Wiji Hoyeon watched him quietly for a moment before speaking.

"Again."

After that, it was again, and again, and again. Wiji Hoyeon kept repeating that single word, and Lee Sungmin kept swinging the spear. He didn't use his inner qi. His body quickly grew tired, dripping with sweat as his breathing became ragged.

"Again."

"...Until when?"

"Until it becomes second nature to your hands."

"You think that's going to happen after just a few times!"

"That's why you have to keep doing it," Wiji Hoyeon said, her face indifferent. "From now on, practice only that, every day."

In those words, Lee Sungmin saw the image of a demon. He shuddered but swung the spear again. It was so hard he felt like he was going to die. He was swinging purely with the strength of his body, and the spear wasn't exactly light, nor did the fundamentals feel easy.

"You can't collapse. If you really think you can't do it because it's too hard, then rest. If you have the energy, swing some more."

Her words felt like a test of his grit. The one to judge whether he could do more was none other than himself. After repeating the movements about ten more times, Lee Sungmin truly felt like he was going to die. His breathing was a ragged mess and his limbs, no, his entire body ached. He wanted to drink water. He wanted to sit down and rest. He wanted to feel the breeze in the shade.

He could see Wiji Hoyeon's face, sitting comfortably in that very shade. Lee Sungmin bit his lower lip hard.

He could do more. He felt like he was going to die from exhaustion right now, but if he squeezed out every last bit of strength, he felt he could manage a few more swings.

So he swung more.
