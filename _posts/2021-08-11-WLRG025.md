"......"

In his past life and even now, the weapon Lee Sungmin chose was the spear. He had first picked it up for a simple reason. It seemed safe. He thought he could just cowardly poke and jab from a distance, too scared of getting close enough to swing a sword.

Though his reasoning had been born of fear, Lee Sungmin grew to like the spear. He had used it for a long time, and it felt familiar in his hands. The more familiar it became, the more he felt suffocated by the weapon's complexity, yet he still chose the spear again in this life. He could not imagine using any other weapon. He had no confidence he could get used to a new one at this point, and he refused to give up the Soul Chasing Spear Art, even if it was only a Second-Rate martial art.

Yes. It was stubbornness.

When he regressed, his inner qi had not carried over. All Lee Sungmin brought with him was the experience of surviving in Eria for thirteen years and his deep understanding of spearmanship. He did not want to give that up. Those memories were proof that the man named Lee Sungmin had survived in this damn world.

His fourteen-year-old body had been too young to properly execute the spearmanship he remembered, but he had grown used to it now. As for inner qi, thanks to the Violet Dawn Divine Art and the twenty percent he received from Wiji Hoyeon, Lee Sungmin now possessed even more than he had in his past life. On top of that, his spearmanship had also improved by leaps and bounds after Wiji Hoyeon coached him on the fundamentals he lacked. She taught him many things, not just how to wield a spear.

In this new life, the greatest miracle Lee Sungmin had encountered was undoubtedly meeting Wiji Hoyeon. She had taught him the Violet Dawn Divine Art and passed on a fifth of her own inner qi. She had taught him the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear and so many other things besides. Among all the memories Lee Sungmin possessed, no one had ever taught him as much as she had, and she never demanded a great price for her teachings.

Wiji Hoyeon was an exceptional Master. If she had allowed it, Lee Sungmin would have performed the Nine Bows to her and officially become her disciple. Wiji Hoyeon, however, did not permit it. They were friends. That was the reason she gave.

*20 moves.*

That was the number of moves Wiji Hoyeon had conceded. She would not counterattack until he finished his twenty attacks. All she could do was block or evade. It was a meaningless handicap. If their skills were similar, or if he was only slightly inferior, he might have been able to take his opponent's life or decide the match within twenty moves.

The gap between Lee Sungmin and Wiji Hoyeon was not like that at all. An overwhelming, absolute difference existed between them. Even knowing this, Lee Sungmin did not move rashly. His attacks would be meaningless. They would never reach her.

So what?

*What should I use for the first form?*

How should he make the first move? It would not reach her anyway, but Lee Sungmin was cautious. He intended to do his best, to attack with the intent to kill Wiji Hoyeon.

The Soul Chasing Spear Art had three forms. The First was One Strike, One Kill. The Second was Reverse Flow Kill. The Third was the Crushing Thunder Strike. None of them were particularly complex, and all three had been incorporated directly into the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear (Sungmin Style) that Wiji Hoyeon had modified for him.

He could not win through the complexity of his forms. Since Wiji Hoyeon was the one who created his style, he had to assume she knew everything about the techniques he used. Lee Sungmin slowly dragged his feet across the ground. Wiji Hoyeon, who had been standing with her hands clasped behind her back, raised only her right hand and held it out before her.

It was early dawn, and the wind was a bit chilly. A sparrow in a nearby tree chirped.

SHWIIK!

The spear shot out from Lee Sungmin's hands. It was the thrust from the sweep, parry, and thrust combination Wiji Hoyeon had taught him. No, it was not a simple thrust. This blow was a single strike from the Supreme Nine Heavens Spear (Sungmin Style). Soul Chasing Elimination. The strike, filled with a fierce rotation, was amplified by the elasticity drawn from his entire body and his inner qi. Aided by his energy release method, the blow shot toward Wiji Hoyeon's chest in an instant.

TAK!

Wiji Hoyeon's hand moved. She lightly flicked the right hand she held near her chest, completely nullifying his Soul Chasing Elimination.

"Next," she muttered.

Lee Sungmin did not get impatient. He had expected this result. His feet moved, though there was no need to advance. The spear's reach was already secured.

*Ah, this is.*

He cut off his breath and thrust the spear again. Stabbing, swinging, striking. He remained faithful to the fundamentals Wiji Hoyeon had taught him and diligently followed the oral formulas of his spear style. The technique, which combined fundamentals with practical combat application, could be said to have already surpassed the level of Second-Rate, but it was useless against Wiji Hoyeon. She did not retreat, moving only her right hand. Her eyes moved faster than his spear, confirming its trajectory and predicting its destination, perfecting her defense. Her hands were small, as befitting a thirteen-year-old girl, but the sharp metal of the spearhead that Lee Sungmin thrust with all his might could not leave a single scratch on her palm.

That was the difference. Lee Sungmin had been practicing martial arts for less than half a year. Even with the great progress he had made thanks to Wiji Hoyeon, he was at best a First-Rate martial artist. Wiji Hoyeon, on the other hand, was born with calamitous talent and had been exposed to various miracle herbs and martial arts from a young age. Though she was young, her understanding and experience of martial arts far surpassed the time Lee Sungmin had lived and remembered.

That was why the result was obvious.

"...I lost," Lee Sungmin muttered, gasping for breath.

He had used the twenty moves Wiji Hoyeon had conceded to repeat the best attacks he could think of.

"You did well."

Unlike Lee Sungmin, who was exhausted and dripping with sweat, Wiji Hoyeon showed no signs of fatigue. She dusted off her right hand as she approached him. During those twenty moves, he had launched twenty attacks, and every single one was deflected by that hand.

When the twenty moves were over, Wiji Hoyeon had moved. Her approach must have been a movement arts technique, for he could not perceive it. He could, however, feel it faintly. A feeling that he should not just stand still.

"Your final judgment was good," Wiji Hoyeon said, placing a hand on his heaving shoulder and smiling brightly. "Thanks to that, I had to use one more strike."

Her body had been caught in the trajectory of his reckless final thrust. To avoid it, she had to use an extra move.

"You're perceptive," she added. It was a sincere compliment. "Beyond experience, this could be considered an innate talent. To try and perceive the unseen, and to succeed in doing so, that can already be called a gift. Someday, it will become an excellent weapon in your arsenal. I'm looking forward to ten years from now."

"...But I still lost."

"Of course. I'm the Little Heavenly Demon Wiji Hoyeon. There's no way I'd lose here." She laughed loudly, her expression somewhat refreshed. Perhaps leaving the city where she had stayed for several months gave her a sense of liberation. Lee Sungmin, on the other hand, felt a little stifled.

*What a shame.*

He was feeling a sense of loss. Come to think of it, in his past life, there had not been a single person he had been so open with for so long. The Lee Sungmin of his past life had no friends, and his sense of camaraderie was faint. He had mercenary comrades, but after going through all sorts of hardships and bullshit for being Classless, he had never thought of them as true comrades. There was no trust in such relationships. Friendship was out of the question.

However, Wiji Hoyeon was different. Compared to the calculative Lee Sungmin, she was pure. Among all the types of people he had met and experienced, she was an unusual one. Just as she had called him her first friend, Wiji Hoyeon was his first friend as well. That was why he felt this loss at the thought of parting with her. He never thought he would feel such an emotion, and it left him a little flustered.

"Why the long face?" Wiji Hoyeon asked, tilting her head.

Lee Sungmin let out a deep sigh and roughly wiped the sweat streaming down his face. "It's because I'll miss this."

"Really? Same here." She let out a small laugh and turned around, shaking her sleeves. As she picked up the bag she had tossed under a tree, she continued, "I'm sad to be parting with you. If I had my way, I'd want to keep traveling with you."

"Then why don't you?"

"I think I already told you why. For your sake, and for mine."

"I understand," Lee Sungmin said. "I was just whining a bit."

"Haha! When you think about it, isn't your mental age twenty-seven? A twenty-seven-year-old whining to a thirteen-year-old like me... It's a little disgusting when I imagine it."

Disgusting? That was taking it a bit too far, but no, Wiji Hoyeon was always like this. Sometimes her words were too direct, too vicious and severe.

"It's a shame, but we have to part," she said. "Just as you have things you must do, I have things I want to do, and by parting, we can look forward to ten years from now."

"...Look forward to?"

"Yes. I'm very much looking forward to it. That fighting spirit duel just now was easy. Too easy, but I was satisfied with your skill. You performed as much as I expected while I was teaching you. That last move, in particular, was quite good."

"It was just a coincidence."

"Your job is to make that move no longer a coincidence. Huhu! I'm looking forward to ten years from now. Well, I'll still win then, of course." Wiji Hoyeon laughed as she lifted the bag onto her back. Lee Sungmin watched her with a sullen expression.

"Don't be sad. We can meet in ten years."

"Ten years is a long time."

"Suddenly you're whining again. Why? Have you developed a desire to copulate with me?" she asked, looking back at him.

Lee Sungmin blinked, staring at her. Silence followed. He did not understand what she meant by the word 'copulate'.

"Co... what?"

"Hmm. You're making me repeat an embarrassing word. In other words, I'm asking if you want to make a child with me," she explained, stroking her chin.

Lee Sungmin's jaw dropped. "Wha-what?"

"If not, then never mind. Then again, you're too young for that sort of thing. No. Or are you?" she muttered, tilting her head.

Wiji Hoyeon appeared nonchalant, but Lee Sungmin's face flushed bright red. His lips moved wordlessly for a moment before he suddenly yelled.

"You little brat, is there anything you won't say!"

"Haha! It's a joke, a joke. Isn't this the kind of joke friends can make?"

"No!"

"Really? I didn't know that. This is my first time having a friend. I'll be careful from now on," Wiji Hoyeon said with a laugh. She shifted the bag on her back once and walked over to him. "Well, but with you, I think it might be okay. You're quite amusing to watch. Yes... spend the next ten years thinking about that. About how big my breasts will get in ten years."

"I won't!"

"Why not? It's not like they'll wear out just from you thinking about them. To be honest, I'm curious too. In the past life you lived, I was known as a man, right? That would mean my breasts won't grow much if things continue as they are... Huhu! It might be quite fun to grow them out before I meet you. You'd be so surprised."

"Crazy..." Lee Sungmin, at a loss for words, muttered a curse.

Wiji Hoyeon patted his shoulder lightly as she walked past him. "See you in ten years. Central Plaza in Ruvus, March 14th. I'll remember."

"...Yeah."

"Don't die."

She left him with those words as if it were a passing remark. Lee Sungmin took them to heart more than anything else.

"I won't die."

He looked back and saw Wiji Hoyeon's back as she left the inn's courtyard.

"I'll never die," he muttered, as if casting a spell on himself.

And so, Wiji Hoyeon left, promising they would meet again in ten years.
