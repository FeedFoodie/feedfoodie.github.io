It had been some time since Lee Sungmin had last seen a corpse, certainly not during his stay in Shaolin. He approached the body sprawled on the ground, recognizing the robes of Mount Hua, but the state of the man within them stopped him cold.

"What… is this?" he muttered, bewildered.

A deep sword wound gaped across the man's chest, but that was not the true horror. The body was shriveled like a mummy. As Lee Sungmin bent to take a closer look, Kim Jonghyun's voice cut through the air.

"Don't touch it."

"……"

Lee Sungmin froze. He had no intention of touching it anyway, not a corpse so bizarre, but he stepped back as Kim Jonghyun moved forward to examine the body.

"Indeed." Kim Jonghyun stroked his chin, nodding as he took in the sight. "So they said the Sword Phantom uses evil arts… Huhu! I thought it was something simple, like hallucinations in personal combat, but it seems it wasn't so simple after all. Indeed. This certainly makes him worthy of the name 'the Sword Phantom'."

"What do you mean?"

"You know of Energy Drain, don't you?"

"I do."

"It takes many forms. A vampire's absorption of acupoints is a type of Energy Drain, and among martial arts, that… what is it… the Star Absorption Technique is also an Energy Drain. The methods vary as much as the types. However, the bottom line is this. It's the act of stealing from an opponent and making it your own."

Saying this, Kim Jonghyun lifted the shriveled corpse's arm.

"I'm not sure what method of Energy Drain the Sword Phantom uses, but… know this. He's growing stronger with every passing moment."

With those words, Kim Jonghyun straightened up.

"We need to change our method of pursuit. Until now, I've been tracking him with my familiars, and the Daoists of Mount Hua have been pursuing him based on that information. Continuing like this will only give the Sword Phantom more power. We have to corner him and finish him off for good."

The words he had exchanged with the Sword Phantom yesterday flashed through Lee Sungmin's mind. *Have you ever hit a wall?* The Sword Phantom had hit a wall and felt Despair. Instead of collapsing, he had chosen to overcome that wall using a method other than the sword. Lee Sungmin felt no desire to blame the man, for he had no right.

They began to move again, and soon found that the bodies were not limited to Mount Hua's Daoists. They kept appearing.

"The Sword Phantom has crossed the line. He's crossed far too many lines." Kim Jonghyun's voice was grim. "This will no longer end as a simple matter between him and Mount Hua."

There was the corpse of a robed mage. The corpse of an armored mercenary. The corpses of Information Guild members in their tight-fitting black clothes.

"I really don't know what gives him this confidence," Kim Jonghyun muttered. "Does he think he can handle the fallout? Or is he just thrashing about after being cornered? Ultimately, if he keeps this up, only destruction awaits him."

Lee Sungmin couldn't help but agree. The Sword Phantom was heading for ruin. If he had succeeded in killing only Daoist Seongha and escaping, he would have become the arch-enemy of Mount Hua, pursued for the rest of his life, but the situation had already escalated far beyond that. The Mages Guild had suffered considerable losses, as had the Mercenary Guild and the Information Guild.

"Even if he's lucky enough to escape this place, the Sword Phantom will no longer be able to live in Eria."

As he spoke, Kim Jonghyun moved his hand. A crow circling high above descended, drawing near his ear. Kim Jonghyun whispered softly to the bird, and the moment his lips closed, it flapped its wings powerfully and soared back into the air.

"He's nearby."

After traveling for some time, Kim Jonghyun came to a halt. The sun was already beginning to set. Fist-sized black spheres floated in the air around him as he stood with his back to the sunset.

"We traveled much faster, so it'll take a little more time for the Daoists of Mount Hua to arrive. To be honest, I played a little trick with the information I sent. Made them arrive a bit later."

"Why would you do that?"

"Didn't you want to meet the Sword Phantom?" Kim Jonghyun bared his teeth in a smile. "I'm arranging for you to meet him. I'm curious, you see. I'm curious about what you'll do when you finally meet him, and I wonder how the flow of events, which started to change because of you, will be affected when you two meet again. Though I suppose I can't be an Observer."

Lee Sungmin couldn't fully understand everything he was saying, but he could sense Kim Jonghyun's profound interest in the matter.

The Sword Phantom was standing there motionlessly. Lee Sungmin had expected a frantic fugitive, not this still figure. He took a silent step forward.

"It seems you haven't learned the art of stealth," the Sword Phantom muttered, his back still turned.

Lee Sungmin flinched, freezing in his tracks.

"There's no need to be surprised. Though, I never thought you'd be the one to come here."

"…Were you waiting for me?"

"That's right. I remember your presence. If someone… else had come, I wouldn't have waited so calmly."

The Sword Phantom turned, and the expression on his face was surprisingly composed.

"Have you come to kill me, too?" he asked.

Lee Sungmin hesitated to answer. Should he reach for the spear on his back? Seeing his hand clench and unclench, the Sword Phantom didn't press the question. *That's right.* Lee Sungmin realized. He was the Sword Phantom. The man standing before him was not the martial artist named Dok Bijun, but the one called the Sword Phantom.

"…I have something to ask."

"This isn't exactly the right place for a chat, and my situation isn't the best, but…" the Sword Phantom trailed off, glancing behind him. The sun was setting. "It shouldn't… matter. It'll be night soon."

"If," Lee Sungmin began, struggling for the words. "What if I… hadn't met you, Elder? If I hadn't met you at the gates of Mount Hua." He had to ask. "If I hadn't told you… why Daoist Seongha was refusing the duel. What… would you have done?"

"You ask a strange question," the Sword Phantom murmured, tilting his head. Despite calling it strange, he didn't answer right away. After a moment of gathering his thoughts, he spoke.

"If that had been the case, I would have given up on my duel with Daoist Seongha."

Hearing that, Lee Sungmin wasn't sure what kind of expression to make.

"I would have thought, 'I'm not yet worthy of fighting a Sect Leader of the Nine Sects One Gang,' and backed down," the Sword Phantom continued. "But… then I heard it from you. The reason why Daoist Seongha refused to duel me. It wasn't about worthiness. It was the fact that I use evil arts. That was the important part."

He lifted his head to look at the sky as night began to fall.

"If that was the reason, then no matter how much time passed, I would never have been able to fight him. That's why I crossed the gates of Mount Hua in the dead of night and, knowing it was rude, I challenged him to a fight. When he refused and only scolded me for my insolence… I simply committed an even greater insolence."

"Why did you kill him?"

"A sword has no eyes. Daoist Seongha was strong. He was an opponent I couldn't possibly defeat without using killing techniques, but… but, you see."

The Sword Phantom lowered his gaze from the sky. The expression on his face sent a horrifying chill through Lee Sungmin.

He was smiling. It was a smile of pure joy, of utter fulfillment, as if nothing could make him happier.

"The fight with Daoist Seongha was enjoyable," the Sword Phantom said with a nod. "I realized how vast the world is. Of all the opponents I've ever fought, he was the strongest. It was a sudden battle, but it was worth it. I'm sure Daoist Seongha died satisfied, too."

His words were distorted, twisted by a madness that held not a single trace of reason. Hearing them, Lee Sungmin felt his stomach churn as if he had swallowed poison.

It was because of him. Because he had spoken, Daoist Seongha, who should not have died, was now dead.

"But why do you ask such things?"

Lee Sungmin did not answer. The hand that had been clenching and unclenching, debating whether to grab his spear, now moved decisively behind his back. His fingers closed around the weapon's shaft.

"Daoist Seongha died because of me."

"You're saying strange things. I'm the one who killed him. My sword killed him."

"If I hadn't met you, Elder, Daoist Seongha wouldn't have died."

"Haha! That's ridiculous. The ways of the world are impossible to predict. Why should you feel any responsibility for this? You and I meeting at the gates of Mount Hua was a coincidence."

"Not for me," Lee Sungmin insisted, bringing his spear forward.

The Sword Phantom watched him, his eyes narrowing. "You… you're overestimating yourself. I admit your skill is exceptional for your age, but you can't defeat me. Though I may Despair at the limits of my own vessel, I have still overcome the wall of the Supreme Peak. Do you, who haven't even reached that threshold, believe you can strike me down?"

"…I know. I can't defeat you."

Lee Sungmin wasn't overconfident. On the contrary, he knew himself better than anyone, and his pessimism fueled a strong sense of self-loathing. At the same time, he was afraid of death. Knowing what it was made him fear it all the more.

Nevertheless, Lee Sungmin did not back down. The reason was simple.

Responsibility.

He couldn't dress up this paltry emotion in any plausible justification. Throughout the life he had lived so far, there must have been other instances just like this. Since he was living a life different from his past one, people had surely been swept up by his actions and diverted from their original course. Because he had claimed the Holy Spirit Pill in Zenavis, the Classless who was meant to have it, did not. Because he had found the martial arts manuals in the old bookstore first, someone else who was supposed to have them, did not.

There was no end to it. In the eight years he had lived, countless people had surely been forced to live different lives because of him.

*So what?*

He knew there was no end to thinking like that. He knew it was a contradiction to ignore them. What did it matter? It was just as Kim Jonghyun had said. In the end, it was all for self-satisfaction. Lee Sungmin knew that his current actions and the thoughts he harbored were nothing more than a contradictory act of self-satisfaction. An impulsive act that could not be explained logically or packaged neatly, done simply 'because he wanted to'.

Admitting it was, strangely, a relief. He faced this paltry sense of responsibility head-on. He had wanted to meet the Sword Phantom and talk, but there was no need for that anymore. He now knew that the Sword Phantom's actions had diverged from the past because of his own existence, and because of that, Daoist Seongha was dead.

Therefore.

He had to take responsibility.

[Are you planning to fight the Sword Phantom? I'm telling you now, I can't help you. I'm not very skilled in combat.]

[I'm going to buy time.]

He lowered his stance. The Sword Phantom watched blankly as Lee Sungmin leveled his spear, then raised a hand to the sword at his waist.

[I'll buy time until the Daoists of Mount Hua arrive.]

[Well… if that's what you're going to do. Good luck.]

Kim Jonghyun didn't try to stop Lee Sungmin. On the contrary, he seemed to be enjoying his actions.

Then, the Sword Phantom drew his sword.

"I don't run from a fight that comes to me," he muttered.

Lee Sungmin charged forward.
