*The Demonic Coliseum should be destroyed?*

The statement hit like a thunderclap. Anyone from the Divine Cult would be shocked to say or hear such words.

Do Heon felt even more stunned because Lee Cheonsang had said them. He knew the man had changed, but never expected this.

"What do you mean?" he demanded.

Lee Cheonsang's voice carried deadly seriousness. "That place is rotten."

"Explain it."

"It is exactly what I said. People shouldn't belong to a place like that. They shouldn't fight that way either."

"......"

"And people shouldn't get excited about a gambling den filled with nothing but unwanted deaths."

Do Heon's eyes wavered. One shock after another crashed over him. His impression of Lee Cheonsang kept shifting. The man who'd been rigid as a stone statue was cracking before his eyes. "They shouldn't?"

"That's right."

"What made you think that way?"

"I already told you."

"I'm curious about what led you to see the Demonic Coliseum as rotten."

A slight frown creased Lee Cheonsang's forehead.

Do Heon's eyes widened. Another shock hit before the last one could fade.

*Did this guy even know how to make expressions like that?*

It was just a hairline crack on a boulder, barely an expression at all. Still, something peculiar flickered across his face.

*Is that anguish?*

After a moment, Lee Cheonsang shook his head. "Let's go."

He didn't give a proper answer.

Hwang Museok's gaze sharpened. He didn't know where this bastard had crawled from, but he'd never seen anyone so stiff-necked before the Bright Demon Commander.

Do Heon nodded readily. "You must be tired too. I've been talking too much. Go back, get some rest, and we can talk later."

The two men climbed into the carriage moments later. Hwang Museok took the driver's seat and gripped the reins. He couldn't shake the anger surging through him, or the even stronger curiosity that followed.

*Just who is that guy?*

When they arrived at the Bright Demon Pavilion, Lee Cheonsang went into the separate room Do Heon had prepared and immediately collapsed to rest. Do Heon offered to send for a physician, but Lee Cheonsang refused.

Do Heon didn't push further. In his eyes, the young man's condition wasn't serious. His wounds wouldn't worsen as long as he controlled his demonic qi properly.

After settling Lee Cheonsang into his room and showing him where to wash, Do Heon headed straight for his office.

"Vice Commander Hwang."

"Yes, Commander?"

"Tell the Black Demon Commander I want to see him. If he's not available now, later will do."

"Understood."

"Good." Do Heon had been staring out the window with his back pressed against his chair. Now he turned around. Hwang Museok hadn't moved. He remained in his respectful posture. "Is there something else?"

"Commander."

"Speak."

"This may overstep my bounds, but may I ask a few questions?"

Do Heon could easily guess what questions would come. He nodded. "Go ahead."

"That guy is..." Hwang Museok cleared his throat quickly. "Who is that man?"

"A demonic gladiator."

"......"

"Didn't you see for yourself? He was a demonic gladiator from Demonic Coliseum Squad B."

Hwang Museok stared at Do Heon with bewildered eyes.

Do Heon laughed softly. "I'm joking. Your posture is so rigid that a joke slipped out."

"Ah, I apologize."

"No need to apologize. He's a man I've taken personal interest in. Just know that."

"I understand."

The explanation felt unconvincing somehow. The upright and strict Commander tried to pull him from the Demonic Coliseum just because of simple interest?

Do Heon watched Hwang Museok, who was lost in thought, then spoke as if tossing out a casual comment. "And he's my benefactor."

"Pardon?"

"I said, he's my benefactor."

"B-Benefactor?"

"That's right."

"......"

"Thanks to him, I've learned a great deal. If it weren't for him, I'd still be living like a frog in a well, trapped by my own stubbornness and ego."

"Ah..."

"Don't think too deeply about it. He's a benefactor, but he's not some great figure I'd give my all to. He wouldn't want that either. However..." Do Heon stroked his chin. "He's someone I want by my side, in my own way."

Hwang Museok's eyes trembled as if an earthquake had struck.

Do Heon asked again. "Any more questions?"

"......"

"If not, then..."

"I'm neither a traitor nor a spy, Commander."

Do Heon tilted his head. "What do you mean?"

Hwang Museok felt flustered. The words had slipped out unconsciously. He hadn't expected to blurt out something like that without thinking.

The words were already out, so he spoke as respectfully as he could. "You're a great man, Commander. Your caliber is different from someone like me."

"Haha, my good man. Why the sudden flattery?"

"You must be very busy with various matters, and I'm the one who assists you, Commander."

"......"

"I serve no one other than you, Commander. I'll always live for you and the Bright Demon Company, so if there's anything you need to entrust to me, please tell me anytime."

Do Heon's calm gaze fell upon Hwang Museok.

Hwang Museok felt needless sweat break out on his back. He didn't regret his statement, however. A liege and his subordinate had to be bound by trust. There was no need to show that he felt slighted. It was important for a subordinate to constantly prove his own worth. It was also a subordinate's duty to always remind his superior that he wasn't a threat, that he was trustworthy.

The trust in him might have fallen because of the incident six months ago. That might be why he was receiving information about orders from above directly, instead of through his Vice Commander. If so, he had to regain that trust, no matter what.

Brief silence stretched between them.

Do Heon broke it. "Vice Commander Hwang."

"Yes, Commander?"

"You're doing well enough in your position. Regardless of our past relationship, I've never once doubted your abilities."

"I'm grateful."

"I'm well aware of your loyalty and pride for the main company even without you saying so."

"......"

"You may go now."

Hwang Museok bowed his head even lower. "I'll take my leave."

"Alright."

As Hwang Museok left the office, his steps seemed somewhat lighter.

Do Heon quietly gazed at the spot where Hwang Museok had been standing.

*It's understandable.*

He wasn't foolish enough to cast someone aside for a single mistake. He looked at the person, not their actions.

However, the head of an organization couldn't hold that position just by being a good person. Hwang Museok had committed a clear error. Even if he claimed ignorance, the result was that he'd paid less attention to the warriors. The responsibility for that also lay with Do Heon.

Sometimes one had to offer a carrot, and sometimes one had to use a whip. Sometimes, one had to maintain a suitable distance. The way one presented oneself had to change depending on the subordinate's personality and situation.

Do Heon realized he'd treated his subordinates in an overly personal manner until now. Having realized this, he sought to further hone himself as the Bright Demon Commander. He tried to become stricter at some things yet gentler at others, and more thorough.

*Of course, that's not all.*

He looked out the window. The world was gradually darkening. The southern Central Plains was generally humid, but the evenings in the Shiwan Mountain Range boasted quite a cool temperature.

*I'll have to wait and see.*

He'd resolved to burn his very life for the reform of the Divine Cult. Hwang Museok hadn't.

To be precise, Do Heon wasn't certain. Hwang Museok was clearly a loyal subordinate who served him well, but his eyes were fixed only on the Bright Demon Company, not the Divine Cult.

It would be perfect if Hwang Museok would work for his cause, but Do Heon had no certainty that he actually would.

That was why Do Heon had been receiving any new information from the higher-ups himself. To carry out a reform that hadn't even begun, he needed to receive and react to information sensitively.

If he truly disliked Hwang Museok, he would have cut him off long ago.

*Still, it's understandable that he'd feel slighted.*

He felt a little sorry, but decided not to dwell on it. Hwang Museok, as he himself had said, was a man who would lay down his life for the Bright Demon Company. That was enough, at least for now.

WHOOOOSH!

The wind blowing through the open window cooled his head.

He suddenly looked down at his own sleeve. The sleeve, cut off in a chunk and revealing his forearm, looked somehow empty.

Do Heon laughed softly. "What an expensive young friend we have."

Lee Cheonsang cleansed his entire body with a bath that lasted over two hours, then collapsed onto the bed.

*It's comfortable.*

The bed was perfectly soft, neither too much nor too little. Just lying down seemed to make all his accumulated fatigue disappear.

After lying still, he sat cross-legged, but he didn't do it specifically to perform qi circulation. He'd gathered enough demonic qi in the carriage, and his healing rate would increase as time passed.

He assumed this posture not only for qi circulation but also when he was lost in thought.

*Somehow, I too have become a martial artist.*

Lee Cheonsang released the tension in his body with a long exhale and reflected on the past six months.

*It was intense.*

They were days where he couldn't rest comfortably for even a single day. It was the path he'd chosen, but there were times he'd fainted, unable to overcome his fatigue.

To someone unaccustomed to such a life, the Demonic Coliseum was a hell beyond hell. One's spirit was bound to be worn down because the necessary acts of eating and sleeping weren't guaranteed.

Lee Cheonsang knew this through observation.

*I'm very sure now.*

He'd become more certain of one thing while fighting fiercely for half a year.

*I'm different from everyone else.*

Even when his body was tired, his mind didn't weaken. It wasn't because his mental strength was extraordinary. Instead, it felt as if his body and mind were completely separate.

No matter how tired he was, when the moment came to do something, he would simply do it.

Others were different. When they were tired, they got irritated, spat curses, or even threw punches out of anger.

Lee Cheonsang thoroughly observed and learned from such emotions and actions. There was no point in stubbornly insisting that he himself wasn't like that. To live in this world, it was necessary to carefully observe how ordinary people lived.

There were things he already knew and things he was learning anew.

*Emotions... desire...*

Lee Cheonsang unconsciously touched his chest.

*I must have those things too, right?*

It wasn't a guess, but a certainty. He'd never properly felt them, but it was said that even four-legged beasts had emotions. There was no way he didn't have them.

*But I don't know what it's supposed to feel like!*

Suddenly, he recalled his father's face. The face that had smiled even as he died from the sword that stabbed him.

He flinched involuntarily. The nape of his neck grew gradually hot, and his earlobes flushed red. His deep, calm breathing became faintly ragged. Tension gradually built in his back and shoulders.

WOOOONG!

The demonic qi lurking in his qi center slowly rose toward his chest, but a moment later, the demonic qi that had risen to his middle qi center scattered like smoke.

Lee Cheonsang's gaze regained its original indifference. "Why is it so difficult to understand emotions?"
